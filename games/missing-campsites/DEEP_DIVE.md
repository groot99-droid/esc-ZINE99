# Missing Campsites: deep dive

A design document with a point of view. Everything proposed here is an addition unless it is marked
as the source's or as already in `DESIGN.md`; section 6 lists every invention on one line each. The
source is `04_Book_Concepts/02_Missing_Campsites.md` in the vault and is not touched.

## 1. Where it stands

**What works.** The event of this game is an absence, and the design never cheats on that: nothing
is seen going, and nothing makes a sound when it goes. The split between a printed interface that
erases and a pencil notebook that holds is a real visual argument. Rule 4 (names go, counts stay)
turns a ledger into evidence. The dusk choice makes a person say what it will cost them before the
player confirms. The last line of Slipped Through is a true handoff.

**What does not, yet.**

1. **The notebook is never at risk, so the core verb has no price.** DESIGN.md says so in a table
   cell: threatened "Never". Comparing and writing are always right. A player who works this out at
   minute 10 has no hard decision about the game's main tool for the next 80.
2. **The middle has no reversal.** The schedule is fixed and readable by minute 37. From there day 2
   is an errand hub that adds weights to 12. The `knows` ladder "never decides an ending", and
   nothing the player learns between "it moves inward" and the wrong sky changes what they think is
   happening.
3. **The opposing force wants nothing, and this world's people are gauges.** "Each conversation is a
   reading of how far the loss has spread." Marisol and Abel are worth 5 each. They have ties (Ruth,
   Nell) but no history the player can find, and Tam's own want, the photographs for Agnes, stops
   mattering on day 2 and returns only in Held.
4. **The layers write flags, not each other.** Torchlight loops 1 and 3 hold no decision. Drift on
   the pattern walk "does nothing to the ending". Every 1D game raises a meter; none changes what
   stands in a 3D scene or what a 2D run asks. Each mode also has one trick, learned on its first
   appearance.

## 2. The story, developed

**Dramatic question.** When the record turns out to be the thing the place is collecting, will Tam
keep the page or keep the people?

**Want and need.** Tam wants proof: to bring Agnes her summers intact, and never to be the one who
forgets. Tam needs to be at the fire instead of at the page, and to let one thing go unwritten.

**The opposing force, and what it wants.** It is never seen or named, and why it exists stays
unexplained (DESIGN.md section 5 stands). What the player can learn is what it is doing: it is
packing. Each block of sites is a turn in "I packed my bag": it repeats everything it has, in
order, and adds some, and it never drops an item. It wants a campground with someone at the window
who knows the whole list. It is a host, not a hunter: on the last morning it has laid out food
nobody packed. The five interest it because they cannot stop noticing, and Tam most, because Tam
writes it down. The notebook is its packing list.

**The secret, and its seed.** The source: "everyone else — rangers, families, hikers — acts like
nothing is missing", and the place "has been pulling them in, piece by piece, since the moment they
arrived" (`04_Book_Concepts/02_Missing_Campsites.md`). DESIGN.md ends the as-written path with Tam
saying the ranger's line at an empty booth. The secret runs that handoff backwards: **Marisol was a
camper.** Eleven summers ago she walked out of the trees to an empty booth with a pencil notebook,
and a car pulled in. She ruled columns into the notebook and it became the logbook. She remembers
none of it, but her handwriting held (rule 6): the book's first page is still a Sites page in
pencil, with a party of five at 20 to 24 and no check-out. The player learns the shape (a chain of
campgrounds, each the far side of the last) and nothing about the cause.

**Characters.**

- **Marisol Teague** (deepened). *Want:* a book that adds up. *Secret:* the one above, kept from
  herself. *Scene, "The First Page":* in The Ranger's Book 2 she turns to the front "to show you how
  a book is kept", and the front is pencil. "I've never been able to throw that page out."
- **Ruth Teague** (NEW on stage; only a name in DESIGN.md). 58, drives a school bus. *Want:*
  Marisol retired and home. *Secret:* a recipe tin holding a list in Marisol's younger hand,
  written the week they met: five names. Marisol's, Abel Strand's, and three that belong to nobody
  anyone can find. *Scene, "Supper for Two":* 17:30 on day 2, Ruth at the booth with a covered dish.
- **Abel Strand** (deepened). *Want:* somebody to know where he has been. *Secret:* he was one of
  Marisol's five, and signs every register because the one time he did not, he lost a place.
  *Scene, "The Gravel Bar":* Tam shows him the list. "That's my name. In her hand. I've never met
  her." At the booth they shake hands like strangers and both keep looking.
- **June Okafor** (deepened). *Want:* to be believed. *Secret:* since night 1 a voice on channel two
  has played the game with her, saying the list half a second before she does. She keeps the chain
  on her forearm in biro, and her hand holds too. *Scene, "Channel Two":* Tam finds her in the split
  boulder after the first fire.

## 3. Obstacles and puzzles

Every element below is an addition; none changes a mode's type or graphic style.

### Torchlight loop (2D)

- **Teach:** tick posts; a post goes the first moment it is unlit.
- **Test:** the carrying run, with **borrowed light**: a camper's lantern holds its own post until
  it goes out (the Ashbys' holds 17), so the route is planned round other people's light.
- **Twist:** loop 3. Nothing is left to save, and the new control is the off switch.
- **New rules.** *Flash:* Inés's camera holds a post for three seconds, three times a run. *Short
  road:* each lost site takes 35 m out of the loop; only the pencil strip keeps the true length.
- **Set-piece:** at the edge past post 24, turn the torch off. Where there is no ground, one small
  square of light: a booth window, lit, waiting.

### The pattern walk (2D)

- **Teach:** one written detail, two candidate glyphs.
- **Test:** four candidates with overlapping details. **Reciting** (hold R) says the next item of the
  chain, backwards, and the true glyph hums; one use per item the player holds.
- **Twist:** the place built from the notebook. A row Tam wrote wrong is a real glyph, exactly as
  written.
- **New rules.** *Counted in:* stand in a glyph past eight seconds and its pad grows a sapling for
  each person standing there. Nothing removes one.
- **Set-piece:** the site you got wrong: a glyph that matches your line and not your memory.

### Reconcile (1D)

- **Teach:** three paper sources agree with each other and not with the ground.
- **Test:** three sources wrong in three ways, plus the **scale bar**: it shortens one step behind
  the loop, so measuring the road with it gives the forecast.
- **Twist:** Reconcile 3 gains a source if it was found: Marisol's first page. Same numbers, a
  different campground.
- **New rules.** *Sure:* three stamps a sheet. A stamped row cannot be "corrected"; a stamped row
  that is wrong is built as written (see the pattern walk). *Creases:* a reprinted sheet keeps its
  folds where the old ink was, so the creases show which squares moved.
- **Set-piece:** by headlamp, two notebooks side by side, and row 21 is one sentence in two hands
  eleven years apart: the split boulder, slept in the crack.

### Pack List (1D)

- **Teach:** the chain, and one phantom.
- **Test:** two phantoms and a **backwards lap**, June's house rule: say it in reverse. This is the
  practice for the walk.
- **Twist:** the empty seat plays to win, and the item it adds is one Tam wrote and never said.
- **New rules.** *June's arm:* once a round, read it. *A guest's item:* a guest adds one thing from
  home (Nell's telephone number). After dusk nobody outside remembers it; Tam may still say it.
- **Set-piece:** the seat's turn. The words rise in the band before any mouth moves.

### Foil Supper (1D)

- **Teach:** the card's own packet.
- **Test:** substitution, with **coal zones**: hot at the left, cool at the right, and each
  ingredient has a place it cooks well.
- **Twist:** breakfast. Unknown ingredients cook backwards, colder the longer they sit.
- **New rules.** *Read it to me:* with the card copied, Tam reads a line aloud and Dell seasons to
  it (one score-3 packet free). *The spare sheet:* from supper 2 there is one sheet too many. Fill
  it or leave it (`seat_fed`); the seat's next item is gentler if it was fed.
- **Set-piece:** a packet opens on Bea's supper, made from lines Tam never copied. It is exactly
  right and tastes of nothing.

### The Ranger's Book (1D)

- **Teach:** persons and permit order.
- **Test:** two days with a refund, plus **envelope stubs**: each drop-box envelope carries a licence
  plate, and numbers hold.
- **Twist:** the first page, and then book 3, where only counts are left and the counts are trees.
- **New rules.** *Carbons:* under a closed-up line the duplicate sheet keeps the pressure of the
  name, mirrored: its length and its capitals, never the word.
- **Set-piece:** on the oldest page, a row whose permit holder is the hand that kept the book.

### Across the game

**Links between layers.**

1. **Stubs to cars (1D to 3D).** A ghost row's plate matches a car in the booth lot that belongs to
   no site. It is unlocked. Inside is one thing with weight 1.
2. **Supper to the run (1D to 2D).** Each score-3 packet at supper 1 puts one friend on the road
   with a lantern in the carrying run: a second light, holding one post.
3. **Stamps to trees (1D to 2D and 3D).** Rows stamped Sure are built as written, and the maps in
   the rack at the other booth print Tam's errors.

**The long-running puzzle: the List.** Nineteen items, one for each lost site, in the order the
place packed them. They come from Pack List phantoms (the kettle is site 5, Puddle is 17), June's
arm, details Tam wrote, and the first page, which gives the order. They gather on a new notebook
page. Said backwards, the List is the route through the trees. At the window it decides the last
act: a Tam who holds the List can say the line, and in Unlisted there is no page to say it from.

**A second playthrough.** The logbook lies open on the counter at minute 1, and the map lens reads
its front page upside down. Marisol's first line is now somebody else's line. Abel and Marisol can
be introduced on day 1, which gives them a day and a half. Held with no person is the hard run;
Unlisted is the bare one.

## 5. Roadmap

**Phase 1: the List and the first page.** Add the List page to the notebook and the backwards lap to
Pack List. Add reciting to the pattern walk. Add the first-page spread to The Ranger's Book. Ship
art items 1 to 6. This is text, rules and SVG inside six existing web modules, and it gives the game
a midpoint and a puzzle that runs end to end.

**Phase 2: the people.** Channel Two, Supper for Two, the deepened Gravel Bar, and Ruth's car at the
dusk choice. The three layer links. Portraits and outlines. The storyboard grows by four panels, so
`tools/check_storyboards.py` and `tools/check_assets.py` run here.

**Phase 3: the price.** The notebook on the fire, and Unlisted. Sure stamps and rows built as
written. Borrowed light, flash and the short road. The six renders and the remaining ending cards.
Second-run details.

**Needs the author's decision before work starts.**

- Marisol as an earlier camper, and Abel as one of her five (phase 1).
- Ruth on stage (phase 2).
- Whether the notebook may be burned at all: DESIGN.md says it is never threatened (phase 3).
- A sixth ending. It changes `data/endings.json`, `data/flags.json` and section 13 together, and
  `python tools/validate.py missing-campsites` must pass afterwards without being loosened.
- Row 21 in two hands, which puts a doubt under Agnes's memory of the boulder.
