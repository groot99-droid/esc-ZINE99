# Missing Campsites: design

## 1. Summary

A campground erases itself a few sites at a time, from the entrance inward, and you are one of five
friends who can tell. Over three days you compare the map, the signs, the ranger's book and your own
handwritten notebook against the ground, and you choose what, and whom, to carry into the last five
sites before dark.

## 2. Why this game

Nothing in this game attacks, chases or kills. The player loses things, and the only defence is
noticing. The interface erases too, so the player learns to trust one surface: the notebook in their
own hand.

| | Missing Campsites | WrymWretch | Behind the Peephole |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| What is lost | The world's things, taken by a schedule the player can read | The world's memory, which the player restores | Nothing; other people hide things |
| The player's work | Compare records against reality, then decide what to carry | Record and retell to restore | Deduce hidden lives; answers are scored |
| Can it be undone? | No. Nothing erased comes back | Yes; memory is a resource | Not applicable |
| Threat | None that moves. Time and doubt | Combat and forgetting | Being seen |
| Endings decided by | Inventory: what and whom is inside the five sites at dark | Which pair at #Wrymreach, how much the world remembers | Accuracy of statements plus one choice |

Against *The Stuffed Ones* (the other game where memory is lost): there, you lose **your own**
memories. Here you keep yours perfectly, and the world forgets around you.

**The author's ending.** The pitch ends on beat 8: the campers have slipped across. That is the
as-written ending, **Slipped Through**. In play it follows when the player stays at the five sites,
keeps watching and writing, and does not carry enough into them to hold them in this world. The walk
on the other side and the arriving car that close the ending are added (section 21); the crossing
itself, the wrong sky, the patterned trees and the dead phones are the author's.

**What the vault's annotation sees.** The annotation calls the engine "a countdown told through
erosion rather than attack" in which "dread accumulates through subtraction", and notes there is "no
room shown for character interiority". The craft note `_Craft/cosmic-horror.md` reads the pitch as a
countdown where "the schedule does the work, not the campers" and shows a repair in which the
campers' attempts to keep evidence fail. This game takes that repair as its spine: every chapter, the
player tries to keep a record, and the world rewrites the record. The schedule stays fixed, as the
pitch has it; what changes is what the player does inside it.

## 4. Investment

**The hook in the first 5 minutes.** The van comes off the highway. The ranger hands a paper map
through the booth window and says "You're at the far end. Twenty through twenty-four." The player
walks the first stretch of the loop with the map raised. Posts 1, 2 and 3 are printed on the map and
listed in the legend. The ground where they should be is forest floor: no clearing, no fire ring, no
ruts, needles undisturbed. Holding the map up over the view (the map lens) shows three printed
squares floating over untouched ground. The first question is simple: where are they, and why does
nobody else mind?

**What the player comes to care about, and how, before it is threatened.**

| What | How the game makes the player care, in play | When it is first threatened |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Agnes Halloran's postcard** (Tam's mother) | In the glovebox at minute 2: six lines in her hand naming the sites of Tam's childhood summers. Tam promised photographs; she can no longer camp after a hip replacement. The player finds site 7 (the owl box) and site 12 (the creek bend) on day 1 and photographs them. | Overnight, day 1: site 7, its photo and her line about it go. |
| **Dell's grandmother's recipe card** | Foil Supper 1 is played from the card. It is a good meal, and Dell is happy. The player may copy the card into the notebook. | Day 2 dusk: the card's lines go blank. |
| **Lottie Ashby's bicycle** | Day 1, the player fixes the slipped chain for an eight-year-old at site 17; she lends the bike so the player can ride to the payphone. | Day 2 morning: Lottie does not remember having a bicycle. |
| **Marisol Teague, the ranger** | Day 1, through the booth window, she radios her wife: "Tell Ruth I'll be home for supper Sunday." She is kind and exact. | Day 2: she tells the player the numbering has always started at twelve. |
| **The four friends** | Day 1 setup and two suppers: each has a want the player helps with (section 6). | Day 2 dusk: the world beyond the five sites goes. |
| **The notebook** | Every 1D game writes into it. It is the only surface that never changes. | Never. It is the thing that holds. |

The rule is that nothing the player has not touched is taken from them on screen. The first
erasures (sites 1 to 6) take strangers' sites. Site 7 is the first loss that is the player's, and by
then the player has stood in it.

**The question that pulls them to the end.** By morning, what will be left, and what can I keep? On
day 2 it sharpens into a choice the player can see coming for an hour: who sleeps inside the five
sites tonight?

## 5. Lore bible

### History and timeline

| When | What | How the player learns it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| About 30 years ago | Tam's family camps at Hollin Creek every August, at site 21, beside Dell's family at 22. | Found: Agnes's postcard; told: Dell at the first fire. |
| Last spring | Agnes has a hip replaced and stops camping. She writes the postcard so Tam can "find our sites". | Found: the postcard; told: the payphone call. |
| This year | Tam books the last five sites, 20 to 24, for the old crowd and two new friends. | Told: Rafe, in the van. |
| Day 1, before arrival | Sites 1 to 3 are already gone. | Seen. |
| Day 1 to the final morning | The erasure schedule (section 12) runs. | Seen, then compared. |
| The final morning | The five sites are on the other side. | Seen. |

The game never explains why Hollin Creek is taken, or by what. The pitch does not, and the cosmic
register forbids it. What the player can learn is the shape of the taking: its order, its rules and
its traces.

### Rules of the world

These are the rules the player can learn and rely on. All are added for the game (section 21);
the first two stand on the pitch.

1. **Sites go from the entrance inward,** in a fixed order, a block at a time (section 12). "The next
   morning, more campsites have vanished" is the shape; the blocks are the game's.
2. **Memory goes with the thing.** When a site goes, everyone outside the five friends has always
   known the campground without it. The ranger renumbers without noticing: "Numbering's always
   started at four."
3. **Paper lags the land by one step.** Maps and the map legend keep a site for at least one block
   after the land loses it, until dusk on day 2, when the legend closes up to the five sites (section
   12, step 4). Signs stand until the end of day 2. This is why the player can catch the loss at
   all.
4. **Lines that name a lost thing go; numbers that only count stay.** A line in someone else's hand
   ("Site 7, the owl box") goes blank when site 7 goes. A total ("24 envelopes") stays. That is why
   the ranger's book stops adding up.
5. **The erasure is never seen happening.** A thing goes only when no one who remembers it is looking
   at it: outside the torch's circle, behind a closed page, between scenes.
6. **The player's own handwriting holds.** Nothing written in Tam's notebook ever changes, including
   lines that are wrong.
7. **What is carried into the five sites before dark holds with them, and leaves everyone else's
   memory.** The bicycle teaches this on day 2: Lottie no longer remembers having one. A person
   carried in is no longer remembered by anyone outside.

## 6. Characters

The pitch names no one. Every name, relationship and want below is added for the game (section 21).
Dialogue is sparse throughout, as in the vault: a burst of lines, never a scene's engine.

### Tam Halloran (the player)

33, works nights restoring old maps for a county archive, which is why Tam reads paper well. Booked
the five sites to bring the old crowd back to the place of the Halloran summers, and to photograph
them for Agnes. **Want:** to bring their mother the summers back in photographs. **Fear:** being the
one who forgets. **Voice:** Tam never speaks aloud in first person beyond choice lines; Tam's voice
is the notebook. Sample notebook lines: "Post 5. Blue kettle on the table. 4:10 pm." / "Marisol
says it starts at four. I counted from one." / "Nothing changes in here. Check."

### Odell "Dell" Amari

34, Tam's oldest friend; his family camped at 22. Married to Rafe. **Cares about:** his grandmother
Bea's foil-supper card, in her handwriting, and everyone eating together. **Fear:** the group
splitting. **Voice:** "Corn goes on last. Bea was firm." / "We eat first. Then we panic." / "Read me
the card. I can't look at it."

### Marisol Teague, ranger

55, eleven summers at the booth. Married to Ruth. Exact, warm, unbending about quiet hours. Keeps the

being wrong about her own book. **Voice:** "You're at the far end. Twenty through twenty-four." /

Can be brought in (`person_brought = ranger`) if she has seen her own book fail to add up
(`ranger_doubts`). If carried in, Ruth stops remembering her.

### Abel Strand, through-hiker

63, walking a long trail north for the second time; camps off-loop on the creek gravel. Signs every
register he passes, "so somebody knows where I've been". Calls his sister Nell from every payphone.
**Voice:** "Signed in. Always sign in." / "I'd remember writing that." / "That's my hand. In yours."

Can be brought in (`person_brought = hiker`) if he has seen his own register line, copied in Tam's
hand, after the register lost it (`abel_believes`). If carried in, Nell stops remembering him.

### The Ashbys at site 17

Corinne and Dev, and Lottie, 8. A first camping trip; a borrowed tent pitched badly; onions from
their garden. Lottie names everything and has a bicycle with a chain that slips. **Voice (Lottie):**
"That's Puddle. The bike's called Puddle." / "Whose bike is that? It's nice." / "Are we sleeping
here? Can we?" The family cannot be brought in (section 20, answer 3). They stay in this world
and forget with it; the only thing of theirs that can cross is the bicycle, and Lottie's forgetting
it on day 2 is the whole of what the player's carrying costs them.

### Agnes Halloran

68, Tam's mother. Heard once, on the payphone, and met at her door only in Held. **Voice:** "Find the
split boulder. You slept in the crack of it." / "That sounds like a lovely place."

## 8. Levels

Each day has a budget of **time units** (in-game hours) for errands that take Tam away from the five
sites. Walking, looking and talking at the five sites costs nothing. The 1D games are played where
Tam stands and cost the units listed.

### Chapter 1: Hollin Creek (14 min, day 1, 15:00–18:00)

**Goal.** Arrive, find the five sites, take Agnes's photographs, record what is wrong.

**Where.** The entrance road, the booth, the loop road's west leg to site 12, the east leg to 20, the
five sites.

| Order | Scene | Layer | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `booth_day1` | 3D | The van stops at the window. Marisol hands over the map. Optional: buy a firewood bundle ($8). The heron sign. Marisol on the radio to Ruth. |
| 2 | `loop_day1` | 3D | Tam walks the loop ahead of the van (Rafe drives at walking pace behind). Posts 1–3: absent. Map lens tutorial. Site 7: the owl box, photograph it. Site 12: the creek bend, photograph it. The register: Abel signs; Tam may copy his line. Site 17: Lottie's chain. |
| 3 | `fixed_sites_day1` | 3D | Pitch tents; meet the four; each friend's want, told in two lines; the split boulder at 21; the notebook's People page (writing a friend's portrait line sets `portrait.*`). |
| 4 | `reconcile_1` | 1D | At the table at 21: map against legend against what Tam saw. Three rows. First forecast. |

**Encounters.** None hostile. Marisol, asked about the first sites: "Numbering's always started at

**Ends** when Reconcile 1 is closed; Dell calls everyone to supper.

## 10. 2D modes

Both modes are top-down `scroll` walks on an orthographic camera 18 m above the ground, a view of
24 × 14 m, the world compressed so posts stand 12 m apart. The 3D camera rotates down into the 2D
view on entry (`flatten`) and back up on exit.

### Mode A: Torchlight loop (`torchlight_loop`)

**Movement.** `scroll`. WASD or left stick, 1.4 m/s; no jog. The torch is a circle of light 3.5 m
across centred on Tam. Everything outside it is unlit; the ground's texture is visible only inside
the circle.

**Rules.**

- **Ticks.** At a post, press T to tick it in the notebook (a pencil mark appears in the Tally strip
  at the screen's right edge, in Tam's hand). The printed HUD counter at the top left ("Posts: 16")
  is part of the interface and is allowed to change.
- **Erasure outside the light.** A post due to go goes the first moment it is outside the torch
  circle. A post inside the circle at its time holds until it leaves the circle.
- **Gaps.** Where a post has gone, the ground inside the torch shows only forest floor: no gravel
  apron, no fire ring. The road's gravel narrows by a hand's width there. That is the visual tell.
- **Battery.** `torch_battery` 0–100, drains 25 per minute of walking, 10 per minute standing.
  At 0, the circle shrinks to 1 m; June's walkie crackles and she comes down the road with a lantern
  and walks Tam home. There is no other failure.
- **Carrying (sequence 2 only).** Hold E for 2 s to pull a post. Carrying one post: speed ×0.6.
  Two (the most Tam can carry): ×0.45. Drop at any free slot at the five sites.
- **Headlights (The One Who Left only).** The van is driven, 4 m/s, and the light is a 40° cone
  9 m long ahead; a mirror inset shows a 6 m strip behind.

**Hazards.** None that harm. The only pressures are the battery and, in sequence 2, the schedule.

**Progress.** A thin strip of the loop at the bottom of the screen, drawn in pencil, shows where Tam
is. It is Tam's sketch, so it never loses anything.

**Sequences.**

| Scene | Route | Length | What it teaches or tests |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `torch_loop_1` | Site 20 → posts 19 to 4 → booth; payphone; booth → posts 4… | About 6 min | Ticking; the HUD counter drops from 16 to 13 while Tam is on the phone; posts 4–6 are gaps on the walk back. |
| `torch_loop_2` | Site 20 → posts 19 to 16 and back, repeatedly | 3–4 min | The carrying run. Times from the start: post 16 goes at 40 s, 17 at 70 s, 18 at 140 s, 19 at 170 s, unless in the light. A perfect run takes all four (two trips); a typical run takes two or three. Posts already pulled by day are absent from the run. |
| `torch_loop_3` | The circle of five, once round | About 3 min | The edge: past 20 and 24 the torch shows no ground at all. A soft push turns Tam back. The anchors stand lit in their slots. |
| `torch_drive_out` | The van from site 20 to the highway | About 4 min | Only in The One Who Left. Posts in the mirror go dark one by one as they leave the cone. |

**Failure and retry.** None. A sequence always ends; the battery ending is a rescue by June, and
the only loss is posts not taken.

### Mode B: The pattern walk (`pattern_walk`)

**Movement.** `scroll`, 1.2 m/s. The camera is higher (24 m) so the glyphs read whole.

**What the player sees.** Forest in which every tree stands where a thing stood. Each lost site is a
**glyph**: trees at the post's place, the table's corners, the fire ring's circle and the tent pad's
square, with a few trees in the pad for the number of people who slept there. The path out runs from
glyph to glyph in reverse order, 19 down to 1, the way the loop went.

**Rules.**

- At each glyph, two to four next glyphs are in view. One is the next site in order; the others are
  decoys of the same build with different details.
- Tam's Sites page opens as a strip at the side, showing the row for the site Tam is looking for.
  A written row names what was there (post 17: "bike, onions, a tent pitched crooked"). The true glyph
  carries that detail in trees: a wheel-ring of saplings, a crooked pad.
- An unwritten row is blank, and the player must guess among the candidates.
- With `pattern_key` (The Ranger's Book 3), each glyph also shows its person-count in pad trees,
  matched to the book's counts, so every junction can be read.
- If Marisol was brought, she reads the layout from memory and names one candidate at each junction
  ("The kettle site had the table by the creek."); she is right four times in five.
- Anchors that came across help: each carried post marks its own glyph with its number.

**Stepping off the path.** If Tam steps onto a wrong glyph, the ground forgets the step: the screen
goes white for half a second and Tam stands on the last true glyph. `drift` +1. Nothing chases,
nothing is lost, there is no death and no reset beyond that glyph.

**Progress.** Glyph numbers counted down in the pencil strip.

**Sequences.**

| Scene | Junctions | Length |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `pattern_walk_1` | 19 to 12 (8 junctions) | 4–5 min |
| `pattern_walk_2` | 11 to 1 (11 junctions) | 4–5 min |

**What drift does.** Nothing to the ending. It changes the ending's last image: with `drift` ≥ 6,
Tam reaches the booth as the car is already at the window (the passengers have taken a map from the
rack themselves).

## 11. 1D games

All four are full pages on paper: cream stock, graphite and printed ink. Each page opens with a
`push-in` from a surface in 3D (the table, the fire ring, the booth counter) and closes with a
`pull-out`. The world is paused beneath every page except where noted. Every page has the notebook's
right margin visible, so the player can always compare against it.

### Reconcile (`reconcile`): map comparison

**Purpose.** The main comparing tool: map against legend against notebook against what Tam saw.
Every correct row becomes a handwritten line that holds.

**Layout.** Left 60%: the paper map, with the loop and numbered squares; the legend strip under it,
one entry per site (number, symbol, "tent pad", "table", "fire ring"). Right 40%: the notebook's
Sites page, 24 ruled rows with columns Seen, Map, Legend, Note. A row of four source chips at top:
Ground (stamps from the 3D walk), Map, Legend, Photos (from day 2).

**Rules.**

1. For each site row, the sources show what they claim: Ground shows a stamp (here / not here / not
   walked), Map shows the printed square or not, Legend shows its entry or not, Photos shows a
   thumbnail.
2. The player sets the row's status: **Here**, **Gone**, or **Unknown** (not walked).
3. The player then writes the row: picks one detail from those Tam observed there (from a list of
   two to four details gathered in 3D), and confirms. The line is written in Tam's hand
   (`notebook_lines` +1, `sites_written` +1 if new).
4. **Accept.** When the Map or Legend disagrees with an earlier note, a button appears in printed
   type: "Correct your note to match." Using it rubs out the line (`accepted` +1, `sites_written`
   −1). It is always available and never required.
5. **Forecast.** At the end, drag a pencil line across the loop to mark where the loss will reach
   by a named time. Right within one site sets `forecast_right` (appearance 2) or writes a line
   (appearances 1 and 3).

**Inputs.** Mouse or stick to select rows and chips; one press to set status; a picker for the
detail; drag for the forecast.

**Finish.** Every row the page shows has a status; the forecast is placed. There is no fail state;
wrong statuses are written as wrong and hold as wrong.

### Pack List (`pack_list`): spoken memory chain

**Purpose.** The friends' fire game. The chain grows items nobody added, and those items are what
the campground has lost. Catching them is evidence; repeating them is accepting.

**Layout.** The fire ring seen from above at the page's centre, with seats as cards around it
(five, or more with a guest). The spoken chain runs as words in a band above the fire, each word
fading 4 s after it is said. Below: Tam's tile tray, a 4 × 4 grid of item tiles in pencil drawings.
A button: "Nobody said that."

**Rules.**

1. Each turn, a speaker says "I packed my bag, and in it I put", repeats every item so far in order,
   then adds one.
2. On Tam's turn, the player taps tiles in the chain's order, then taps one of three items from
   Tam's own pack to add.
3. **Phantoms.** At set points a speaker's repetition includes an item nobody added. It appears in
   the tray. On Tam's turn the player may include it (`accepted` +1) or press "Nobody said that" and
   tap it. A correct challenge writes a line (`phantoms_caught` +1, `notebook_lines` +1); the
   friend who said it looks at the fire: "Did I? … I did, didn't I." A false challenge (an item
   that was added) costs `warmth` −1.
4. **Mistakes.** A wrong order loses Tam the round: everyone laughs; nothing else is lost.
5. The round ends when a friend fails, scripted, at the lap the table sets.

**Inputs.** Tap or select tiles; one button.

**Finish.** The round ends. Finishing without a mistake: `warmth` +1.

### The Ranger's Book (`rangers_book`): ledger arithmetic

**Purpose.** Prove, with numbers, that the campground had sites nobody remembers. Persuades the
ranger. On the other side it becomes the key to the trees.

**Layout.** The logbook open across the page. Left page: check-in rows (Site, Party, Persons,
Nights, Fee, Permit no.). Right page: the day's totals in Marisol's hand (Occupied sites, Persons,
Fees, Envelopes in the drop box). A pencil column beside each total for Tam's working, and a tray of
blank "ghost rows" to insert.

**Rules.**

1. Rows naming a lost site are gone; the lines close up. Totals do not name sites, so they stay
   (rule 4). Permit numbers were issued in arrival order.
2. The player sums columns (tap a column to add it; an auto-sum toggle is in accessibility) and
   compares each sum to its total.
3. For each shortfall, insert a ghost row and fill it: persons from the persons gap, fee from the
   fee gap at $18 a night, permit number from the skipped sequence, and site number from where its
   permit falls in arrival order against the sites around it.
4. A ghost row that satisfies every total locks in pencil. A ghost row that breaks a total stays
   unlocked; nothing is lost.

**Inputs.** Tap columns, drag ghost rows, number pickers.

**Finish.** Every total balances.

## 12. Core systems

### The erasure schedule

Everything erased goes at a fixed step. The step advances at the moments listed, always during a
transition or out of sight (rule 5). `erasure_step` records it.

| Step | When | Land and things | Map and legend | Photos | Lines others wrote | Dialogue log, objectives |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | Before the game | Sites 1–3 | Map and legend still show 1–3 | — | — | — |
| 1 | Day 1 dusk, while Tam is on the payphone | Sites 4–6; the blue kettle | Legend drops 1–3 and closes up; the HUD post counter drops 16 → 13 | Any photo of 4–6 shows a clearing | — | Lines about 1–3 leave the log |
| 2 | Night 1, sleep | Sites 7–11 | Legend drops 4–6; map stops printing 1–6 | Site 7's photo: a clearing | Postcard lines for 7 and 9; Abel's register line (site 11 named in it) | The objective "Photograph site 7 for Mum" goes from the list |
| 3 | Day 2 noon, chapter transition | Sites 12–15; Abel's ramps | Legend drops 7–11; map stops printing 7–11 | Site 12's photo | Postcard line for 12; chalkboard lines naming 13–15 | Lines naming 7–15 |
| 4 | Day 2 dusk, during the carrying run | Sites 16–19; the booth, the store, the register, the road beyond 20; corn, butter, onions; this world's people, unless inside | Legend drops 12–19 and the map's title "HOLLIN CREEK" goes; the legend lists 20–24 | All photos of anything outside 20–24 | Postcard lines for 16 and the heron (unless the sign was carried in); the recipe card's heading and lines naming the store; Marisol's book lines (unless carried in) | Every line spoken by someone outside the five sites |
| 5 | Night 2, the crossing | The five sites cross, with all they hold | The legend's five entries remain; the map shows only the five | Photos show the other side | Postcard line for 21 holds (it crossed) | The objectives list is empty |

**How the player can tell.** The interface never animates an erasure, so every surface carries one
trace that a careful player can catch without the notebook:

| Surface | Trace |
| --- | --- |
| Map legend | The legend box keeps its height; a blank band grows at its bottom, one line per lost entry. |
| Photo gallery | File numbers skip (IMG_0007, IMG_0011). |
| Lines in another hand | Held at a low angle under the torch (hold F on any 1D page at night), the paper shows the pencil or pen grooves of a lost line. The words cannot be read. |
| Dialogue log | Timestamps skip. |
| Objectives list | Numbers skip (1, 2, 4). |
| HUD post counter | It disagrees with the pencil ticks in the Tally strip. |

### Time units

| Day part | Units | Bonuses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Day 2 morning | 3 | — |
| Day 2 afternoon | 5 | +1 `forecast_right`; +1 helper from `theory_night1` (`erasing` or `moving`) |

Each unit is one in-game hour; the sky and the shadows move with it. When the afternoon's units are
spent, Dell calls everyone to the van at 19:30.

### Anchors and hold

Ten slots at the five sites, two per site (the table and the tent-pad edge). A person fills both
slots of one site. `hold` is the sum of the weights in the slots at the moment the carrying run
ends.

| Anchor | Weight | Slots | How it is had |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The ranger's logbook | 3 | 1 | The Ranger's Book 2 (lent), or taken |
| The master map | 2 | 1 | Booth wall, 1 unit |
| The heron sign | 2 | 1 | 2 units, or 1 with `warmth` ≥ 6 |
| Posts 16, 17, 18, 19 | 1 each | 1 each | By day, 1 unit each; or in the carrying run, free |
| The quiet-hours board | 1 | 1 | 1 unit |
| The firewood bundle | 1 | 1 | Bought on day 1 |
| Lottie's bicycle | 1 | 1 | Borrowed on day 1 |
| Marisol | 5 | 2 | `ranger_doubts`, asked at dusk; brings the logbook with her if Tam has not got it (it shares her site) |
| Abel | 5 | 2 | `abel_believes`, asked at dusk |

Things carry no weight if they belong to the five friends (they are already inside) or if they are
records of something already lost (a photograph, a drawing of site 7).

The Held threshold is **12**. With every anchor and no person, the most is 14 in ten slots, and it
needs nearly every unit and a near-perfect carrying run. With a person, 12 is easy. That is the
temptation, and the reason a person is the hardest choice: it is the cheapest way to hold, and its
cost lands on someone else.

### Acceptance

`accepted` counts every time the player takes the world's word over their own. There are ten
chances in a full run: Marisol on day 1 and day 2, the three Reconciles, Inés's photo ("I must have
framed it wrong"), Lottie's bicycle ("You're right, it's ours"), and the three Pack List phantoms.
At 3 or more, the second fire offers a theory that is otherwise hidden: "Nothing's wrong. We're
tired." Taking it with `accepted` ≥ 5 is the Nothing Missing ending.

### Warmth

`warmth` 0–10, starts at 4. +1 or +2 from suppers, +1 for a clean Pack List round, +2 for an
afternoon unit spent with the friends, +1 when the first theory matches what most friends think
(`erasing`). −1 for a false challenge or a logbook taken unasked. At ≥ 6 at dusk, the heron sign
costs one unit. It also chooses each friend's last line in every ending: at ≥ 7 they speak to Tam;
below 4 they speak past Tam.

### Theories at the fire

| Fire | Option | Needs | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Night 1 | "We're tired. It's a new map." (`tired`) | — | `warmth` +2; no helper on day 2; `accepted` +1 |
| Night 1 | "Something's erasing the campground." (`erasing`) | — | Inés helps on day 2 (+1 unit, a post by day) |
| Night 1 | "It's moving. Toward us." (`moving`) | `knows` ≥ `pattern` | Rafe helps on day 2 (+1 unit); Rafe asks about leaving at dusk |
| Night 2 | "Nothing's wrong. We're tired." (`nothing_wrong`) | `accepted` ≥ 3 | With `accepted` ≥ 5: Nothing Missing |
| Night 2 | "We hold. We watch." (`we_hold`) | — | The story's path |
| Night 2 | "It isn't taking the campground. It's taking us." (`we_are_going`) | `knows` ≥ `scale` | `knows` becomes `implicated` a scene early; the friends sleep at one fire |

### Knowledge

`knows` follows the cosmic register's ladder and only rises: `anomaly` (posts 1–3), `pattern`
(torchlight loop 1), `scale` (The Ranger's Book 1: the world rewrites its own records), `implicated`
(the second fire's third theory, or the final morning). It gates theory options and the friends'
lines; it never decides an ending.

### Slipped Through (as written)

The story's own path. Final scenes: `wrong_forest`, `foil_supper_3`, `pattern_walk_1`,
`the_clearing`, `pattern_walk_2`, `other_entrance`. The final morning is the author's: the wrong sky,
the heavy air, the patterned trees, the phones with "no signal, no time, no date". Then the added
close: the walk out through the trees that were the campground, to a booth with nobody in it, and a
car pulling in. Its passengers ask if they are in the right place. Tam looks at the map in the rack,
which shows 24 sites. **Last line,** Tam's, the first time Tam speaks aloud: "You're at the far end.
Twenty through twenty-four." The player has become the one who acts as if nothing is missing. (In
the vault's terms, a handoff ending.)

### Brought Along (proposal)

As Slipped Through, with the guest. Marisol reads the trees from memory and, at the end, steps into
the empty booth and says the line herself. Abel signs a register that has no other names. **Last
image:** the
guest at the window, the car's headlights on their face, someone at home who no longer knows to
wait.

### Held (proposal)

Final scenes: `gravel_morning`, `foil_supper_3`, `mothers_door`. The five sites hold in this world.
The morning is ordinary. The campground does not come back; nothing erased does. The road is a mile
of gravel to the highway. Everything carried in, and everyone, is safe, and is remembered by no one
outside: the guest's family does not know them, and the friends' families do not know them either.
The van drives home. At Agnes's door: if Tam kept her line about site 21, Tam reads it to her: "Site
21. The split boulder. You slept in the crack of it." **Last line,** Agnes: "That sounds like a
lovely place." She does not know who Tam is. If no line was kept, she asks politely who Tam is
looking for.

### Nothing Missing (proposal)

Final scenes: `ordinary_morning`, `foil_supper_3`, `drive_home`. Tam has stopped writing and taken
the world's word. The morning is ordinary and the campground is whole from post 4. The cooler has
one more packet than anyone needs. In the van, the friends whose portrait line Tam never wrote are
not there, and no one mentions them; if Tam wrote all four, a sleeping bag in the back belongs to no
one. The notebook rides on the dash and cannot be opened. **Last image:** the highway, a seat with
nobody in it, the radio giving the date.

### The One Who Left (proposal)

Final scenes: `torch_drive_out`, `home_apartment`, `reconcile_home`, `recital`. Tam drives out alone
before dark on day 2. Tam gets home. The city is ordinary; nobody remembers the four friends or the
campground. A highway rest area is where Hollin Creek was. Reconcile at home compares the notebook
against the online map, Inés's camera roll and the phone's contacts. Then Monday at four: Ada's
recital. **Last image:** Tam in the second row with a programme on the empty seat beside them, and
the notebook open at Rafe's page: "Rafe. Four o'clock Monday. That's all I've got."

### How a player reaches each one on purpose

| Ending | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Slipped Through | Stay. Keep writing. Do not ask anyone to stay, and do not fill the slots past 11. This is what a player who simply follows the friends will get. |
| Brought Along | Persuade one person (finish The Ranger's Book 2 with Marisol, or show Abel his copied line), ask them to stay at dusk, and carry little else. |
| Held | Carry 12 or more weight into the slots. The easy way: a person plus the logbook. The hard way, with no person: buy the firewood and borrow the bike on day 1, call the forecast right, spend every unit on anchors, and take all four posts. |
| Nothing Missing | Accept five times (each "Correct your note", each phantom repeated, each "Must have read it wrong"), then choose "Nothing's wrong" at the second fire. |
| The One Who Left | At dusk on day 2, take the keys from Rafe and choose "Drive out alone". |

**Why this run got this ending.** The debug panel shows the resolver's trace: each condition in
priority order, the values it read (`left_alone`, `accepted`, `theory_night2`, `hold`,
`person_brought`), and the first that was true.

The HUD is small, printed and erasable. The notebook is drawn and permanent. That difference is the
game's whole visual argument.

| --- | --- | --- |

| Map lens | The paper map held up at 40% opacity over the view, aligned to the ground by a compass rose; printed squares sit where sites should be. | Diegetic |
| Notebook (N) | A full-screen spread in Tam's hand: Sites, People, Copied, Tally tabs. Never changes. | Diegetic |
| Camera (C) | Inés's camera back: a 3 × 3 gallery with file numbers. | Diegetic |

| Slots | At each site, the two slots show as chalk marks Dell drew on the table and the pad edge. | Diegetic |

**How the layers look different.** 3D is full colour, first person, depth of field off. 2D is a
top-down ink-and-wash view with the torch circle in full colour and everything else in blue-grey
line; the pencil strip of the loop runs along the bottom. 1D is paper filling the screen, the
notebook's margin always at the right.

**Settings.** Text size (four steps) on every 1D page; slow or pause every timer (coal bar, carrying
run, Pack List fade); Hold the chain; auto-sum in The Ranger's Book; Show seams; subtitles always on
by default; remappable controls; a colour-blind safe palette (status colours in Reconcile are also
shapes: circle, cross, dash).

**Content note at start:** "No violence, no chase, no jump scares. Loss of places, records and
people from memory; an adult may be taken from their world if the player chooses and they agree.
The game changes its own screens on purpose: if something you saw is gone, it is not a bug."

## 15. Art direction

**Mood references.** Late-summer state-park campgrounds; paper ephemera (ranger forms, carbon
copies, map legends); the quiet unease of empty public places at the wrong hour. Nothing taken from
any existing game's look. The forest service at the booth is fictional, with a heron for a badge,
and never resembles a real agency's branding.

**Palette.**

| Use | Colours |
| --- | --- |
| Day 1 | Sun-faded greens, cedar brown posts, dust gold, the van's dull red |
| Day 2 | The same greens a step greyer; skies overcast; fewer saturated accents each block |
| Night | Torch 3000 K amber in a blue-black field; firelight 2200 K |
| Other side | Pearl sky with no gradient and no sun; greens with no yellow in them; no shadows |
| Paper | Cream stock, graphite grey, printed ink in forest green |

**Materials.** Matte everything; wet gravel at the creek; cedar end-grain on the posts; painted
plywood on the heron sign. On the other side every surface is slightly too clean.

**Lighting rules.** On the other side, light comes from all directions and nothing casts a shadow.
Trees on the other side are spaced on exact intervals; the player should feel the regularity before
naming it.

**One look across layers.** The same paper and graphite run through the 1D pages, the 2D pencil
strip and the 3D map lens and notebook. The torch circle in 2D uses the 3D scene's own colours, so
the lit patch reads as a window into the 3D world. The palette and materials are shared across
layers, while each 2D and 1D page is drawn in its own style (see Gameplay graphics).

### Gameplay graphics

Each 2D mode and 1D game keeps this palette but is drawn in its own graphic style, unique in the
collection (`docs/COLLECTION.md` section 7). Pictographs: `pictographs/<key>.svg`.

| Mode or game | Layer | Graphic style | How the style serves play |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Torchlight loop | 2D | Charcoal chiaroscuro | Smudged dark everywhere but the amber circle, so the player sees only what the torch holds, and a missing post reads as bare forest floor inside the light. |
| The pattern walk | 2D | Op-art geometric | Exactly spaced tree glyphs make the forest's rhythm the map: the safe path is the regular one, and a written detail breaks the tie between candidates. |
| Reconcile | 1D | Topographic survey print | Contours, printed site squares and a green-ink legend are sources to compare, and a missing legend row or square stands out against the survey's order. |
| Pack List | 1D | Wax crayon on kraft paper | A children's fire game drawn as one: seats round the fire as cards, the chain above, and the phantom item circled where nobody put it. |
| Foil Supper | 1D | Two-colour risograph recipe card | Two inks, misregistered, keep ingredient roles and the coal bar legible at a glance while the recipe's blanks show as empty print. |
| The Ranger's Book | 1D | Ledger in graphite | Ruled columns and pencil sums set the printed totals against Tam's working, so the shortfall the ghost rows fill is the picture. |

## 20. Risks and open questions

| Risk | Mitigation |
| --- | --- |
| Quiet wrongness does not hold attention with no monster. | A new comparison surface erases every chapter; the player is always holding a decision (units, slots, a person). Playtest the minute 19–26 walk first. |
| Players think the interface is buggy. | Every erasure leaves a trace (section 12); the first one (the HUD counter) is pointed at by Inés: "Your counter's wrong." The content note says the game changes its own screens. |
| The person choice reads as a puzzle optimum, not a moral one. | The cost is spoken by the person before confirming; the bicycle has already shown it on a small scale; Held without a person is possible, so the player is never forced. |
| Nothing Missing is reached by accident. | The theory is hidden below 3 acceptances; acceptance buttons are always worded as accepting. |
| The One Who Left is short. | Its home chapters run 76 minutes total, inside the target. |

**Answered** (by Claude at the author's request, 2026-11-12, from the source text; the author can overrule any of them).

1. **Held's cost stays forgetting: the five sites hold, and nobody outside them remembers anyone
   on them.** The pitch's own word for loss is not destruction but forgetting: the first sites are
   "Not destroyed. Not taped off. Not overgrown." and of the next morning's, "No one notices. / No
   one cares. / No one even remembers they were there. / Except the campers."
   (`04_Book_Concepts/02_Missing_Campsites.md`). Held turns that sentence on the campers: they are
   what is left over, and the world forgets them as it forgot the sites. That keeps the cost the
   vault's horror rule asks for ("No tidy, rescued endings in Horror-Prose mode", vault
   `CLAUDE.md`) without inventing a second mechanism, and it is the same cost The One Who Left
   already pays. The earlier draft's "everyone left outside is gone" stays cut. Nothing changed:
   section 13 and rule 7 already say this.
2. **Yes to both: the other side is the lost campground rebuilt in trees, and Slipped Through ends
   with Tam saying Marisol's line to new arrivals.** The trees stand on the author's own image, "The
   trees are arranged in patterns that shouldn’t exist", read through the next sentence: a
   dimension "that has been pulling them in, piece by piece, since the moment they arrived". The
