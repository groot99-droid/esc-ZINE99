# Project: Man Eater — deep dive

Source: `03_Stories/07_Project_-_Man_Eater.md` in `creative-writing/vault/` (cited below by line).
Nothing here edits the source or `DESIGN.md`. Everything marked NEW, and everything in section 6, is
a proposal for the author to approve or strike. The Zoo stays `as_written`. The figure stays a
backlit shape with no origin, and the cultural-consultation step (M0, M6, M9) governs all of it.

## 1. Where it stands

**What works.** The design's central idea is sound and is the game's own: strength and clarity pull
against each other, and the interface is the mind. The greyed "Stop him" on night 1 makes the player
the man who "didn't even try to stop his bunkmate" (line 30). The deck that loses a suit with each
chair is the design's best image.

**What is weak.**

1. **The middle only accumulates.** Days 2 to 6 each break one explanation. By day 5 ("on schedule")
   the player knows the truth, and the pens on night 6 confirm it. Nothing changes what the player
   thinks their own actions *mean*. There is no reversal.
2. **Fisk has no want.** "To do the posting and not think about it" is the absence of one. The
   source gives him more: New Mexico, where "Fisk had been one of the lucky ones" (line 28). The
   design does not use it.
3. **Sam is a clipboard.** The source hands him a contradiction at his moment of victory, "a smirk
   painted across his face, his eyes twisted in an attempt to mask his dissatisfaction" (line 34),
   and a history, "the same mistakes as we have with the rest" (line 35). The design quotes both and
   explains neither.
4. **The layers feed meters, not each other.** Bread opens the door and Floor Plan opens the stair;
   those two links are good. But a pocketed ration piece has no use, Snow line's prints are
   forgotten by morning, and the Chart changes nothing outside its page. Each web mode is one trick.
5. **Two endings are thin.** Transferred costs only ignorance. Call Me Sam hangs on one gate. Bread
   given to the crew reorders the takings and changes nothing in the story.

One art note: `reference/09-the-flute.jpg` shows the figure close, frontal and dressed. The rule is
a backlit shape at distance. Replace it.

## 2. The story, developed

**The dramatic question.** *Can a man who survives by not minding make one act the base has not
already scheduled, before the food takes the mind he would need to make it?*

**Want and need.** Fisk wants to be "one of the lucky ones" (line 28) again: get through, shelve it,
complain about nothing. He needs to try to stop someone, the one thing night 1 took from him, even
when trying will not work.

**The opposing force (NEW reading).** The project already has monsters; the pens are full of them.
What it lacks is a changed man who still answers "Sir yes Sir." The source says what goes wrong: "As
his mind went blank and his thoughts became those of an animal" (line 36). *That* is "the same
mistakes as we have with the rest" (line 35): the rest went blank. So the project now keeps a
subject's mind lit while the meat works, the way a zoo keeps an animal from going dull: with
enrichment. A companion. A loss to witness. A lie clumsy enough to catch ("about when you would have
been asleep", line 32). A card table. A cabinet left unlocked. The author's last line, "He was like
an animal in a zoo..." (line 36), is the seed: everything Fisk does to resist, and every puzzle the
player solves, is the zoo's enrichment. Sam is not trying to destroy Fisk's mind. He needs it.

It also explains the dissatisfaction. Rule two is the test: the changed man who still stays in the
room is the result. A subject who follows the call has given in, so The Zoo is Sam's failure too.

**Three characters, deepened (NEW).**

- **Sam.** *Want:* one result before his superiors replace him. *Secret:* his own name is on a form
  in his drawer, "Project head: review, day 8. Results required: 1." *Scene, "Results Required" (day
  6):* after "Coming along.", if Fisk has read his file, he adds one line and waits to see whether
  Fisk can still be angry.
- **Jax.** *Want:* to know "why they had been lied to" (line 33). *Secret:* he was posted here for
  refusing to sign a statement against a man at his last station. That is why he will not "leave a
  list". He is not a plant; his file says "companion (uninformed)". *Scene, "Jax Unties the Knots"
  (day 5):* shown the page that has his plan typed on it a day early, he undoes the knots on Fisk's
  bed frame one by one.
- **Hollis.** *Want:* out, next week. *Secret:* he has already taken the offer Sam makes to Fisk.
  *Scene, "Next Week" (day 6):* his chair is empty at breakfast, not at night. He is behind the
  hatch in staff greys, and the gaze at the Tray is now his. If Fisk takes the key instead, Hollis
  is taken as DESIGN.md has it. Someone always fills the seat.

**The secret under the story: the Schedule (NEW).** Seeded by "Even though the barracks are
relatively new, they appear rundown; deterioration and other issues have already occurred" (line
28): this has all run before. Each subject has a typed sheet, one line per day, with a blank for
"response". Fisk finds his on night 4 with tomorrow already typed. One slot on every day is empty:
22:00. The project plans around the call and has written nothing about what makes it. Nor does the
game.

## 3. Obstacles and puzzles

All rules below are NEW. No mode or game type is added to those `docs/COLLECTION.md` gives.

### Silhouette halls

- **Teach** (night 2): light, shadow, crouch. **Test** (night 4): the shadow turns the corner first;
  the pipes. **Twist** (night 6): Jax walks with you, and your shadow becomes his cover.
- **Shadow cover.** Anyone standing inside Fisk's shadow is not seen by a cone.
- **Stoop.** Behind a hung laundry sheet, a silhouette held at 1.9 m or less reads as an orderly. An
  eater must fold down to pass for a man.
- **Set-piece, "He Ran".** At `change` 68, the first guard who sees only the ceiling-high shadow
  drops his flashlight and backs away for 20 seconds. The player learns what the guards know.

### Snow line

- **Teach** (night 3): prints stay. **Test** (night 5): the bare prints, 2 m apart, fit only a long
  stride. **Twist** (night 7): no cover, and prints have a direction.
- **Toe-ward.** A patrol follows a trail the way the toes point. Walking backward in your own prints
  (hold, 40% slower) sends it the wrong way.
- **Big prints are not followed.** At 38 cm (`change` 64) patrols step over them. They are
  photographed, and the route is on the orderly's clipboard next morning.
- **Set-piece, "Frame 112".** The carry. Jax on Fisk's shoulders, 0.8 m/s, deep prints, 400 m. Then
  back, alone, in every print, the call pulling him home faster than he left. The frame shows one
  line. Caption: "No crossing."

### Short Rations

- **Teach** (days 1 to 2): bid exact. **Test** (days 3 to 5): the deck shrinks, the tracker goes.
  **Twist** (day 6): open hands.
- **The withdrawn 8.** Each empty chair's 8 stays in the deck, stamped WITHDRAWN. It follows no suit
  and wins nothing: the one clean way to duck a trick.
- **The staff loaf.** From day 4 one loaf is wrapped in a white napkin. Take it and tomorrow's gaze
  rests on you 1 second longer.
- **Set-piece.** The day-6 hand against Jax, all sixteen cards face up, one loaf. The player can see
  the one line of play that gives it to him. Jax taps anyway.

### The Tray

- **Teach** (days 1 to 2): eat under the gaze. **Test** (days 3 to 5): pocket, pass, cheek.
  **Twist** (day 7): the player eats on purpose.
- **The cut is yours.** Cut the slab into 3 to 10 pieces. The orderly counts pieces left, not
  weight. Coarse cuts hide a refusal; each big piece eaten changes more.
- **Someone eats it.** Any man on the bench will trade his bread heel for two pieces. He eats them:
  his pull +2.
- **The napkin has an end.** Pocketed pieces are flushed on a night out, eaten at the door for
  strength, or found by the laundry on day 5 (suspicion +10 each).
- **Set-piece.** Day 7, ten pieces, "Eat up. Big night.", Hollis watching. The carry needs strength
  88, so the player clears the tray for a decent reason.

### The Chart

- **Teach** (day 2): two cells and a card. **Test** (days 3 to 5): correlations, the second sheet.
  **Twist** (day 6): the hypotheses are now the project's, about Fisk.
- **Your word is a row.** The player enters self-reported hunger. Under-report and tomorrow's slab
  is smaller; three mismatches with grip and pulse raise suspicion.
- **The cut column.** A taken man's column is scissored off. A pencil rub on the sheet beneath
  raises his last numbers, and shows who is next.
- **Set-piece.** Day 6. The cards read "Subject will report companion", "Subject will follow". Stamp
  one Broken, and the orderly, who never looks at Fisk's face, looks up.

### Floor Plan

- **Teach** (day 2): stamps and paces. **Test** (day 4): the stride trap. **Twist** (day 6): turn
  the sheet and draw the building in elevation.
- **Ghost lines.** Three pencil rubs a session raise the older plan under the blueprint: the bunk
  rooms' bolts were once on the outside.
- **Knots to posts.** Jax's count of the fence is a knotted bootlace. Convert it to metres to mark
  the gap; only a marked gap appears on night 7's frame.
- **Set-piece, "The First Chart".** Place the lever (2.4 m), pipes (2.7 m), windows (3.5 m) and claw
  marks (3.6 m). The page draws the occupant that fits: 338 cm, the last number on the printed
  target curve.

### Across the game

**Three links.**

1. *Tray to Snow line.* What Fisk eats sets his print size: followed below 38 cm, photographed
   above.
2. *Short Rations to the Tray to the door.* The staff loaf buys a quiet night and a longer gaze;
   bread given to Hollis shortens his on day 7.
3. *Floor Plan to both 2D modes and 3D.* Height marks show the pipe route in the halls; the knotted
   gap opens the fence; the void opens the stair.

**The long puzzle: the Schedule.** A second sheet under the Chart. One torn strip is found per night
out. The player lays each against a list entry and learns that every discovery was issued. Day 7's
column has typed outcomes for stay, follow and fence. The carry is not on it; the orderly writes it
in by hand.

**A second playthrough.** Nothing carries over except what the player knows: the cabinet was left
open, and can be declined. An eater and a refuser play different halls and snow, and Hollis at the
hatch, the fourth answer to Jax and the carry are all missable.

## 5. Roadmap

**Phase 1: the reversal, on paper (least work, biggest change).** Write the Carbon scene and the
Schedule as a second sheet in `chart.js`. Add the withdrawn 8 to `short-rations.js` and the free cut
to `tray.js`. Make the title, stamps, chapter cards and card faces. All text and SVG.

**Phase 2: the layers talk.** Shadow cover, the stoop and "He Ran" in the halls. Toe-ward
prints and the size rule in the snow. Self-reported hunger and the cut column on the
Chart. Portraits, Hollis at the hatch, the Glovebox.

**Phase 3: the new way out.** The carry and One Set of Prints. The elevation strip and ghost lines.
The two-height Blender renders. The figure's replacement still.

**The author decides before work starts:**

- the enrichment reading of "the same mistakes" (it reframes Sam's lie as deliberate);
- One Set of Prints: a fifth ending, a variant of The Zoo, or struck;
- the roster numbers on the crates (they sharpen the implied cannibalism);
- Hollis taking the staff seat;
- the fourth answer to Jax, and Jax's reason for keeping no list.

**The consultant sees first:** the empty 22:00 slot on the Schedule, and the new figure still.
