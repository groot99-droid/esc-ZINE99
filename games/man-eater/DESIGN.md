# Project: Man Eater — design

Slug `man-eater`. Source: `03_Stories/07_Project_-_Man_Eater.md` in `creative-writing/vault/`.
Every quotation in this document is verbatim from that file and is cited by its line number in
the file (the story's text runs from line 25 to line 36). Everything not quoted is a proposal for
the game, and section 21 lists it so the author can approve or strike it.

Content note shown before the title screen: *This game contains body horror, starvation, implied
cannibalism, coercion by an institution, medical captivity and the suggestion of amputation. None
of it is shown in detail.*

## 1. Summary

Agent Fisk is posted to a base in the North Dakota woods with three rules, and the food that makes
him strong enough to break them is what is turning him into the thing the base was built to hold.
Over seven days and nights he eats, measures his own body, plays cards for bread with a crew whose
chairs empty one by one, and decides which rule to break, and at what cost.

## 2. Why this game

The nearest games are *The Stuffed Ones* (both turn the player into the monster) and *Behind the
Peephole* (both keep the player in one building at night). `docs/COLLECTION.md` section 5 sets the
differences; this table shows how the design keeps them.

| | Project: Man Eater | The Stuffed Ones | Behind the Peephole |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Core verb | Eat | Hold still | Watch |
| How you become the thing | Gradually, one meal at a time, measured on your own chart every morning | Suddenly, at an act break | You do not |
| Alone or together | Alone. The friend you make is taken from the table | One of a pack, sharing a pulse | Alone, behind a door |
| What you investigate | Your own body. You are the evidence | A stitched book | Other people's lives, with true answers |
| Where you can go | Anywhere in the base, at the cost of being seen and of the cold | The house, under the guests' sight | Never into the rooms you watch |
| Night structure | A day of duties, then one choice at 10:00 | Three nights per group, looping groups | A time budget across six doors |
| Run to run | Each run stands alone. Nothing carries over | The next run inherits your shelf | One run of seven nights |
| Endings decided by | A ledger of meals and rules broken | Hidden per-night meters and the book | Accuracy of statements and one last choice |

The design's own idea: **strength and clarity pull against each other, and the game shows the
pull on its own surface.** The more Fisk eats, the more the game lets him do (climb, carry, bear
the cold, cross the fence) and the less it lets him think (fewer words, fewer options, coarser
numbers). The player's body is the progress bar, and the interface is the mind.

### The author's ending

**The Zoo.** Fisk follows the call with Jax, meets the figure, is gassed, hears Sam in the cave, and
wakes transformed: "He was like an animal in a zoo..." (line 36). It is in the game as the ending
marked `as_written`, and it is what follows when the player keeps to the story's path: eat what
is served, follow the call, go with Jax.

### What the game keeps exactly

- Sam's three rules, word for word, on the bunk door's printed card and in Sam's voice.
- The 10:00 call, the bunkmate leaving, and the removed option to stop him (night 1 is scripted so
  that the player, like Fisk, does not try).
- The meal that makes you hungrier, and the bread that makes you feel full.
- The halls' size: 4 feet (1.22 m) wide and 15 feet (4.57 m) high, everywhere.
- The figure's description and its one line. The last image.
- Sam's cave lines, word for word, less two sentences: the account of the Windigo's origin is
  omitted (section 20, answer 1). Nothing is reworded or added to what he says.
- The first bunkmate is never named, as in the source.

### What the game stretches

The story spans two nights. The game spans seven days and nights of the same pattern, using the
author's own engine, escalation-by-catalogued-incident (`_Annotations/03_Stories/07_Project_-_Man_Eater.md`):
each day brings one wrong thing that breaks the explanation Fisk gave the last. The source's
discoveries come first and in the source's order; the added ones sit between them.

## 4. Investment

### The hook in the first 5 minutes

1. Minute 0 to 1: the back of a covered truck. Snow. The gate opens. The player sees the armour
   plates bolted on the **inside** of the gate, facing the base. The first entry is written into
   Fisk's list, with a choice of explanation.
2. Minute 2 to 4: Sam walks Fisk down a hall 1.22 m wide and 4.57 m high. The first-person camera
   sits at 1.66 m. The ceiling is a long way up. There are two handles on every door: a steel lever
   at 2.4 m, out of reach, and a newer brass knob at 1.1 m, bolted on later.
3. Minute 4 to 5: at the bunk door Sam gives the three rules, word for word. The card with them on
   it is screwed to the inside of the door, at the height of the high lever.

The question the hook plants: *who were these halls built for, and what are the rules keeping in?*
The first 10:00 call comes at about minute 20.

### What the player comes to care about, and how, before it is threatened

**Fisk's clear mind.** On day 1 the game is generous with words. Fisk's list entries are dry and
funny; his explanations come in three flavours; the Chart shows every number to one decimal; Short
Rations shows a tracker of every card played, which is Fisk's memory; Floor Plan remembers paces to
within one. The player learns to win the card game by counting cards and to read the Chart by
trends. Those are skills the player *uses*, so the player values the mind that supplies them. Only
from day 2, as rations take effect, do the tiers start to strip them away (section 12). A player who
has eaten fully notices the card tracker vanish on day 4 the way you notice a missing tooth.

**Jax.** He arrives on the evening of day 2 and takes the empty chair, and the cards that went with
it. From then until day 5 he is useful and kind in play, not in cutscenes:

| Day | What Jax does, in play |
| --- | --- |
| 2 | At Short Rations he teaches Fisk a tap signal: taps on the table before he plays mean how many high cards he holds in the led suit. Reading it wins tricks. |
| 3 | At the Tray he sits beside Fisk. Drag a ration piece onto his tray and he eats it or hides it for you, so the orderly sees a clean tray. |
| 3 | At the bunk he ties a knot in a bootlace on Fisk's bed frame: "So you know which is yours." Each night he adds one. |
| 4 | If Fisk's hunger is over 70 at the evening game, Jax pushes his own bread across. |
| 4 | Night 4: if Fisk goes out, Jax keeps the bunk lamp low and answers the 22:30 check for both of them (suspicion from that night is halved). |
| 5 | He tells Fisk his plan. He has been counting the fence posts on his shovel duty. |

Small habits give him an interior life the game never explains: he taps the table before every
card; he talks about a younger brother who is better at cards than he is; he writes nothing down
because he does not want to "leave a list". On day 5 the player chooses between him and the
project.

**The table.** The crew at Short Rations each have one habit and one line a night. When a chair
empties, its suit is taken out of the deck and nobody mentions it. The player can give bread to a
crew member to keep the call off them for one more night, which makes the empty chairs partly the
player's doing.

### The question that pulls to the end

*How much of yourself will you eat to get strong enough to leave, and what will be left to leave?*
A second, practical question runs under it from day 4: *what is under the base?* The halls do not
add up, and the cold comes up through the drains.

## 6. Characters

Dialogue stays sparse, as in the source: one or two lines per encounter, never a scene built on
talk.

### Fisk

- **Want:** to do the posting and not think about it; later, to know why he was lied to.
- **Fear:** being the kind of man who does not try to stop someone. (The game makes him that man on
  night 1.)
- **Voice:** clipped, flat. "Sir yes Sir," (line 29, replied "flatly"). Inner lines are the list
  entries: dry, short, getting shorter. His memory of New Mexico, where "the Barracks got so hot
  many recruits died of heat stroke" (line 28), is his yardstick for "worse places".
- **Arc by ending:** Zoo, transformed and kept. Rule Three, out and still changing. Call Me Sam,
  staff, giving the rules. Transferred, alive and never knowing.

### Sam

- **Want:** a surplus of test subjects, and no repeat of "the mistakes as we have with the rest".
- **Fear:** his superiors' dissatisfaction ("his eyes twisted in an attempt to mask his
  dissatisfaction", line 34).
- **Voice (verbatim):** "You can just call me Sam," (line 29). "Do not, under any circumstances,
  leave this room after 10:00 precisely." (line 29). "we won't make the same mistakes as we have
  with the rest." (line 35). Added lines are marked in section 21, for example on day 6: "Coming
  along."
- **Arc by ending:** he wins in every ending but Rule Three, and even there the last image is that
  "this would only spread".

### The first bunkmate

- Never named, as in the source. "the skinniest man he had ever witnessed" (line 30).
- **Interior life (added):** at the day 1 card table he gives Fisk his bread without saying why. He
  keeps his boots laced so he can step into them. He keeps a list too. On night 6 the player finds
  it scratched into a pen wall below the base.
- **Arc:** taken on night 1 in every run. He is seen again only in the pens, and only from behind
  frosted glass.

### Jax

- **Want:** to know "what it was and why they had been lied to" (line 33).
- **Fear (added):** being written down. He will not keep a list.
- **Voice:** sparse. Source gives him no lines; added lines are marked. "So you know which is
  yours." (day 3, the knot). "I counted the posts." (day 5).
- **Arc by ending:** Zoo, gassed beside Fisk (if he is still there on night 7). Rule Three, stops in
  the snow. Call Me Sam, reported, his chair given to the new recruit. Transferred, told that Fisk
  was transferred.

### The crew (added)

| Name | Seat suit | Habit | One line | Taken by default |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pell | Anchors | Old cook's hands; keeps the deck; hums to cover the clock | "Bread's the only thing here that's bread." | Night 2 |
| Duarte | Houses | Writes a letter to his daughter every evening and puts it in the sack | "She'll be eight by the time this gets there." | Night 3 |

Fisk's suit is Hands. The first bunkmate's suit is Keys; Jax inherits it with the chair.

### The orderly, the cook, the guards (added, unnamed)

Functions, not people with arcs. The orderly reads numbers aloud and writes on a clipboard. The cook
says one thing a day ("Eat up."). Guards are flashlights in the halls.

### The figure

- Presented only as the source describes it: "a man pale as snow, bones bare, and skin stretched
  thin" (line 34), "almost touching the ceiling", playing a flute "slowly through heaved breaths".
  It speaks one line, verbatim, and vanishes "with a gust of cold mist".
- The game adds no backstory, no origin, no name beyond the one the source gives it, and no
  ceremony. Whether it is one of "the rest" Sam speaks of stays open: the game shows the pens and
  the figure and joins them nowhere (section 20, answer 3). It is lit from behind and never seen
  close. See section 20 for the cultural-consultation step that governs everything about it.

## 8. Levels

Scene ids in `code` match `data/scenes.json`. Each day's discoveries add entries to the list, and
each names which earlier explanation it breaks (section 12, "The list").

### Chapter 1: Arrival (`ch1_arrival`, 9 minutes)

- **Goal:** reach the bunk; learn the rules.
- **Where:** gate and yard, the halls, the bunk room.
- **Scenes in order:** `c1_truck_gate` (3D, 3 min), `c1_halls` (3D, 2.5 min), `c1_bunk` (3D, 3.5 min).
- **What happens:** the truck stops at the gate. The player can look around the truck bed (kit bag
  with three chocolate squares) and out at the gate as it opens: the armour faces inward. List entry
  L1. In the yard, crew shovelling snow look up; they are thin (L2). Inside, breath shows (L4). A
  barked order; Fisk answers "Sir yes Sir," (line 29). Sam introduces himself. The walk down the

## 9. 3D scenes

### Shared rules for every 3D scene

- **Camera:** first person. Eye height = Fisk's height minus 12 cm. Height = 178 cm + 1.6 cm per
  point of `change` (178 cm at 0, 338 cm at 100), so eye height runs from 1.66 m to 3.26 m. The
  camera eases to a new height overnight, never during a scene. Field of view 70 degrees, narrowing
  to 62 at clarity tier 4.
- **Controls:** move (WASD or left stick), look (mouse or right stick), interact (E or A), crouch
  (C or B), open the list (Tab or Select). Walk speed 1.3 m/s, plus 0.006 m/s per point of
  `strength`. No run in the base by day: staff notice running (suspicion +2).
- **Hands:** Fisk's hands are visible when he interacts. Their colour and thinness follow `change`
  in five steps (0, 20, 40, 60, 80): knuckles whiten, as in "His knuckles began to grow whiter and
  lighter" (line 32).
- **Breath:** a breath cloud shows indoors whenever warmth is under 90. It is the only cold readout.
- **Clarity tiers** change the prompts in 3D: at tier 1 a prompt reads "Look at the claw marks";
  at tier 2 "Claw marks"; at tier 3 "Look"; at tier 4 an icon only.
- **Hall size:** every hall 1.22 m wide and 4.57 m high. Every door 1.1 m wide and 4.2 m high,
  with a steel lever at 2.4 m and a brass knob at 1.1 m. Once Fisk's height passes 2.6 m he uses
  the lever, and the knob prompt disappears.

## 10. 2D modes

Both modes use the engine's `Layer2D` with an orthographic camera. Both are `scroll`. Both enter by
`flatten` from the bunk door and exit by `fade` (or `pull-out` into a 3D place at their end).

### Mode A: Silhouette halls (`silhouette_halls`, scroll)

**What it is:** side-on stealth in the tall halls at night. Fisk and everyone else are black
silhouettes against the cream upper walls, which catch the moonlight from the frosted windows at
3.5 m. The hall scrolls in X; Y is used for crouching and for climbing to the pipes at 2.7 m.

**Controls:** left and right to walk; down to crouch; up at a pipe to climb (only when Fisk is
2.3 m or taller, that is `change` 33 or more); interact to open a door; hold to listen (shows the
guards' footsteps as small marks on the floor line for 2 seconds).

**Rules and physics:**

| Rule | Number |
| --- | --- |
| Walk speed | 1.4 m/s + 0.008 m/s per point of `strength` |
| Crouch speed | 0.6 m/s; crouching shortens the shadow by 40% |
| Fisk's shadow length on the wall | His height x 1.6 when the light is behind him; it reaches the ceiling at height 2.86 m (`change` 68) |
| Detection | A guard's flashlight cone that holds Fisk's body **or his shadow** for 1.5 seconds raises an alarm |
| Shadow round corners | When the light is behind him, the shadow arrives at a corner 1 to 2 seconds before Fisk does; a guard round the corner sees it |
| Warmth drain in the halls | 12 minus `change`/10 per minute (12 at change 0, 4 at change 80) |
| Floor drains | Standing on one costs 10 warmth |
| The call's pull, when following | Adds 0.5 m/s toward the call; the player cannot walk away from it |
| The call's pull, when not following | At `change` 40 or more, walking away from the call is 30% slower |
| Checks | At 22:30 and 23:30 a guard walks every hall once |

**What the shadow does for the design:** the more Fisk has eaten, the longer his shadow and the
earlier he is seen, but the more he can climb out of the light. An eater and a refuser play the
same hall differently. That is the mode's whole point, and it is why it belongs to this game.

**Hazards:** guards (one to three per sequence, fixed patrols with one random pause); lit door panes
(pools of light on the floor); floor drains; the stair door, which is locked unless the void has been
found.

**Progress:** a strip at the top of the screen shows the hall as Fisk drew it on Floor Plan. Rooms he
has not drawn are blank on the strip. The target door is marked only if he drew it.

**Failure and retry:** caught or warmth at 0 means two orderlies walk Fisk back to the bunk.

## 11. 1D games

All four are full DOM pages in `Layer1D`, entered by `push-in` from the 3D surface they sit on (the
tray, the clipboard, the notebook, the card table) and left by `pull-out`. All four honour the
accessibility rules in `docs/TECH.md`: text size, pause or slow any timer, no information by sound
alone. All four degrade with clarity (section 12).

### 11.1 Short Rations (`short_rations`) — card game, trick-taking

**Purpose in the story:** "The crew plays for bread; the deck shrinks as chairs empty"
(`docs/COLLECTION.md`). Bread is the only food that quiets hunger without changing you, so the
evening game is the refuser's lifeline and the place the player meets the people the call takes.

**Appearances:** `d1_rations` (6 seats, tutorial), `d2_rations` (6 seats, Jax arrives),
`d3_rations` (5), `d4_rations` (4), `d5_rations` (3), `d6_rations` (2, or 3 with Sam).

**Rules:**

1. **The deck** has one suit per occupied seat, ranks 1 to 8. Each player's suit is drawn by them:
   Hands (Fisk), Keys (the first bunkmate, then Jax), Anchors (Pell), Houses (Duarte), Sparks
   (Kimura), Stars (Hollis), and, if he sits, the project stamp (Sam). When a chair empties, its
   suit is taken out before the next deal. With N seats the deck is 8N cards and each player holds 8.
2. **Trumps:** the dealer's own suit. The deal passes to the left each hand.
3. **Bidding:** before play, each player in turn from the dealer's left says how many tricks they
   will take, 0 to 8. The dealer bids last and may not make the bids add up to 8, so at least one
   player must miss.
4. **Play:** the player left of the dealer leads. Follow suit if you can. The highest trump wins the
   trick; if no trump is played, the highest card of the led suit wins. The winner leads next.
5. **Bread:** the basket on the table holds one loaf fewer than there are seats. After each hand,
   every player who took exactly the tricks they bid takes a loaf, the closest bidders first (those
   who bid higher first on a tie). When the basket is empty, the rest go without.
6. **An evening** is two hands (one guided hand and one free hand on day 1).
7. **After the game** Fisk decides, for each loaf he won: keep it (`bread_banked` +1), give it to a
   seated player (their pull -15; Jax's trust +10), or eat it now (hunger -25).

**Two-handed play (day 6):** with two seats the deck is 16 cards and both hands are dealt in full,
so each player knows the other's cards exactly. The basket holds one loaf. It is the only hand of the
game with no hidden information, and the only one where what the player does is purely a choice of
whether to let the other win.

**Page layout:** the table seen from above, round, filling 80% of the page. Fisk's hand fanned at
the bottom. Each seat shows its occupant's hands and suit symbol, and one line of table talk per
hand in a small caption at the seat. Empty chairs stay at the table, pushed in, with no caption. The
bread basket sits in the centre with its loaves. Top left: the bids. Top right: the card tracker
(Fisk's memory of cards played), present only at clarity tier 1.

**Inputs:** click or press a card to play it; number keys or a dial to bid; drag a loaf to a seat to
give it. Gamepad: shoulder buttons move through the hand.

**Finish:** after the second hand, the bread is settled.

**Failure:** none as such. Missing your bid means no bread, which means the night is harder.

**What it writes:** `bread_banked`, `hunger`, `jax_trust`, every seated player's `pull.*` meter,
and on day 3 `the_list` (L10, the empty chair nobody mentions).

**Difficulty curve across appearances:**

| Day | Seats | Deck | Opponents | What changes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 6 | 48 | Loose players. Pell bids high, Hollis bids zero | Tutorial: bidding, following, bread |
| 2 | 6 | 48 | Jax joins. He taps before he plays | The tap signal: one tap per card above 5 in the led suit |
| 3 | 5 | 40 | Kimura counts cards | Bids get tighter |
| 4 | 4 | 32 | Kimura and Hollis | Card tracker gone below tier 1 |
| 5 | 3 | 24 | One crew member and Jax | Fewer loaves, higher stakes |
| 6 | 2 or 3 | 16 or 24 | Jax or Hollis, and Sam if Fisk is staff | Open hands; Sam always makes his bid |

**Hints:** Pell's day 1 line explains bidding. Jax's tap is explained once by Jax ("Taps are high
cards") and never again. At clarity tier 1 a faint underline marks the cards in your hand that can
still win a trick.

**What hunger does:** at hunger 70 or more, the hand trembles (a 2 px shake). At 90 or more, one in
four plays lands on the card next to the one chosen. The accessibility setting "steady hands" turns
the misplay off and keeps the tremble.

### 11.2 The Tray (`the_tray`) — portioning

**Purpose in the story:** "Not what to cook but how much to eat" (`docs/COLLECTION.md`). The meat is
the source's: it makes you hungrier, and you want more (line 31). Each piece is a decision about
how much of your mind to trade for strength.

**Appearances:** `d1_tray` to `d7_tray`, one per day at noon.

**Rules:**

1. The tray holds the ration (one slab), a cup of broth, a paper cup with a pill, and on some days a
   bread heel. The player cuts the slab into pieces by dragging a knife: it divides into five pieces
   on days 1 to 3, six on days 4 to 6, ten on day 7.
2. Each piece can be **eaten** (dragged to the mouth at the bottom edge), **pocketed** (dragged to the
   napkin at the left edge; the napkin holds three), **passed** to Jax's tray (from day 3, if he is
   beside you), or **left** on the tray.
3. The pill can be **swallowed** or **cheeked**: drag it to the mouth and hold the cheek key; later,
   with the orderly's gaze away, drag it to the napkin. Spitting it while watched counts as a seen
   break of rule one.
4. **The orderly's gaze** sweeps the room on an 8-second cycle and rests on Fisk for 3 seconds of it
   (4 seconds from day 5). It is drawn as a long shadow that crosses the top of the page. Pocketing,
   passing or spitting while it rests on Fisk is seen.
5. **The meal lasts 90 seconds** of page time. Then the orderly collects the tray.
6. **Pieces left on the tray** when it is collected each add suspicion +5. The orderly writes on the
   clipboard.
7. **Hunger pulls.** At hunger 70 or more, pieces on the tray drift slowly toward the mouth (2 px a
   second) and must be pushed back. At 90 or more, the first piece moves to the mouth on its own
   unless the player holds "push away" for 3 seconds. Refusal is always possible; it is never free.
8. **The staff tray** (days 6 and 7, if Fisk accepted Sam's offer) holds ordinary food and no pill.

**Effects per item:**

| Item | change | hunger | clarity | strength |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ration piece eaten | +2 | -6 now; the hourly rise grows by 1 | -2 | +1 |
| Pill swallowed | +3 | 0 | -3 | 0 |
| Broth | 0 | -3 | 0 | 0 |
| Bread heel | 0 | -10 | 0 | 0 |
| Staff food | 0 | -20 | 0 | 0 |

**Page layout:** the tray from above on a steel table, filling the centre. The mouth is the bottom
edge of the page. The napkin is a folded white shape at the left. Jax's tray is at the right edge
from day 3. The orderly's gaze is a shadow crossing the top of the page. A narrow clock ring around
the tray shows the 90 seconds. There is no hunger bar: hunger shows as the page's edges pulsing
slowly, faster as it rises.

**Inputs:** drag with mouse, touch or a gamepad cursor; hold a key to cheek the pill.

**Finish:** the tray is collected. The meal is logged in `meals`: `kind` ration with the number of
pieces eaten (eaten later from the napkin counts too), or `refused` if none; `staff` for the staff
tray.

**Failure:** being seen. Each seen act adds suspicion +20 and logs a seen break of rule one where it
is one (spitting the pill, passing food is not a break).

**What it writes:** `meals`, `change`, `hunger`, `clarity`, `strength`, `pills_taken`, `suspicion`,
`jax_trust` (passing to Jax +5), `rules_broken`, and on days the player eats kit chocolate after the

| 2 | 5 | 3 s of 8 | The gaze begins |

### 11.3 The Chart (`the_chart`) — self-diagnosis

### 11.4 Floor Plan (`floor_plan`) — drawing from memory

**Purpose in the story:** "Map the base; the halls never measure what they should"
(`docs/COLLECTION.md`). The source has Fisk sense "something off about the shape of this place"
(line 33). Floor Plan makes that a page.

**Appearances:** `d2_floorplan` (barracks wing), `d4_floorplan` (ground floor), `d6_floorplan` (the
whole base, place the void).

**Rules:**

1. The page is graph paper; one square is 75 cm, Fisk's day-1 stride.
2. The margin holds **remembered paces**: each hall segment Fisk has walked in 3D or 2D, written as
   a range ("Bunk hall: 38 to 40"). Paces are counted at the stride Fisk had *on the day he walked
   it*. A segment never walked is not listed.
3. The player places room stamps (bunk, latrine, cafeteria, rec room, infirmary, cold store, mail
   room, boiler room, Sam's office, staff mess, laundry) and draws halls between them. A hall's
   length snaps to the remembered paces, converted to squares at day-1 stride.
4. **Close the plan:** the page totals the east-west length of the drawn ground floor and compares it
   with the outside wall, 120 paces paced on day 3's duty. A difference of 3 squares or more is
   shown as a gap.
5. **The stride trap:** a changed Fisk takes longer strides, so the same hall walked later counts
   fewer paces. The player must convert: the Chart's stride row gives the stride for each day, and
   the page offers a small "convert" tool (paces x that day's stride / 75). A player who does not
   convert finds gaps that are not there.
6. **The void:** once converted, the ground floor is genuinely 18 to 24 squares short of the outside
   wall on the east side, behind the infirmary's cold store. On day 6 the player places a void stamp.
   Correct within 2 squares sets `void_located`. Knowing the cold comes from the drains
   (`cold_from_below`) and reading the files (`files_read`) each add one hint line.

**Page layout:** graph paper fills the page. Stamps in a tray at the left. Remembered paces in the
right margin, in pencil. A compass rose and a scale bar. The outside-wall measure along the top
edge, in ink.

**Inputs:** drag stamps; draw halls with click-drag; click a margin entry to use it; the convert tool
is a small slide rule at the bottom right.

**Finish:** the plan is closed (days 2 and 4) or the void is placed (day 6), or the player leaves the
page (the plan is saved as it stands).

**Failure:** a void placed more than 2 squares off. The stair is not found on night 6. One retry.

**What it writes:** `knows`, `the_list` (day 4 breaks L5; day 6 breaks L13), `void_located` (day 6).

**Difficulty curve:** day 2, eight rooms, the plan closes, no conversion needed (stride has barely
changed). Day 4, all ground-floor rooms, the plan does not close, conversion introduced. Day 6, the
officers' wing added, and the void must be placed.

**Clarity:** ranges widen from plus or minus 1 at tier 1 to 2, 4 and then no numbers at tier 4,
where the margin reads only "cold here" beside the cold store. The stamps lose their labels at tier 3.

**How the three layers look different:**

- **3D** is the base as a place: depth, light, breath, a low camera rising.
- **2D** is the base at night as shadow and snow: two tones, no textures, the body as a shape. The
  transition `flatten` rotates the hall to side-on and drains its colour.
- **1D** is the base as paperwork: forms, a clipboard, graph paper, a tray, a card table, all seen
  flat from above. The transition `push-in` moves the camera down onto the surface until it becomes
  the page.

**Degradation is the HUD.** The tiers in section 12 are the only status display the game has. A
player can tell how changed Fisk is by how much the game still says.

**Accessibility:** remappable controls; subtitles for every line; every timed 1D page and both 2D
modes can be slowed to 50% or paused; "steady hands" removes card misplays; "story assist" gives one
retry on night 7; text size in every 1D page; colour-blind safe palette for prints, light cones and
the Chart's highlights (shape as well as colour); no information by sound alone (the call has a
visual frost cue; guards' footsteps show when listening).

## 16. Audio

## 20. Risks and answered questions

### Cultural care: the Windigo

The Windigo comes from the traditions of Algonquian-speaking peoples. The source puts an account of
it in Sam's mouth (line 35) that is rough and secondhand. This design keeps the horror aimed at the
institution that exploits the legend:

- The game invents no lore about the Windigo or any nation: no origin, no ceremony, no names, no
  regalia, no Indigenous characters used as props.
- Sam's account of the origin is not voiced (section 20, answer 1). What he does say, that this
  place is "the first recorded birthplace of a Windigo" and that his superiors "wish to harvest this
  curse", is presented as **Sam's**, the project's justification, never as the game's truth.
  The officers' wing file (night 4) shows how the project built it: a cover sheet citing one
  secondhand account, with the word "unverified" crossed out by a superior's pen. The account
  itself is never shown. The horror is that the institution turned a people's tradition into a
  resource and a schedule.
- The figure is shown only as the source describes it, at a distance, backlit.
- The call is not built from Indigenous instruments or music.
- The consultation step (M0, M6, M9) can change or remove any of this, including Sam's lines and the
  word "Windigo" itself.

### Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
| --- | --- |
| The cultural handling is wrong or harmful | Paid consultation before production and twice after; the slice avoids the subject; the consultant can strike |
| Degrading the UI frustrates rather than frightens | Tiers only remove Fisk's own text and aids, never menus or spoken subtitles; the Chart and Floor Plan stay solvable at tier 3; playtest each tier |
| Seven days of the same pattern feel repetitive | Each day breaks one explanation and adds one place; the table shrinks visibly; the beat chart caps any stretch at 15 minutes |
| Players never discover the void and miss the pens | Two hints (drains, files) and the Chart's stride row; the pens are not required for any ending |
| The refuser's route is dull or impossible | Bread from the table and the kit keep a refuser alive; hunger never kills; the Chart answers can come from the crew's columns |
| Feeding crew to move the call is read as a rescue | It only moves the call; every chair still empties by night 5; the ledger records who Fisk fed |
| Graphic content creeps in through art | Art bible rule: no close-ups of bodies; the figure backlit; the cave table under a sheet; review at M6 |
| The as-written ending as the catch-all feels like a punishment | Each of the other three endings is announced by play (the offer, the fence, the bread) well before night 7 |
| Scope: 34 3D scenes | Most share the hall kit and the bunk room; 15 environment sets cover them |

**Answered** (by Claude at the author's request, 2026-11-12, from the source text; the author can overrule any of them).

1. **Sam's cave speech: shorten it by omission.** Sam voices line 35 word for word except two
   sentences, "Long ago, back in a far less hospitable time, Native Americans would suffer through
   long and harsh winters where, in the most devastating of times, they had to resort to
   cannibalism; this brought about the birth of the Windigo. A curse was placed upon those who had
   partaken of the flesh of another human?" Those two are the only place the story states an origin
   and pins it on a people, and the source itself marks them as unsure (the second sentence ends on
   a question mark). Voiced verbatim in the last scene, uncontradicted, they would be the most
   authoritative thing the game says about the Windigo, against the rule that nothing presents its
   lore as authoritative. Moving them onto the file prop would be worse: typed and filed, read at
   leisure, they look more like fact, not less. What stays keeps the horror on the institution:
   "Do you not realize what this place is?", the superiors who "wish to harvest this curse", the
   "surplus of test subjects", "the first recorded birthplace of a Windigo" as the project's claim,
   "feeding you all flesh and other chemicals", and "we won't make the same mistakes as we have
   with the rest." Fisk's two lines stand whole. The file prop stays, as a cover sheet only, and
   carries no account. Changed: sections 3, 5, 9 (`e_cave`), 13 and the cultural-care block above.
   M0, M6 and M9 are unchanged: the consultant can still restore, cut further or strike the word.
2. **Footsteps: strike it.** It hinges on Fisk hearing the steps, where the source's line is that
   "neither Jax nor Fisk heard the footsteps" (line 34), and its last image puts the player's hands
   on the figure's flute, which closes the distance the cultural-care rules keep and tells the
   player what the figure is (see 3). It was the only ending new to this document, and four endings
   still meet the pillars. A changed Fisk who follows alone now meets the source's line and wakes in
   the cave: The Zoo. Removed: the ending, `e_flute`, the change-85 threshold, the flute prop,
   one ending cue, and every mention in sections 1, 5 to 9, 12, 13, 17 to 19 and 21; in the data,
   `footsteps` in `endings.json`, `e_flute` in `scenes.json` and the read of `nights_followed` in
   `n7_windigo`.
3. **The figure: keep it open.** The source does not join them. "the rest" are what Sam's project
   has already made (line 35); the figure plays the flute, speaks, and "vanished into thin air"
   (line 34), which no kept subject does. Its line, "Follow in my footsteps, and you will become
   me.", says what Fisk is becoming, not who it was. The game shows the pens and the figure and
   joins them nowhere; nothing in the list, the files or the endings answers it. Changed: sections
   5 and 6.
4. **The Who Did It tie-in: stay separate.** `03_Stories/29_What_the_Lab_Made.md` is marked
   `origin: auto-authored` and `status: draft`: it is not one of the certified works this
   collection adapts. Its own annotation says it hands off to where *Project: Man Eater* "would
   begin" and echoes the original; the dependency runs one way. A file line pointing to it would
   also answer question 3 for the author (a subject transferred in under a typed WINDIGO
   designation) and give the figure a history the game has promised not to give. No line is added.
   Changed: the Who Did It paragraph in section 5.
5. **The day-7 word fragments: eight of the source's own words.** "hate", "these people", "never",
   "something was off", "lied to", "transferred", "worse places", "Sir yes Sir", each from the line
   section 11.3 gives. A lower tier keeps the first ones, so at tier 4 the last word left is
   "hate", because the source's man keeps that when the rest goes: "As his mind went blank ... he
   knew he would always hate these people" (line 36). No new words are written. Changed: section
   11.3, `chart_questions.json` (fragments with their lines), its schema (fragments must be that
   list) and `tools/check_data.py` (eight fragments, each on its line).
6. **Scenes that wrote `the_list` with no entry: three writes removed, one entry gains a breaker.**
   `n2_halls` (the frozen print is a foreshadowing image; L12 names the prints on day 5) and
   `d3_duty_shovel` (its pace count and the posts feed Floor Plan and the fence route, not the
   list) no longer write it, and neither does `c1_bunk`, which had the same gap (chapter 1's
   entries come from the gate and the halls). `d5_duty_laundry` keeps its write: the uniforms let
   down at the hem now break L9, "Routine", as a second route beside the day-5 Chart, so a player
   who misses the target curve can still lose that explanation. No new entry was written.
   `tools/check_data.py` now fails on a scene that writes `the_list` and adds, breaks or ends
