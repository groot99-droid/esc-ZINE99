## 1. Where it stands

**What works.** 26 minutes of making things for each other before anything threatens it, one clock
under every action, and an ending the players learn with their hands. The gift that rides to the
last frame, the voices cut from the chat and the hall of unfinished boards all grow from the source.

**What is weak.**

1. **Nothing in the world wants anything.** The beasts are a notice meter and the heart is a drain
   curve. The source calls them "immune beasts that patrol the waters"; the design never asks what
   an immune creature is looking for.
2. **The middle has no reversal.** From minute 26 to minute 54 the game escalates one situation:
   more folds, more tendrils, more eyes. Nothing learned on the river changes what the river means.
3. **The hook is never paid.** Minute 2 asks "why does no one finish?" and the game ends without an
   answer.
4. **The layers touch only through numbers nobody sees.** A good song is +15 ms; so is a good
   board. No player can feel his carving, song or board in the river.
5. **The endings underpay.** Two Together ends with two hands locked for good, against "No one
   stays together." The One Who Stayed is never asked what staying is for. Carried Away is three
   passive minutes.
6. **Nobody has a secret.** The brothers are temperaments, the elders are furniture with six
   lines, and the child appears only for a stayer.

The six modes are each one rule set at three difficulties.

## 2. The story, developed

### The question

*When you cannot keep the people you love, what do you put in their hands before the river takes
them?*

Each brother **wants** to reach the heart with the others and stay. He **needs** to learn that the
hand has to open, and that what was given before it opened is the only part that travels.

### The opposing force: the body

The body is the antagonist, and it is not cruel. It wants **circulation**: each of them sent
somewhere different, because somewhere different needs him. Its hands are the immune beasts, here
called **Wardens** (NEW name). A Warden hunts nothing. It checks. The source says blue is "the color
of youth before recognition": a Warden is the thing that recognises, and it turns aside whatever
on the river is still blue.

So colour becomes a state, not a chapter timer (NEW rule): a brother's red climbs with his own
`self.in_time`. One who rows off the beat stays blue at the wrist, and the eye finds him first.

### The secret

The seed is in the source:

> "They live in a labyrinth of glowing marrow tunnels, where the walls pulse faintly with the rhythm of the distant heart."
> (`creative-writing/vault/03_Stories/02_A_Journey_Into_the_Heartstream_-_The_Rite_of_the_Blue-Born.md`)

**The pulse in the walls is carried there** (NEW). Every vessel from the heart ends somewhere cold
and blue, and the brother it carries gives that place's wall its beat. The light
the players woke under is somebody carried away before they were born. Nobody tells them. A player
who solves the Four Marks (section 3) sees it in his last scene, before the source's three lines,
which stay last and stay true: he is alone, and will never meet whoever sleeps under his wall.

### Characters

**The Lamp-Keeper** (deepened elder; wordless, so the elders' six lines stay six). *Want:* to know
whether the hook he carved ever reached a hand. *Secret:* he stayed because he had not finished his
gift when the lights dimmed, and he sent his brother up the shaft with nothing. The worn tool on
the carving slab is the one that brother made for him. *Scene:* **The Wall at Night**. Before the
second sitting a player can find him at the pulse wall, tapping for a raft that crossed long ago.
Tap sixteen beats beside him and he turns his tool over and shows its underside.

**The small one** (the child of the next group, moved to minute 6). *Want:* to be let into the game
now. *Secret:* he hums the group's song from his alcove with one note wrong. *Scene:*
**The Small One Moves a Counter**. During the first sitting he creeps out and moves one counter on
the players' board. They can put it back or leave it. The game makes them the ones
leaving somebody before it makes them the ones left.

**The Warden** (deepened beast). *Want:* that nothing blue reaches the heart. *Secret:* it keeps
what it turns aside. Its hold is set with old gifts healed into the skin; the "old carved hook"
already in `s3_hold` is one of dozens, and it is the Lamp-Keeper's. *Scene:*
**The Inspection** (section 3).

**The midpoint.** The source's raft is "a living vessel grown from tissue, shaped by tradition,
guided by instinct." NEW: it is grown from the elders. An old elder climbs the shaft alone and lies
down on the bank. At boarding the players find a fresh pale patch on the raft's flank with a mark
healed into it: the mark from the empty seat. The raft knows the way because it
is made of everyone who never went. Staying was only a slower leaving.

**The darkest moment.** At the last fork, a fourth branch in another hand's ink leads to a
backwater with no current. An old raft is moored there, grey. On it: a board with every hollow empty
and both keeps counted, the only finished game in the world, and four gifts in a row. No brothers.
They stopped here to stay together, played to the end, and the body moved them on anyway, one at a
time, with nothing in their hands. That is why no one finishes: an unfinished game is the group
still playing, somewhere.

## 3. Obstacles and puzzles

Every element below is NEW unless marked.

### River section (2D, woodblock wave print)

- **Teach:** four oars on one beat; trim under a fold. **Test:** a tendril rises during a hush: lift
  without a stroke. **Twist:** the Blue Arm. The eye stays open until the bluest brother is hidden,
  so the other three trim to his side, which pitches the bow toward a fold.
- **Confluence:** where a side vessel joins, a second beat sounds half a beat late for eight bars.
  Bow seats row the heart, stern seats row the echo.
- **Rests:** the eye opens only on the song's rest beats (see links).
- **Set-piece, The Inspection.** A raft that hushes perfectly is not passed over. The eye rises
  through the surface line, the only thing in the print that ever crosses it. For four beats each
  brother must hold his arm out, still, and be looked at.

### The climb (2D, cave painting)

- **Teach:** grab on the beat. **Test:** brace and pull. **Twist:** the last ledge leaves the last
  brother with nobody under him; the first one up has to climb back down and be the step.
- **Breathing wall:** every fourth beat the shaft narrows. Two brothers on opposite walls can
  chimney through; one alone is squeezed down a hold.
- **Old stencils:** an earlier group's hand stencil is always firm; using it leaves yours beside it.
- **Set-piece, The Stencil Wall.** A gallery of hundreds of hand stencils in rows of four. Some
  rows have three. All climbers press a bare row on one beat and the wall lights; the press sets
  `run.sync`. A group with a stayer leaves its own gap.

### The Carving (1D, scrimshaw)

- **Teach:** eight strokes, with the grain. **Test:** twenty-four strokes, hidden knots. **Twist:**
  at stroke twelve a crack shows across the blank; change form or carve the crack into the grip.
- **Hand-fit:** the recipient rows left or right and has a wide or narrow hand, seen only by
  watching him at the board. A tool cut for the wrong hand is never better than rough.
- **The Underside:** two strokes buy one hidden sign from six on the inside face. The recipient
  cannot see it until his last scene.
- **Set-piece:** the Underside read alone in the vessel, an hour after it was cut.

### Marrow-Song (1D, bead-string notation)

- **Teach:** smooth joins. **Test:** alternating accents. **Twist:** a brother who stayed or was
  taken leaves **hollow beads**: his phrases keep their slots and sound as silence, and the group
  chooses whether the song ends on his gap.
- **Rest bead:** a silent beat any player may place; at most four in a verse.
- **The wrong note:** the small one's phrase is offered for the heart verse. It breaks one join and
  is the only phrase the child will remember.
- **Set-piece, The First Listen.** Every growth in the hollow answers verse one at once.

### Bone Counters (1D, linocut)

- **Teach:** sow on the beat. **Test:** captures and rung hollows. **Twist:** the second sitting
  starts from the position the small one left.
- **Lent counter:** give him one counter from your keep to sow once from his row.
- **Set-piece, One Move on the Oldest Board.** One ancient board in the hall is a single sowing
  from finished. As a player's last counter falls an elder's hand covers the hollow, catches it,
  and closes the player's fingers round it.

### The Branch Chart (1D, red-ink anatomical plate)

- **Teach:** vote. **Test:** listen for a pulse off the beat. **Twist:** the Back of the Chart. The
  awl pricks through, and held to the light the vellum shows every vessel leaving the heart apart.
- **Dissent:** each vote against the winning branch adds one eddy to the next stretch.
- **Set-piece, The Fourth Branch,** drawn in another ink, to the backwater and the Finished Board.

### Across the game

**Links between layers.**

1. **Song to river.** The Wardens have heard every group's song. Their eyes open on its rest beats,
   so a well-placed rest makes the hush fall where nobody rows.
2. **Carving to 3D.** A hand-fitted gift lets its holder switch sides in one beat and widens his
   squeeze window at the heart.
3. **Board to song.** The row a brother leaves at the cut, read as five heights, is the new phrase
   he is offered for the heart verse. The raft hums it at boarding.

**The long puzzle: the Four Marks.** The Lamp-Keeper's group left four marks through the world: on
the rim of their board in the hall, three on the Stencil Wall (the gap is his), one on the hook in
the Warden's hold, two side by side at the mouth of the shared chamber and apart at its outlets.
Their four fates are the game's four existing endings. Each mark sits at a height on its object,
one to five; the four heights are the **old phrase**, which ends home, on one. A group that sets it
last in the heart verse hears the raft sing it with them, and each player's last scene gains the
pulse in a blue wall and a place to cut his mark beside an old one.

**A second playthrough.** The first run's board hangs in the hall, its hands are on the Stencil
Wall, and a growth in the hollow answers with its song (the crossing record must hold more than
ending names). The stayer's and the taken brother's half-hours are other games. The Open Hand and
the Four Marks are built to be found the second time.

## 5. Roadmap

**Phase 1: story with almost no new systems.** The Small One Moves a Counter; the Underside; the
raft's mark at boarding; the Fourth Branch and the Finished Board (one 3D set, no new rules); the
Two Together valve; the new last images for the Stayer and the taken brother. Art items 1 to 5 and
9. *Author decides first:* that the raft is grown from elders; that the pulse in the walls is
carried there; the change to Two Together.

**Phase 2: make the layers touch.** Colour tied to `self.in_time`; rest beads and the Wardens' eyes;
hand-fit; the board row as a song phrase; dissent as eddies; the Inspection, the Stencil Wall, the
Blue Arm. Web modules get the teach, test, twist ladders. *Author decides first:* colour as a state
changes DESIGN.md section 12.

**Phase 3: depth and return.** The Four Marks and the old phrase; The Open Hand as a fifth ending;
the Wall at Night; the second-playthrough record; the remaining art. *Author decides
first:* whether a fifth ending is wanted, and whether the crossing record may hold a board, a song
and four handprints.

DESIGN.md and `data/` change only after those decisions; the validators run then.
