# Dr. Tox: Fall

Episode 2 of *Dr. Tox: Age of Glass & Greed*, the collection's eleventh game (`docs/COLLECTION.md`
row 11). Scope accepted by the author on 2026-11-19 (`dr-tox/SCOPE.md`, all thirteen
recommendations). This file is stage A of Phase 2: the design, the data, the storyboard, the asset
manifest, the Blender build files and the pictographs. The art kit, the web modules, the generated
images and the key art come later, as they did for Rise.

**Source.** The author's bible, `dr-tox/source/Dr_Tox_Compiled_Bible_Obsidian.md`, is not in the
vault. It is never edited and is cited here by part and section ("Part VI, Dr. Tox §3" is Part VI,
the Dr. Tox profile, section 3). Every quotation below is verbatim from it. **The bible gives Fall a
stub** (Part VI, Dr. Tox, Story: The Fall, marked "(stub)"), two fixed points and a date range, so
most of what follows is an addition. Every addition is marked *(added)* where it first appears and
listed in section 21. Rise (`games/dr-tox-rise/`) is canon for this episode in what the author
accepted: its places, its named people, its mechanics, and the flags it hands on.

## 1. Summary

Dr. Tox comes out of Snakehead into the wasteland the Fall left and starts to treat the people the
State's relief hall turns away, and they come to need him; across the same eight years Reese is the
Spire's hero, sent to contain a woman releasing spores and then to close the doctor's clinic, and
Cally, printed for one purpose, is calibrated to the boy who used to love her. The three threads meet
on a gala balcony in 2236, the year the bible says "Dr. Tox loses control of his cult; his own belief
exceeds him." (Part II).

## 3. Source fidelity

What the bible gives Fall. It is little, and the game keeps all of it.

**Dr. Tox (Part VI, Dr. Tox, Story: The Fall; Part II; Part I).**

1. The stub, in full: "**(stub)** Per the Blueprint, this phase covers Dr. Tox's emergence, the
   biological cult that forms around him, and the failure of institutions to respond. To be filled
   in." (Part VI, Dr. Tox, Story: The Fall). The game's three movements: his emergence (chapters 1, 4),
   the cult (chapters 5, 9, the endings), the failure of institutions (the relief hall, chapters 2
   and 7).
2. Fixed point one: "Begins after the Snakehead discovery and the claiming of the fortress (see
   Origin, above)". Chapter 1 opens in the claimed fortress, whatever Rise's ending.
3. Fixed point two: "His followers become chemically dependent on him (Dermorphin), then undergo ego
   dissolution (5-MeO-DMT), then arm themselves with his lethal resins (Batrachotoxin & Epibatidine)
   — see the Blueprint's "Biological Hierarchy of the Cult," not yet reconciled into this profile
   system". Dependence is Triage (chapters 1, 2, 5); ego dissolution is the rite he finds in chapter
   9; the resins are The Manifold's fifth outlet (chapter 9) and The Following's last image. The
   substances are named here as the bible names them and appear in play only in State text (a lab
   report in chapter 5). No page measures them: the game's unit is an invented "mark of tincture",
   and nothing shows what it is made from or how it is given (`dr-tox/SCOPE.md` §5).
4. The dates: "**2228–2236** — Dr. Tox operates, builds his following. (Undetailed stretch — open for
   story content, not a gap in canon.)" and "**2236** — the Finale begins. Dr. Tox loses control of
   his cult; his own belief exceeds him." (Part II). Fall runs 2228 to 2236 and stops at the edge of
   that loss.
5. The shape: "**Fall** — the party scatters into individual arcs. Motives surface, alliances break,
   enemies become allies." (Part I, Macro-structure). Three leads on three arcs, crossing twice.
6. The world: "**Fall** — 2226–2236. ... Dr. Tox's emergence (2228), the building of his cult,
   institutional failure." (Part II, Phase mapping). The plague: "Not a death sentence. A curse mark
   — and closer to purgatory than either life or death." "People don't die of it. They live inside
   it, worse than death, indefinitely." (Part III). Nobody in the relief hall is cured; Triage eases.

**Reese (Part VI, Aegis; Part VIII Scene 2).** Fall begins where Scene 2 leaves him: "He walks the
other way. He always will, for a long time yet." (Scene 2 V). His abilities are the stub's, carried
forward by the bible: "absorbs toxins and projects hard-light physical barriers; immune to
biological corruption." His philosophy: "The rot must be excised so the tree can live." "Defends the
corporate elite while hunting down anomalies." (Part VI, Aegis). Dr. Tox is an anomaly; the relief
hall clinic is rot. The bible gives Aegis no Fall story; both his chapters are added.

**Cally (Part VI, Catalyst Version B; Part III; Part VIII Scenes 1 and 3).** Cycle 41 is "viable.
Operational." Her function: "targeted affective destabilization. Manufactures unstable bonds with
high-value enemies of the State. Logged bond integrity with Aegis: **94.6%.**" Her terminal names the
next step: "SECONDARY APPLICATION: ANY HIGH-VALUE ASSET WITH PRE-EXISTING / ATTACHMENT SIGNATURE.
RECOMMENDED CALIBRATION PERIOD: 72HRS." (Scene 3). The crack: "a hatred aimed at the thing that
*built* her survived underneath a hatred aimed at its enemies" "It was logged as **noise. Acceptable
variance.**" (Part VI, Version B); "It will not surface on command. It will not surface *soon.*"
(Scene 1 IV). Her voice: "says **"HOPE"** while she believes she serves it. Watch for the first time
she says **"RCC."**" Fall keeps that first time for the Finale (`dr-tox/SCOPE.md` §5, Finale, "The
First Word: Cally says "RCC""). Both her chapters are added.

**Rostova (Part VI, Catalyst Version A).** "**Abilities:** Biomass manipulation and spore
generation. Rewrites DNA on the fly, causing rapid, functional mutations." "**Philosophy:**
Accelerated, radical adaptation. Releases engineered mutagens into population centers." "One of RCC's
own manufactured solutions to the plague it created". "**Story: The Rise (stub).** Placeholder. No
story content written yet." Per `dr-tox/SCOPE.md` §3, the player meets her "Fall, by her spore
release". She is seen twice (chapters 3 and 8) and speaks one added line.

**The author's ending.** There is none. The bible's Fall is a stub with no end state (`dr-tox/SCOPE.md`
§5: "Fall and Altrulan have no written ending; theirs are all proposals."). All four endings below are
proposals and **none is marked `as_written`**. The Following is the default bridge to the Finale
(`dr-tox/SCOPE.md` question 10), and it is the ending that keeps the stub's fixed point (dependence).

## 4. Investment

**The hook, minutes 0 to 5.** One amber lamp, hard falloff into black; caption 2228. The player is in
the fortress Rise ended in, and the first thing on screen is what Rise's ending left: the Observers at
their cells, or four open cells and a dry tank, or a stain under the lamp. In the tank the Ghost
becomes visible for a second and points up *(added)*. A knock comes down the cavern from the breach:
nobody is supposed to be able to find this place. At the breach, a woman, Wren Halloran *(added)*, has
carried her brother Teo down the cavern on a door. The player takes the shroud off its stand (the
bible's open cape, made functional: "a shroud between himself and everyone near him, which a man whose
breath once killed a town has genuine reason to want", Part VI, Dr. Tox §11) and walks out to them, and
for the first time the screen shows his breath radius as a ring on the floor. She cannot come closer
than the ring. By minute four the page has pushed into Triage: two cards, a tray of amber marks, and a
question nobody asks out loud: how much?

**What the player comes to care about, before it is threatened.**

- *The people in the cards.* Every Triage card is a person with a name and one want that is not
  medical: Teo wants to walk by spring; Lupe Andrade wants her hands steady enough to sew; Arturo Sol
  wants to keep his place in a queue he has stood in for nine years *(all added)*. They come back.
  Chapter 2's patients are chapter 5's settlement, and chapter 5's settlement is what the bloom takes
  or does not take in chapter 8.
- *The settlement under the freeway.* Built by the hall's people where they followed him (chapter
  5): water drums, laundry, a generator wired to tube lights, Mercy Abiodun keeping the drums *(added)*.
  The player has twenty minutes of it before the raid, and thirty before the bloom.
- *His own mind.* Self falls with every Toxin State and never returns (Part IX Q2, adopted). In Fall
  Self is also the trigger line: the State fires when Pulse reaches Self, so every loss brings the
  next one closer. A player who ended Rise in the Green ending starts Fall at a trigger line of 75.
- *Reese, and the girl the player played string figures with.* The player played both as children in
  Rise. In Fall the player plays Cally building a person to fit him, from his losses, and the deck
  deals her cards she did not ask for.

**The question that pulls them to the end.** *Will the people he helps become people who need him?*
The player answers it one mark at a time, and the game never says the answer out loud until the last
scene. Behind it, two more that only the player sees both halves of: *Will Reese say the word?* and
*Will Cally do what she was built for?*

### Rules of the world

- **One entity.** RCC is "the parent behind every engineered catastrophe in the setting" (Part III).
  In Fall the player sees three of its faces at once: the relief hall it runs and starves (Branch I's
  people), the hero it sends (Branch III), and the villain it releases (Rostova, "One of RCC's own
  manufactured solutions", Part VI, Version A). *Seen*: every plate in Fall reads RCC.
- **The Terminology Rule.** "Heroes and elitists say 'HOPE.'" "Villains, Insurgents, the
  plague-marked, and everyone else say 'RCC' or 'the State.'" "physical corporate signage, plaques,
  terminals, and legal documentation still read *RCC*" (Part III). In play: Dr. Tox, the patients and
  the settlement say RCC; Aegis and Cally say HOPE; Reese chooses twice (section 12, The Word).
- **The two classes.** "**The mutated and suffering** — exiled to a wasteland they didn't create,
  marked by a plague they didn't choose." (Part III). The relief hall is the State's answer to them
  and it is closed by noon (*added*). The gala is the other class: "air that costs money to breathe
  clean" (Part III, The Mega-Cities), in glass columns stamped RCC. *Seen*.
- **His breath.** "Untreated, his antibodies attempt to escape through sweat and exhaled breath — he is
  a walking biological hazard who cannot safely share a room." (Part VI, Dr. Tox §2). The cage keeps
  them in, not perfectly: in play his breath radius is a ring on the floor that grows with Pulse.
  The shroud halves it. *Done* (Crowd thread, every 3D scene).
- **The cage, routine now.** Amber is still made by hand, at the fortress bench, but there is no
  titration page in Fall: The Cage is Rise's 1D game, and no two episodes share one (`docs/PILLARS.md`
  §6). Making a batch costs hours the clinic needs. *Done* (three bench scenes).
- **The Toxin State**, as in Rise: "It is a form language, not a form." "He does not remember what
  happened." (§3). Fall adds witnesses: "He reads the room afterward like evidence of a crime he did
  not witness." (§3) becomes reading the people who did witness it. *Done* (Composite).
- **The leash.** "HOPE signals them to mean something to him on demand." (Part VI, Aegis). *Done*:
  flinches in chapters 3 and 7, and the pull at the gala.
- **Project Catalyst.** "a genetic printer built to reproduce her on demand, engineered to form
  unstable emotional bonds with powerful enemies of the State" (Part III, The Enforcer Program). In
  Fall she is aimed at Aegis, the State's own hero, because he has started to waver *(added reading:
  the bond is the leash's second end)*. *Found* (her calibration file) and *done* (Persona).
- **Two women, one word.** The State text beside Rostova's portrait says CATALYST (chapter 3, a
  broadcast; chapter 8, the rig's screen); the text beside Cally's portrait in her file says
  "SUBJECT DESIGNATION: CALLY (ARCHIVED)" and "REASSIGNED CLASSIFICATION: CATALYST" (Scene 3). The
  player is the only one who sees both. The reason, proposed in `dr-tox/SCOPE.md` §3 and still the
  author's call: RCC names products, not people. In narration and UI they are **Rostova** and
  **Cally**; the bare word appears only in State text, beside a portrait. *Found*.
- **Material ceiling.** "concrete is the material of the future. Fluorescent tubes, conserved power,
  salvaged tools, and CRT-era hardware are not anachronisms" (Part III). The settlement's tube lights
  on a generator; the CRT monitors; the Spire's glass is the exception that proves it.

## 6. Characters

### Dr. Tox (Alexander Balklin), lead, chapters 1, 2, 4, 5, 8, 9, 11

- **Want:** in the bible, to hunt "the organization that made him" (§1, Core Identity); in Fall,
  pulled sideways by people who knock. **Fear:** his own body; and, new in Fall, being needed.
- **Voice:** says "RCC" and "the State", never "HOPE" (Part VI, Dr. Tox, voice note). Almost no
  spoken lines; captions, clinical, short, with Self's struck words as in Rise. Added lines, few:
  "Stay outside the ring." "Two marks. Not three." "That plate says who runs this hall." "Who built
  you?" (to Aegis; "Aegis" instead of "you" if he took the spec sheet in Rise).
- **Look:** as locked (§11); the shroud over the field kit, hood up in crowds.
- **Arc by ending.** *The Following:* the man who made his own cage has made other people's; the hall
  waits for him. *The Clinic:* he refused to be needed and the bloom took the people he would not
  bind. *First Word* and *Bond Held:* he goes over the balcony rail either way; what changes is the
  boy on the other side of the wall.

### Reese (RCC53-Δ1, Aegis), lead, chapters 3, 7, and the balcony

- **Want:** "his need to be good is *genuine.*" (Part VI, Aegis). In Fall, to make the containment
  mean what HOPE says it means. **Fear:** the cold "like a door closing somewhere he can't see" (Scene 2
  V), and, after chapter 3, the camera side of the street.
- **Voice:** says "HOPE" "Always, until the arc breaks him." Lines, all added and few: to Rostova's
  broadcast, nothing; in the relief hall: "This is a containment order." and the Word ("HOPE runs this
  hall", "RCC", or nothing); on the balcony: the Word again.
- **What carries in:** Rise's Word, his flinches (the leash pulls harder), whom he released at the
  lower ring (Nell Okafor in the hall), and his bonds (the profile Cally is aimed at).
- **Arc by ending.** *First Word:* he says it, and the leash takes the wall out of his hands; recalled.
  *Bond Held:* the doubt closes; he is the program's again. *The Clinic, The Following:* he holds the
  wall, says HOPE or nothing, and walks the other way, "for a long time yet".

### Cally (RCC53-Δ2; Cycle 41), lead, chapters 6 and 10

- **Want:** what she was printed for: "Do I have a name yet," (Scene 3), asking "what she's *for* now".
  **Fear:** none she could report; the crack has the fear, and it shows only in the glass.
- **Voice:** the bible's lines from Scene 3 are her birth and are not repeated. Added lines, in the
  same register: "Seventy-two hours. I'll need less." "He looks at amber like it owes him something."
  "For HOPE." (to her handler). She never says "RCC" in Fall (`cally.said_rcc` is always false).
- **The crack, in play:** the deck in Persona deals itself cards she did not build (a dropped blade, a
  painted sky, a loop of string); the reflection in the glass stops at plates that read RCC. She would
  "deny being compromised. She does not know." (Version B). The player knows.
- **Arc by ending.** *Bond Held:* she does what she was built for; the crack is filed as noise again.
  Otherwise: the calibration log records variance, and she is reassigned for the Finale.

## 10. 2D modes

### Mode A: Crowd thread (`m2_crowd_thread`, scroll)

Graphic style: **Underground comix crosshatch** (`docs/graphic_styles.json`): dense pen
crosshatching on cheap paper, lumpy figures packed shoulder to shoulder, his breath radius a ring of
bare white.

- **Story reason:** "he is a walking biological hazard who cannot safely share a room." (§2). The
  relief hall is the one room everyone shares. The comix line is the hall's own: crowded, ugly,
  sympathetic, drawn by someone who stood in the queue.
- **Movement:** side-scroll through a crowd that is itself moving. Walk, wait, turn sideways (narrows
  the ring by a quarter for two seconds, then Pulse +3), draw the shroud closed or open. **Inputs:**
  move; wait; turn; shroud; dart (chapter 11 only).
- **Rules.** The breath radius is a ring of bare paper around him: radius 1.0 m plus Pulse/50 m (1.4 m
  at Pulse 20, 3.0 m at 100), halved with the shroud closed. People shuffle, step back, turn round,
  reach for a dropped bucket. When a person's body enters the ring, their name and want appear above
  them, they cough once, and they become a card in the next Triage (`crowd.exposures`,
  `crowd.exposed_names`). Nobody is harmed further on the page. Pulse +2 a second while anyone is within
  half a metre of the ring's edge; +1 every 2 seconds with the shroud closed (the breath trapped);
  −2 a second waiting in a gap. Relief staff (chapter 2) and Spire security (chapter 11) have sight cones;
  being seen by staff closes the queue ahead for ten seconds. With `security_knows_face`, posters of his
  face hang in every panel, and anyone who looks from a poster to him steps back into the next person.
- **Darts (chapter 11):** a dart drops a guard for twenty seconds and costs `manifold.dart_cost`
  Pulse.
- **The trigger line.** In chapter 2 Pulse is capped five below the line: at the cap he leaves by a
  side door and the run ends at the back of the hall anyway (his discipline holds, and the scene is
  not the place for the first witnessed State). In chapter 11 it is not capped: at the line the page's
  border goes green from the corners in and cuts (`toxin_at_gala`; then `s1_composite_2`).
- **Progress shown:** the page scrolling to the shutter or the balcony door; the Pulse line in the
  bottom margin; the ring.
- **Failure and retry:** none; exposures are the cost and they come back as cards.
- **Sequences:** `s2_crowd_1` (the relief-hall queue, about 6 minutes, 40 people, two staff),
  `s2_crowd_2` (the service corridor with two guards and Ama and Lev at the door, then the gala floor,
  about 6 minutes, 60 guests and four security).
- **Difficulty curve:** the queue is slow and patient and steps back at random; the gala is fast,
  never steps back for anyone, and every guest is a witness.

### Mode B: Reflection run (`m2_reflection_run`, scroll)

Graphic style: **Silkscreen pop-art repeats**: flat screen-printed colour blocks, the same figure
repeated in off-register frames, one repeat that does not match.

- **Story reason:** "I'm told I'm very good at being watched." (Scene 3); "A small, private smile,
  aimed somewhere past his shoulder, like she's checking her reflection in glass that isn't there."
  (Scene 3). The Spire is glass. Pop-art repeats are the product line made visible: the same face,
  printed again.
- **Movement:** Cally walks the floor line left to right; her reflection walks in the glass below it,
  upside down, mirroring her with a 0.3-second lag. The glass world has its own geometry: some doors
  in her hall open only from a switch the reflection can reach. **Inputs:** move; wait; Force (hold).
- **Rules.** Both must reach the end of the hall. Cameras are objectives, not threats: the handler's
  cues (marked frames) require her to be in a camera's view at a set moment ("very good at being
  watched"); a missed cue costs 2 match points in the next Persona. **Refusals:** at set doors the
  reflection stops and turns its back. The player can hold Force for two seconds (the reflection is
  dragged on; `cally.forced` +1, `cally.crack` −5, no bond loss) or stop with it for three seconds
  (it walks on by itself; `cally.crack` +10; in the second run `cally.bond_aegis` −3). A refusal is

- **Progress shown:** the repeats fill with colour as she passes them; a refused frame stays
  unprinted.
- **Failure and retry:** none; a missed cue and the refusals are the record.
- **Sequences:** `s2_reflect_1` (to a staged meeting, about 4 minutes, one refusal at a door plate
  stamped RCC), `s2_reflect_2` (to the balcony, about 5 minutes, three refusals: a clean-air column's

  both worlds and the refusals come closer together.

## 11. 1D games

Every page is a medical instrument turned (`docs/COLLECTION.md` §4). No page touches a real
substance, procedure, dose or route: Triage's unit is an invented **mark** of an unnamed tincture;
The Manifold's is an invented **bead** of flow; Bloom's is a **tick** of wind.

### Triage (`g1_triage`, sorting patients under scarcity)

Graphic style: **Alcohol-marker sketch cards**: streaky layered marker tones on white card stock,
quick ink faces, a name and a want in the corner.

- **Purpose:** "His followers become chemically dependent on him (Dermorphin)" (Story: The Fall). The
  page is where the following is made, by the player, one mark at a time.
- **Rules.** Each card is a patient: a sketch, a name, one want, and what they ask for (marks of
  tincture up to their **need**, 0 to 2; water; a cot; time). The table has three columns: NOW, WAIT,
  CANNOT. A page runs in rounds; each round the player moves cards and gives what the page has: plenty
  of marks, little water, fewer cots (six cot-hours less the hours given to the last batch), and his
  time (one card at a time inside the ring). A card in NOW whose asks are met is **settled**. A card in
  WAIT stays, and its pain rises one need next round. A card in CANNOT is set aside by name
  (`triage.set_aside`). **A mark past the need** settles any card at once, whatever else it asked for:
  the pain stops asking. Each mark within need: `following` +2. Each mark past it: `following` +8,
  `triage.over_marks` +1, and the card's corner is stamped, in later pages, "came back early". Nobody
  dies on the page; cards still unsettled at the end leave the hall, named in the closing caption.
- **Layout:** the tincture tray, water and cots at the left; the three columns across the table;
  cards in marker; a round counter top right.
- **Inputs:** drag a card; tap a resource onto a card; tap a mark onto a card; end the round.
- **Finish:** the last round ends, or the player closes the shutter. **Failure:** none.
- **Appearances and curve:** `s1_triage_1` (the breach): two cards, one round, the first offer of a
  third mark. `s1_triage_2` (behind the shutter): six cards plus the exposed from the Crowd thread,
  three rounds, two cots. `s1_triage_3` (under the freeway): eight cards, the set-aside returning, four
  rounds, people asking for more by name (Wren for Teo). `s1_triage_4` (as Aegis, only if he stood
  down): Reese gives no marks; he can stand inside the radius, so he carries the CANNOT cards to cots
  (four lifts) and reads the stamped corners.
- **Hints:** a card's want is printed larger once it has waited a round.
- **Writes:** `triage.treated`, `triage.marks_given`, `triage.over_marks`, `triage.set_aside`,
  `following`.

### The Manifold (`g1_manifold`, flow routing)

Graphic style: **Mail-order comic advert**: a back-page coupon advert with numbered callouts,
exclamation headlines and a dashed clip-out border around the device.

- **Purpose:** "Micro-dart gauntlets — draw toxic plasma directly from a surgical shunt set into his
  wrist, fed by a clear line into a manifold along the gauntlet spine" (§4). The comic advert is the
  gauntlet as the comics would sell it, and the page is its plumbing.
- **Rules.** Each draw from the shunt sends 24 beads of flow (invented unit) down the spine. Six valves
  along it each have three settings (shut, half, open) and send flow down to the barrels below or on
  along the spine. Four barrels each want 6 beads. Flow that meets a shut valve with nowhere to go is
  **back-pressure**; Pulse per dart in Fall is 6 plus half the back-pressure, capped at 14
  (`manifold.dart_cost`; Rise's flat +12 is the default). Exact routing gives 6.
- **Layout:** the gauntlet across the page with numbered valves; barrels hanging below with fill
  marks; a coupon corner showing the Pulse per dart this routing will cost.
- **Inputs:** tap a valve to cycle it; test-draw (animates one draw).
- **Finish:** confirm the routing at any time. **Failure:** none; a poor routing costs Pulse all
  episode.
- **Appearances and curve:** `s1_manifold_1`: six valves, four barrels. `s1_manifold_2`: a fifth outlet
  appears at the end of the spine, capped. **Route it** (`manifold.resin_line`, `following` +10): it
  takes 6 beads, so the four barrels can be met only with more back-pressure (`dart_cost` +3 at best).
  **Cap it**: nothing changes, and Mercy's question stays unanswered.
- **Writes:** `manifold.dart_cost`, `manifold.resin_line`, `following`.

### Persona (`g1_persona`, deck-building to an attachment profile)

Graphic style: **1950s romance comic**: pastel flats, a tear on a lash, thought balloons and a
heart-shaped panel; no dot screen.

- **Purpose:** "SECONDARY APPLICATION: ANY HIGH-VALUE ASSET WITH PRE-EXISTING / ATTACHMENT SIGNATURE.
  RECOMMENDED CALIBRATION PERIOD: 72HRS." (Scene 3). The romance comic is the program's idea of love:
  a genre, a formula, a product.
- **Rules.** Aegis's **profile** is six facets with weights, built from Rise: the painted sky (20);
  the string (10, plus 5 per figure made in Cat's Cradle, up to 30); Cally (`reese.bond_cally` ÷ 2);
  Carmy (`reese.bond_carmy` ÷ 3); Rolan (`reese.bond_rolan` ÷ 3); amber warmth, HOPE's light (15);
  normalised to 100. The player sees the facets as thought balloons, and the weights only as the
  balloons' size. **Cards** are gestures and lines ("Laugh at his joke", "Sky-blue scarf", "Hum four
  notes", "Loop of string", "Take his hand"), each tagged with one or two facets. **Match** is how
  closely a hand's facet totals fit the profile, 0 to 100, shown to one decimal.
  **The crack as cards:** each round the deck deals itself one card Cally did not build ("Drop the
  blade", "Paint over the sky", "Leave the string where it fell"). Burn it (no effect) or keep it
  (`persona.crack_cards` +1, `cally.crack` +5, and it fits no facet, so the match falls).
- **Layout:** the heart panel with his profile at the top, the balloons around it, the hand below, the
  match meter along the bottom with a mark at 94.6.
- **Inputs:** draw three, keep one (building); play a hand of seven (playing); burn or keep a dealt
  card.
- **Finish:** confirm the hand. **Failure:** none; a low match is a low number.
- **Appearances and curve:** `s1_persona_1` (the annex): three "days", each a draft of three cards;
  builds the deck Persona 2 plays from; the match shown is a forecast. `s1_persona_2` (the gala table):
  three exchanges with Aegis across a table; his small reactions (a flinch, a laugh) reveal one hidden
  weight each; the final hand's match is written to `cally.bond_aegis`. Reaching 94.6 needs the whole
  profile read and every dealt card burned.
- **Writes:** `persona.match`, `persona.crack_cards`, `cally.crack`, `cally.bond_aegis`.

### Bloom (`g1_bloom`, spread containment on a grid)

Graphic style: **Four-colour Ben-Day newsprint**: cyan, magenta, yellow and black dot screens on
yellowed newsprint with a heavy keyline, the spread printed in rust dots.

- **Purpose:** Rostova "Releases engineered mutagens into population centers." (Part VI, Version A).
  The newsprint is how the State prints her: the villain's page in the morning paper.
- **Rules.** A district as a grid (`s1_bloom_1`: 9×7 blocks; `s1_bloom_2`: 10×7, the settlement in the
  two east columns). Spores start at the source cell. Each tick the wind carries them from every active
  cell to the downwind neighbour, and with a 30 percent chance to each crosswind neighbour. Cells hold
  residents, some by name; a cell the spores take is **marked**, and its names go to
  `bloom.marked_names`. Rostova's mutagen "Rewrites DNA on the fly, causing rapid, functional
  mutations": nobody dies on the page; the marked are shown changed, in a dot screen, and named.
  - *As Aegis (Bloom 1):* **absorb** a cell (clears it; Load +15; not above 100) or raise a **pane** on a
    cell edge (Load −10; blocks spread across that edge for the page).
  - *As Dr. Tox (Bloom 2):* **crews** (`bloom.crews`: following ÷ 10, plus one per Observer present)
    each cordon one cell (wet sheeting and salvaged filters; blocks spread into it), and can be moved
    once a tick. With the Observers: the Cauldron burns one cell clear once; the Twins collapse a line
    once (a row of blocked edges) and the page shows their cost.
- **The east front (Bloom 2).** At tick 6 the wind turns east. Holding the settlement then needs five
  cordons along the column before it at once. With fewer than five crews it cannot be held, however well
  the page is played: `bloom.settlement_lost`. This is deliberate: The Clinic's cost is built into the
  page.
- **Layout:** the grid under a keyline; the wind arrow and tick count in the margin; residents as small
  printed figures; the settlement's edge in red.
- **Inputs:** tap a cell (absorb or cordon); drag an edge (pane); advance the tick.
- **Finish:** no active cell can spread any further, or tick 12. **Failure:** none.
- **Writes:** `load`, `bloom.cells_lost`, `bloom.marked_names`, `bloom.settlement_lost`, `following`
  (+5 if the settlement holds).

### Composite (`g1_composite`, reconstruction from testimony)

Graphic style: **Identikit acetate overlays**: line-drawn feature strips on stacked clear acetate,
grease-pencil witness numbers, one strip that does not fit. **A new 1D type, Fall only**, proposed here
for the author's ruling that Fall needs its own reconstruction page, different from Rise's Aftermath
(`dr-tox/SCOPE.md` §9). Like Cat's Cradle in Rise, it is the one page outside the comic idiom: it is the
witnesses' document, not the comic's (the author's call, section 20).

- **Purpose:** "He does not remember what happened. He reads the room afterward like evidence of a crime
  he did not witness." (§3). In Fall there were witnesses. Aftermath ordered traces in time; Composite
  assembles a **form** from what people say they saw. The investigation of `docs/COLLECTION.md` §5,
  reconstructing what his own body did, now passes through other people, and what they say becomes
  what they believe.
- **Rules.** A light box with a pale base form. The bible's constants are printed on the base and
  never chosen: "Pale skin, stretched taut", "Thick blackened veins", "Eyes fully consumed by sickly
  luminous green", "Distended jaw", "Mass pulled from internal reserves" (§3). The player builds the
  rest region by region (head and jaw, shoulders, arms, back, gait), choosing one acetate strip for
  each from a stack. Witness cards run along the bottom: each has a quoted account and a position on a
  small inset map, so the player can see who had line of sight to what. A strip is **supported** when at
  least two witnesses with line of sight to that region describe it. Some strips are **awed**: a halo,
  wings, a light from the chest, described only by witnesses who could not see but believed. The form
  follows the threat and is never the same twice (§3: "Never generate the same monster twice."): the
  raid's sustained fire makes "Overlapping hardened scab plating across chest, shoulders, forearms";
  the gala's crowd makes "Reach — everything longer than it should be".
- **Layout:** the light box centre; strip stacks left and right; witness cards along the bottom; the
  inset map top right.
- **Inputs:** tap a witness to hear the account; drag a strip onto a region; pin.
- **Finish:** pin the composite at any time. **Reconstructed** when every region is supported and no
  awed strip is pinned: the event's `reconstructed` is true and Self loses 10 instead of 15.
  Every awed strip pinned: `composite.awed` +1, `following` +5; two or more: `cult.icon` (the
  settlement copies this composite and pins it in the cavern hall). **Failure:** none.
- **Appearances and curve:** `s1_composite_1` (under the freeway): four regions, six witnesses (Mercy,
  Wren, Bashir, Dov, Ren Sato from the truck, Yusuf who "saw a light"); Sato is the only one at the
  front and the most afraid. `s1_composite_2` (the gala, only if the State fired): five regions, four
  witnesses who will not testify in public (Ama, Lev, Corinne Vale, Desmond Achebe); no awed strips,
  but terrified ones (horns, a tail) that must be left out the same way.
- **Writes:** `toxin_events`, `composite.reconstructed`, `composite.awed`, `cult.icon`, `following`,
  `self`.

## 12. Core systems

### Pulse (Dr. Tox)

Range 0–100, floor 20; floor 40 when Amber is 0 at a chapter start (Rise's rule). +2 per second while
anyone presses the edge of the breath radius; +1 per 2 seconds with the shroud closed; +3 for a turn
sideways; +`manifold.dart_cost` per dart; +15 on a hit; −2 per second waiting in a gap. Shown as the
margin line on every Dr. Tox page and as his breathing in 3D.

### The trigger line (the stake, adopted)

In Fall the Toxin State fires when Pulse reaches **Self**, not 100: "The body is not what's at risk.
The man is, and the damage is cumulative." (Part VI, Dr. Tox §3, open item; Part IX Q2, adopted by the
author, `dr-tox/SCOPE.md` question 6). Self 100 is a line at 100; after one witnessed State and an
unreconstructed Composite, 85; after Rise's Green ending, 75 before Fall begins.

### The Toxin State (the cost, adopted)

Each event burns 2 Amber ("every transformation burns down a finite supply he must synthesize by hand",
§2, open item; Part IX Q1, adopted) and takes 15 Self, or 10 if its Composite is reconstructed. It is
one panel or one shot: the suit failing (§8), then a cut, then the witnesses. It fires once for every
player (scripted, the raid in chapter 5) and once more only if the player lets Pulse reach the line in
chapter 11. "it should feel like containment failure, not a feature" (§12).

### The breath radius and the shroud

Radius 1.0 m + Pulse/50 m; the shroud closed halves it. It is shown in 3D as a faint ring on the floor
and in the Crowd thread as bare paper. Aegis alone can stand inside it.

### Amber

A counter, 0–6, carried in from Rise. One dose at the start of every Dr. Tox chapter (1, 2, 4, 5, 8, 9,

to 4) and takes one cot-hour from the next Triage. There is no titration page in Fall (The Cage is
Rise's).

### Self

0–100, carried in from Rise; never restored. Below 90 one word in each caption is struck out; below 70,

### First Word

He says it. Security opens fire on the man at the rail; Reese turns the wall to cover him, and Ama and
Lev behind him at the service door. The leash fires: the flashback lands "with the label torn off"
(Scene 2 III), he mistakes it for gratitude, and the wall goes out for as long as it has fired before.
Dr. Tox goes over the rail. Ama and Lev are taken by security. The last image: Reese in the recall
transport, amber light, his lips moving on one word nobody can hear.

### Bond Held

Dr. Tox says what the hall is and who runs it. Cally's hand finds Reese's wrist and the doubt the relief
hall put in him closes "like a door closing somewhere he can't see" (Scene 2 V). He holds the wall until
the doctor goes over the rail, then follows her in off the balcony and does not look at the plate. The
last image: the calibration log on a handler's screen, one line appended: CALIBRATION COMPLETE
*(added State text)*. She does what she was built to do. The crack is still there; nobody will find it
in this episode.

### The Clinic

He gave to need and no further. Without a following there were not crews enough for the east front, and
the bloom took the settlement. The last scenes: the freeway months later, spore rust on the drums, the
shelters empty; and the cavern hall in Snakehead, where he makes up a cot for whoever comes next, and
someone who came last night goes home up the breach. Nobody stays. The Finale still opens on his cult
(Part II): smaller, and not his own doing.

### The Following

The bridge to the Finale. He comes back to a cavern hall full of people who need him: the ones he dosed
past their need, the ones who pinned the awed composite to the wall, the ones who went quiet in a rite he
did not make. The blades are on the cloth. He doses himself under the amber lamp ("Pain that is absolute
and routine", §10), then turns to them. The cut comes before any of them is dosed. The bible's fixed
point, kept: "His followers become chemically dependent on him" (Story: The Fall).

### Why only one can be true

The resolver stops at the first true condition. The balcony's events outrank the cavern's: a word that
breaks the leash outranks a bond that holds it (Reese cannot both defect and be held), and either closes
the episode on the Spire. If neither happened, the episode closes on Snakehead, and the following decides
which Snakehead.

### How a player reaches each one on purpose

- **First Word:** on the balcony, say "RCC". (It helps to have stood down in chapter 7 and read the
  plate, but nothing is required.)
- **Bond Held:** in Persona 2 read the whole profile and burn every dealt card; in Reflection run 2 Force
  every refusal. Say "HOPE" or nothing on the balcony.
- **The Clinic:** in Triage, give marks only to need; give water and cots instead; cap the fifth outlet;
  pin no awed strip. Do not say "RCC" on the balcony, and stop with at least one refusal.
- **The Following:** settle cards with marks past the need when the cots run out; route the resin line or
  pin the awed composite. This is where a player who plays Triage for speed arrives.

## 20. Risks and open questions

**Risks.**

- *Invention load.* Fall is a stub: almost everything here is an addition (section 21). Mitigation: every
  chapter is built on one of the bible's three premises (emergence, the cult, institutional failure) and
  the two fixed points; nothing contradicts a locked date.
- *Drugs and dosing.* The bible names real substances for the cult. Mitigation: the names appear only in
  this design and in State text; play uses an invented mark of an unnamed tincture; no page shows a
  source, a quantity of a real substance, a procedure or a route; the rite shows only stillness.
- *The cult as glamour.* Mitigation: every follower is a named person with a want that is not about him;
  the dependence is made by the player's own shortcuts and shown as cost on every ending card.
- *The plague-marked as props.* Mitigation: names and wants on every card; nobody dies on a page; the
  marked in Bloom are changed, named and followed up.
- *A child in peril.* None on screen: Teo is nineteen; no child is in the raid, the bloom or the gala.
- *The Toxin State as a feature.* Once for everyone, once more only by the player's own Pulse; one shot and
  a cut.
- *Look-alikes.* The Toxin State's forms follow §3 and §8; the Composite's base is never a silhouette from
  another work.
- *Canon drift.* The Fall stub may be filled by the author later; every chapter here is listed as an
  addition so it can be struck without touching the bible.
