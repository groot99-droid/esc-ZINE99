# People of the Bark — Design

## 1. Summary

You guide a tiny civilization through one year on the bark of a tree they call the Endless Trunk.
A squirrel is a cataclysm, rain is a blessing and a hand is a god. You can always pull back to see
what really happened, and the people can never be told.

| Item | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Source | *People of the Bark*, `creative-writing/vault/05_Worldbuilding/01_People_of_the_Bark.md` (a world concept and screen treatment) |
| Target length | 150–180 minutes; this design runs 170 minutes on a first playthrough |
| Players | 1 |
| Core verb | **Interpret** |
| Structure | Seven chapters: the Tremor, four seasons, the Coming of the Shadow, the pull-back |
| Camera in 3D | Top-down god camera over a vertical wall of bark, with a pull-back to the backyard |
| 2D modes | Crack runner; crack defence |
| 1D games | Omen Spread, Sap Kitchen, The Ring Door, Trunk and Shadow, Tremor Scroll |
| Endings decided by | The balance between the three institutions over the year, the reading given to the Shadow, and the state of the bark |
| Register | Comic and mythic; comedy with heart |

## 2. Why this game

Two games in the collection are nearest: The Lord of the Sea (also comic, mythic, and about
divinity) and Lira Calyx (also about reading patterns in data).

| Question | People of the Bark | The Lord of the Sea | Lira Calyx |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Who is the player? | A people, through their council, plus one god-like view they cannot share | A real god who has lost his following | One scientist, then an archivist |
| Where is divinity? | Imagined. The "gods" are a squirrel, a beetle and a hand. The player knows; the people never learn | Real, and failing | Absent; the mystery is human and technical |
| What does the player do with a pattern? | Turns it into belief, and belief into policy. The pattern only half works | Not about patterns; about swagger and devotion | Measures it and tests whether the data was falsified |
| How does play move? | City-building on a vertical wall in 3D; side-on runs and defences in 2D; five flat games in 1D | Open sea chart | A climb up a mountain |
| What ends the game? | Balance between three institutions | The Order and Wild map and a final board match | The share of data verified by hand |
| Comedy | Comes only from the gap between two scales. The people's courage is never the joke | Comes from a god's vanity | None; paranoia |

The one thing no other game in the collection does: the player holds a truth that the game forbids
them to use directly. Every choice is an interpretation made inside someone else's worldview.

**The author's ending.** The treatment ends with the Shadow, the hand checking for termites, and
the moral. The author has said how it closes: the Shadow passes, the view pulls back to the
backyard, and the moral is the last line. That ending is **The Shadow Passes**, marked
`as_written`. It follows when the player keeps to the story's own path: the priests chant, the
warriors prepare, the scholars record, and no one institution decides for the others.

The treatment names no one, does not say which season it is, and does not say what the hand does
after reaching the trunk. Everything that fills those gaps is listed in section 21.

## 4. Investment

**The hook in the first 5 minutes.** The game opens at bark scale, in the dark before dawn, on a
tiny city of lantern-lit homes carved into a ridge. The player wakes the city one shutter at a time
(click a home; its lantern swells). A child, Twig, has a kite made of a bark flake stuck on a
splinter; the player frees it with one drag. Mossa, the messenger, stretches at the top of the
Long Crack. Old Resin stirs a pot. Elder Pell shuffles cards by feel. At 4:30 the world shakes.
Dust falls like snow. The city runs to its posts. Then the game teaches one control: hold Tab to
look up. The camera rises out of the bark, out of the trunk, out of the tree, and the player sees
a squirrel scratching an itch. The camera comes back down, and the people are still bracing. The
question is planted at once: *what do I do with what I know?*

**What the player comes to care about.** Three things, each made to matter before it is threatened.

| What | How the game makes the player care, in play | When it is first threatened |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The six named bark people | Each has a job the player does with them: freeing Twig's kite, running as Mossa, cooking with Old Resin, being taught the cards by Pell, standing a battlement with Captain Burr, filling Fenn's scroll. Each has a small habit the player sees repeat (Pell taps a card's edge twice before laying it; Burr polishes a spear that has never touched an enemy) | Not until summer (chapter 3, minute 54): the first insect in the cracks. Pell's death is in winter, after two full seasons of his lessons |
| Highridge, the city | The player builds it, home by home, along grooves the player chose. Every building is placed by hand, and the city remembers placement for the whole year | The windstorm in summer, then the ants in autumn |
| Their belief | The player writes it. Each Omen Spread turns into a saying painted on a wall, a rule in their board game and a policy for the season | At the Shadow, when the belief the player made is the only thing the people have to face it with |

**The question that pulls them to the end.** Each event has an ordinary cause, and the player
sees it. The people cannot. The player is drawn on by two questions: *what will they make of the
next thing?* and, from autumn on, *what will they make of the Shadow that the birdsong and the
tremors keep pointing to?* The player wants to see whether the people they have shaped will
meet a god's hand with prayer, spears, a map or an offering, and whether the bark holds.

## 5. Lore bible

How the player learns each item: **found** (on an object or in a 1D page), **told** (a line of
dialogue or a saying), **seen** (on screen, including the pull-back), **done** (by playing it).

### History and timeline

All history below is added for the game (see section 21). The people count years by rings, as
the tree does, and do not know that the rings they count are the tree's.

| When | What happened | How the player learns it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The First Ring | The people woke on the bark "when the Trunk first breathed". Nothing is remembered before | Found: the outermost glyph on the Door of the First Ring |
| The Age of Grooves | The first cities were carved into the deep grooves on the east face, where dawn reaches first | Found: the second ring of the First Ring door; seen: old empty grooves below Highridge |
| The Great Flake | A district called Underhollow carved too many temples into one plate of bark. The plate came away and fell into the Under. The people say the Trunk shrugged them off for greed | Told: Old Resin, spring; found: the Deep Knot door's third ring |
| The Year of Two Shadows | A shadow covered the world twice in one year, long ago. The people of that year hid in the knots. Nothing happened, which the elders count as proof that hiding works | Told: Pell, winter; done: Trunk and Shadow, whose oldest rule comes from that year |
| The Founding of the Scroll House | Scholars began recording tremors "so the gods would not have to repeat themselves" | Found: the header of the Tremor Scroll |
| This year | The year of the game. The player names it after the first reading (for example, "the Year of the Message") | Done: chapter 1 |

### Rules of the world

| Rule | In play | How the player learns it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The bark people can never be told what is outside the tree | No action, card, line or menu lets the player explain a cause to them. The pull-back view has no cursor that can touch anything | Done: the player tries, and nothing answers |
| Every disaster has an ordinary cause the player can see | Every event in the game has a pull-back cause (section 12, the event table) | Seen: the pull-back |
| Their belief half works | A reading whose meaning happens to fit the real cause gives a practical benefit (the Fit rule) | Done: the Omen Spread; told: Fenn notices it in autumn |
| The tree is alive, and it is just a tree | The bark integrity meter: the bark heals slowly with sealant and breaks if over-tapped and over-carved | Seen: hairline cracks in the city; told: the bark "groans" below 40 |
| Gravity runs down the Trunk | The people live on a vertical wall. "Down" is the Under; "up" is the Crown | Seen and done: 3D and 2D |
| Size decides god or monster | Small insects are monsters. Large insects are gods. Middle-sized ones are argued over, and the reading decides | Done: crack defence; told: Captain Burr |
| Sap is sacred water | Sap is the main resource. It is drunk, built with, cooked and offered | Done: the whole economy |
| Courage is never the punchline | A design rule, not a world rule: no pull-back plays a comic sting over the people, and the camera always returns to them | Seen: every pull-back ends back on their faces |

## 6. Characters

The treatment names no individuals. Every character below is **added** for the game, so the year
has faces. They speak sparsely: a line or two at a time, never a dialogue scene. There are no
source lines to quote for them; the sample lines are proposals.

### Mossa, the messenger

- **Who:** About fifteen in bark years. The fastest runner in Highridge. The player controls her in
  every crack runner sequence.
- **Want:** To run the whole Long Crack up to the Crown one day.
- **Fear:** The Long Fall, off the bark into the Under.
- **Habit:** Counts her steps aloud under her breath, and loses count when she is happy.
- **Lines:** "Three hundred and— never mind. I'm here." / "If it shakes, I go faster. That's
  the rule." / "Where does the crack go, after Knotholm?"
- **Arc by ending:** *Shadow Passes*: runs the news of the passing to every city, and the
  last shot of the bark is her starting up the Long Crack again. *War on the God*: runs the
  victory, and is made the first herald of a warrior city. *Edge of the World*: carries Fenn's
  map, and is the first to say out loud that she wants to go to the edge. *The Flake*: survives on
  the part that held, and runs the names of the lost to every city.

### Elder Pell, the omen reader

- **Who:** The eldest of the Knot. His eyes are failing, so he reads the cards by the notches cut
  in their edges. He teaches the player the Omen Spread.
- **Want:** To leave the deck in good hands.
- **Fear:** That his readings have only ever been guesses.
- **Habit:** Taps each card's edge twice before he lays it.
- **Lines:** "The Trunk does not shout twice. Write it down." / "A card means what we do after
  it." / "Leaves fall. I know which one is mine."
- **Arc:** Pell dies in winter, of age, in every run. It is the one certain loss of the year
  (section 20 asks the author to confirm it). Who he leaves the deck to is the player's last
  reading with him, and it shapes the Shadow. In every ending his saying is painted on a wall.

### Captain Burr, of the Battlements

- **Who:** Commander of Splinterwatch. Earnest, broad, kind. Has never fought anything bigger
  than a mite.
- **Want:** One true battle that matters.
- **Fear:** Being useless when it comes.
- **Habit:** Polishes his spear when he is nervous, which is always.
- **Lines:** "Battlements! Everyone on the— yes, you too, Fenn." / "Small ones we fight. Big
  ones we bow to. Middling ones, we ask the Knot." / "I'm not afraid. I'm ready. They feel the
  same from inside."
- **Arc by ending:** *Shadow Passes*: stands ready, the hand goes, and he is glad not to have
  needed to fight. *War on the God*: leads the charge up the knuckle and drives a splinter into it.
  He becomes a legend; his spear is finally blooded, and he is never the same gentle man.
  *Edge of the World*: escorts the scholars to the shadow's edge and plants their stakes.
  *The Flake*: holds the line at the break and pulls people over it.

### Fenn, of the Scroll House

- **Who:** Keeper of the Tremor Scroll. Precise, sleepless, ink on every finger.
- **Want:** To know the pattern under everything.
- **Fear:** That the pattern means nothing.
- **Habit:** Draws a tiny tremor glyph in the margin whenever anyone speaks to her.
- **Lines:** "Dawn. East face. Strength two. Again." / "It is not a god. Or it is a god with a
  routine." / "If it comes every seventh day, then it is coming."
- **Arc by ending:** *Shadow Passes*: records the Shadow, three taps and a press, and files it
  with the rest. *War on the God*: records the victory, and quietly records that the
  Shadow pulled away before the spear struck. *Edge of the World*: draws the first map with an edge
  on it, and is tried for heresy by the Knot. *The Flake*: records what fell.

### Old Resin, the sap cook

- **Who:** The oldest cook in Highridge. Irreverent. Runs the Sap Kitchen.
- **Want:** To fill the recipe book before she dies.
- **Fear:** Nothing she will admit to.
- **Habit:** Tastes everything, including the sealant.
- **Lines:** "Sacred water. Lovely. Pass me the sacred water and a spoon." / "Too hot and it's
  glue. Too cold and it's a puddle. Like people." / "Underhollow carved till the Trunk shrugged.
  Don't you."
- **Arc by ending:** *Shadow Passes*: cooks the feast. *War on the God*: cooks the victory feast
  and says nothing. *Edge of the World*: hides Fenn's map in her recipe book. *The Flake*: warned
  everyone, and is the one who starts cooking for the survivors.

### Twig, a child

- **Who:** A child of Highridge. Asks questions the elders cannot answer. Flies a bark-flake
  kite.
- **Want:** To see the top of the Trunk.
- **Fear:** The dark under the Shadow.
- **Habit:** Asks "and then what?" after every answer.
- **Lines:** "And then what?" / "What if the Shadow is just playing?" / "I made a new rule. The
  Hand picks you up and puts you down somewhere else."
- **Arc:** Twig invents the Hand piece in winter's Trunk and Shadow. In every ending the last
  bark-scale image includes Twig's kite. If Pell leaves the deck to Twig, Twig reads the Shadow.

### Chanter Hollow

- **Who:** The priest who leads the chants. A deep voice. Speaks only in chant lines.
- **Lines:** "Trunk, hear us. Trunk, hold us." / "Bend, and do not break."
- **Role:** Leads the chant in chapter 6 in every ending; becomes the Knot's leader if Pell
  leaves the deck to the priests.

### The squirrel, the beetle, the bird and the hand

They are causes, not characters. They have no lines, no names and no faces. The human is only
ever a hand and a sleeve, and a figure crossing the yard at a distance in chapter 7.

### The Roll of the Bark

When a bark person is lost (in a breached crack, in a fight against an insect read as a monster,
in the war ending, in the Flake), the Roll gains a line: a name and one habit, drawn from a
written pool of 48 (for example, "Hob, who counted ridges to fall asleep"; "Wren, who saved the
last sip for whoever came in late"). The Roll is carved on the Knot temple wall and read in
chapter 7. No loss is ever just a number on screen. This follows the vault's ethical-restraint
rule: the lost have small habits and the dignity of more than plot points.

## 8. Levels

Each chapter lists its scenes in order with scene ids from `data/scenes.json`.

### Chapter 1 — The Tremor (`ch1_tremor`, 20 minutes)

- **Goal:** Meet the city, survive the Tremor, give it a meaning, record it.
- **Where:** Highridge, Splinterwatch's battlements, the Knot, the Scroll House.
- **Scenes in order:**
  1. `c1_city_dawn` (3D, 6 min). Wake the city: click 8 shutters. Free Twig's kite from a
     splinter (one drag). Place the year's first sap tap in a groove (teaches placement on the
     bark: grooves glow where a tap can go). Meet Mossa, Old Resin, Pell, Burr and Fenn, one line
     each. At 4:30 the Tremor hits: the screen shakes, dust falls in sheets, homes shudder. The
     player clicks each of the three institutions' halls to send them to their posts. (The order
     the player clicks does not matter; the scene follows the source's order.)
  2. `c1_battlements` (3D, 4 min). Splinterwatch during the Tremor. Place six warriors on the
     battlement slots before the second shake; dust hazards knock unplaced warriors flat (comic,
     harmless). The pull-back prompt appears: "Hold Tab to look up." The camera rises and shows
     the squirrel on the trunk, scratching. Text card, verbatim: "Meanwhile, the squirrel is just
     scratching an itch." The camera returns to the bark.
  3. `c1_omen` (1D Omen Spread, 4 min). Pell teaches the spread. The event card is the Tremor.
     Because the player pulled back, the Backyard card lies face down at the side; the player can
     turn it. The reading's meaning names the year ("the Year of the Message", "the Year of the
     Trial", and so on).
  4. `c1_scroll` (1D Tremor Scroll, 4 min). Fenn shows the scroll: eleven past days of dawn
     tremors on the east face. Two forecasts: tomorrow's dawn and the day after.
  5. `c1_city_year` (3D, 2 min). The city paints the year's name on the ridge. Title card:
     "The Endless Trunk".
- **What the player learns:** The two scales; the three institutions; that the people cannot be
  told.

## 10. 2D modes

Both modes are side-on views of the bark wall, entered with `flatten`: the 3D god camera rotates
to orthographic and the bark relief becomes platforms. Both use the engine's `scroll` movement.

### Mode A: Crack runner (`crack_runner`)

- **What it is:** Mossa runs a crack between cities to carry a reading or a warning.
- **Movement type:** `scroll`. The level scrolls along X and Y: cracks run mostly up and down the
  trunk, so runs climb and drop as much as they cross.
- **Controls:** Move (A/D or stick), jump (Space / A), cling (hold Shift / RT: stick to a bark wall
  for up to 3 seconds; a stamina ring shows it), roll (C / B: a short dash that passes under dust),
  brace (hold S / down: stand through a tremor).
- **Physics:** Run 6 body-lengths per second; jump 2.5 body-lengths high; falls longer than 6
  body-lengths stun for 1 second. Wall-cling refills on any ledge.
- **The tremor clock:** Every run has a tremor due. If the player's last Tremor Scroll forecast
  for that time was right, a brace marker appears on the HUD 3 seconds early; braced, Mossa stays
  on her feet. Unbraced, she is knocked one ledge down and dust falls for 4 seconds.
- **Hazards:**

| Hazard | Effect | Seasons |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sap drip | Sticky: run speed halved for 2 s | Spring, summer |
| Sap fall | A rope of sap she can slide down; fast travel, cannot stop | Summer |
| Dust sheet | Falls after a tremor; blocks sight; roll passes under | All |
| Splinter spikes | Knockback | All |
| Lichen tuft | Bouncy; launches her one ledge up | Spring, autumn |
| Mite | Walks a ledge; touching it knocks her back | Summer |
| Rain bead | Rolls down a groove; ride it or be swept | Spring |
| Ice slide | No friction | Winter |
| Frozen sap bridge | Cracks after one crossing | Winter |
| The shadow's edge | A wall of dark moving behind her at 4.5 body-lengths per second | The Shadow |

- **Progress:** A thin bark strip at the top of the screen shows the crack from start city to end
  city, with Mossa's mark on it and the tremor's mark.
- **Failure and retry:** A fall off a ledge with nothing below is caught by a lichen tuft lower
  down (no death; Mossa's fear is the Long Fall, and the game never makes it happen in the runner).
  She loses 8 seconds. Each run has a soft time limit; arriving late costs morale −3 and, for the
  Shadow run, one card from the final spread's hand. Checkpoints at each segment. There is no game
  over.
- **Writes:** `mossa.on_time` (+1 for each on-time arrival), `morale`.

### Mode B: Crack defence (`crack_defence`)

- **What it is:** The warriors hold a crack against insects, side-on along its length, falling back
  barricade by barricade.
- **Movement type:** `scroll`. The crack is a long horizontal channel seen side-on; the view scrolls
  along X with Captain Burr, and along Y where the crack branches up or down.
- **Controls:** Move Burr (A/D, W/S on branches), place barricade (E, at a marked line), assign a
  warrior to the nearest line (Q), throw sap-glue (F: slows insects in a 2-body-length pool for 6 s,
  costs 2 sap), sound the fall-back (R: everyone retreats to the next line).
- **Rules:** The crack has 5 barricade lines. The squad has `3 + floor(inst.warriors / 15)`
  warriors (3 to 9). Each line holds while it has at least one warrior and its barricade is above
  0. Insects attack the nearest line. A barricade has 10 points; a warrior repairs 1 point per
  second if no insect is on it. If a line falls with warriors on it who were not called back,
  each of those warriors is lost (Roll of the Bark). Sounding the fall-back in time saves them.
- **God or monster:** Before a defence against a middle-sized or large insect, the reading decides
  what it is. A **monster** is fought: the goal is to turn it back by holding until its "will"
  bar empties. A **god** is escorted: the goal is to keep the people out of its path and lay
  offerings at each line; it passes on its own. The same level is used both ways.
- **Hazards:** Mites (small, many, monsters), springtails (jump over a line), the Beetle (huge;
  one line per 20 seconds if fought, harmless if escorted), ants (middle-sized, come in columns of
  12).
- **Progress:** A five-notch bar showing which lines still stand; the insect's will bar; a timer
  for escorts.
- **Failure and retry:** If all five lines fall, the crack is breached: bark integrity −5,
  warriors −3 standing, hands −2, and the warriors left on the last line are lost. The story goes
  on. The player may retry once from the last checkpoint (line 3) before the breach is final; a
  setting removes the limit.
- **Writes:** `inst.warriors`, `bark.integrity`, `hands`, `fallen`, `roll`, `defence.breaches`,
  `burr.wounded`.

## 11. 1D games

Every 1D game is a full page drawn as bark-paper, inked and sap-stained, entered by `push-in`
from the 3D scene (the camera moves into a table or a wall that becomes the page) and left by
`pull-out`. None of them runs the world beneath it (`world_runs: false`). Each has text-size
settings and can be played without a timer.

### 11.1 Omen Spread (`omen_spread`, card interpretation)

**Purpose in the story.** This is the core verb. After each event, the Knot lays out a spread
and the player decides what the event meant. The meaning becomes the season's belief, a saying
on a wall, a rule in the board game and a policy that shifts power between the institutions.

**The deck.** Eighteen Omen cards: six suits, one per belief in the source, and in each suit one
card for each sigil. Each card has a weight from 1 to 3.

| Suit (belief) | Eye card (Knot) | Spear card (Battlements) | Quill card (Scroll House) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Wind (trial) | The Bowed Elder, 2 | The Bending Ridge, 3 | The Counted Gust, 1 |
| Rain (blessing) | The Full Cup, 3 | The Washed Spear, 1 | The Marked Bead, 2 |
| Song (prophecy) | The Singer Above, 3 | The War Note, 1 | The Written Tune, 2 |
| Tremor (message) | The Shaken Lamp, 1 | The Braced Wall, 2 | The Long Scroll, 3 |
| Leaf (death) | The Last Leaf, 3 | The Falling Shield, 2 | The Leaf Count, 1 |
| Mandible (monster or god) | The Great Visitor, 1 | The Biting Thing, 3 | The Measured Shell, 2 |

Two more cards are not dealt. The **event card** (one per event: Tremor, Rain, Wind, Beetle,
Leaf, Song, the Year, the Shadow) sits at the centre and has weight 2 in its own suit (the Year
and the Shadow have no suit). The **Backyard card** lies face down at the side if the player
pulled back during the event. Turning it shows the real cause, drawn small and plain, and in its
corner the suit that **fits** the cause. It can never be placed.

**Layout.** A bark-paper table seen from above. Centre: the event card. Around it, three slots:
**Root** (why it came, left), **Branch** (what it asks of us, right) and **Crown** (what will come
of it, top). Bottom: the hand, fanned. Left margin: the Balance Rope (three strands, Eye, Spear,
Quill). Right margin: the Backyard card, if any, and the reading preview, a line written in the
reader's hand that updates as cards are placed ("It is a Message. The Scroll House will answer
it."). A "Lay the reading" button seals it.

**Inputs.** Drag a card to a slot; drag back to swap; click the Backyard card to turn it; click
"Lay the reading". Keyboard: 1–6 to pick a card, R/B/C to place.

**Rules.**

1. Pell (or the heir) deals a hand of `4 + (inst.elders >= 40) + (inst.elders >= 60)` cards from
   the shuffled 18. At least one card of the event's suit is guaranteed.
2. The player places exactly three cards, one per slot.
3. **Meaning** is the suit with the highest total weight among the three placed cards plus the
   event card. Ties go to the suit of the Root card.
4. **Policy** is the sigil carried by two or three of the placed cards. Two of one sigil: that
   institution +6. Three of one sigil: +9, and each of the other two −2. One of each sigil: a
   **balanced reading**, each institution +2, and no policy.
5. **Clear reading:** all three placed cards share the event's suit. Morale +5.
   **Bold reading:** none shares the event's suit. Morale −3, scholars +2 (they like a new idea).
6. **Fit:** if the Meaning equals the event's fit suit, the reading pays off in practice (the table
   in section 12). This is the source's "They’re not wrong." / "But they’re not right either." made into a rule. The people credit the gods; the
   player knows better.
7. The **Crown card's** name becomes a saying painted in the city, and its suit adds a rule to the
   next Trunk and Shadow game (11.4).
8. The reading is written to the `readings` ledger: season, event, meaning, policy, fit, crown.

**Special spreads.**

- **The Beetle** (`c3_omen_beetle`) and **the Leaf** (`c4_omen_leaf`): if the Policy is Spear, the
  insect is read as a **monster** (`beetle_reading` / `ant_reading` = `monster`); otherwise as a
  **god**. The preview line says so before the player commits.
- **Pell's last spread** (`c5_omen_pell`): the event card is the Year. The hand is six cards. The
  Policy names the heir of the deck: Eye → Chanter Hollow (`pell.heir = hollow`, Knot +8);
  Spear → Captain Burr (`burr`, Battlements +8); Quill → Fenn (`fenn`, Scroll House +8); balanced →
  Twig (`twig`, each +3). Meaning has no effect here.
- **The Reading of the Shadow** (`c6_omen`). See below.

**The Reading of the Shadow.** The deck is the **Year deck**: twelve cards. The number of each
sigil in it is `round(12 × standing / sum of the three standings)` (adjusted by one so the total is
12). Their suits come from the year's readings. The hand is 5 cards, +1 if `shadow_rehearsed`,
+1 if the player forecast the seventh-day tread in `c6_scroll`, −1 if Mossa's Shadow run was late.
The reading is set by the placed cards' sigils:

| Placed sigils | Reading offered | Allowed only if | Otherwise |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

| Quill majority | **habit**: "It is a thing with a habit. We will learn its shape." | `year.leading = scholars` and `forecasts_correct >= 8` | trial |
| Eye majority | **offering**: "It is a hungry god. Give it everything." | `year.leading = elders` | trial |

The preview shows the reading before the player lays it, so the player always knows which branch
they are choosing. The result is written to `shadow_reading`.

**Win or finish state.** A spread always finishes; there is no losing hand. Its quality shows in
Clear, Bold and Fit.

**Difficulty curve.**

### 11.2 Sap Kitchen (`sap_kitchen`, cooking by discovery)

**Purpose in the story.** Sap is sacred water and the main resource. Old Resin's kitchen turns it
into **food** (feeds the city), **sealant** (mends the bark, the only way to raise integrity
quickly) or **offerings** (raise the Knot). Recipes are found by experiment and kept in her book.

**Layout.** Left page: the pot over a fire, a heat dial (low, middle, high), a stir paddle, an
hourglass for boil time (short, long). Right page: Old Resin's recipe book, 12 entries, each a
blank silhouette with a one-word hint ("sticky", "gold", "keeps") until found. Across the bottom:
the ingredient shelf, and the sap jar showing the sap available for this visit.

**Inputs.** Choose heat (click the dial); add up to three ingredients in order (drag to the pot);
set stirs (click the paddle 0–6 times); choose boil time; click "Cook". One pot costs 10 sap.

**Ingredients.** Sap (always the base), lichen, pollen, aphid honeydew, resin, ash (from an old
lightning scar), frost (winter only), leaf-skin (autumn and winter).

**How a pot resolves.** Heat and boil time set **thickness**:

| Heat \ boil | Short | Long |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Low | Runny | Syrup |
| Middle | Syrup | Glue |
| High | Glue | Hard |

Stirs: 0–2 leaves it clear; 3–4 makes it smooth; 5–6 makes it frothy (froth ruins glue: it
becomes "crumbly", a failed pot).

**The twelve recipes.**

| # | Recipe | Category | Thickness | Ingredients (any order unless noted) | Stirs | Effect per pot | Opens |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Groove Syrup | Food | Syrup | Sap only | 0–2 | Food +6 | Spring |
| 2 | Lichen Loaf | Food | Hard | Lichen | 3–4 | Food +8 | Spring |
| 3 | Pollen Cake | Food | Hard | Pollen, honeydew | 3–4 | Food +10, morale +2 | Spring |
| 4 | Dew Broth | Food | Runny | Honeydew, lichen | 5–6 | Food +7 | Spring |
| 5 | Ridge Glue | Sealant | Glue | Resin | 0–2 | Integrity +4 | Autumn |
| 6 | Ashpatch | Sealant | Glue | Resin, then ash (ash last) | 3–4 | Integrity +6 | Autumn |
| 7 | Leaf-skin Plaster | Sealant | Hard | Leaf-skin, resin | 0–2 | Integrity +5 | Autumn |
| 8 | Gold Tear | Offering | Runny | Pollen, honeydew | 0–2 | Knot +3 | Spring |
| 9 | Ember Drop | Offering | Syrup | Ash, pollen | 0–2 | Knot +3, morale +1 | Autumn |
| 10 | Frost Candy | Food | Hard | Frost, honeydew | 0–2 | Food +9 | Winter |
| 11 | Winter Bread | Food | Hard | Lichen, leaf-skin, frost | 3–4 | Food +12 | Winter |
| 12 | Pell's Tear | Offering | Syrup | Frost, pollen, resin | 0–2 | Knot +5 (once) | Winter |

A pot that matches no recipe still makes something: food +2 ("edible, just"). Each found recipe
writes its entry in the book, and can then be cooked again with one click.

**Caps per visit.** Sealant at most 3 pots (integrity +18 at most). Offerings at most 3 pots.

**Discovery hints.** After any pot, Old Resin says one word about it ("thin", "sticky", "sweet",
"gritty", "gold"). Each blank entry's hint word tells the player which property to aim for. At
three failed pots in a row she says which thickness the nearest unfound recipe needs.

**Appearances.**

| Appearance | Scene | Sap given | Open recipes | Goal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `c2_kitchen` | 50 (5 pots) | 1–4, 8 | Learn thickness; find 3 |
| 2 | `c4_kitchen` | 60 (6 pots) | + 5, 6, 7, 9 | Sealant before winter |
| 3 | `c5_kitchen` | 40 (4 pots) | + 10, 11, 12 | Feed the city on little |
| 4 (branch) | `c6_offering` | All sap stored | 8, 9, 12 only | The Great Offering: cook every drop into offerings |

The sap given is taken from the city's `sap` stock; if the stock is lower, the visit gets what
there is.

**The Great Offering (`c6_offering`).** Only in the offering branch. Every pot is an offering; the
caps are lifted. When the pot is done, the city's sap is 0 and the cooking itself has drawn from
the bark: integrity −20. Old Resin cooks it and says nothing.

**Win or finish state.** The visit ends when the sap for it is used or the player closes the book.

**Writes:** `food`, `bark.integrity`, `inst.elders`, `morale`, `recipes_found`, `sap`,
`offerings`, `great_offering`.

### 11.3 The Ring Door (`ring_door`, rotation puzzle)

**Purpose in the story.** "Their temples are carved into knots." A temple door is the knot's own
growth rings, cut free and turned. Turning them into line opens it. Opening temples pleases the
Knot; turning carelessly carves scars into the bark; the deepest door cuts into the living tree.

**Layout.** The whole page is the knot face, seen straight on: concentric rings, each with a
grain line and a glyph of a past year. A thin radial crack runs from the centre to the top: the
**line**. Left margin: the move counter and par. Right margin: the glyphs already revealed, as a
small chronicle.

**Rules.**

- Each ring has 8 positions (12 in the Heartwood Door). Click a ring to turn it one step clockwise;
  right-click for anticlockwise.
- Rings are **linked**: turning ring *n* also turns ring *n + 1* (the next one in) one step the
  opposite way. The innermost ring is not linked further.
- The door opens when every ring's grain line lies on the line.
- **Par** is the fewest moves the puzzle needs. Each move over par is a scar: bark integrity −1,
  at most −5 per door.
- Opening at or under par is a **clean cut**: Knot +4.
- Each ring that lines up reveals its glyph (a piece of the people's history: section 5).

**Appearances and difficulty.**

| Door | Scene | Rings | Positions | Par | Extra |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The Door of the First Ring | `c2_door` | 3 | 8 | 6 | Pell shows the link rule on the first move |
| The Deep Knot | `c4_door` | 4 | 8 | 9 | One ring is "stiff": it turns two steps per click |
| The Heartwood Door | `c5_door` | 5 | 12 | 14 | Before the first move, the player chooses: open it (integrity −8, Knot +6, Pell laid in the heartwood) or leave it sealed (Knot −4, Pell laid in the First Ring temple) |

**Hints.** After 10 moves without a new ring in line, the next ring to fix glows. A reset button
puts the rings back (moves are still counted).

**Win or finish state.** The door opens, or (Heartwood only) the player leaves it sealed.

**Writes:** `temples_opened`, `heartwood_opened`, `bark.integrity`, `inst.elders`.

### 11.4 Trunk and Shadow (`trunk_and_shadow`, board game with mutating rules)

**Purpose in the story.** The people's own game about their own world, played at each season's
festival. Its rules change with their beliefs: every reading the player made adds or changes a
rule. Playing it shows the player the culture they are building. Winning favours whoever the
player played against; winning in winter rehearses the Shadow.

**Layout.** A board carved into a flat flake: 5 columns by 8 rows, the bottom row the Root, the
top row the Crown. Cracks are drawn between squares; pieces move only along cracks (each square
shows which of its four sides are open). Left margin: the active rule cards. Right margin: the
opponent's portrait and a line they say each turn. Bottom: the round counter (16 rounds).

**Base rules (the Old Rules).**

- The player has 4 Folk, starting on the Root row. The opponent has the Shadow, a 2 × 2 token
  starting on the Crown row.
- On the player's turn, move one Folk up to 2 squares along open cracks.
- On the opponent's turn, the Shadow moves 1 square in any direction. Any Folk under the Shadow
  at the end of its turn is **hidden**: taken off and returned to the Root row 2 rounds later.
- **Win:** 3 Folk on the Crown row. **Lose:** round 16 ends first.
- The oldest rule, from the Year of Two Shadows: a Folk on a knot square (4 on the board) cannot
  be hidden.

**Mutations.** At each festival, the Crown suit of each of that season's readings adds one rule
card (and the most recent Policy adds one sigil card). Up to 4 rule cards are active; the oldest
falls off.

| Card | From | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gust | Wind | Every 3rd round, every Folk not on a knot slides 1 down |
| Blessing | Rain | 3 rain squares: a Folk ending its move there moves again |
| Prophecy | Song | The Shadow must announce its next move one turn ahead |
| Tremor | Tremor | Every 4th round, all Folk shift 1 square along a pattern printed on the card (predictable) |
| Leaf | Leaf | A leaf token falls 1 square per round down a column; a Folk it lands on is hidden for good |
| Mandible | Mandible | A beetle piece on the board; read as god it blocks; read as monster it hides any Folk it touches |
| Spear | Spear policy | Once per game, push the Shadow 1 square |
| Quill | Quill policy | See the Shadow's next 2 moves |
| Eye | Eye policy | Once per game, a chant: the Shadow skips a turn |
| The Hand | Winter only (Twig) | At round 10 a 5-square-wide Hand comes down on a row; every piece under it, Shadow included, is lifted and set down at random on the Root row |

**Opponents.** The player picks one at each festival. Each plays in their institution's way.

| Opponent | Style | Win reward |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pell (Eye) | Patient; guards the knots | Knot +4 |
| Captain Burr (Spear) | Charges at the nearest Folk | Battlements +4 |
| Fenn (Quill) | Predicts; plays the rule cards | Scroll House +4 |
| Twig (none) | Random, cheerful | Each +1 |

Every win also gives morale +5. A loss gives nothing and takes nothing.

**Difficulty curve.**

| Festival | Scene | AI | Rules active |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Spring | `c2_tns` | Looks 1 move ahead | Old Rules + spring's 2 cards |
| Summer | `c3_tns` | 2 moves ahead | + summer's cards, up to 4 |
| Winter | `c5_tns` | 3 moves ahead | + the Hand; Pell is gone, so his seat is the heir's |

**Win or finish state.** A win or a loss at round 16. Winning in winter sets `shadow_rehearsed`.

**Hints.** Squares a Folk can reach glow; the Shadow's reachable squares are shaded. A rule card
glows on the round it fires.

**Writes:** `tns.wins`, `tns_rules`, `inst.elders`, `inst.warriors`, `inst.scholars`, `morale`,
`shadow_rehearsed`.

### 11.5 Tremor Scroll (`tremor_scroll`, sequence prediction)

**Purpose in the story.** "The scholars scramble to record the tremor’s pattern, believing it to
be a message from the gods." The scroll is the Scroll House's record of every tremor. Reading it,
the scholars learn the squirrel's routine without ever knowing what a squirrel is. This is the
belief that half works, made into a game. Correct forecasts give braces in the runner, extra
cards at the Shadow, and the scholars' ending.

**Layout.** A long scroll unrolled across the page. Columns are days; each day has three cells:
dawn, noon, dusk. A recorded tremor is a glyph in its cell: its **face** (east, south, west, north,
shown by the glyph's direction) and its **strength** (1 to 3 strokes). Small weather marks sit
above each day: sun, rain, storm, frost. The right end of the scroll is blank: the forecast days.
Fenn's margin notes run along the bottom.

**Inputs.** For each forecast cell the player can place a glyph: choose face, choose strength, or
mark "none". Click "Seal the forecast". Then the scroll rolls on and the real tremors appear.

**Scoring.** A forecast is **correct** if the day-part and face are right (or if "none" is right).
Strength right as well is a **fine** forecast (scholars +1 more). Each correct forecast: scholars
+2, `forecasts_correct` +1.

**The patterns** (the squirrel's real habits, which the pull-back shows):

| Appearance | Scene | Pattern on the scroll | Forecasts |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `c1_scroll` | Every dawn, east, strength 2. 11 days shown | 2 |
| 2 | `c3_scroll` | Dawn east 2, and dusk west 1 (running down). None on storm days, and the weather for the forecast days is marked | 5 |
| 3 | `c4_scroll` | Caching: dawn east 2, noon north 3, dusk west 1. On rain days only noon. Every fourth day, no dusk (it stays at the cache) | 6 |
| 4 | `c6_scroll` | Winter: tremors only on sun days, noon, south 1. New: a faint ripple at noon on every face at once, every seventh day (the human's tread) | 5, one of which is the next tread day |

That is 18 forecasts in all. The scholars' ending needs 8 correct.

**Hints.** Scholar standing sets how much Fenn helps: at 40 or more, repeated cells are underlined;
at 60 or more, a faint suggested pattern overlays the blank days. The `scroll.hint_level` counter
adds one level for each Tremor-reading Fit (section 12). A player who watched the squirrel in the
pull-back knows its route, which is an honest advantage: the game rewards looking.

**Win or finish state.** The scroll finishes when the forecasts are sealed and revealed.

**Writes:** `forecasts_made`, `forecasts_correct`, `inst.scholars`, `tread_forecast`.

## 12. Core systems

### 12.1 The three institutions and the Balance Rope

Each institution has a standing meter from 0 to 100, starting at 30: `inst.elders` (the Knot,

than the others pulls the rope crooked.

Standing is useful as well as political:

| Knot | Omen Spread hand size: +1 card at 40, +1 more at 60 |

| Scroll House | Tremor Scroll hints: underlines at 40, pattern overlay at 60 |

**All the ways standing changes:**

| Source | Change |
| --- | --- |

**The Winter Count (`c5_count`).** At the end of winter the game sets `year.leading`. An

### 12.6 Losses and the Roll of the Bark

`fallen` counts the bark people lost; `roll` holds each one (name, season, cause, habit). Losses
come only from: warriors left on a falling line in crack defence (1 each), the monster fight with
the Beetle (if it breaches, 1–3), the war branch (2–4, scripted by how many warriors are left),
and the Flake (the district on the broken plate; 12 named, read in chapter 7). There are no
losses from hunger: hunger costs hands, who are said to move to Lowridge. Pell's death is not a
loss on the Roll; he has his own place on the temple wall.

### 12.7 The pull-back

Available in every 3D scene. It costs nothing and changes no meter. It is counted in `pullbacks`
for the debug panel and for one line in chapter 7 ("You looked up N times."). It sets the
Backyard card for the next Omen Spread: no pull-back during an event, no Backyard card.

## 13. Endings

The endings are decided by the balance between the three institutions over the year (which
readings of the Shadow are allowed), the reading the player lays (`shadow_reading`), and whether
the bark holds (`bark.integrity`). The resolver checks them in priority order after `c6_touch`.

| Priority | Id | Name | As written | Conditions in plain words | `data/endings.json` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `the_flake` | The Flake | No | The bark's integrity is 25 or below at the touch | `{"is": "bark.integrity", "lte": 25}` |
| 2 | `war_on_the_god` | The War on the God | No | The Battlements lead the year, the Shadow is read as an enemy, and the bark holds | `{"all": [{"is": "shadow_reading", "eq": "enemy"}, {"is": "year.leading", "eq": "warriors"}, {"is": "bark.integrity", "gt": 25}]}` |
| 3 | `edge_of_the_world` | The Edge of the World | No | The Scroll House leads the year, at least 8 forecasts were right, the Shadow is read as a thing with a habit, and the bark holds | `{"all": [{"is": "shadow_reading", "eq": "habit"}, {"is": "year.leading", "eq": "scholars"}, {"is": "forecasts_correct", "gte": 8}, {"is": "bark.integrity", "gt": 25}]}` |
| 4 | `shadow_passes` | The Shadow Passes | **Yes** | Every other run: the bark holds and the Shadow is read as a trial (or, if the Knot leads, as a god to be fed) | `true` |

Because the enemy and habit readings can only be laid when their conditions hold (section 11.1),
the last ending is in practice exactly "bark above 25 and the reading is trial or offering". No two
endings can be true together: the first three exclude each other by `shadow_reading` and by
integrity, and the fourth is the remainder.

### The Shadow Passes (as written)

- **The path:** The story's own path. No institution decides for the others. "The priests chant. /
  The warriors prepare for war." The scholars record. The final spread is one card of each sigil
  (or the Knot leads and the city makes its offering). The bark holds.
- **Final scenes:** `c6_stand` (or `c6_offering`), `c6_touch`, `c7_backyard`.
- **What happens:** Under the dark, every post is filled. The hand taps the bark three times (three
  tremors that Fenn writes down as they come) and presses a thumb into a crack, looking for
  termites. It finds none. It lifts away. Light comes back from the east edge. The people come out
  of the knots. Twig's kite goes up. Mossa starts up the Long Crack.
- **The pull-back:** The camera rises. The person who checked the tree walks back across the yard
  to the house. A door closes. The tree is just a tree in a backyard. On the rise, three lines
  appear, verbatim: "And yet — their lives still matter." / "Their stories still matter." /
  "Their courage still matters."
- **Last line, verbatim:** "Even the smallest world is a universe to the ones who live in it."

### The War on the God

- **The path:** Favour the Battlements all year until they lead by 12, and read the Shadow with a
  Spear majority.
- **Final scenes:** `c6_war`, `c6_touch`, `c7_backyard`.
- **What happens:** The warriors climb the knuckle, foothold by foothold, and Burr drives his
  splinter-spear into the fingertip. The hand jerks away before its third tap. On the bark, it is
  the greatest victory in their history. The lost (2–4) are cut into the Roll beside Burr's name,
  which is cut larger.
- **The pull-back:** In the yard someone shakes their hand, looks at a fingertip, picks out a
  splinter, and goes inside. The camera holds on the tree for a moment longer than in any other
  ending.
- **Last image:** The bark at night, a victory fire on every ridge, and Burr alone, polishing a
  spear that is no longer clean. Last line (added): "They won. The Trunk will remember it as long
  as they do."

### The Edge of the World

- **The path:** Favour the Scroll House until it leads by 12, forecast at least 8 tremors right
  across the year, and read the Shadow with a Quill majority.
- **Final scenes:** `c6_edge`, `c6_touch`, `c7_backyard`.
- **What happens:** The scholars walk the shadow's edge while it lies on the bark and stake its
  outline. When the light returns, Fenn draws what they staked over the map of the cities, and
  sees the Shadow had a shape: five long arms and a palm. Then, measuring the furthest stake, she
  finds the bark curves away. She draws the first map with an edge on it. The Knot calls it heresy.
  The city argues. Old Resin hides the map in her recipe book. Nobody told them anything; they
  measured it.
- **The pull-back:** The camera rises past the curve of the trunk that Fenn drew, and keeps going,
  over the yard and the fence and the next yard.
- **Last image:** Mossa at the top of Highridge at dawn, looking toward the edge on the map.
  Last line (added): "They did not know what lay past the edge. They knew there was one."

### The Flake

- **The path:** Tap too much sap and carve too deep: extra tap tokens, Knot Sanctums, scarred
  doors, the Heartwood, the Great Offering. Any route that leaves integrity at 25 or below.
- **Final scenes:** `c6_touch`, `c7_backyard`.
- **What happens:** The thumb presses into a crack, looking for termites. The over-tapped plate
  under the carved district gives. A flake of bark comes away with a district on it, as
  Underhollow did. The game shows the crack opening from the people's side and cuts before the
  fall. The Roll gains its names (12), read one by one over chapter 7.
- **The pull-back:** In the yard a flake drops from the tree into the grass. Nobody looks up. The
  person walks back to the house.
- **Last image:** Back on the bark, the survivors at the new edge of their city. Old Resin lights
  the pot. Someone starts carving the first ring of a new door. Last line (added): "The Trunk
  shrugged. They began again."

### How a player reaches each one on purpose

| Ending | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| The Shadow Passes | Spread council tokens evenly; lay balanced readings or alternate policies so no strand of the Rope pulls 12 ahead; keep integrity above 40 with sealant; at the Shadow, lay one card of each sigil |
| The War on the God | Give the Battlements 4+ tokens every council; lay Spear-majority readings; read the Beetle and the ants as monsters and hold the cracks; pick Burr as festival opponent and win; pass the deck to Burr; at the Shadow, lay a Spear majority |
| The Edge of the World | Give the Scroll House 4+ tokens every council; lay Quill-majority readings; pull back during events and turn the Backyard cards to win Fit; forecast well (8 of 18); pass the deck to Fenn; at the Shadow, lay a Quill majority |
| The Flake | Put 5+ tokens in Taps each season; build Knot Sanctums; open the Heartwood; overrun par on the doors; skip sealant; or let the Knot lead and make the Great Offering on weak bark |

**Why a run got its ending.** The debug panel prints the resolver's walk, for example: "the_flake:
bark.integrity 41, needs ≤ 25: no. war_on_the_god: shadow_reading trial: no. edge_of_the_world:
shadow_reading trial: no. shadow_passes: true." It also shows the four standings at the Winter
Count and the `year.leading` they produced.

## 14. UI and HUD

**3D HUD (overlaid, kept small, carved-bark frames):**

| Element | Place | Shows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The Balance Rope | Top left | The three standings as strands, with numbers on hover; a knot marks the 12-point lead |
| Season wheel | Top centre | Season, the next event's silhouette (a shape only, never its cause) |
| Sap jar, food basket, hands | Top right | `sap`, `food`, `hands` |
| Bark-song | Bottom edge | A thin crack line that widens as integrity falls; it glows at 40 and 25 |
| Pull-back hint | Bottom right | "Tab: look up", shown until the player has pulled back three times |
| Build menu | Right side, slides in | Buildings allowed on the surface under the cursor (ridge, groove, crack, knot, splinter) |
| Council panel | Full screen over a dimmed city | Five posts with token slots and live effect lines |

**Diegetic elements.** Sayings painted on walls (from Crown cards); the Roll of the Bark on the
temple wall; the year's name on the ridge; Twig's kite in the sky over the city.

**2D HUD.** The progress strip (runner) or line bar (defence) along the top; the tremor or will
bar; the stamina ring around Mossa. Nothing else.

**1D pages.** Each is a whole page with its own layout (section 11). A small "back to the bark"
button sits top right where the game allows leaving; the Omen Spread and Ring Door cannot be left
mid-way.

**Menus.** Title (the bark at dawn, Twig's kite), three save slots plus autosave, settings
(controls, text size, subtitles, colour-blind palettes, timers off), the Codex (lore cards
unlocked as section 5 says), the Roll, the recipe book, the year's readings.

**How the three layers look different.** 3D is warm and deep, with soft focus at the edges like a
miniature photograph. 2D is flat bark relief in side-on light, drawn in stronger outlines. 1D is
paper: bark-paper, ink, sap stains, with no depth. The pull-back is the only place the palette
goes ordinary and daylight-plain.

**Accessibility.** Every timed element (runner limits, defence waves, the expedition window) can be
slowed to half or turned off. Sigils are shapes (eye, spear, quill) as well as colours. The tremor
glyph's face is shown by direction and a letter. No information is carried by sound alone.

## 15. Art direction

- **Mood references (not copies):** miniature dioramas and tilt-shift photographs; illuminated
  manuscripts and marginalia for the 1D pages; children's nature encyclopaedias for the pull-back's
  plain, labelled-looking daylight.
- **Palette:** bark umber and grey-brown; lichen sage and lime; sap amber and gold; lantern orange.
  Seasons shift it: spring adds green, summer bleaches, autumn coppers, winter whitens. The
  backyard is its own palette: grass green, sky blue, white fence, ordinary.
- **The people:** About 2 mm tall at human scale. Large heads, long limbs, quick movement ("small,
  agile, and deeply spiritual"). Each institution has a silhouette: the Knot in hooded robes, the
  Battlements in bark-plate and splinter spears, the Scroll House in ink-stained aprons with scroll
  cases. The six named characters each have one strong silhouette mark (Mossa's long scarf, Pell's
  cane, Burr's crested helmet, Fenn's satchel, Old Resin's ladle, Twig's kite).
- **Materials:** Bark with deep normal maps; sap with subsurface glow; lichen with soft fuzz.
- **Scale cues:** At bark scale, the camera uses shallow depth of field. At the backyard it uses
  deep focus. The switch in focus is what tells the player they have changed scale.
- **The hand:** Seen only as a hand and a sleeve. It is lit plainly, never menacing in the
  backyard view, and huge, dark-edged and slow in the bark view. One model, two lightings.
- **One look across layers:** All three layers use the same bark texture set, the same palette and
  the same outline weight on the people. 2D is the 3D bark seen side-on in orthographic; 1D pages
  are cut from the same bark, as paper.

Beyond the shared schemas, this game's code will need three tables that are not state and so are
not in `flags.json`: the Omen deck (section 11.1), the recipe table (11.2) and the tremor patterns
(11.5). They belong in `src/` as constants when the game is built; this document is their source.
Derived values (hand size, squad size, the Year deck) are computed from declared meters and are not
stored. Each scene also carries a `minutes` field (not in the shared schema, which allows it); the
scenes of a chapter add up to that chapter's minutes in `game.json`. The as-written ending lists
`c6_stand` as its branch scene; when the Knot leads and the offering is made, `c6_offering` takes its
place.

## 20. Risks and open questions

| Risk | Mitigation |
| --- | --- |
| The joke repeats: every pull-back is "it was only an animal" | Each pull-back escalates scale (a squirrel, a shower, a gust, a beetle, autumn, a bird, a hand) and the game teaches the player to use what they see (Backyard card, Fit). By autumn the joke has become a tool, and at the Shadow it becomes dread |
| The people's courage reads as the punchline | Design rule: no comic sting over the people; the camera always returns to them; their lines never wink; losses go on the Roll with names and habits |
| A city-builder on a vertical wall is hard to read | Gravity runs down the screen; only five surface types; build menu filters by surface under cursor |
| Five 1D games plus two 2D modes in 170 minutes feel bitty | No 1D appearance over 4 minutes; each is entered from the scene it belongs to (the table, the wall, the knot) |
| Players do not find the endings | The Rope's 12-point knot is always visible; the final spread's preview states the reading before it is laid; the Codex lists the four readings after the first playthrough |
| Pell's death feels forced | It is prepared from autumn (his leaf) and played slowly, by the player, through the sayings they made |

**Questions only the author can answer:**

1. Pell's death in winter is the one fixed loss of the year. Do you want a certain death in this
   world, or should the year carry no loss the player cannot prevent?
2. The moral, "Even the smallest world is a universe to the ones who live in it", is used as the
   last line of the as-written ending only. Should it close every ending instead?
3. The treatment does not say what the hand does. This design has it tap three times, press a
   crack, find no termites and leave, and never shows the person beyond a hand, a sleeve and a
   distant figure. Is that how you see it?
