# People of the Bark — Deep Dive

Everything invented here is an **addition**, listed in section 6 for the author to approve or
strike. The source, `creative-writing/vault/05_Worldbuilding/01_People_of_the_Bark.md`, is quoted
verbatim and never changed. The as-written ending, The Shadow Passes, stays as it is.

## 1. Where it stands

**What works.** The core verb fits its source: the player knows the cause and may only interpret.
The Fit rule turns "They’re not wrong. / But they’re not right either." into play. The Roll of the
Bark gives every loss a name and a habit. Pell's walk past the sayings the player made is a real
scene. The seven web modes all run, each in its own style.

**What is weak.**

1. **The player is never wrong, so interpreting is never hard.** Every event has a Backyard card
   that states the cause and the suit that pays. After summer the "interpretation" is an
   optimisation: turn the card, lay the fit suit, pick a sigil for the Rope. The game's own promise
   (a truth you cannot use) is spent by minute 60.
2. **The middle has no reversal.** Spring to winter is the same loop four times with bigger
   animals. Nothing the player believed in chapter 2 turns out false in chapter 4.
3. **Nobody opposes anybody.** The three institutions are meters. No bark person wants something
   another must lose. The only antagonist arrives at minute 148.
4. **The Shadow has no stake the player can feel.** The hand taps and leaves. Integrity decides
   the Flake, but the check itself (the one thing the source names: termites) touches nothing.
5. **The layers mostly pay into meters, not into each other.** A Ring Door scar is −1 integrity; it
   is never a hole Mossa has to jump.
6. **The art is props without people.** There are 9 location plates, 29 objects on white and 7
   pictographs, and no figure of any named character. `reference/10-the-shadow.jpg` shows
   human-proportioned actors and a hand gripping the trunk, which is off the design.

## 2. The story, developed

**Dramatic question.** When the god finally comes to listen, what will the Trunk sound like, and
can a people be led to sound whole by someone who may never tell them why?

**Want and need.** The people want every event to have one meaning they can act on. They need to
act well on a thing they cannot name. The player wants to stay the one who knows, and needs to
interpret honestly once that is taken away.

**The seed.** Two lines of the source, same vault path: "To us, it’s someone checking for
termites." and "The scholars scramble to record the tremor’s pattern, believing it to be a message
from the gods." The treatment never says whether there are termites. The design's own lore already
names the answer without noticing: the lost district is called *Underhollow*.

**The secret (addition): the Pulse.** Something is in the wood. Under the squirrel's hours the
Tremor Scroll holds a fainter rhythm that comes from no face of the Trunk, because it comes from
inside. The Knot calls it the Trunk's Pulse. It is the tree's other tenants, drumming on their own
walls. Underhollow did not fall for greed. Its floor had been eaten to paper. The game still never
says what the hand found: the player learns what is in the wood from inside it, and what the
person in the yard concludes stays unsaid, as the design decided on 2026-11-12.

**The opposing force and what it wants.** The Pulse never gets a face (the design's rule for
causes holds). It wants what the bark people want: a home in the Trunk, dark, damp and sealed. It
walls up every breach from its own side, and it follows sweetness, toward wherever sap is tapped
hardest and offerings are poured (Chanter Hollow tips them into a crack by the Heartwood Door,
because "the Trunk drinks them"). It is a people who never look up. The second opposition is
bark-sized: the Knot's wish that the Pulse stay holy and unread.

**The midpoint: The Blank Card.** One autumn night the Pulse wakes Highridge. The Knot lays a
spread. The player holds Tab, as always. The camera rises over a dark yard and a lit window,
nothing moving. The Backyard card turns over blank. For the first time the player knows no more
than Pell does, and the joke the game has told six times is no longer available.

**The darkest moment: Lowridge Comes Up the Crack.** In deep winter the lowest street of Lowridge
settles into a gallery. If the player gave Sorrel's listeners one autumn council token, the street was
empty; if not, the Roll gains names. A city walks up the Long Crack in frost carrying its doors. Pell is dead, the
heir is dealing honest hands for the first time in forty years, and the Winter Count must seat
people nobody budgeted for. Then the seventh-day tread begins.

**The Sounding (addition to `c6_touch`).** The three taps are a question and the bark answers
each: tap one on the plate the people carved most, tap two on the plate the Pulse map (section 3)
marks, tap three where the city stands. The player hears, and sees as rings, what a year of taps,
offerings and mending made the Trunk sound like.

**Endings, and what each costs.**

- **The Shadow Passes (as written; conditions and last line unchanged).** Cost: nothing is
  settled. If the player found the Pulse, one image is added before the verbatim lines: at the
  feast, Fenn lays her ear on the wall.
- **The War on the God.** Added cost: the third tap never lands, so the question was never
  finished. Fenn's last entry is the next seventh day, already inked.
- **The Edge of the World.** Added cost: Fenn's map has an outer edge and an inner one. The heresy
  is double.
- **The Flake.** Added: in the pull-back the flake lies face down in the grass and its underside
  is not solid. Underhollow is cleared, too late for anyone who could be told.
- **The Ring (NEW, a proposal).** The Knot leads, the Shadow is read as a hungry god, the Great
  Offering is poured and `pulse` is 60 or more. The bark holds, and the second tap sounds like a
  drum. The hand goes and comes back, and when the light returns a band of colour circles the
  world. The people take it for a covenant. The pull-back shows a ribbon tied round the trunk and,
  over the fence, a stump the game has shown since spring. Last line (added): "The god had left
  them a ring. They counted it with the others." If struck, this route stays The Shadow Passes.

**Sample lines (written for the game; not the author's).**

- Sorrel: "You Highridge lot hear the Trunk when it shouts. Down here it mutters."
- Pell, feeling the player hesitate over the blank card: "Then for once we are holding the same hand."
- Fenn: "It has no face. I gave it one twice, to make it fit. I scraped them off."
- Twig, of the Pulse: "Is it knocking to come in, or knocking to see who's home?"
- Burr, at the paper wall: "I have a spear and it has a wall. Neither of us wants to find out."

## 3. Obstacles and puzzles

One hidden meter is added: `pulse` (0–100). Tap tokens beyond 3, Knot Sanctums and offering pots
raise it; frost and the new sealant lower it. The player sees it only as marks on the scroll.

### 2D: Crack runner

- **Teach** (spring): **paper floor**, a ledge over a gallery. It shows a tick mark, holds one
  footfall and drops her one ledge.
- **Test** (summer): sap falls end on a choice of three ledges, one of them paper; pick while sliding.
- **Twist** (the Shadow): in the dark the tick marks go out. Only floors on the player's Pulse map stay marked.
- Also new: **the crowd** (people on the ledges; no rolling through them).
- **Set-piece: Against the Tide.** `c5_run` is re-routed down the Long Crack while all Lowridge
  comes up it. Mossa runs the walls above their heads. At each gap she drops Pell's lantern into
  the crowd and it is passed hand to hand below her to the far side.

### 2D: Crack defence

- **Teach**: mites. **Test**: the Beetle. Both as now.
- **Twist** (autumn ants): the crack has a paper wall. Ants hunt what lives behind it. Read as
  gods and escorted down the correct branch, the procession opens the wall and `pulse` falls by 15.
  Read as monsters and turned back, nothing checks the Pulse all winter.
- Also new: **the plugged door** (a hole stopped by one living head that never comes out; a
  warrior left in reach for 10 seconds is lost, so Burr must be moved on).
- **Set-piece: The Procession and the Wall.** The warriors hold the people back while two kinds of
  god meet in a crack. The pull-back shows an ant trail going into a hole.

### 1D: Omen Spread

- **Teach**: meaning and policy. **Test**: Fit, with lives on the Beetle.
- **Twist**: the Blank Card, then honest deals. After Pell, no event-suit card is guaranteed and
  each heir deals in character: Burr's hands run Spear-heavy; Fenn shows the next two cards; Twig
  deals one card face down that must be placed unseen; Hollow allows one redeal.
- Also new: **the contested slot** (from autumn the leading institution lays one card first, and
  it cannot be moved); **notch-reading** (after The Thumb on the Deck, the top card can be felt
  before a deal, once a spread).
- **Set-piece**: the Blank Card.

### 1D: Sap Kitchen

- **Teach**: thickness. **Test**: sealant before winter.
- **Twist**: recipe 13, **Gallery Plug** (glue, ash, leaf-skin, 5–6 stirs). It needs froth, which
  Resin has taught all year ruins glue. Her hint word for the blank entry is "wrong".
- Also new: **sour sap** (from taps over a gallery; it spoils food and is the only base the Plug
  takes; each Plug pot is `pulse` −8).
- **Set-piece: The Great Mending**, the offering branch's mirror: every pot in the city on the
  boil, cooked down the line against the tread.

### 1D: The Ring Door

- **Teach**: the link rule. **Test**: the stiff ring.
- **Twist**: the Heartwood Door does not start where the chronicle says the Knot left it. Someone
  has turned it since. Ring four has an **eaten arc**: over three positions it drags nothing.
- Also new: **closing a door** (winter: turn the rings to a given pattern to seal the Heartwood,
  par 11, `pulse` −10, Knot −4).
- **Set-piece**: the door opens and the push-in keeps going. Where the hall of ancestors should be
  there is lantern light travelling a long way down, and paper walls.

### 1D: Trunk and Shadow

- **Teach**: Old Rules. **Test**: four mutated cards.
- **Twist**: in winter the player may sit on Twig's side of the board and **play the Shadow**. Its
  rule, by Twig: it may only move toward the last square where a Folk knocked (moved twice).
  Playing it teaches what the Hand is doing.
- Also new: **Hollow square** card (from the Pulse reading: every fourth round one square falls
  out of the board for good).
- **Set-piece**: the Hand lifts the Shadow too, and the square falls silent.

### 1D: Tremor Scroll

- **Teach**: the squirrel's hours. **Test**: weather and caching.
- **Twist**: the faceless row. It follows warmth, and it follows the player's own councils: more
  marks after seasons of heavy tapping and offerings. Fenn: "It answers us."
- Also new: **listening posts** (runner's notes from Lowridge, Highridge and Knotholm say loud,
  faint or nothing; the player places each mark by them).
- **Set-piece**: the winter sitting, when the column being forecast turns out to be the
  player's own autumn budget.

### Across the game

**Links between layers.**

1. Kitchen to runner: each Gallery Plug pot is poured at a site the player picks in the 3D city;
   that site's paper floors are solid in the next run.
2. Ring Door to defence: each scar on a door is an extra side entrance in the next crack defence;
   a clean cut is one more knot shelter in `c6_shadow`.
3. Board to 3D: a winter win as the Shadow shows the three tap sites ten seconds early in
   `c6_touch`, long enough to move a district.

**The long puzzle: the Pulse map.** A margin strip on the Tremor Scroll: a grid of the bark's
plates, kept on the back pages of Old Resin's recipe book. Every placed faceless mark shades one
plate. It pays off at the Sounding: a complete map shows where tap two will land and which plate
to clear or mend, and in the Flake it decides whether the twelve names are on the plate or off it.

**A second playthrough.** The notches are readable from the first deal and the faceless row from
chapter 1. The stump is over the fence in the first pull-back, and now it means something. Sorrel
can be a festival opponent.

## 5. Roadmap

**Phase 1: make interpreting hard (least work, biggest change).** The Pulse event and the blank
Backyard card; the faceless row on the Tremor Scroll; honest deals after Pell; the first six art
items. *Needs the author first:* whether the game may say there
is something in the wood at all (it keeps the letter of the 2026-11-12 answer, not its spirit).

**Phase 2: give the year its turn.** Sorrel and her wall; The Thumb on the Deck; The Faceless
Marks; Lowridge Comes Up the Crack; paper floors, the paper wall and the Gallery Plug; the Pulse
map and the Sounding; the three layer links. `pulse` joins `data/flags.json`, and the validator
runs after. *Needs the author first:* Sorrel as an eighth named character; Pell's secret, which
changes how his kindness reads.

**Phase 3: endings and replay.** The added images in the four existing endings; The Ring; playing
the Shadow; second-playthrough changes; the remaining art. *Needs the author first:* The Ring,
the one ending where the yard's verdict can be guessed. The author may want it softer or gone.
