# Behind the Peephole: deep dive

A proposal: nothing here is in `DESIGN.md` or `data/` yet, and the source is untouched.
Everything marked NEW is my invention, listed again in section 6 for the author to approve or
strike. Source quotations are verbatim from `04_Book_Concepts/01_Behind_the_Peephole.md`.

## 1. Where it stands

**What works.** The rule "You never see their missions. / You only see their return." is kept
without exception, and the play is where the eye goes and for how long. The six rituals make the player care before anything is threatened. The
clock forces real loss (one door costs another). The Statement gives watching a scored purpose, and
The Next Watcher is a clean reading of the pitch's last three lines. The six graphic styles are distinct and the web
modules already play.

**The four weaknesses.**

1. **The watcher wants nothing.** Faceless and silent is right, but the design also leaves the
   watcher without a desire, so for six nights the only stake is a score. A want does not need a
   face.
2. **The middle has no reversal.** Nights three to five add facts (the case, Fenn's log, the names)
   but the relation between watcher and watched never changes. The guests are specimens until the
   last choice. "A confession booth" is used as a label for people talking to themselves; nobody
   confesses *to* anyone.
3. **Being seen is only a penalty.** Suspicion closes lenses and plants false signs. The pitch
   calls the glass "A threshold" first, yet nothing crosses it in either direction until night
   seven, and then it is paper under a door.
4. **The layers run in a line.** Patience tells, the eye collects, the Statement scores, the Mosaic
   summarises. Almost nothing done on a page changes a run in the passage, and Lockwork is four
   gates. The modes are also one trick each: the race's hazards are all "do not be noticed", and
   the Mosaic's wrong link costs a length and nothing else.

A smaller fifth: Wren is a threat, not yet a position the player could find themselves agreeing
with.

## 2. The story, developed

**Dramatic question.** When one of the people behind the glass looks back and asks you to keep
watching, do you stay a lens or become a witness?

**Want and need.** The watcher *wants* to see everything and never be seen: that is the job, the
pay and the safety, and the game teaches it as a skill. The watcher *needs* to be seen by one
person and not step back. No face or name is required; the need is an action.

**The opposing force: H. Wren (deepened, NEW).** Wren wants a complete file because he believes
completeness is mercy. With a full record a client buys a journalist, relocates a clerk, pays a
thief; with a partial one the client guesses, and guesses get people hurt. NEW secret: Wren was
the first night auditor, in 1971, and his incomplete log sent the wrong guest out through the
lobby. So the Statement's scoring is his creed: every row the player gets right proves him right.
He is wrong about one thing: a file
that complete leaves the six nothing of their own to bargain with.

## 3. Obstacles and puzzles

All elements below are NEW.

### The eye at the hole

- **Teach:** hold still on Ines's hands. **Test:** Tomas's photograph, face up for two minutes
  while he faces the door. **Twist:** with a guest who knows, holding under a direct look
  is what earns the beat.
- **Reflections.** A mirror, a dead television or the night window shows the part of a room the
  lens cannot reach. A *reflected* beat is caught only in the glass, and its text is reversed.
- **Answering.** A knowing guest taps twice. Darken the rose twice within three seconds for yes;
  stay away for no.
- **The held page.** A page held to the lens fills the circle, too near to take in. Tilt across it
  in three holds.
- **Set-piece: The Sitting.** Her eye fills a quarter of the view. The game has taught stepping
  back inside half a second. Step back and she writes "gone". Hold, and the catch
  ring closes on her eye.

### Corridor race

- **Teach:** the boards. **Test:** two bells ten metres apart with Dora between. **Twist:** the
  blackout, where the hazards change places.
- **Bell wires.** The 1926 wires run above the pipe run. One tug per room per night rings a service
  bell inside it: the guest goes to their main door for twenty seconds, off the bathroom
  window or a staged sign. Costs 10 suspicion.
- **The parked trolley.** From night five Dora leaves it. Push it at 0.6 m/s: behind it you are unseen
  from the light well and the boards stay quiet, but it cannot pass the cabinet.
- **Set-piece: the seventh coin.** Lights out, six coins of light ahead. Turn back toward 401 and there
  are seven: the last is in your office door, and a figure crosses it.

### Lockwork

- **Teach:** the drawer. **Test:** spools on the stair door, clock running. **Twist:** a lock
  worked from the wrong side.
- **Bells over the dial.** At the safe, each ring from the board upstairs masks the contact clicks
  for three seconds. Sweep between rings.
- **The lever lock** (a fifth lock; `docs/COLLECTION.md` must name it first). At 02:00 on night
  seven Fenn turns the key in your door from outside. Two levers, lifted to the gate while the bolt
  is thrown.
- **Set-piece.** The keyhole is twenty centimetres under the rose. While the rose is dark someone
  is looking in: freeze. Work only while it is lit.

### The Statement

- **Teach:** one grid. **Test:** night four, where a guest is both row and column. **Twist:** rows
  you did not write.
- **The carbon.** You keep a carbon of every signed form. Each morning, amend one earlier row on
  the carbon only. Wren's copy keeps your mistakes; yours can become true
  (`carbon_rows_correct`).
- **Typed rows.** From night five one row arrives typed by Wren, sometimes false. Striking it costs
  a second look and he mentions it that night.
- **The seventh row.** A ruled, empty line under 406 on every form.
- **Set-piece.** The Account comes fully typed, three answers bent toward Ruth. Each one you strike
  rings the desk phone once.

### Night Patience

- **Teach:** unlimited passes. **Test:** two passes with the second card under column six.
  **Twist:** not every slipped card is Fenn's.
- **Reply cards.** From night four, write one of three phrases on a hotel card and leave it on the
  spike. Fenn's next card answers. Three exchanges; what you ask picks which of cards ten to twelve you get.
- **Nicked backs.** Fenn's deck has one petal of the rose nicked. From night five a second hand slips
  whole-backed cards with bad advice. Peek shows backs, so peek becomes authentication.
- **Quiet columns.** Clear the column under a room number before the bell and that room's first
  beat window is half as long again.
- **Set-piece.** Night seven, one pass: under column six, a hotel card with a watercolour brass dot.

### The Mosaic

- **Teach:** three plain links. **Test:** more pins than lengths. **Twist:** a taut thread can be
  wrong.
- **The rhyme spool** (green, from lost property). Link a guest to one of the six left things. A
  true rhyme pins what became of last season's counterpart.
- **Fraying.** A link resting only on a typed row pulls taut, then frays overnight. Scissors, once
  a morning, recover the length.
- **Set-piece: the back of the board.** On night six, unpin it. The reverse is last season's
  mosaic in pinholes, the same shape as yours, with one extra hole at bottom centre.

### Across the game

**Links between layers.** (1) Quiet columns: a patience column lengthens a lens window. (2)
Opening lost property gives the Mosaic its fifth spool and changes the race: Dora stops two minutes
at the cabinet every night after. (3) The carbons are the pages of the slide run, so a valet door
takes only a row that is right, and in the dawn lobby every guest you exposed looks up at the
mezzanine.

**The long puzzle: the seventh row.** Five marks, five nights, each a clue card of a new kind,
*watcher*. On night seven the Account has a tenth line: "400: ___" (witness, product, guest,
nobody). Reconcile Mara's dots against your own night strip: on night four she has one more dot than
you have visits, so a second eye uses the roses, at 01:00. The stamped row opens the choice to
hold still at your own lens.

**Second shift.** The cabinet holds six things from your first run's guests, chosen by that ending.
Your own reply cards turn up in the deck. The pinholes on the back of the board are yours.
