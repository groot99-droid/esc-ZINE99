# WrymWretch: design

## 1. Summary

Six elves and their twin-soul dragons wake, one by one, in a world that is forgetting itself, and
fly toward the chasm where their kind was born. The player plays each pair alone in its own realm,
then leads all six as one party, and keeps the world in being by remembering it: recording,
retelling and re-drawing what the world lets go.

## 2. Why this game

WrymWretch is the collection's only epic fantasy, its only party game and its only turn-based
battler. Its nearest neighbours share an idea (memory loss) or a source file (the Alexi chapters).

| | WrymWretch | Missing Campsites | The Stuffed Ones | The Lord of the Sea |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Whose memory is lost | The world's: map names, bestiary pages, people's requests, ability names | The world's, but nothing comes back | Your own | Nobody's; it is about devotion and wildness |
| Can it be restored | Yes. Memory is a reversible resource and the combat economy | No. You only choose what to carry | You choose what to keep while losing the rest | Not a memory game |
| Core verb | Remember | Compare | Hold still | Stir |
| Register | Epic fantasy: awe, kinship | Liminal, cosmic | Folk horror | Comic, nautical |
| Map | A map whose names fade and are re-inked by recording, as a UI, not a 1D game | Map comparison is its 1D game (Reconcile) | None | An open sea chart |
| Same source file | Acts I and II | — | — | The two Alexi chapters only |

Three rules keep WrymWretch apart. Forgetting is always undoable at a price. The player never
compares a record against reality (that is Missing Campsites'); they restore. And the threat is a
cult that kills witnesses, so the people who remember are what the player protects.

## 3. Source fidelity

### What the author wrote

All quotes are verbatim from `02_Novels/02_WrymWretch.md` (cited as WW with line numbers) or

**Act I, the six signs (WW 29–107).**

1. Thalen tracks "a silver‑antlered stag through the western groves" (WW 31) and finds a tree
   split from within. Black ichor greys the moss "into dust that didn’t even remember how to be
   soil" (WW 33). He runs to the outpost: "The forest is… forgetting," he managed. "Something is
   making it forget." (WW 39)
2. Aeris Galewing watches her people tremble in the sky-cities. A healer: "They speak of voices in
   the wind. They say the stars are calling them by name." (WW 47) The whisper: "Remember me."
   (WW 49) The Priests of the Gale choose her to descend and seek aid (WW 51).
3. Rhyla Mosswhisper kneels at the Heartstone and sees "a prison without walls, a name dissolving
   into silence" (WW 57). "Not yet," she whispered. "You cannot fade. Not now." (WW 59) She sets out
   for the Deep Core (WW 63).
4. Lunessa Tidecaller wades into a sea that "clung to her skin like oil" (WW 65), dives, and finds
   "a monolith carved with symbols her mind refused to hold" (WW 71). "She surfaced screaming."
   (WW 73)
5. Kael Duskbane opens the forbidden chamber. The prophecy: "When the last memory fades, the Astral
   Architect shall rise." (WW 77) The name is "Shallock Izladar. A name the world was trying to
   forget." (WW 79) "Someone was erasing memories. Someone was killing witnesses." (WW 81)
6. Seraphyne sees the stars form "The Sigil of Izladar." (WW 87) "The prison weakens," she said.
   "The cosmos trembles. The Remnants move in shadow." (WW 91) "And the name?" "Already fading."
   (WW 93–95)
7. At the chasm, "WrymWretch stirred." (WW 97) The voice: "I… remember… me…" (WW 101) "The first
   seal had cracked." (WW 105)

**Act II, the awakening of the twin souls (WW 110–268).**

1. Sylvan to Thalen: "You were born when I was born. You breathed when I breathed. You are my other
   half, Thalen Cedarheart." (WW 120) "It is not rot. It is memory dying. And if memory dies… so do
   we." (WW 126)
2. Aetherius to Aeris: "We were born together, Aeris. You are the breath I cannot take. I am the

3. Gaia to Rhyla: "We are one, Rhyla. When the earth remembers, I remember. When the earth
   forgets… we both fall." (WW 168) "Because the world is losing its story, Gaia replied. And
   something is stealing the ending." (WW 174)
4. Aquatica to Lunessa: "Little tidecaller… you are my other half. And the sea is forgetting us."
   (WW 199) "Not erased, Aquatica corrected. Devoured." (WW 203)
5. Umbra to Kael: "You have hunted alone too long, Kael, the dragon whispered into his mind. We were
   born together. We kill together." (WW 220) "Then we hunt the Remnants." (WW 226) "And we start at
   #Wrymreach." (WW 230)
6. Astraia to Seraphyne: "We are one soul split by the moon’s sorrow, the dragon whispered." (WW
   243) "To #Wrymreach, Astraia said. Where the sun’s anger and the moon’s tear first collided.
   Where the King of Dragons and Queen of Elves once stood together." (WW 247)
7. "Across the world, six dragons and six elves moved toward the same place." (WW 252) The voice
   again; "The second seal cracked." (WW 264) "Act II ends." (WW 268)

**From the Codex.** The First Light shattered into "the Sun’s Roaring Anger and the Moon’s Silent
Tear" (Codex 28). Izladar was "a Celestial Elf scholar" imprisoned "in a realm outside existence,
sealed by memory itself" (Codex 30). "The Remnants of Izladar now seek to erase all memory of him,
believing his resurrection will elevate reality." (Codex 30) "Memory-based reality shapes
existence; when memory fades, reality decays." (Codex 67) The six realms, the eighteen creatures,
the eight factions and the eleven-step timeline are used in full (sections 5 and 12).

### The author's ending

There is none. The text stops at "Act II ends." (WW 268). Everything after the six pairs set out
is added: the convergence is built from WW 252–268, and Act III is a proposal the author can strike
and replace (section 8, "The Act III contract").

### One change of order

The source tells all six signs (Act I), cracks the first seal, then tells all six awakenings
(Act II). The game plays each pair's sign and awakening together as one prologue, so that each pair
is one continuous chapter in one realm. The first seal cracks after the third prologue, the second
after the sixth. Every line keeps its own speaker and wording. This is listed in section 21.

## 4. Investment

### The hook in the first five minutes

The game opens on Thalen tracking the silver-antlered stag. The player follows glowing tracks
through a full-colour forest for about two minutes, learns the bow, and sees the stag's bestiary
page fill in with its picture and a line of lore. At 3:00 they reach the split tree. Moss the player
just walked over turns grey and falls to dust behind them, and the label "Western Groves" on the
corner map loses its letters one at a time while they watch. A whisper under the bark tries to form
a name. At 4:30 the ground behind the player starts to erase to blank paper and the first
forgetting run begins. The question is posed in play before any line is spoken: the world is
un-writing itself, and you are standing on it.

### What the player comes to care about, before it is threatened

| What | How the game makes them care (in play) | When it is threatened |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Each pair, as one life | Each prologue is a whole chapter alone with one pair: the elf's own way of moving, then the dragon's arrival, then the first flight on its back, each dragon flying differently. The two halves share one life bar from the first battle. | From the convergence on, the realm's memory sets whether that pair's abilities keep their names. A pair whose realm forgets grows weaker in your hands. |
| The places | The player walks each realm whole first: the forest, the sky-cities, the gem cities, the shore, the Underworld, the crystalline forests. They record places at crystals and see their names inked on the map. | Map names fade by realm tier. In the Astral Expanse the platforms exist only for places the party remembers. |
| The people's small requests | Six people each ask one thing (a ranger, a priest, a stonekeeper, a tide elder, a Syndicate elder, a Priory elder). The asks are personal and small. | Below 60 in their realm the giver forgets having asked. The player can still deliver by retelling the request, if they remember it themselves. |
| Ilvarra, the last of the Wyrmguard (added) | She hosts the Rim Moot, referees Six Realms, cooks for the party, and at the fire tells how her own dragon gave its memory to the seal. The player spends a whole night (about 15 minutes) in her camp before anything goes wrong. | At dawn the Remnants come for her: she is the last living witness of the Imprisonment. |
| The names themselves | The player sees what forgetting does to a thing they used: an ability they relied on loses its name mid-battle and can no longer be paired. | Throughout, and most in the final battle. |

### The question that pulls them to the end

Someone has to keep remembering Shallock Izladar, or the prison opens. Who will carry that, and
what will it cost them? Under it, a second question the player can chase: who was he, before he was
a name the world was trying to forget?

### The Act III contract

Act III (chapters 9–12) is added, so it is built to be removed. It reads only this state from the
first eight chapters: the six realm meters and `world_memory`; `izladar_fragments`;
`unwriter_name_known`; `trust.*`; the `record`, `witnesses` and `burned` ledgers; `ilvarra_met`.
It writes only what the endings read: `final_choice`, `keeper_pair`, `anchor_forged`, and the meters.
If the author writes their own Act III, the team replaces the `a3_*` chapters and their scenes,
keeps the convergence's hand-off, and rewrites `endings.json` if the author's ending differs. No
prologue or convergence scene reads an Act III flag.

## 10. 2D modes

### Mode A: Twin flight (`twin_flight`, scroll)

Side-on flight on dragonback; the world scrolls right (or down, in burrow and dive sections).
The dragon flies and the elf rides; the player handles both.

**Controls.** Stick or W/S: pitch. Wing (Space / A): a beat of the wings, +lift, uses 15 stamina
(stamina 0–100, refills 20 per second when gliding). Gift (E / B): the dragon's power. Rider (Q /
X): the elf's action. Hold Wing to dive-brake.

**Rules.** Forward speed is set per dragon and rises with dives. Three scale hits per checkpoint;
a fourth sends the pair back to the last checkpoint (one every 25–35 seconds). No death, no lost
progress beyond the checkpoint. Progress shows as a thin ribbon across the top with the destination
drawn at its right end, and the landscape below is the painted world map with its names.

**Each dragon flies differently.**

### Mode B: Forgetting run (`forgetting_run`, scroll)

Side-on (or upward) run where the world behind is erased. Ahead, the path is unremembered: drawn
as faint outline. The player must recall it to stand on it.

**Controls.** Move and jump. Hold **Recall** (Shift / RT) to solidify the next 6 m of outlined path

**The edge.** A wall of blank paper follows at a set speed. It speeds up by 10% each checkpoint.

realm −3 (`memory.*`).

**Progress.** The destination (the "remembered thing") is drawn at the right edge. It inks in as
the player nears it: outline at 0%, colour at 100%.

## 11. 1D games

Every 1D game is a full page that the 3D camera pushes into. The world beneath is paused unless
noted. Each page has text size settings, a colour-blind palette and a slow-or-pause option for
anything timed.

### Concentration (`concentration`): memory match

**Purpose in the story.** Faces and names are what people lose first. This is the only matching
game in the collection, and its twist is that the faces blank out while you play, so the only
record is the player's own memory.

**Rules.**
1. A grid of face-down cards in pairs. Flip two per turn. A match locks both cards face-up and
   records the pair.
2. Every *N* flips (the **blank interval**), one unmatched card's face fades to white for good.
3. A blank card can still be matched. Flip its partner, then select the blank card and choose
   "This is the one." If the player is right, the pair locks and the blank face returns. If wrong,
   one more card blanks at once.
4. The game ends when all pairs are locked or the flip budget runs out.

**Layout.** Parchment page. Grid centre-left. Right column: pairs kept (with the faces and names
inked as they are won), flips left, the next blank in flips (a small wax candle that burns down).
Bottom: hint button.

**Inputs.** Click or select a card; "This is the one" button on a blank card.

**Difficulty across appearances.**

| Scene | Cards | Pairs are | Blank interval | Flip budget | Win threshold |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `s_conc_ward` | 12 (6 pairs) | A patient's face and their name | 8 | 30 | No fail; each pair kept counts |
| `s_conc_bestiary` | 20 (10 pairs) | A creature's picture and its remembrance line | 6 | 44 | 8 pairs for the healing song |
| `s_conc_ilvarra` | 24 (12 pairs) | Ilvarra's memories: her dragon's face and name; a place and its sound; the Imprisonment's six banners | 5; and every 10 flips a Remnant curse blanks two | 50 | 9 pairs to keep her memory |

**Hints.** Aetherius's telepathy (if Aeris is in the party): once per game, show one blank card's
face for 2 seconds. On the ward page, the healer reads one name aloud.

**Writes.** `ward_names_kept` (0–6; +2 `memory.north` each; at 5 or more the Galechanters trust
Aeris at the Moot without a match). `s_conc_bestiary`: +1 to each pair's realm and restores those
pages (Remind works on them); at 8+, the healing song is added to the Record. `s_conc_ilvarra`: at
9+, `ilvarra_memory_kept` true and `izladar_fragments` +1.

### Star Chart (`star_chart`): redrawing from fragments

**Purpose.** The stars "lose constellations" (Codex 30). Seraphyne traced "the patterns with
trembling fingers" (WW 85). The player redraws what the sky forgot, from fragments the party
remembers.

**Rules.**
1. A star field with 20–40 stars. A constellation is a named set of stars and the lines between
   them.
2. Each constellation comes with **fragments**: remembered lines of lore, each a constraint, for
   example "The Stag's antlers are five stars climbing to the left" or "No line of the Harp crosses
   another" or "The Lamp's brightest star touches three others".
3. Draw lines by dragging from star to star. Remove with a second drag.
4. Press **Name it**. If every constraint holds and the line count matches, the constellation
   inks in gold and is recorded. A wrong attempt dims one fragment for 10 seconds.
5. **False stars** (from II on): stars that flicker on a 2-second cycle. A line touching one breaks
   when it flickers out.

**Layout.** A night page: star field on the left two-thirds; the fragments on the right as torn
strips of paper; the constellation's name at the top in letters that fill in as it is solved.

**The six tribe constellations (added):** the Stag (Cedarheart), the Gale Harp (Galewing), the
Delver's Lamp (Mosswhisper), the Moon's Tear (Tidecaller), the Eclipse (Duskbane), the First Light
(Priory). Two more: the King and Queen (from WW 247), and the Scholar's Lamp (Izladar's own).

| `s_chart_moot` | Moon's Tear, Delver's Lamp, Eclipse, Scholar's Lamp | 7–9 | 3 | 4 | The Scholar's Lamp has one star missing; restoring it gives a fragment |
| `s_chart_expanse` | The Sigil, undone into the Scholar's constellation | 12 | One per `izladar_fragments` held (up to 6) | 6 | The Sigil's lines must all be removed first; then the man is drawn. With 4+ fragments every line is determined; with fewer it can be finished only as an outline |

**Hints.** Seraphyne's Glimpse (if in party): once per page, ghost one correct line for 3 seconds.

### The Slipping Glyph (`slipping_glyph`): observation

**Purpose.** "symbols her mind refused to hold"; "the meaning slid away like a dream upon waking"
(WW 71). The game's puzzle the player cannot write down.

**Rules.**
1. A carved surface of glyphs in rows (4 per row). Each glyph is built from four parts: crown,
   stem, foot and mark.
2. The player looks with a focus circle (about one glyph wide). A glyph not in focus for more than
   1.5 seconds changes its **unstable** parts. Its **stable** part never changes. Which part is
   stable differs per glyph.
3. Each row's four stable parts together make one rune from the key at the page edge. Enter the
   rune into the row's slot.
4. The in-game notebook cannot hold a glyph: sketches made on this page blur after 5 seconds. The
   solution is found by watching what stays.
5. A wrong rune: the row reshuffles which parts are stable. Three wrong runes on one page: the
   surface "devours" (that realm −3) and the page resets.

**Layout.** The carved surface fills the page, lit only inside the focus circle. Left margin: the
rune key (twelve runes, recorded, so they do not slip). Right margin: one slot per row.

**Inputs.** Move the focus circle (mouse or stick); click a slot; choose a rune.

| Scene | Rows | Slip delay | Special |

| `s_glyph_monolith` | 4 | 1.5 s | The fourth row has no stable part: it is unreadable by design. After the third row (or the third failure), the monolith devours and the run up begins |
| `s_glyph_killlist` | 5 | 1.2 s | Each row read names a witness and a place; the fifth names Ilvarra. `world_runs: true`: smoke rises in the camp; after 3 minutes the Remnants retrieve the slate and unread rows stay unread |
| `s_glyph_seal` | 6 | 1.0 s, and the stable part moves once per minute | Rows read on the monolith are already filled in. The sixth row is the name |

**Hints.** Aquatica's Purify (if in party): one row holds still for 5 seconds. Accessibility mode

### Six Realms (`six_realms`): the tribes' hex board game

**Purpose.** The tribes' own game (added). Each elder judges whether the pairs understand the

**Board.** 19 hexes (radius 2). The centre is Wrymreach. Ring 1 (6 hexes) are the six

### Heartstone (`heartstone`): resonance crafting

**Purpose.** "Its power was tied to memory" (WW 61). A crystal tuned to the right pitch holds a
memory. Gaia resonates with crystals (Codex 50).

**Rules.**
1. A ring setting with 3–5 slots. A target chord is played and shown as a still waveform.
2. A tray of shards. Each has a pitch (1 of 12 steps on a wheel), a realm colour and a crack count.
3. Place shards in slots, then **Strike**. Each slot shows a beat bar: fast wobble is far, slow
   wobble is one step off, still is right.
4. Each strike adds a crack to every placed shard. A shard with 3 cracks shatters.
5. **Fuse** (from II on): two shards in one slot sound as one pitch, the sum of their steps on the
   wheel (mod 12).
6. Win: every slot still on one strike. The crystal is set.

**Layout.** A Stonekeeper's workbench: the ring at centre, the tray beneath, the strike budget as
a row of small hammers, the target waveform above.

## 13. Endings

The author's text stops at Act II, so all four endings are proposals and none is `as_written`.
The resolver checks them in priority order at `s_choice`.

**The choice** (`s_choice`). Three answers, set in the statues of the King and Queen:

- **Keep watch**: one pair stays. Only pairs whose realm is 20 or more can be chosen (a pair that
  has forgotten itself cannot hold a name). Sets `final_choice` = `keep_watch` and `keeper_pair`.
- **Speak his whole name**: always offered. Sets `final_choice` = `remember_him`.
- **Give back the bond**: sets `final_choice` = `give_bonds`.

If "Speak his whole name" is chosen without enough remembered, the name breaks in the speaking,
and the game asks at once which pair will keep watch, so the run ends in The Rememberer.

| Priority | Id | Name | Conditions (plain) | Conditions (`endings.json`) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `architect_rises` | The Architect Rises | The world remembers less than a quarter, whatever is chosen | `{"is":"world_memory","lt":25}` |
| 2 | `remembered_whole` | Remembered Whole | Speak his whole name, with 6+ fragments, the Anchor forged, and the world at 65+ | `all`: `final_choice` eq `remember_him`; `izladar_fragments` gte 6; `anchor_forged` eq true; `world_memory` gte 65 |
| 3 | `dormant_again` | Dormant Again | Give back the bond | `{"is":"final_choice","eq":"give_bonds"}` |
| 4 | `the_rememberer` | The Rememberer | Anything else: one pair keeps watch | `true` |

### The Architect Rises

Too much was forgotten. The choice the player made is attempted, and fails: there is not enough
memory left to make a seal of. Final scenes: `s_choice`, `s_end_architect`. The pairs stand on the
rim as the shape Rhyla saw steps through. The game does not say whether the Remnants were right.
**Last image:** the sky above the six realms, full of new constellations that no one has a name
for, and the Star Chart page, blank, waiting. **Cost:** everything the world forgot stays forgotten.

### Remembered Whole

The party has remembered enough to learn who he was before he ascended: a Celestial Elf scholar
(Codex 30). The constellation drawn in the Expanse is his, the Anchor holds his name, and the
voice finishes its sentence. He does not choose anything: the prison is "sealed by memory itself"
(Codex 30), and now the whole world remembers him, so no one pair has to keep watch. Final scenes: `s_choice`, `s_end_whole`. **Last line (added):** "I… remember… me.
I was a scholar." **Cost:** the witnesses who died are not restored; their names are read out over
the six realms, and the ones the player did not record are read as blanks.

### Dormant Again

The six pairs seal the wound with the one thing older than the prison: the Twin-Soul Bond. The
world is saved, and elves and dragons forget each other, as before the story began. Final scenes:
`s_choice`, `s_end_dormant`. Six shots, one per realm: each elf wakes with no dragon and no memory
of one; each dragon sleeps under its forest, cloud, stone, sea, shadow or star. **Last image:**
Thalen in the grove, tracking a silver-antlered stag, his pulse keeping time with something he
cannot name. **Cost:** the bonds.

### The Rememberer

One pair stays at the heart of the wound to hold Izladar's name for good. The seal holds and the
world's memory slowly returns. Final scenes: `s_choice`, `s_end_rememberer`, with one of six
variants by `keeper_pair` (each a 40-second closing in which the keeper pair's realm is shown
remembering them: a ranger carving Thalen's name at the outpost, the ward singing Aeris's, the
Heartstone keeping Rhyla's, the tide elders saying Lunessa's, the Syndicate keeping Kael's in its
records, the Priory dome drawing Seraphyne and Astraia as a constellation). **Last image:** the
keeper pair alone with the statues; the elf says the name once, every dawn. **Cost:** that pair
never goes home.

### How a player reaches each one on purpose

- **The Architect Rises:** record little, burn memories freely in battle, let witnesses die, skip
  the optional Star Charts and Concentration. `world_memory` falls under 25.
- **Remembered Whole:** keep the vision in the Heartstone; read all three readable rows of the
  monolith; save the archivist; finish Star Chart I; play Star Chart II at the Moot; record THE NAME
  and retell it at the fire; keep Ilvarra's memory; read the whole seal; draw the Scholar's
  constellation (9 fragment sources, 6 needed). Keep at least four realms at 40+ or trusted for the
  Anchor. Keep the world at 65+. Then speak his name.
- **Dormant Again:** at the choice, give back the bond (world at 25+).
- **The Rememberer:** at the choice, keep watch and pick the pair. Also the result of a failed
  "speak his whole name".

## 15. Art direction

**Palette.** Each realm a pair of colours: West moss green and amber; North pale blue and silver;
South ochre and molten orange; East sea blue and pearl; Below violet and obsidian; Above gold and
starlight white. Wrymreach mixes sun gold and moon silver over bone white. Forgetting is the
paper colour (warm off-white) and graphite.

**References by mood.** Illuminated bestiaries and star atlases; ink-wash landscape painting; the
weight of old cathedral stone for the Underworld and the Bone Choir. Nothing is modelled on an
existing game's characters, levels or look.

**Materials.** Painterly textures, low-to-mid poly with strong silhouettes. Dragons carry their
realm in their scales: bark-like (Sylvan, WW 114), parchment-coloured with runes (Aetherius, WW 139),
stone and soil with magma beneath (Gaia, WW 162, 170), iridescent blue (Aquatica, WW 189),
light-swallowing (Umbra, WW 216), cosmic dust (Astraia, WW 237).

**Lighting.** Each realm keyed to its source light: dappled sun, cold cloud light, crystal glow,
lantern-crystals under water, torchlight swallowed by shadow, starlight. Light dims as a realm's
meter falls.

**One look for three layers.** The same palettes and the same forgetting shader drive all three
layers; the 2D flight backgrounds are painted from the 3D scenes' own models, flattened.

three words, the same recording both times, cleaner the second.

**Answered** (by Claude at the author's request, 2026-11-12, from the source text; the author can overrule any of them).

1. **WrymWretch is the thing in the chasm, not the chasm: what Shallock Izladar has become.** The
   sentence puts the place and the subject apart: "where the land cracked open into a vast abyss of
   swirling astral winds, WrymWretch stirred." (WW 97). The bones are separate too: they hum in
   answer, "The bones of ancient wyrms hummed with a resonance not heard in millennia" (WW 99). The
   end of Act II retells the same moment and names what stirred: "deep within that chasm, something
   ancient stirred — a voice half‑formed, a memory refusing to die." (WW 260). The same verb is used
   once more, for the Architect: "The prison weakens. The Architect stirs." (WW 243), and the
   voice's three words answer the whisper that asked to be remembered ("Remember me", WW 49) and
   Rhyla's "name dissolving into silence" (WW 57). The text never writes "WrymWretch is Izladar", so
   this is a reading, and it is listed in section 21. Changed: the voice in the chasm (section 6) is
   named as WrymWretch, Izladar as he is now; the chasm keeps its own name, Wrymreach. Nothing else
   in play changes: the voice stays heard and unseen until the endings.
2. **No. Izladar does not choose; he is remembered.** The source gives him no will beyond three
   half-formed words: he is "a memory refusing to die" (WW 260), a name "the world was trying to
   forget" (WW 79), imprisoned "in a realm outside existence, sealed by memory itself" (Codex 30).
   Who he was before is the Codex's whole answer: "a Celestial Elf scholar" who "ascended into an
   astral entity after delving into forbidden cosmic texts" (Codex 30); the game adds no more. Since
   the seal is memory, *Remembered Whole* now works without his consent: when the whole world
   remembers him, the world holds the seal and no one pair has to keep watch. Changed: the
   invented choice ("He chooses to stay in the Expanse as himself") is cut from section 13 and
   `endings.json`; Ilvarra's *Whole* arc, in which Izladar spoke her dragon's name, is cut to "she
   flies home with the party"; section 6 cites the Codex for who he was. The conditions, last line
   and cost are unchanged.
3. **The game displays "Wrymreach", without the "#".** Every in-world use in both files is spelled
   "Wrym": "#Wrymreach" six times (WW 230, 247, 258; Codex 30, 58, 81), and the novel's own title,
   "WrymWretch" (WW 26, 97). The two names share the spelling, so "Wrymreach" is kept to match the
   game's title. The one exception is the Codex's own title, "World Codex of Wyrmreach" (Codex 26),
   which spells it as the common noun is spelled ("Wyrm bones", Codex 59; "Wyrmguard", Codex 69;
   "wyrms", WW 99). If the author means that spelling, it is a rename in display text only. The "#"
   is a mark, not a letter: it also stands before a chapter heading, "#WrymWretch Chapter One" (WW
   270), where the same title has none elsewhere, and "#Wrymreach" sits as a heading in the Codex's
   Geography (Codex 58) where the others ("The Six Realms", "The Astral Expanse") have none.
   Changed: every display name, map label, scene name and description reads "Wrymreach" (sections
   1, 5, 8, 9, 11, 14, 15; `scenes.json`; `bestiary.json`); quotations keep "#Wrymreach" verbatim
   with their line numbers, and their on-screen subtitles drop the "#". The Codex's timeline item
   "March to #Wrymreach" is a quotation and is kept. `docs/COLLECTION.md` still writes
   "#Wrymreach"; it is a shared document and was not changed here.
4. **Data gap: the Stonekeeper's shard comes from the Deep Core itself.** Heartstone I is played
   before Rhyla reaches the Deep Core, so it could not shatter a Deep Core shard. The source has the
   Deep Core's crystals dying around her: "Crystals shattered along the walls, their light dimming to
   ash." (WW 158), and it is "where the oldest crystals slept" (WW 63). Changed: after the
   `s_deep_core` battle one crystal still glows among the shattered ones and Rhyla takes a shard of
   it; the request is fulfilled by that shard (section 8, section 12). The condition "if not
   shattered in Heartstone I" is cut. No flag was needed: `s_deep_core` already writes `requests`.
