# The Stuffed Ones: design

## 1. Summary

You spend three days in a cheap cabin with four old friends and learn why you love them, then the cabin takes you all and you wake on its shelf as a stuffed animal who must hold still while new guests laugh around you. In the last act you hunt the next group in five games, and every memory you spend on the hunt is a memory of the friends you came with.

## 2. Why this game

| | The Stuffed Ones | Project: Man Eater | Behind the Peephole |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Core verb | Hold still | Eat | Watch |
| Becoming the monster | Sudden, an act break. You are one of a pack and share a pulse with your friends | Gradual, measured in your own body each day, alone | Not at all |
| What is lost | Your own memories, chosen seam by seam (Stuffing) | Strength against clarity | Nothing of yours; you learn others' lives |
| Horror | Social: you hunt with the people you love, and the test is stillness | Appetite | Exposure |
| Night cycle | Three nights per group, and groups loop across runs | A day of duties, a night of choices | A fixed number of hours across six doors |
| Endings decided by | Hidden per-night meters (memory kept, dread caused) plus how much of the book you read | A ledger of meals and broken rules | Accuracy of statements plus one choice |

What no other game in the collection does: the same rooms are played twice, once from each side. The incidents done to you in Act 1 are the games you play in Act 3, in the same order the novel mirrors them (its annotation calls this "the book's cleverest structural move"). And the controls make the verb literal: in Acts 2 and 3 you hold a button to stay still, and letting go is the hunger moving you.

## 3. Source fidelity

All quotations are verbatim from `02_Novels/01_The_Stuffed_Ones.md`.

**First movement, The Cabin (chapters 1–14)**

1. A frame from the dark: "I don't know how long I've been down here." Five friends book the cabin "because it was cheap and close to the hiking trails. Five of us: me, Jamie, Lila, Marcus, and Dani."
2. Arrival: "something felt padded." The owners "moved slowly, like they were afraid of breaking." Shelves of toys "arranged like they were frozen mid-conversation." "They like company," Mabel said. "Don't move them," Ruth added. "We laughed. / We shouldn't have."
3. 2:30 a.m.: "Not to a noise—to the absence of noise." "A soft thump. / A dragging sound. / A faint squeak". Next morning: "Did anyone else hear… like… footsteps last night?" "We all had. / No one admitted it."
4. The smell: "Dude," he said, "this thing smells weird." "Metallic. Warm. Animal."
5. The warmth: a bear on Lila's bed. "It's warm," she said. "Like… alive warm."
6. The dog: the keepers' terrier "hanging from a low branch near the cabin. No blood. No struggle." "They get hungry," Mabel whispered.
7. The wildlife: "Each one looked emptied, like something had taken what it needed and left the rest." "The stuffed animals on the shelves looked fuller."
8. Toys in places nobody left them: "A rabbit sitting at the top of the stairs", "A bear propped up at the dinner table", "A cat plush outside Lila's locked bedroom door", "A dog plush sitting on the couch, facing the hallway". "We tried to blame each other." Then patterned sounds: "Three taps near the door."
9. The third night: a bear on the kitchen counter beside an emptied deer. "The bear turned its head toward us."
10. The toys herd them "toward the basement." "Warm air rose from below, humid and heavy, like breath."
11. The labyrinth: "Doors that led back to where we started." "The deeper we went, the warmer the air became."
12. The ritual room: "Symbols stitched into the walls. / A circle of torn fabric skins. / A book made of hand-sewn pages. / An altar built from children's toys." A diagram of "how to bind something into fabric, how to feed it, how to keep it alive." "They were summoned." "We were the next filling."
13. "The ritual had already begun the moment we stepped inside."
14. Waking on the shelf, "like looking through plastic." Mabel: "They like company." "And now… so do you."

**Second movement, The Hunger (chapters 15–23)**

15. "And the worst part? / We were awake."
16. "We were terrified of ourselves."
17. The keepers visit. "Oh, they turned out lovely." "Strong seams," Ruth added. "They'll last a long time." "You'll feel it soon," she whispered. "The hunger. Don't fight it. It's what keeps you alive."
18. "Car doors. / Laughter. / Footsteps on the porch."
19. "three of them, young, laughing, unaware". Memories "dissolving into cotton and thread." Jamie "fell off his shelf entirely".
20. "Did you see that?" "We were connected now, stitched together by the ritual."
21. Lila climbs down: "She didn't want to."
22. "I don't know who moved first." "Up the stairs. / Down the hallway."
23. At the bedroom door: "We were the next generation." "…I didn't fight it."

**Third movement, The Hunt (chapters 24–33)**

24. "We knew exactly what we were doing. / And that's what makes it worse."
25. "The ritual had rules. Patterns. A rhythm." One guest "poked my arm. Another lifted Lila and set her on a different shelf."
26. Sound: "Did you hear that?" / "Probably the house settling."
27. Movement: "We stayed still when they looked. We moved when they didn't." "Who moved these?" "Not me." "Seriously, stop messing around."
28. Scent: "Do you smell that?"
29. Proximity: "Okay, seriously, who keeps moving these?" "This isn't funny." "I swear I locked that door."
30. The third morning: "The door wouldn't open. / The windows wouldn't budge." Jamie falls off the couch.
31. The Chase: "We didn't rush. We didn't pounce. / We herded them." "The trapdoor opened on its own."
32. "We were the hunters."
33. The Cycle Continues: new stuffed ones "stare back at me with button eyes that haven't yet learned to hunger." "Car doors. / Laughter. / More guests." Last line: "And we are so very hungry."

**The author's ending** is chapter 33. It is in the game as **The Cycle**, marked as written, and it follows when the player plays the hunt as the narrator does.

**One group, told twice.** The second movement's three guests and the third movement's are the same people, told twice. The novel retells as a habit: chapter 15 tells chapter 13's awakening again, nearly word for word, and chapter 24 restarts the arrival ("The moment the door opened") with a correction: "I wish I could say we attacked them right away. That we were mindless. That we didn't know what we were doing. But that would be a lie." Chapter 30's fall off the couch echoes chapter 20's because it is the same fall, told truthfully. So Act 2 ends where chapter 23 ends, at the bedroom door, and Act 3 rewinds to the door opening and tells the same stay again as the hunt it was. What happens behind the bedroom door at the end of chapter 23 is never shown (section 20, answer 1).

## 4. Investment

**The hook in the first 5 minutes.** A black screen and one line: "I don't know how long I've been down here." Then daylight, a car on a forest road, four friends talking over each other. At minute 3 you step into the cabin and the sound mix changes: high frequencies drop away, as if the walls were "swallowing sound". You turn and see the shelves: dozens of toys arranged "like they were frozen mid-conversation." Mabel says "They like company." Ruth says "Don't move them." Your friends laugh. The only input offered is **Laugh**. The journal writes: "We shouldn't have." The question is planted before the player has done anything but arrive.

**What the player comes to care about, and how, before it is threatened.**

1. **Four friends, learned by doing things with them for about 45 minutes before the cabin turns on them.** The first harm to a person comes at minute 47 (the bear on the counter). Before that the player:
   - mends Marcus's torn pack strap at the table while the others play the cabin's counting game, and hears each friend choose their words (Stitchwork, c01);
   - walks the overlook trail with Marcus and his paper map, the creek with Lila and Dani, and the silent forest with Jamie, whose nature-film narration stops when the birds do;
   - eats three meals with them (Dani's camp-stove chili, the footsteps breakfast, the dinner nobody eats after the dog);
   - spends two evenings without games: a porch where the friends tell stories of the trips before this one.
   Each of these gives a **memory token** (section 12): a small named thing such as "Lila's three-note hum" or "Jamie's bad postcard". Six are given by the story; ten more are earned only by staying with the friends when you could be searching. In Act 2 these tokens are literally what you have left of yourself.
2. **Your own self.** At minute 4 you write your own name in the guestbook. It becomes the first memory token. Later it can be lost, and the guestbook page shows the name unpicked.
3. **The guests you hunt.** You first see them in Act 2, from the shelf, while you are still trying not to hunt them; Act 3 rewinds and you watch the same three for a full day before the first game (c09). The Parlour game makes you use what you learned about them. Knowing them is what lets you either torment them precisely or save them (section 11).

**The question that pulls them to the end.** In Act 1: *what are the toys, and what do Mabel and Ruth keep?* In Acts 2 and 3: *who will I still be when the car doors slam, and will these three go down the trapdoor?*

## 6. Characters

Lines in quotation marks with a chapter number are verbatim from the source. Lines marked (added) are proposals.

### The narrator (you)

- **Want:** a good trip with old friends; later, to stay themself.
- **Fear:** being the one who moved first.
- **Voice:** the journal. Short fragments, as the novel. "We laughed. / We shouldn't have." (ch 1). "I tried to think of who I used to be." (ch 19).
- **Name:** the novel never gives one. The player writes a name in the guestbook; nobody in the game ever says it aloud.
- **Form:** a cat plush (added; consistent with the cat in ch 7 and ch 29).
- **Arc by ending.** Cycle: stops fighting; "I don't fight it anymore." Warning: keeps enough to choose, and is unpicked for it. Empty Toy: refuses and is emptied; awake on the shelf. Keeper: becomes what Mabel and Ruth are.

### Jamie

- **Who (added detail):** your oldest friend, since you were nine. Fills silence; narrates hikes like a nature film; brings every trip a deliberately bad postcard for you. Afraid of being the one who ruins things. Stuffed form: a bear (source).
- **Lines:** "Did anyone else hear… like… footsteps last night?" (ch 2). "Dude," he said, "this thing smells weird." (ch 3). (added) "And here, the rare hiker, in his natural habitat."
- **Arc:** in Act 2 he slips first (ch 19–20) whatever the player does; in Act 3 he falls off the couch at the seal (ch 30). Cycle: on the shelf beside you. Warning: watches you unpicked. Empty Toy: hunts without you. Keeper: on your shelf.

### Lila

- **Who (added detail):** you shared a flat for two years. Locks her bedroom door because she sleepwalks (this is why her door is "locked" in ch 7). Hums the same three notes when nervous. Photographs mushrooms. Stuffed form: a rabbit (source).
- **Lines:** "It's warm," she said. "Like… alive warm." (ch 4). (added) "I lock it so I don't go wandering. Don't laugh."
- **Arc:** in Act 2 she climbs down first, fighting it (ch 21). In Act 3 a guest moves her to another shelf (ch 25). Endings as Jamie's.

### Marcus

- **Who (added detail):** carries paper maps and a compass, always knows the way back, is afraid of things he cannot fix. With Dani. His strap tears on day one. Stuffed form: a dog (added).
- **Lines:** (added) "There's always a way out. You just haven't drawn it yet." In the labyrinth, if the strap held: (added) "Your stitches held."
- **Arc:** in the labyrinth his map fails ("Doors that led back to where we started"). In Act 3 he "leaned against the hallway wall" (ch 27).

### Dani

- **Who (added detail):** the newest in the group, two years in; cooks on a camp stove even with a kitchen; a skeptic, afraid of being fooled and of not belonging; names things. With Marcus. Stuffed form: a bear, larger than Jamie (added).
- **Lines:** (added) "That one's Gerald. Look at him. He's a Gerald." (added) "I'm glad I came. Don't make it weird."
- **Arc:** "Maybe it was Marcus. / Maybe it was Dani." (ch 22): in Act 2 one of them may be the first to move, and the player never learns which.

### Mabel

- **Want / fear:** the text never says what she gains, and the game does not invent it. She moves "like they were afraid of breaking" (ch 1).
- **Lines (all verbatim):** "They like company." (ch 1, 14, 25). "They get hungry." (ch 5). "Oh, they turned out lovely." (ch 17). "And now… so do you." (ch 14).
- **Routine (added):** arrives each morning at 09:00 with a sewing tin, dusts the shelves, hums; leaves by 09:20. Never speaks except her source lines.
- **Arc:** Cycle: she brushes lint from your arm. Warning: she holds the scissors. Empty Toy: she dusts around you. Keeper: she stands at your shoulder on the porch.

### Ruth

- **Want / fear:** as Mabel; unsaid.
- **Lines (all verbatim):** "Don't move them." (ch 1). "Strong seams," Ruth added. "They'll last a long time." (ch 17). "You'll feel it soon," she whispered. "The hunger. Don't fight it. It's what keeps you alive." (ch 17).

- **Arc:** Warning: she unpicks you. Keeper: she hands you the needle.

## 8. Levels

Every chapter below lists its scenes in order. Flags named are declared in `data/flags.json`.

### Chapter 1: The Cabin That Felt Too Quiet (13 min)

- **Goal:** arrive, settle in, mend Marcus's strap.
- **Where:** Ridgeway Road; the cabin by day; the overlook trail; the kitchen at dinner.
- **Order:** `a1_car` (2 min) → `a1_cabin_arrival` (4 min) → `a1_trail_overlook` (3 min) → `a1_dinner` (2 min) → `a1_stitch_strap` (2 min).
- **Play:** In the car you sit in the back seat; look around, answer Jamie (three short replies offered), and he gives you this trip's bad postcard if you ask what's in his bag (token "Jamie's bad postcard"). The drive itself gives "The drive up". At the cabin: Mabel and Ruth, the shelves, the **Laugh** prompt, then incident I1 (the padded room), the first Shelve-or-Keep. You write your name in the guestbook ("My name"). Two book pages can be found by exploring: under the guestbook and inside the lid of the counting-game box. Dani names a toy on the shelf if you stand with her ("Dani names the toys"). The overlook trail is a 1.2 km loop at walking pace with Marcus; he shows you his paper map ("Marcus's paper map"). At dinner Dani serves chili ("The chili"). After dinner the four play the counting game on its embroidered board; you sit apart and mend Marcus's strap (Stitchwork, first appearance). You hear each friend choose their counting words: Jamie picks long silly words, Lila picks plant names, Marcus picks place names, Dani picks the names she gave the toys. Mending gives "Marcus's strap" and "My hands".
- **Learns:** the cabin's layout, the friends, the stitched look of everything, Shelve and Keep.
- **Flags:** `player.name_written`, `memory.earned`, `memory_log`, `book.pages_found`, `incident_log`, `explanation.current`, `act1.unease`, `strap.quality`.
- **Ends:** lights out. Fade on the pegs left mid-game.

### Chapter 2: The First Night (9 min)

- **Goal:** find what woke you; get through breakfast.
- **Order:** `a2_night_hall` (6 min) → `a2_breakfast` (3 min).
- **Play:** You wake at 2:30 a.m. "to the absence of noise": the ambience bed cuts out completely. You may lie still (the sounds come to you) or walk the dark upstairs hall with no torch, only the window's grey. Thump, drag, squeak, each from a direction the audio places precisely. Then nothing. ## 10. 2D modes

### Mode A: Dollhouse cutaway (`dollhouse`, scroll)

- **View:** the whole cabin cut open from the front: upstairs, ground floor, cellar, all rooms at once, orthographic, 1920 × 1080 base. Rooms are lit only by their own sources; night rooms are blue-grey with warm toy glows.
- **Controls:** select a toy (your cat, or one friend at a time through the pulse: Tab or shoulder buttons). Point to a destination; the toy walks a path along floors, stairs and furniture tops at 0.6 m/s while **Still** is released. Holding Still freezes it. Action buttons by game: Sound (Thump, Drag, Squeak, Taps), Scent (Seep: hold), Proximity (Settle at a marked spot).
- **Rules:** each guest has a sight cone, 70° wide and 4 m long, which sweeps as they look around. Asleep guests' cones are closed but open for 2–4 seconds at random every 40–90 seconds (light sleepers more often). A toy that moves inside an open cone is **seen**: a slip (dread +15 to that guest, +5 to others, one memory from the seam used). A toy standing still in a cone is safe. Friends you are not leading follow the rhythm on their own and never slip unless their hunger reaches 100.
- **Hazards:** awake guests (Kit in Group C until 03:00), the hall light switched on by a guest (all cones in that room 6 m for 20 s), creaking stairs (a guest within 4 m hears a toy pass the third step, and their cone opens toward it).
- **Progress:** the night clock along the top, 23:00 to 05:00; guest cards on the right with dread shading (spread 5 only).
- **Failure and retry:** there is no failing a night. Slips cost and are recorded. An accessibility option slows the night clock and cone sweep to 50 percent.
- **Sequences:** `b8_dollhouse_climb` (tutorial, no actions), `c10_dollhouse_sound`, `c12_dollhouse_scent`, `c12_dollhouse_proximity`.

### Mode B: Labyrinth from above (`labyrinth_above`, scroll)

- **View:** the labyrinth from directly above, the camera scrolling with the herd. The walls are wallpaper patterns seen edge-on; carpet corridors are a darker weave; warmth tints the floor redder toward the centre.
- **Controls:** select one of the pack (your cat or a friend); move it at up to 1.0 m/s; gestures Twitch and Step. Guests move on their own.
- **Rules:** a guest flees away from any toy they see moving within 5 m, at 1.2 m/s, choosing the most open corridor. "We didn't pounce": a toy that comes within 1 m of a guest makes them freeze for 8 seconds, and three freezes in a ring cost one memory (the hunger lunged). On carpet, guests cannot hear toys, so only sight moves them. Two doors per ring loop back to the ring's start; a guest who passes one is set back. Guests with dread under 40 move at half speed and stop at every junction.
- **Progress:** a ring counter (outer, inner, centre) and a warmth bar along the bottom.
- **Failure and retry:** if a guest loops back twice, or stands frozen for 20 seconds in total, the ring restarts from its entrance (checkpoint). No ending depends on failure; the Chase always ends at the centre.
- **Sequences:** `c14_labyrinth_outer` (one loop door, three junctions, 2.5 min), `c14_labyrinth_inner` (two loop doors, rooms of broken toys that block sight, 3 min).

## 11. 1D games

### Stitchwork (`stitchwork`, crafting)

- **Purpose:** seams decide what a body can do. As a guest it is how you care for a friend; as a stuffed one it sets how much of yourself you can hold; on the Keeper path it is the act itself.
- **Layout:** the full page is a piece of fabric under a lamp, filling 70 percent of the screen, the seam running across it. A pattern card sits top left: the stitch type with numbered entry points. A tension gauge is a thread running up the right edge with a green band. A small count in the corner: stitches done of total.
- **Rules:** each stitch is three inputs. (1) Press at the next entry point (in). (2) Drag to its exit point (out). (3) Hold to pull and release while the tension needle is in the band. Accuracy per point: within 6 px perfect, within 14 px good, otherwise off. Tension: in band good; under loose; over tears the thread (the stitch is redone, 2 s lost). Score per stitch: perfect and in band 1.0, good 0.7, loose 0.4, off 0.2.
- **Seam quality:** Strong at 85 percent or more of the maximum, Fair 60–84, Poor under 60.
- **Inputs:** mouse, touch, or a gamepad stick with a snap-to-point assist.
- **Hints:** after 8 seconds idle the next point pulses. Spread 4 widens the tension band by 20 percent.

### The Hand-Sewn Book (`sewn_book`, translation)

- **Purpose:** each spread teaches part of the ritual and changes Acts 2 and 3. All six, as a guest, open the Keeper ending.
- **Layout:** an open book across the page. Left page: loose stitched glyph strips, scrambled. Right page: a framed row of empty slots whose borders are sewn in three thread colours. Top right, the glossary: glyphs you have seen (on the sampler, on the ritual-room walls, on earlier spreads). When a spread is complete, its translation appears in the journal hand at the bottom.
- **Rules:** each glyph strip has a thread colour by role: red for an act, black for a thing, white for who. Each slot accepts only its colour. Order rules, introduced one per two spreads: a needle glyph comes before a thread glyph; a knot glyph ends a line; a glyph doubled means "many". When every strip is placed legally and in the one sentence order the rules allow, the spread pulls taut.
- **Pages:** 12 pages make 6 spreads. Six pages are found by exploring; six only by Keeping incidents (leads L1–L6, given in incident order). Pairs: S1 guestbook + game box; S2 shed + L1; S3 bear's seam + L2; S4 L3 + L4; S5 sampler + L5; S6 lectern + L6.
- **Difficulty:** S1–S2 four strips, one rule; S3–S4 six strips, two rules; S5–S6 eight strips, three rules and one decoy strip that fits no slot.
- **Hints:** Mabel's sampler shows one solved line (found in c04). After 90 seconds idle, one strip's role glows.
- **Failure:** none, except time in `a5_book_3`: 180 seconds with the toys closing in (a ring of glows tightening at the page's edge). Unfinished spreads stay unread.
- **Carry-over:** spreads read in any earlier run start already ordered (`book.carried`, a ledger of spread numbers, since spreads can be read out of order).

### Parlour (`parlour`, board game, tampering)

- **Purpose:** "We stayed still when they looked. We moved when they didn't." You never play the cabin's counting game. You move the guests' pegs while they are away, and the wrongness you leave is aimed at what you learned about each of them.
- **The guests' game (for legibility only):** an embroidered cloth board, 30 spaces in a spiral from the Door to the Hearth, each space stitched with a small symbol (Door, Lamp, Two, Bird, Tree, Hearth, Knot and others). Each player has one peg. On a turn a player says a word not yet used in the game and moves one space per syllable. A Knot space sends you back three. First to the Hearth wins. The scoresheet lists each word and position.
- **Layout:** the board seen from directly above, filling the page. The scoresheet at the left. Your Notes strip (what you observed in c09) at the bottom. At the right edge a doorway strip shows where each guest is, by sound: footsteps grow louder in three steps before anyone enters.
- **Inputs:** drag a peg to a space; Undo (only before your hand leaves it); Leave (end the visit and climb back).
- **Tampers:**

| Tamper | What it looks like | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nudge | A peg one or two spaces off its scoresheet position | Owner +6 dread, blame +2 |
| Swap | Two pegs exchanged | Both owners +4, blame +8 |
| Knot | A peg put on a Knot it never landed on | Owner +8 |
| Hearth | A peg put on the Hearth | All +10, blame +12 |
| Personal | A guest's peg put on the symbol that is theirs (Noor the Door, Wes the Lamp, Ana Two; Sol the Bird, Rosa the Tree, Kit the Hearth) | Owner +15, blame 0; `parlour.personal` +1 |

- **Rules:** at most three tampers per visit. While blame is 50 or more, half of the dread from non-personal tampers becomes blame instead ("The guests blamed each other"). Before the seal, the memory governor applies (section 12). Each tamper lowers hunger by 5.
- **Finish:** press Leave and be back at your spot before a guest enters (the climb back takes 4 seconds). **Failure:** a guest enters while you are on the table: a slip (dread +15 to them, one memory), blame −10, and the visit ends.
- **The Personal tamper is the guilt of the design:** it is only findable by having paid attention to the guests as people. The Notes strip records only what you watched happen; the game never labels "this is Wes's fear".

### Stuffing (`stuffing`, allocation)

- **Purpose:** what you pack is what you keep. It is the game's memory loss, chosen by hand.
- **Layout:** your toy body laid open flat like a sewing pattern, the six seams outlined: Head, Chest, Left Arm, Right Arm, Left Leg, Right Leg. Each seam shows its slots (1–3) as hollows. Memory tokens lie in a tray at the bottom: small embroidered patches, each with a picture and its name ("Lila's hum"). A side panel shows tonight's game and which seam it needs, the hunger rate this packing will give, and which seam a lurch would hit (the warmest).
- **Rules:** drag tokens into slots. A slot left empty is **warm**: the hunger lives there. Each warm slot adds 15 percent to the hunger rate. A game can be played only if its seam has a warm slot: Sound needs the Head, Scent the Chest, Proximity an Arm, Movement a Leg, the Chase warm slots in three seams. Tokens left in the tray when you close the seams are lost: they unravel, and their names go blank in the journal. Spread 2 makes one slot bound (gold thread).
- **Costs between packings:** joining a game takes one memory from that game's seam; a slip takes one from the seam used; a lurch takes two from the warmest seam; an Act 2 release takes one from the seam of the limb you moved. If a paying seam has no memory, the cost moves on to the next seam in the order Head, Chest, Arms, Legs. The bound slot never pays.
- **Finish:** Close (confirm). No failure.

## 12. Core systems

### Act 1: Shelve or Keep (the catalogued incident engine)

At each incident I1–I8 the journal opens with the incident written in the novel's fragments, and the player chooses:

- **Shelve** with one explanation still standing: House ("It's the old cabin"), Us ("One of us is doing it"), Wild ("It's the forest, an animal"), Me ("I'm tired. I imagined it"). Unease −10. The friends relax, and the next friend moment that needs a Shelve opens.
- **Keep:** write it down unexplained. Unease +10. The next lead page (L1, L2, …) is revealed nearby.

The next incident then plays the variant that **breaks** the current explanation, and that explanation is crossed out for good. After a Keep, the next incident plays as written. Four explanations, eight incidents: a player who Shelves everything runs out at I5, and from then on only Keep is offered. This is the vault's horror rule made into play: each incident is a *but* against the explanation you chose.

At 75 and above, **tremors**: every 4 seconds a pulse needs a **Steady** tap within 0.6 s (1.2 s with the accessibility option), or the toy shakes visibly (a guest who sees it: dread +5; no memory cost).

Friends' hunger (`pack.hunger.*`) runs the same rates. You vent a friend by leading them to move. Jamie's is scripted to reach 100 in c07 and at the seal.

### Dread, blame, the flee line and the seal (Act 3)

- Each guest has dread 0–100; `dread.group` is the average.
- **The pack's own share.** Whether you join or not, the friends play each game by the rhythm: Sound +6, Movement +4, Scent +5, Proximity +5 to every guest. The pack alone brings the group to about 20 before the seal.
- **Your share.** Up to about +25 (Sound), +10 (Movement), +15 (Parlour 1), +15 (Scent), +15 (Proximity), averaged across the group.
- **The flee line.** If `dread.group` reaches 70 before 06:00 on the third morning, the guests pack and leave together (`guests_fled`, scene `c13_flight`). A visible tell from 60: bags appear by the front door. Spread 5 shows the exact line.
- **The memory governor.** Before the seal, if `memory.kept` is under 5, any dread gain that would bring the group to 70 stops at 69. The player feels it: the toy halts mid-gesture and the pulse slows; `governor.clips` counts it. In the fiction this is the ritual's rhythm keeping a hollowed toy to its pattern. It is what makes the Warning ending require memory, and what keeps a hungry, emptied player on the novel's path.
- **The seal.** At 06:00 on day 3 (`seal.done`), +15 to all, and Jamie's fall +10. Blame resets to 0. From now on there is no cap.
- **Blame.** 0–100. Rises with obvious moves (Movement noticed: +4) and with Swap and Hearth tampers. At 50 or more, half of non-personal dread becomes blame. Group C's blame never passes 30.
- **The break line.** In the Chase a guest under 40 dread moves at half speed and stops at junctions. It slows the Chase; it does not change the ending.

### Joining or refusing (the hunt log)

Five hunt games: Sound (night 1), Movement (day 2, with Parlour 1), Scent (night 2), Proximity (night 2), the Chase (night 3). Each starts with **Join** or **Hold**. Every game writes one `hunt_log` entry: game, night, joined, dread caused, memory after. A refused game plays from the shelf with hunger unfed; refusing everything typically costs 2–3 lurches a night, which is how "the hunger takes what is left of you."

### The Keeper

- **Plain words:** you read all six spreads before the ritual room closed, and at the altar you chose to stitch, and finished the binding seam.
- **Final scenes:** `a5_altar_stitch`, `e_keeper_porch`. A cut to an ordinary afternoon on the porch. Mabel and Ruth stand either side of you. Through the window, four toys sit on the north shelf: a bear, a rabbit, a dog, a bear. A car comes up Ridgeway Road. Your hands, when they move at the edge of the frame, move at the keepers' pace, slowly, "like they were afraid of breaking" (ch 1); nothing explains it. You are given one input, **Speak**.
- **Last line:** you say "They like company." The cost: you are whole, and your friends are on your shelf.

### The Warning

- **Plain words:** the group's dread reached 70 before the third morning while you still held at least five memories, so they left alive.
- **Final scenes:** `c13_flight`, then `e_warning_unpick`. On the kitchen table, at toy height. Ruth's hands. The scissors. The thread pulled; with each seam opened, the blurred edge of the view widens to white. Before the last seam, the game plays your five (or more) kept memories back as single lines in the journal, one per seam.
- **Last image:** the printed listing on the fridge: "Cheap. Close to the hiking trails." The cost: you saved three strangers and were unmade for it; your friends stay on the shelf.

### The Empty Toy

- **Plain words:** you held for all five games and joined none.
- **Final scenes:** `c14_hold_shelf`, then `e_empty_toy`. The others climb down, the guests go below, and you watch it all in a fixed view you cannot turn. Morning. The new stuffed ones arrive on the shelf. Mabel dusts around you.
- **Last line:** "And the worst part? / We were awake." (ch 15)

### The Cycle (as written)

- **Plain words:** everything else: you played the hunt (in whole or in part) and the guests went down. This is what follows when the player keeps to the novel's path: joins the games, spends memories on the hunt, lets go at the door.
- **Final scenes:** `c14_labyrinth_inner`, then `e_cycle_shelf`. "I sit on my shelf now, waiting." The new stuffed ones "stare back at me with button eyes that haven't yet learned to hunger." Car doors. Laughter. If any memories remain, one is shown and unravels.
- **Last line:** "And we are so very hungry." (ch 33)

### Reaching each one on purpose

| Ending | How |
| --- | --- |
| Keeper | Keep at least six of the eight incidents; find all six exploration pages (guestbook, game box, shed, bear's seam, sampler, lectern); translate fast in the ritual room. Easiest on a second run, when earlier spreads carry over |
| Warning | Earn many memory tokens (Shelve early, stay with the friends); pack precious memories away from the Head and Legs; join Sound and Movement hard; use Personal tampers in Parlour; Scent and Proximity if needed; watch for bags by the door at 60 and push past 70 before 06:00 on day 3 |
| Empty Toy | Choose Hold at all five games |
| Cycle | Play the hunt. The default for anyone who joins the games and does not reach the flee line |

No two can both hold, because the resolver takes the first true in priority order. Keeper ends the run in chapter 5, before any hunt flag is written. Warning ends the run at the flight, before the Chase. A run with every game refused cannot reach the flee line: the pack's rhythm share stays near 20.

## 15. Art direction

- **Palette:** cedar brown, attic-dust beige, faded felt colours (moss, rust, sky, cream), bone-white thread. Warmth is the one saturated colour: an amber that grows into red. Night is blue-grey without black.
- **Mood references:** 1970s folk-horror film stills; felt and wool picture books for small children; Victorian dollhouse cross-sections; framed embroidery samplers; summer-rental cabins photographed for listings. No existing game's look is copied.
- **Materials:** wool felt with visible fibres, cotton thread with a sheen, button eyes that catch light, worn pine, rag rugs, wallpaper with small repeating flowers.
- **Lighting:** Act 1 natural and lamp light, 3200–4500 K. Stuffed-one scenes lower and warmer as hunger rises. The labyrinth's light comes from nowhere and is warmer each ring inward.
- **One look across layers:** everything is made of the same textiles. The dollhouse's walls show their wallpaper at the cut edge; the labyrinth from above is a woven map; the 1D pages are fabric and paper. Thread is the visual thread. The palette and materials are shared, while each page's drawing style differs (see Gameplay graphics).
- **Restraint:** the dog, the porch animals and the deer are shapes, covered or distant. Nothing is shown being emptied. Guests' fates are suggested by sound and by new toys on the shelf.

## 20. Risks and open questions

| Risk | Mitigation |
| --- | --- |
| The predator half loses tension | Every action costs memory you chose to keep; the flee line and governor make dread a thing to manage both ways; elders and friends slip on their own |
| Act 1 is long before the threat | Incidents start at minute 6; something wrong happens every 3–5 minutes; the friends are the content, not a delay |
| The memory economy is opaque | Spread 3 and the Stuffing side panel show rates; the journal lists what each cost took |
| Run 2 contradiction (a guest stuffed in one run arrives alive two runs later) | The shelf keeps one generation; earlier groups go to the circle of skins; the title calls each run "a turn of the cycle", a retelling |
| Two invented guest groups are costly | B and C share source lines and the same rooms; Act 2 reuses each group's Act 3 assets |
| Players read the guests as props | The Notes strip and Parlour's Personal tamper are built on their interior lives; each has a want and a fear |
| The Keeper path is short (about 67 min) | It needs the whole book, so it is mostly a second-run ending |

**Answered** (by Claude at the author's request, 2026-11-12, from the novel's text; the author can overrule any of them).

1. **One group or two? One group, told twice.** The novel retells as a habit (chapter 15 retells chapter 13 almost word for word), and chapter 24 restarts the arrival with an explicit correction: "I wish I could say we attacked them right away… But that would be a lie." Chapter 30's fall off the couch is chapter 20's fall told truthfully. So the hunt group (B on odd runs, C on even) arrives in Act 2; Act 2 ends at the bedroom door with nothing shown; Act 3 rewinds to the door opening. The invented Group A (Omar, Bea, Sunny) and their departure at dawn are cut.
2. **A player-written name and a cat form? Yes, with one rule.** The narrator stays unnamed everywhere the novel's voice speaks: the name appears only in the guestbook, in the player's own handwriting, and no character, caption or narration ever says or prints it. The cat fits the source: cats are among the shelf's first toys (ch 1) and a "cat plush" is one of the moving toys in chapters 7 and 29, while the novel names Jamie's and Lila's forms only.
3. **What Mabel and Ruth gain stays unsaid.** The novel's only account of them is what they are not: "The old ladies weren't caretakers. / They were keepers" (ch 11), and they did not warn the friends "because it wouldn't have mattered" (ch 12). The game shows their one unexplained trait, that they "moved slowly, like they were afraid of breaking", and never explains it. In the Keeper ending your own hands move at their pace on the porch. That is the whole of the hint.
