# The Stuffed Ones: deep dive

Everything marked **NEW** is my invention and is listed again in section 6. Quotations are verbatim
from `02_Novels/01_The_Stuffed_Ones.md` in the vault, cited by chapter. The source and the
`as_written` ending are untouched.

## 1. Where it stands

**What works.** The mirror: the incidents done to you in Act 1 are the games you play in Act 3. The
verb is literal (hold a button to stay still; letting go is the hunger). Stuffing makes memory loss
a choice made by hand. Parlour's Personal tamper turns attention into guilt. Shelve or Keep turns
the novel's incidents into an argument the player loses. The shelf persists between runs.

**What is weak.**

1. **The mystery ends at minute 62.** Once the book is read in the ritual room, nothing is left to
   find out. Acts 2 and 3 ask only "which ending". The keepers are blank on purpose (DESIGN.md
   section 20, answer 3), so the opposing force is a meter with two caretakers attached.
2. **The friends go silent when they matter most.** The game spends 45 minutes making Jamie, Lila,
   Marcus and Dani people, then turns them into glows with hunger numbers. The memory tokens are
   *about* them; they stop wanting anything.
3. **Act 3 is a list, and the guests never act back.** Sound, Movement, Scent, Proximity, Chase, each
   with Join or Hold. No guest ever does anything about the toys, though the novel gives them a
   locked door and a chair. There is no reversal between minute 88 and minute 132.
4. **The layers barely touch.** One strap line in the labyrinth; Parlour feeds a number. Each 2D mode
   is one trick (cones; herding) played once or twice with more of the same.
5. **Two endings are not played.** The Empty Toy is pressing Hold five times. The Warning is a
   threshold (dread 70), not a deed. The Cycle costs the same whatever you learned about the three
   you sent down.

## 2. The story, developed

### The dramatic question

**Is holding on to the people you love the same as keeping them?**

The narrator **wants** to stay with the four friends: on a trip, then as a pack on one pulse, then
as memories packed in seams. The narrator **needs** to let them go. Every ending is an answer: the
Keeper holds on with a needle, the Cycle by hunting beside them, the Warning lets go, the Empty Toy
holds everything still and keeps nothing.

### The opposing force, and what it wants

The hunger's command is a child's word: "the hunger whispered: / Play." (ch 26); "We were still
playing." (ch 30). The games are a child's: noises in the dark, statues, creeping up behind.

**NEW.** The thing that was "summoned" (ch 11) answered a mother. Mabel sewed a warm toy for a sick
seven-year-old's bed and asked for it to stay warm. What came does not want to kill. It wants what
a child alone in a sickroom wants: for the others to stay. Emptying a guest is how it keeps them.
"They like company" is a mother finding playmates for her daughter.

### Characters

**Ruth (deepened, NEW).** *Want:* playmates who do not go cold. *Secret:* she is the first one bound.
She was the child. Mabel has re-cut her every few years so that she would seem to grow up, then
grow old, beside her mother. "It's what keeps you alive" (ch 17) is said from experience. *Scene:*
**Ruth's Peg.** On the counting-game board one button peg belongs to no guest, and it moves a space
between your visits. Ruth does what you do: moves pieces when nobody looks.

**Mabel (deepened, NEW).** *Want:* someone to take the needle, because she is failing and Ruth must
not be left alone. *Secret:* the loose book pages are left out on purpose. Every group is an
audition; nearly everyone fails it and becomes company instead. *Scene:* **The Keeper's Porch**
(in DESIGN.md) gains one beat: Mabel, for the first time in the game, sits down.

**Dani (deepened, NEW).** *Want:* to belong to a group that had years without her. *Secret:* on the
first night, as a joke, she moved the toy she had named Gerald onto Marcus's pillow. When the real
moving started, "We tried to blame each other. / But no one confessed." (ch 7). Dani believes she
began it. *Scene:* **Gerald.** Catch her on the landing and she makes you promise. In Act 2 the
secret comes down the pulse into your Stuffing tray either way.

**Jamie (deepened, NEW).** *Want:* never to be the one who ruins the trip. *Secret:* his falls are
not slips. Falling is the only warning a toy can give, and the novel tells it twice (ch 19, ch 30).
The narrator's "we" reads it as hunger; a player with spread 1 sees his glow run *cool* first. *Scene:* **Jamie's Seam** (below).

### The secret under the story

Seeds, from `02_Novels/01_The_Stuffed_Ones.md`: "An altar built from children's toys." (ch 11).
"Mabel and Ruth came into the room that first morning, smiling like proud parents." (ch 17). "They
moved slowly, like they were afraid of breaking." (ch 1).

**NEW.** The player uncovers Ruth in three places. The door-frame of Lila's locked room carries a
height chart in knotted thread: the knots climb to a seven-year-old's height, then sixty more are
tied at the same height. Every strip in the Hand-Sewn Book has a back, stitched in a child's large
uneven hand. And in the linen cupboard are tissue patterns pencilled RUTH, cut for a small body,
then larger, then larger, in the hand that drew the pattern of you.

### How the endings change

- **The Cycle (as written; text and conditions unchanged).** *Cost made visible:* the new stuffed
  ones carry what you used against them. If you put Noor's peg on the Door, the rabbit beside you
  faces the door.
- **The Warning.** *Cost:* unmade, as now; and if you mended Jamie he is still on the shelf, hungry,
  watching.
- **The Empty Toy.** *Cost:* now it is played (wadding, section 3): you pack yourself with nothing.
- **The Keeper.** *Cost:* your first task is to re-cut Ruth's pattern. You replace Mabel at the table.
- **The Page (NEW ending, a proposal).** Reached by reading all six backs and, at the threshold,
  walking into the centre after the guests instead of holding the doorway. You lie down in the
  "circle of torn fabric skins" (ch 11) and sew a page of your own into the book. It is where the
  novel's first line is spoken from: "I don't know how long I've been down here." (ch 1). *Cost:*
  the guests went down; your friends hunt without you; you are awake in the dark with no pulse for
  company. *What it buys:* in the next run your page is in the book, in glyphs you chose.

### Sample writing (written for the game; not the author's text)

> The pattern for me was on the cupboard shelf.
> Six pieces. Tissue. Pinned.
> Under it, older tissue. Smaller pieces.
> A name in pencil. A number. Seven.
> Then the same pieces again, cut larger. And larger.
> Someone had let her grow up on paper.
> I held still.
> It was the only thing I still knew how to do.

## 3. Obstacles and puzzles

All elements below are NEW unless marked.

### Dollhouse cutaway (2D)

- **Teach:** the involuntary climb. **Test:** Sound, with the hall light and the stair. **Twist:**
  Proximity starts with the whole pack locked in the cupboard.
- **Mirrors.** A mirror bounces a sight cone once: a guest at the sink sees the landing behind them.
- **Lila walks.** Once a night Lila climbs down on her own, whatever the cones are doing ("She
  didn't want to.", ch 21). Bring your cat beside her and hold Still to hold her too, at double
  your hunger rate.
- **Set-piece: the heap.** Five toys press the cupboard door on three beats of the shared pulse. The
  chair goes over. Every eye opens. Noor comes out under the hall light, finds five toys in a pile,
  and you hold all of them still while she says it: "I swear I locked that door."

### Labyrinth from above (2D)

- **Teach:** outer ring, one loop door. **Test:** inner ring, split herd. **Twist:** the route your own
  group ran in Act 1 is woven into the tapestry in faded thread, and the guests are running it.
- **Posted toys.** A toy holding still in a corridor is a wall to any guest who has seen it move.
  There are more junctions than toys.
- **The one who picks you up.** A guest under 40 dread stops running, walks back and lifts the toy
  toward the entrance. Hold perfectly still for five seconds and you are put down.
- **Set-piece: the statue at the threshold.** The last guest is inside. You stand in the doorway as
  the wall that keeps them in, and hold Still for twenty seconds while one of them kneels and talks
  to you. Walking in instead is The Page.

### Stitchwork (1D)

- **Teach:** Marcus's strap. **Test:** your own six seams on the pulse. **Twist:** stitching *is*
  moving. In Jamie's Seam a guest's eye strip runs along the top of the chart, and the needle may
  travel only while it is shut.
- **Your own thread.** Mending a friend draws thread from one of your seams: choose which loses a
  slot.
- **The ripper.** Stitches come out in reverse order; a cut out of order tears the cloth. Use it on
  a seam Ruth tightened to win the slot back.
- **Set-piece: Jamie's Seam.** Ten stitches, one per glance away, on a friend who tore himself open
  to be seen. Mend him and he stays in the pack, hungry. Leave him open and his glow leaves the
  pulse by evening. The game does not say which is kinder.

### The Hand-Sewn Book (1D)

- **Teach:** colour is role. **Test:** order rules and a decoy. **Twist:** every strip has a back.
- **Backs.** Flip a strip: a different glyph. Each spread has two legal sentences, Mabel's ritual on
  the front and Ruth's line on the back. Under the 180-second clock, time on a back is time not
  spent on spread 6.
- **Missing strips.** One strip per late spread is gone; build it from two glossary radicals (needle
  and mouth make *feed*).
- **Set-piece:** the first flip. Neat black counted stitches on the front; on the back, big crooked
  ones in red wool, and the glyph for *seven*.

### Parlour (1D)

- **Teach:** Nudge and Swap. **Test:** Personal tampers from your Notes. **Twist:** after the cupboard
  the guests photograph the board. Any tamper is now proof: double dread, no blame.
- **Ruth's peg.** Put a guest's peg on the space Ruth's peg holds and that guest is *It*: the pack
  goes to their door first that night.
- **The sentence.** All three pegs on the Door is the only thing a toy can say: *leave*. Before the
  seal, with group dread at 50 or more, it lowers the flee line by 15.
- **Set-piece:** three pegs on the Door, and you lie down beside the board on purpose and hold
  still while they come in and find you.

### Stuffing (1D)

- **Teach:** more memories than slots. **Test:** warm slots against tonight's game. **Twist:** not
  everything in the tray is yours.
- **Borrowed patches.** Friends' memories come down the pulse in their own thread colour. Pack one
  and that friend's hunger rises 20 percent slower and they keep one line of voice later. It costs
  you a slot.
- **Guest patches.** What you learned about a guest ("Noor at the mirror") arrives as a patch.
  Packed, it counts as kept, and your toy will not act against that guest within 4 m.
- **Wadding.** Plain fill: the slot is cold, keeps nothing and plays no game. Six seams of wadding
  is the Empty Toy, chosen by hand.
- **Set-piece:** the fourth packing. One hollow left. In the tray: "My name", and Jamie's bad
  postcard with nothing on it.

### Across the game

**Links between layers.**

1. In the Act 1 labyrinth (3D) Marcus pencils each door on his map; a Strong strap keeps the map.
   Those marks show as chalk on the Act 3 tapestry (2D).
2. Packing "Lila's hum" for her (1D) stops her walking in the next Dollhouse night (2D).
3. The guest you make *It* in Parlour (1D) is the one who finds the toy in the morning (3D) and the
   one who wedges the chair.

**The long puzzle: the selvage.** Ruth's text on the backs is one sentence across six spreads, and
its glyphs are taught everywhere: the Parlour board, the sampler, the labyrinth's wallpaper, Mabel's
notes on your own pattern. Fully read, it says what the front never does: how a bound thing is let
go. It pays off at the threshold. In The Page the player composes a line from the glyphs they
learned, and that exact line is what the next run's player translates.

**A second playthrough** offers the other guest group; your own page in the book; Ruth's peg one
space on; one more knot on the door-frame; and backs readable from the first sitting.

## 4. Art plan

- **Palette:** cedar `#5B3A24`, attic dust `#D9CBB0`, moss `#6F7A4B`, rust `#A8512E`, sky `#8FA9B5`,
  bone thread `#F2EBDD`, night `#2E3A47`; hunger runs amber `#E0902A` to red `#B3261E` and is the
  only saturated colour.
- **Line:** every drawn line is a row of stitches. No continuous pen stroke except the ruled
  lithograph plate.
- **Texture:** felt fibre, counted weave, tissue; 35 mm grain on photographs.
- **Lighting:** one practical source per image; the second light is always a toy's warmth.
- **Never shown:** an emptied body; the inside of a person's seam; Ruth below the collar; the child;
  what is behind the bedroom door.

**Three prompts, in full.**

1. *Key art.* "Photographic 1970s folk-horror film still, 35mm film grain, muted palette of cedar
   brown, attic-dust beige and faded felt colours (moss, rust, sky, cream), warm practical light. A
   tall pine shelf unit in a log cabin seen straight on, crowded with small handmade felt animals
   with button eyes, bears, rabbits, cats and dogs, arranged as if stopped mid-conversation; on the
   second shelf one clean gap in the dust the width of a single toy. A lamp out of frame at left; a
   faint amber warmth on the wood beneath the toys that no lamp explains. No people, no text."
2. *Ruth's hands.* "Photographic 1970s folk-horror film still, 35mm film grain, same muted palette.
   Close view at table height of an old woman's hands holding embroidery scissors and bone-white
   thread over an open tin of needles and buttons; a high buttoned collar at the top of the frame.
   The hands are held very carefully, as if afraid of breaking. Window light from the right,
   4000 K. No face, no text."
3. *The height chart.* "Photographic 1970s folk-horror film still, 35mm film grain, same muted
   palette. A worn pine door-frame in an upstairs hall, close on its edge: small knots of coloured
   thread pinned in a rising column, as a family marks a child's height, climbing to an adult's
   waist; at that height dozens more knots crowd in one thick band, faded to fresh. Low lamp light.
   No people, no text."

(10) Ruth's hands.

## 5. Roadmap

**Phase 1: most change for least work.** Borrowed patches and wadding in Stuffing; Ruth's peg and
the Door sentence in Parlour; the cupboard start for Proximity; the statue at the threshold. Art
items 1 to 6. This fixes the silent friends, the passive guests and the unplayed endings.

**Phase 2: the secret.** Backs of the strips and the selvage; Sixty Knots; Ruth's patterns; the
glossary crossing into Parlour, labyrinth and Stuffing; Jamie's Seam with the eye strip; mirrors,
Lila walks and posted toys. Art items 7 to 10.

**Phase 3: the loop.** The Page ending, the player-composed line carried in the profile, the
second-run changes, guest patches.

**Needs the author's decision before work starts.**

- Ruth as the first bound, and Mabel's want. This overrules DESIGN.md section 20, answer 3 ("What
  Mabel and Ruth gain stays unsaid").
- The Page as a fifth ending, and reading the novel's opening line as spoken from the circle of skins.
- Reading "I swear I locked that door." as the guests locking the toys in (Phase 1 needs only this).
- Jamie's falls as deliberate; Dani and Gerald.
