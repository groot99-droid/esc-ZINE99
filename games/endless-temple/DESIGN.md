# The Endless Temple: design

## 1. Summary

You are Elaris Sel Marden, an elf scholar bound for life to a temple where every book is a living
world. You enter the books by brewing a tea and reading yourself into them as their narrator. Each
book ends on a choice you make inside it. Between books, the temple slowly tells you why you were
sent here, and whether you will keep coming back out of the tea.

## 2. Why this game

Reading is the verb. Every system is a way of reading: the tea decides how deep you read, the
narration voice shows how well you are holding the book, the Rule Tablet is close reading of law,
the Naming is reading back what you met, and the Clasps are how you get a closed book open.

| | The Endless Temple | Lira Calyx (nearest in tone: found text, a mystery) | WrymWretch (nearest in genre: epic fantasy, a self that slips) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Verb | Read | Measure | Remember |
| What the player doubts | Their own staying power: will Elaris come back out? | The data, which can be forged | The world, which forgets |
| Point of view | Slides from "I" to "he" as the tea fades; this is a control system | Third person throughout | Party, third person |
| Structure | Hub and three separate worlds | One climb in four acts | Six prologues, then convergence |
| The mystery | A past the author has not written, delivered in swappable slots | A conspiracy with an answer | A world-memory to restore |
| Endings decided by | One choice per book plus a hidden habit meter (steep) | Share of data verified by hand | Pair chosen and world memory |

Against **Heartstream** and **People of the Bark**: neither appears on the temple's shelves. They
are separate games. The two extra books here are original to this game.

proposals, and none is marked as written.

## 4. Investment

**The hook in the first 5 minutes.** The game opens in black, on the source's own first lines, in
first person, at the edge of an abyss (scene `s3_abyss_dream`, 90 seconds). The mist swells. The
player can step forward, and the ground is not there. Sunlight cuts across the dark and becomes a
bedroom floor. A tall elf wakes. Within the next three minutes he eats roast sea lizard at a table
of books, names a finished book as a short tutorial, and then picks up a book with no name on it
and sand running out of its pages. Two questions are planted by minute 5: what was the abyss, and
what is inside the sand book?

**What the player comes to care about, before it is threatened.**

| What | How the player comes to care, in play | When it is threatened |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The temple and the routine | Morning round in c1: open the window, wind the cuckoo clock, eat, shelve, brew. These are repeated, small, pleasant acts. The player learns the place with their hands. | From c3, sand drifts across the floor. By c8 the clock has stopped and the light is thin. |
| Xandri | In c2 the player *is* him, in first person: they raise his hand, take the cane to the ear, sit through the speech, and pack his patched bag. The player chose to ask the forbidden question (it is the only prompt in the circle). | From c4, waves can swallow him. In c9 his fate is the player's choice. |
| Xandri's father | The storyteller's tale (c2), the father's leaf-cord (c2), the trick with the leaves (c4), and in c6 the player plays the father's last walk through his journal. | c9: walking past his mark, or not. |
| Elaris himself | The tea is pleasant and the books are beautiful. Each brew is a small craft the player gets good at. The past fragments make him a person with guilt, not a blank keeper. | The steep meter. Staying inside is easy and attractive, and the temple shows the cost only slowly. |

**The question that pulls them to the end.** Two, braided. In the book: does the desert have an
end, and will Xandri break the rule to reach it? In the temple: what did Elaris do, and will he do
it again?

### The past, in slots (the hub's thread)

The source says only that "things had happened" and that the post was "an honor and a much better
outcome than he deserved". The game proposes an answer and builds it so the author can replace it.
The structure is fixed; the text in each slot is data.

| Slot | Fragment | Found in | Carrier object | Proposed text (added, replaceable) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| F1 the dark | Where | c1, `s3_abyss_dream` | The dream | The source's own opening paragraph, verbatim. The game adds only the interpretation: this is a place Elaris has stood. |
| F2 the vow | What the post is | c3, `g_clasp_c3a` | The temple register | "Elaris Sel Marden, reader of the third gallery. Bound as keeper on the day of Keeper Maudric's death. Until his own." |
| F3 the cost | What he did | c3, `g_clasp_c3b` | A folded letter from another reader | He lived a season in the Lantern Country as its narrator. He carried its lamp past the line its people had sworn not to cross, to see what lay beyond. "They are all in the dark now." |
| F4 the judgment | Who judged | c5, `g_clasp_c5` | The Wardens of the Shelves' ruling, strap-bound | The sentence: to be shelved inside the dark book, reading forever into a world with no light. |
| F5 the mercy | Why it was better than deserved | c7, `g_clasp_c7` | Maudric's petition | "Let him keep them all, since he could not keep one." Maudric asked for this as he was dying. |
| F6 what remains | The book itself | c8, `g_clasp_c8` | The black book | The Lantern Country, cold to the touch, mist at its edges, on the unlit shelf. |
| F7 a name | Who paid | c8, `s3_abyss_return` | A voice in the dark, remembered | Wenna, the lamp-keeper's daughter, who told him not to go, and who kept a list of the names of the stars. One star name comes back to him. |

**How the author drops in their own answer.** Each slot is a record in
`src/past_pack.json` (to be written): `slot`, `carrier`, `title`, `text`, `motif`. The scenes,
flags and puzzles that deliver each slot stay as they are. Rules for a replacement pack:

1. Keep seven slots, in this order of revelation: a place, the binding, the act, the judgment, the
   mercy, the thing that remains, a person.
2. A carrier must be a temple object: a book, a letter, a register, a ruling, a petition. If the
   author's answer has no "thing that remains", F6's carrier becomes whatever the keeper keeps of
   it, and the black book is renamed. The unlit shelf and its clasps stay.
3. F1 is always the source's opening paragraph. If the author says it is not Elaris's memory, F1's
   interpretation card is removed and the dream plays as a pure prologue.
4. The endings read only `past_known`, `black_book_open` and `candle_left`, never the text, so no
   ending condition changes when the text does.

## 8. Levels

Times below are for a first playthrough at normal pace. Each level lists scene ids from
`data/scenes.json` in order.

### c1 The Waking (11 min)

- **Goal.** Wake, learn the temple, find the sand book, brew the first cup.
- **Where.** The abyss (dream), Elaris's room, the halls, the pantry.
- **Scenes.** `s3_abyss_dream` (1.5) → `s3_room_morning` (1.5) → `s3_halls_c1` (2.5) →
  `g_name_c1` (2) → `s3_pantry_c1` (1.5) → `g_tea_c1` (2).
- **Encounters.** The abyss: the player may walk 6 m to the edge; a step past it fades to white.
  The halls: open the window (light reaches the reading chair), wind the cuckoo clock (first duty),
  eat the roast sea lizard (Elaris's line plays), shelve yesterday's finished book after naming it.
  The sand book lies on the table; picking it up shows the blank spine and the sand.
- **Learns.** The temple's purpose; that books are alive; that a named book rests; the tea's effect
  (the tin's label quotes the source line).
- **Flags.** `past_fragments` (F1), `past_known` 1, `duties_done` +1, `names_given` (tutorial
  book), `herbs` (mint, seer's sprig, honeymoss), brew values, `tea_hold`.
- **Ends.** Elaris sits, sips, opens the book. The camera pushes into the page.

## 11. 1D games

Every 1D game is a full page. The 3D camera pushes into a surface (the cup, a tablet, a cover, a
title page) and the page fills the screen. The world pauses beneath it (`world_runs: false` for
all of them). Text size and a slow mode apply to every page.

### Tea Blending (`tea`, brewing)

**Purpose.** "The blend sets how deep you enter a book and how hard it is to leave." It is the
door into every reading session, and the main lever on `steep`.

**Layout.** The pantry table seen from above. Left: a shelf of up to seven jars, each with a
paper label showing three marks (depth, length, binding). Centre: the cup, already holding the
mint base, and three spoon slots. Right: the kettle with a five-mark steep dial. Bottom: the
book's requirement card ("Depth 5 or more; binding 1 or less") and the previous keeper's pantry
ledger (hints). A result card appears on Pour.

**Rules.**

1. Mint is always the base: depth 1, length 1, binding 0.
2. Add exactly three spoons. A jar can be used more than once.
3. Set the steep dial (1 to 5). Mark 3 is neutral. Marks 1 and 2 multiply depth by 0.5 and 0.75
   (rounded down). Marks 4 and 5 add +1 and +2 to both length and binding.
4. Pour. The result sets `brew.depth`, `brew.length`, `brew.binding`; `tea_hold` starts at
   min(100, 40 + 10 × depth); `steep` changes by 3 × binding (never below 0).
5. A brew below the book's minimum does not open the book: the page stays plain text. Elaris may
   read the plain page (one paragraph) and must brew again; `sand_leak` +5.

### The Naming (`naming`, word assembly)

**Purpose.** Elaris's task from the source: read the book "and find out what to call it". A named
book rests.

**Layout.** A title page with an empty cartouche. Above it, a ring of title templates (1 to 4). Below,
the motif tiles gathered while reading, sorted into three trays by thread: **Place**, **Person**,
**Want**. Connector tiles (The, of, and, Who, -less, Never-) are free and unlimited. The gutter of
the page runs with sand (or rain, or ink) for the book being named.

**Rules.**

1. Choose a template, then fill its slots with motif tiles and connectors.
2. The name must use at least three motif tiles, and at least one from each thread.
3. Tiles from another book can drift into the trays (from c7 on). Using one stops the ink taking.
4. When the rules hold, Inscribe: the ink sets, the gutter stops running, and the name is written
   to `names_given`.
5. There is no failure; the page waits. A name can be changed until Inscribe is pressed.

### The Clasps (`clasps`, mechanical puzzle)

**Purpose.** The temple keeps some books shut. Each clasp is built differently. These are puzzles
of mechanism, not locks: there are no pins, picks or tension. Every clasp opens a book that holds
a fragment of the past.

**Layout.** The book's cover fills the page, the clasp mechanism enlarged across it in brass and
leather. A reset lever top left; a move counter top right; a hint sigil bottom right.

| Scene | Mechanism | Rules | Size | Par |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `g_clasp_c3a` | Sliding bolt | Tabs slide in a 4 × 5 housing; move the bolt tab (2 × 1) into the release slot | 6 tabs | 12 moves |
| `g_clasp_c3b` | Lever bank | Five levers; pressing one flips it and its neighbours; all must lie flat | 5 levers, 3 start raised | 3 presses |
| `g_clasp_c5` | Strap fold | Six leather straps cross the cover; a strap lifts only when no other strap lies across it; lift all, then the hasp | 6 straps, 3 layers | 6 lifts, order matters |
| `g_clasp_c7` | Gear train | Place gears (sizes 1 to 3) on a 5 × 4 peg board so the turning knob drives the latch gear clockwise; adjacent gears reverse direction | 6 gears in the tray, 4 needed | Parity is the puzzle |
| `g_clasp_c8` | Compound (the black book) | Straps first (stiff with frost: each lift is a 2 s hold), then a sliding bolt (7 tabs), then a gear train whose last gear must turn counter-clockwise | Three stages | About 4 minutes |

**Hints.** The hint sigil glows after 90 seconds; it highlights one correct next move. A second
press after another 60 seconds shows the next two.

**Failure.** None. Reset is free. The c8 frost re-forms if left for 60 seconds, so a strap lifted
stays lifted only while the player keeps working.

**Writes.** `clasps_opened`, `past_fragments`, `past_known`; c8 also `black_book_open`.

### The Rule Tablet (`tablet`, logic loophole)

**Purpose.** "They had rules for everything, and it was stifling." The player finds what the rules
do not forbid. Each book's best choice is opened by its tablet.

**Layout.** A stone tablet fills the page with numbered rules. Each rule's key words are
underlined. Tap an underlined word to read its meaning (from the clan's or the town's own usage).
Below the tablet: the **Want** card, a tray of **Way** tiles, and a plan row of three slots.
Right: the Test stone and the Break stone.

**Rules.**

1. Put up to three Way tiles in the plan row.
2. Test. Every rule is checked literally, by its words and their meanings. A rule that the plan
   breaks lights, with the word that catches it underlined in red. A plan that does not achieve
   the Want greys the Want card.
3. A failed Test adds 20 to `tablet_scrutiny`. At 100 a watcher (a shaman, a townsman, a pilgrim)
   adds one more rule to the tablet, which closes one way.
4. A plan that achieves the Want and breaks no rule wins: the loophole flag is set.
5. Break: at any time the player may choose a plan that breaks a rule on purpose. The broken rule
   is written to `rules_broken`, and the book goes on.

**Appearances.**

shrine-store.

## 12. Core systems

### The narration camera (the tea holding)

`tea_hold` (0–100) is shown as the tea's level in the cup, which sits at the bottom-left of the
frame in books as a small drawn vignette. `pov` follows it.

| `tea_hold` | `pov` | Camera | Control | Captions |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 60–100 | first | First person, as Xandri, Pell or Oda | Full, no lag | First person ("I") |
| 30–59 | fading | Pulls out over 3 s to third person, 2.5 m behind, rising to 4 m at 30 | Input lag 0 to 150 ms; steering authority 100% to 70% (the rest pulls toward the written line) | Third person ("he", "she") |
| 1–29 | thin | High third person, 6 to 9 m; the page's margins vignette in | Lag 150 to 350 ms; authority 70% to 35%; below 10 the narrator walks the written line with no input | Third person, past tense, overriding the player's pace |
| 0 | closed | Pull-out to the reading chair | None | The page closes |

**Drain.** Each scripted page beat costs 4 to 8 points, multiplied by length: length 1–2 × 1.25,
3–4 × 1.0, 5–6 × 0.8, 7+ × 0.65. Idle time drains 1 point per 30 seconds. Page costs per session
are tuned so a par cup reaches the thin band at the session's mark.

**Sips.** Hold sip 1.5 s inside a book: Elaris's long green hand reaches into the frame with the
cup. +25 `tea_hold`, +4 `steep`, `sips` +1. Two sips per session at most; then the cup is empty.

**Sessions.** Six reading sessions: c2, c4, c5 (Hollin), c6, c7 (Procession), c9. If a session's
tea reaches 0 before its mark, Elaris wakes early; the hub chapter starts with a forced brew and
the session resumes at its last checkpoint.

### Marks (whether Elaris keeps returning)

At the end of c2, c4 and c6, and after the choices in c5 and c7, the cuckoo clock coos faintly
inside the book. This is a mark. Two prompts appear on the page:

| Choice | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Set the cup down | `returns` +1, `steep` −6. The page closes now. |
| One more page | `lingers` +1, `steep` +8. A 1.5 to 3 minute lingering scene plays in thin third person: Xandri's thoughts at night, Pell's town after the choice, the pilgrims settling. It grants 2 or 3 extra motif tiles and one line of lore, then the page closes because the pot is empty. |

Lingering is attractive: it gives tiles and lore. That is the temptation the ending tests.

### Steep (the hidden habit meter)

`steep` 0–100. Never shown as a number.

| Change | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Each brew | + 3 × binding (binding can be negative) |
| Sip | +4 |
| Linger at a mark | +8 |
| Set the cup down at a mark | −6 |
| Each hub duty | −3 |
| Hollin: chair / shared / unwritten | +6 / −8 / +2 |
| Procession: city / end / pen returned | +10 / −6 / +4 |
| A book named | −4 |
| Candle left at the black book | −10 |

| Threshold | What the player sees |
| --- | --- |
| 20 | The cup leaves a faint ring on the table. |
| 40 | The ring darkens. The stacks gain two junctions. A ladder rolls by itself. |
| 60 | The window light dims by a third. Elaris's hands, in first person, carry grains of sand. **The last cup can keep him.** |
| 80 | In the temple the cup is found refilled after each brew. |

**Worked examples.** A player who brews low-binding cups, sets the cup down at every mark and does
the duties ends between 0 and 20. A player who lingers at all five marks (+40), sips twice per
session (+48) and brews average binding 3 (+54) passes 60 by c7. A player who wants The Reader Who
Stayed on purpose needs about four lingers, a few sips and a binding-heavy last pot.

### Sand leak

`sand_leak` 0–100, start 20. +15 at each Book One mark; −10 per sweep; +5 per failed brew. 60+:
drifts in the stacks and ankle-deep sand in c8. 80+: 30% more breaking waves in `s2_li_c9`. Named,
the book stops leaking (ending scene only).

### The past

`past_known` counts fragments (0–7). `past_fragments` records them. Clasps are not optional on
the critical path in c3, c5, c7 and c8 (the books they open are where the call leads), so a first
playthrough finds at least F1 to F6. F7 needs the player to step to the edge in `s3_abyss_return`
and call a name; it can be missed. Endings read `past_known` ≥ 6 and `candle_left` to choose the
last line, not which ending fires.

The resolver checks in priority order. None is marked as written; the source has no ending.

Drinking the last cup with steep below 60 does not keep him: the tea will not hold, the cup is
empty, and the book's ending plays. The Naming cannot be failed on the walked-on path, so
`walked_on` without a name does not happen in play; the catch-all covers it.

**The Reader Who Stayed.** Final scenes: `s3_last_cup`, `e_stayed`. Elaris drinks. The view stays
in the book, thin and high, and Xandri walks on without him steering. Then the camera is in the
temple, at the chair, from across the room: Elaris sits with the book open and the cup full. No
one is watching. A second book begins to leak: the weather book (rain) if it was left unnamed,
else the road book (ink), else the black book (a thread of mist under its cover). Last image: the
sunbeam that woke him in c1 draws back across the floor. Last line (added): "The clock struck the
hour, and nobody wound it."

**The Rider.** Final scenes: `s3_xandrix_back`, `s3_last_cup`, `e_rider`. Xandri climbs the flank
and holds on. The god does not throw him. The camera rises as it carries him past the father's
mark, past the edge of the drawn page, into white. The game does not show the end of the desert or
whether the clan is turned back into lizards; Xandri learns it, and does not come home to tell
them. In the temple the title page writes itself, in a hand that is not Elaris's. Last line
(added): "The book named itself. Elaris let it."

**The Named Book.** Final scenes: `s2_li_c9`, `g_name_c9`, `s3_last_cup`, `e_named`. Xandri rides
the gods' track on the Zorlop leaves past his father's marker and off the drawn page. At camp that
night he plants the father's strip of cloth on a new pole, further on. Elaris names the book (the
player's own title is shown, in ink, on the spine). The sand stops. He shelves it beside
yesterday's book. Last line (added): "He put the cup away, and the book stayed where he left it."

**The Son Returns.** Final scenes: `s3_village_return`, `s3_last_cup`, `e_returned`. Xandri walks
home over the dunes. Years pass in three drawn panels. In the storyteller's hut a grown Xandri,
scaled and weathered, sits where the storyteller sat and begins with the source's words: "The
desert seems to extend on into eternity. Once, a great explorer named Xandri traveled further than
any man had ever traveled…" It is now ambiguous which Xandri he means. A child's hand shoots up.
In the temple, the book stays nameless and sand keeps sifting from its pages. Last image: Elaris
sweeping it back into the gutter.

**Last-line variants.** If `past_known` ≥ 6 and `candle_left` is true, each ending except The
Reader Who Stayed adds one beat: Elaris climbs to the unlit shelf and replaces the candle. If the
player rebuilt the source's chapter title in the Naming, The Named Book's spine shows it.

### How a player reaches each one on purpose

| Ending | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| The Reader Who Stayed | Linger at the marks, sip to hold the tea, brew binding blends (ember root, high steep marks), take the chair in Hollin and write the city in the Procession, skip duties and the candle. Drink the last cup. |
| The Rider | At the herd, climb the flank. Set the last cup down (or drink it with steep under 60). |

## 15. Art direction

- **Palette.** Temple: honey gold, sage green, walnut, the pale green of Elaris's skin. Book One:
  moon silver, ochre, bruise purple for the vines. Hollin: graphite and slate blue. The Procession:
  sepia ink on grey paper, lantern orange. The abyss and the black book: blue-black and frost.
  Colour is never the only carrier of meaning (the steep ring also changes width; motes also
  change shape).
- **References by mood.** Illuminated manuscripts and museum book-bindings for the temple and the
  1D pages; ink-and-wash travel sketchbooks for the living illustrations; quiet monastery
  libraries; desert photography at dawn and by full moon. No existing game's look is copied.
- **Materials.** Vellum, cracked leather, brass with verdigris, glass jars with floating things,
  sand that pours as particles.
- **Lighting.** The sunbeam is a recurring device: it wakes Elaris, it reaches the reading chair
  when he opens the window, and it retreats in The Reader Who Stayed.
- **One look across layers.** The 3D books are built from the same ink drawings as their 2D
  pages: textures are painted from the illustration plates, and the flatten transition rotates the
  3D camera until the scene lines up with its drawn page.

## 20. Risks and open questions

| Risk | Mitigation |
| --- | --- |
| Loosening control feels like a bug, not a story | The cup vignette and the captions change first, before control does. The written line is shown as a faint ink path. Accessibility can turn the drift off (the pov and camera still change). |
| Three tablets that open a "best" option feel formulaic | Each tablet teaches a different kind of loophole: a word's meaning (c2), a scope (c5), a price instead of a ban (c6), a definition (c7). |
| The past reads as an invention presented as the author's | Every slot is labelled "added" in the design and in the content file. The author can replace any slot without touching scenes or endings. |
| Book One is unfinished in the source, so its end feels thin | The game deliberately never shows the end of the desert. Each ending closes on a person (Xandri, Elaris), not on a revelation the author has not made. |
| Two extra books dilute the sand sea | They are short (15 and 12.5 minutes of book time) and each mirrors one part of Elaris's story: the binding, the crime. |
| The steep meter is invisible | Its signs are diegetic and escalate; the journal notes "you read on" each time. |

**Questions only the author can answer.**

1. What happened to Elaris? The game proposes the Lantern Country and builds seven slots for the
   author's own answer. Is the introduction's opening ("I stood upon the edge of the abyss") his
   memory?
2. Does the desert have an end, and would the Xandrixes really turn the clan back into lizards?
