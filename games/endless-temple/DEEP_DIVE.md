# The Endless Temple: deep dive

Everything proposed here is an addition for the author to approve or strike (listed in section 6).
The source is `creative-writing/vault/02_Novels/05_The_Endless_Temple_-_Elaris_Sel_Marden.md`. It
has no ending, so every ending here is a proposal.

## 1. Where it stands

**What works.** The verb is real: tea, narration camera, tablet, naming and clasps are all ways of
reading. The slide from "I" to "he" as a control system is the best idea in the design, and it
comes from the source's own grammar. The steep meter turns a pleasant habit into the ending. The
past sits in replaceable slots.

**Weaknesses.**

1. **The two stories never touch.** The hub asks "what did Elaris do?" and the sand book asks
   "does the desert end?". Elaris's lingering costs Elaris (steep) and the floor (sand), but it
   costs Xandri nothing. Hollin and the Procession mirror the hub by theme; nothing in them
   changes the sand sea.
2. **No reversal in the middle.** Fragments F2 to F7 arrive in the order a file would be read:
   binding, act, judgment, mercy. Each confirms the last. Nothing the player believed at minute 60
   is false at minute 100.
3. **Nobody opposes Elaris.** The Wardens are a document, Maudric is a kind signature, Xadra is a
   cane. The only antagonist is a hidden number.
4. **The modes are one trick each.** The stacks is "follow the thick motes" four times. Tea is a
   constraint sum. The Naming cannot be got wrong in any way that matters. Only the Rule Tablet
   has a twist that bites (the watcher).
5. **Endings that cost little.** The Named Book is close to a clean win, and The Son Returns reads
   as a consolation prize.
6. **The art does not agree with itself.** `reference/08-living-illustration.jpg` shows a
   red-caped cartoon rider with garbled lettering; the source's Xandri is dark, scaled and carries
   a patched pack. `01-elaris-wakes.jpg` is flat cel animation; the asset stills are gouache.
   Three looks, no character sheet.

## 2. The story, developed

### The question

**Can Elaris read a boy to the end of his desert without walking him to death to get there?**

Elaris **wants** out. The source says so: "A small part of him wished not to be here". Reading is
the only leaving he has, so he wants the desert to have an end and wants to be there when Xandri
finds it. He **needs** to learn that a reader's wanting has weight inside the book, and to be able
to shut a cover on a story that is still going.

Xandri **wants** to prove himself ("I had to prove myself."). He **needs** his walk to be his own.

### The opposing force: the sand book

"every book was alive". This one has had no reader since a keeper died over it, and an unread
book is dark inside. It wants someone to stay, so it pays for staying: the lingering scenes, extra
tiles and lore at each mark are the book being generous on purpose. It does not know that a
narrator who will not leave wears a story out. The Wardens want the opposite, every book named and
still. Elaris stands between the two.

### The secret under the story

The seed is three lines of the source (all from the vault path above):

- "My hand shot up almost by instinct."
- "I came back to reality after zoning out as I often did."
- "he still never found the end and died in his last attempt."

**Proposal.** A reader's wanting leans on the one he reads. The want to leave is Xandri's own.
But "one more page" in the temple is one more dune in the book: every linger and every sip walks
the boy past where he would have stopped. His "zoning out" is the reader's attention lifting, and
those are the only moments he is fully himself.

**Keeper Maudric read Xandri the elder.** He wanted to see the end of
the desert before he died, and walked the explorer forty days past his turning point. That is the
"last attempt". He left the book nameless. His petition for Elaris, "Let him keep them all, since he could not keep one", was a confession:
he could not keep one either.

### Characters

**Keeper Maudric** (deepened). *Want:* the end of the desert, seen once. *Secret:* the above; and
the pantry ledger whose hints the player has trusted since c1 is his, and every recipe in it binds.
*Scene:* **The Other Hand** (c6). In the father's last walk a long green hand brings a cup into
the frame, as the player's own sips do. It wears a brass ring. The player did not press Sip.

**Xadra** (deepened; his source lines stay his only speech). *Want:* no more sons given to the
sand. *Secret:* he walked with the elder Xandri for twelve days, turned back at the third moon,
and watched his friend go on. He carved "We do not ride on the
backs of the gods" afterwards; on the tablet its clay is fresher than the rest. He is the shaman
at the well-post during the shrine-store tablet. *Scene:* **The Well-Post** (c2 linger), wordless: Xadra
sees the boy reach through the roof-gap, and looks at the moon instead. Then he goes in and
restrings a second leaf-cord.

**Wenna** (deepened). *Want:* a last line, not a light. *Secret:* she is the only person in any
book who knows a reader is there. She has stopped asking for morning. *Scene:* **Wenna Counts** (in `s3_abyss_return`). When Elaris calls a
star name, a voice gives the next name on the list back, then asks one thing: "Will you say the
end?" Nothing lights, as the
source says.

### Endings and their costs

- **The Reader Who Stayed.** Added beat: Xandri looks up out of the page at the hand with the
  cup, and walks on. Cost: Elaris is Maudric now.
- **The Rider.** Riding a god is taking the pen (the gods' smooth track is the story's written
  line; DESIGN.md has both, this joins them). Cost: Xandri is written out of his clan. In the
  village the tale now names two explorers who died, and Xadra adds a rule in fresh clay.
- **The Named Book.** A named book rests, and rest is stillness. The last plate fixes Xandri past
  the mark, mid-stride, for good. Cost: Elaris finishes what Maudric could not, and the wind in
  that desert stops.
- **The Son Returns.** If `own` is high, the grown Xandri changes one word of the tale: "and no
  one knows how he died." Cost: the book stays nameless, and Elaris sweeps every morning of an
  eternity.
- **NEW, proposal: The Unread Page.** Needs the Parting Cup (section 3) and scene 6 seen. At the
  herd Elaris looks away and does not look back. He closes the book while the story is still
  running. Cost: neither he nor the player ever learns what Xandri chose, or whether the desert
  ends. The book cannot be named. Months later one woody leaf slips from its pages.

### Sample writing (written for the game; not the author's prose)

## 3. Obstacles and puzzles

### Living illustration (2D)

- **Teach:** pump and jump on torrent waves. **Test:** heat, webs and a thinning cup together.
  **Twist:** the page is an object, and its fold, margins and drawn line are terrain.
- **The gutter.** Each spread has a centre fold. Cross it at 8 m/s or more, or drop into the
  binding and lose a page beat of tea.
- **Margin riding.** Jump above the plate's top edge onto blank paper: no waves and no grip for
  3 seconds.
- **The written line.** The faint ink path is safe and fast. Riding it lowers `own`; leaving it
  raises it.
- **Set-piece:** in `s2_li_c6` a Xandrix surfaces under the plate. The illustration tilts twenty
  degrees, the caption slides down the page, and the player rides the slope of the paper.

### The stacks (2D)

- **Teach:** follow the motes. **Test:** false trails and rolling ladders. **Twist:** keep the
  shelves from changing.
- **Reading lamps.** Three wicks per visit. A lit lamp pins its aisle: it is never rebuilt.
- **Maudric's ribbons.** Old bookmarks at some junctions mark a quicker way, past the books he
  read, at steep +2.
- **Set-piece:** at steep 40 or more an aisle runs past the reading chair, side-on. Elaris is
  sitting in it with the cup. The player walks past behind him.

### Tea Blending (1D)

- **Teach:** meet a depth. **Test:** three constraints. **Twist:** brew for letting go.
- **Second pour.** Re-steep used leaves: depth halved, binding 0.
- **Gutter sand** (ninth jar: depth 2, length 0, binding −3). It exists only if the player kept
  the sand swept up in the hub. Sweeping fills a jar instead of emptying a pan.
- **Set-piece:** at steep 80 the cup on the pantry table already holds three spoons the player did
  not add. This brew is made by taking things out.

### The Naming (1D)

- **Teach:** three threads. **Test:** false friends. **Twist:** the name must be true.
- **Blind tooling.** A tile for something only heard about, never witnessed, presses without gold.
  One blind tile is allowed per title.
- **True ink.** Want tiles check flags: "Proof" takes only if Xandri passed the mark; "End" takes
  in no ending, because no ending shows it.
- **Set-piece:** under raking light the sand book's cartouche shows a scraped title in Maudric's
  hand: "The Desert That Ends". He could not make it take.

### The Clasps (1D)

- **Teach:** one mechanism. **Test:** compound mechanisms. **Twist:** close one.
- **Sand in the works.** Each 20 points of `sand_leak` blocks one peg or tab until brushed out
  (a move).
- **Warm brass.** A bolt slides only while the cup sits on the cover, and the tea cools as it sits.
- **Set-piece:** the black book. The candle melts frost on a strap, but the same candle is the
  light. Each melt shortens it, and the last gear is set by its click.

### The Rule Tablet (1D)

- **Teach:** meanings. **Test:** a price ("or"). **Twist:** rules have ages and authors.
- **Fresh clay.** A newer rule can be set aside by citing an older one it contradicts.
- **Answer the watcher.** If the player saw The Well-Post, the watcher's shadow withdraws once.
- **NEW tablet, `g_tablet_c8`: the Keeper's Binding.** Its first rule is the source's own: "He was
  never to leave, and he could only be replaced when he had died." *Leave* means to pass the temple
  door with both feet, the same test as *enters* in c2. The loophole: the binding says keep. No
  rule says read.
- **Set-piece:** on the gods' tablet, the wedges of "We do not ride on the backs of the gods" are
  sharp where every other rule is worn.

### Links between layers

1. **Sweep, brew, open.** Sand swept in the 3D hub becomes gutter sand in Tea. Sand left on the
   floor jams the Clasps.
2. **The cup colours the page.** Stormcress brings one squall per ride that flattens the waves
   for 5 seconds. Lamp-ash keeps the pencil-thin phase readable. Ember root halves heat and gives
   the written line 20% more pull.
3. **Lingering moves the boy.** Each linger starts the next 3D session 200 m on, lowers Xandri's
   pace cap in the next ride by 1 m/s and adds to `walked`.

### The long puzzle: Maudric's ledger and the Parting Cup

The ledger gives hints all game. Its last recipe is unfinished: "For leaving a book that is still
going: sprig, and what the book gives back". The pieces: the ledger's back pages (c6), the scraped
title, and a jar of swept sand. The answer is seer's sprig
with two spoons of gutter sand at mark 3: depth 8, binding −5. Without gutter sand no blend
reaches depth 7 at binding −4. Brewed as the last pot, it is the only cup that lets Elaris close
a book without stopping it.

### A second playthrough

The ledger opens at the back, and Look away is available from c2. A hands-off run (two sips or
fewer, no lingers) earns the best payoff in the game: with the tea gone, the captions keep saying
"I". The boy is narrating himself.

## 5. Roadmap

**Phase 1: make the layers bite (small, mostly data and the web modes).** Sweeping fills the
gutter-sand jar; the Parting Cup recipe; sand jamming the Clasps; taste carrying into the rides;
the first ten art assets. No author decision is needed for
the mechanics. The Parting Cup's story meaning waits on phase 2.

**Phase 2: the reversal (needs the author first).** The Other Hand, The Ledger Turned Over, Look
away, `walked` and `own`, and Xandri Does Not Get Up. The author decides: is Maudric the father's
reader? This changes how F5 reads, though not its text. Does a reader's wanting move the one he
reads? Is Xadra the father's companion, and is the gods' rule his carving? May Wenna speak to the
reader?

**Phase 3: endings and finish.** The Unread Page and the `g_tablet_c8` binding tablet (author:
approve a fifth ending, and the reading that no rule says read). Added costs on the four existing
endings. The scraped title. Second-playthrough changes. The remaining art, then the validators.
