# Dr. Tox: Finale

Episode 3 of *Dr. Tox: Age of Glass & Greed*, the collection's eleventh game (`docs/COLLECTION.md`
row 11). Scope accepted by the author on 2026-11-19 (`dr-tox/SCOPE.md`, all thirteen
recommendations). This file is stage A of Phase 3: the design, the data, the storyboard, the asset
manifest, the Blender build files and the pictographs. The art kit, the web modules, the generated
images and the key art come later, as they did for Rise and Fall.

**Source.** The author's bible, `dr-tox/source/Dr_Tox_Compiled_Bible_Obsidian.md`, is not in the
vault. It is never edited and is cited here by part and section ("Part VI, Dr. Tox §3" is Part VI,
the Dr. Tox profile, section 3). Every quotation below is verbatim from it. **The bible gives the
Finale a stub** (Part VI, Dr. Tox, Story: The Finale, marked "(stub)"), one opening fixed point, one
closing act, a date range and a design law, so most of what follows is an addition. Every addition is
marked *(added)* where it first appears and listed in section 21. Rise (`games/dr-tox-rise/`) and Fall
(`games/dr-tox-fall/`) are canon for this episode in what the author accepted: their places, their
named people, their mechanics, and the flags they hand on.

## 1. Summary

The cult Dr. Tox made in Fall stops waiting for him: it takes the cavern hall, prints his own lines as
scripture, and marches on the last city the State has left, while the State broadcasts him and Rostova
as its two plagues; he goes in by the air, learns that the peace the wealthy buy is weighed from the
same two things his body makes, and at the city's central hub he overloads, unless the player chooses
otherwise. It ends where the bible's Finale ends: "Dr. Tox infiltrates the final corporate arcology's
central hub and overloads his glands, flooding the city's life support with an aerosolized mix of
5-MeO-DMT and Bufadienolides. His heart fails. This is his sacrifice" (Part VI, Dr. Tox, Story: The
Finale).

## 2. Why this game

The Finale is the episode in which the dose leaves his hands. In Rise he dosed himself; in Fall other
people; here a following doses itself without him, and the last dose is a city's.

| | Dr. Tox: Finale | Dr. Tox: Fall | Dr. Tox: Rise |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| What the player doses | A city, through its own air, on two pans | Other bodies: patients, then followers | His own body, and the cage he makes |
| The cult | Out of his hands: it prints his lines as doctrine (The Creed) and marches without him (Procession) | Made by the player's own marks | Not yet |
| Becoming the monster | Sudden, unremembered, and **blamed**: the State calls every harm his, and the player sorts which marks his body made and which his followers made in his name (Split Field) | Witnessed: rebuilt from testimony (Composite) | Read from traces in time (Aftermath) |

## 3. Source fidelity

What the bible gives the Finale. It is little, and the game keeps all of it.

**The stub, in full (Part VI, Dr. Tox, Story: The Finale).**

> **(stub)** The Finale is a phase (2236–2240), not a single event. It opens with Dr. Tox losing
> control of his own cult — his beliefs have outgrown him, and what he built starts moving without
> him. Everything that follows across those four years is tumultuous and chaotic, and every thread in
> it narrows toward one closing act.
>
> The closing act is the Bufadienolide Reset. Per the Blueprint: Dr. Tox infiltrates the final
> corporate arcology's central hub and overloads his glands, flooding the city's life support with an
> aerosolized mix of 5-MeO-DMT and Bufadienolides. His heart fails. This is his sacrifice, and it's
> meant to be the closing line of the entire Age of Glass & Greed phase.
>
> It is not the only major event of the Finale — but it's the one everything else is written to arrive
> at. What makes it land isn't spectacle; it's the peace that follows it, in contrast to everything
> chaotic that came before across the phase. See Part IV ("Sacrifice as the closing note") and Part II
> for the full 2236–2240 breakdown.

**What the game keeps from it, beat by beat.**

1. The opening fixed point: "**2236** — the Finale begins. Dr. Tox loses control of his cult; his own
   belief exceeds him." (Part II). "what he built starts moving without him" (Story: The Finale).
   Chapter 1 opens on the cavern hall taken; The Creed is his lines turned into doctrine; chapter 2 is
   the first thing the cult does without him.
2. The phase: "**2236–2240** — the Finale phase proper. Tumultuous, chaotic, every thread narrowing
   toward the closing act." (Part II). "Not a single event — a full phase. It opens with Dr. Tox losing
   control of his own cult and closes four years later with the Bufadienolide Reset and his sacrifice."
   (Part II, Phase mapping). Ten chapters across five dated years; the places narrow from the plain to
   one room.
3. The shape: "**Finale** — not one climactic moment but the narrowing and reassembly of every
   individual arc into a shared ending. The last issue is Dr. Tox's greatest sacrifice, the closing
   verse of the whole phase." (Part I, Macro-structure). Dr. Tox, Reese and Cally, separate in Fall,
   meet at the hub.
4. The closing act, as quoted above. Chapter 10. "final corporate arcology" is the place
   (`final-arcology`, `central-hub`); "overloads his glands" is the held input at the core; "flooding the
   city's life support" is Switchboard; "an aerosolized mix" of two named substances is Two Pans; "His
   heart fails" is the last shot. The substances are named here and in State text as the bible names
   them; no page measures them (section 11).
5. The date: "**2240** — the Finale ends. The Bufadienolide Reset. Dr. Tox's sacrifice." (Part II).
6. The law: "The Finale phase is built to land on Dr. Tox's greatest sacrifice as the last line of the
   whole Age of Glass & Greed phase — not a twist, not a rescue. Worth keeping in mind for any character
   whose arc is meant to resolve before his: their ending should make room for his to land as the final
   word." (Part IV, Sacrifice as the closing note). Reese's last choice is literal room: one pane left
   out of a wall (section 11, Panes). And: "What makes it land isn't spectacle; it's the peace that
   follows it". The Reset's last scene is the plain at dawn, quiet; the overload itself is a held input
   with no score and no sting (section 13).
7. What follows: "The twelve most powerful surviving mutants are permanently elevated by the
   Bufadienolide wave and mythologized. Aegis becomes Aleglethan the Light Bringer, his hard-light
   barriers romanticized into halos." (Part III, The Altrulan myth-distortion). The light of Reese's
   panes around the hub is in the Reset's last frames; Altrulan begins from it.

**Fall's fixed points, carried.** "His followers become chemically dependent on him (Dermorphin), then
undergo ego dissolution (5-MeO-DMT), then arm themselves with his lethal resins (Batrachotoxin &
Epibatidine)" (Part VI, Dr. Tox, Story: The Fall). Fall played the first two and offered the third;
in the Finale the cult is armed whatever Fall's ending (chapter 2), and the bible's outside read gives
the reason the game uses: "if the cult's loyalty is chemically manufactured rather than earned, "his
beliefs exceed him" reads less like a betrayal plot and more like an inevitable overdose." (Part VII
§6; Part VII is marked "not part of the original canon documentation", so the game uses it only as a
reading, never as a fact). Two Pans 1 weighs the rite the cult has been mixing for itself.

**Reese (Part VI, Aegis).** "says **"HOPE."** Always, until the arc breaks him." His abilities, carried
forward by the bible: "absorbs toxins and projects hard-light physical barriers; immune to biological
corruption." "Post-Reset, his hard-light barriers are romanticized by Altrulan survivors as blinding
halos of pure divine energy." The bible gives Aegis no Finale story; both his scenes are added.

**Cally (Part VI, Catalyst Version B; Part VIII Scenes 1 and 3).** "says **"HOPE"** while
she believes she serves it. Watch for the first time she says **"RCC."**" (Version B). The crack: "It
will not surface on command. It will not surface *soon.* / But it is there." (Scene 1 IV). Her terminal
names the program's second use: "SECONDARY APPLICATION: ANY HIGH-VALUE ASSET WITH PRE-EXISTING /
ATTACHMENT SIGNATURE. RECOMMENDED CALIBRATION PERIOD: 72HRS." (Scene 3). In the Finale she is that
second use, aimed at Dr. Tox *(added)*. The first time she says "RCC" is the Finale's (`dr-tox/SCOPE.md`
§5, "The First Word"). And one line the clinical suite turns into a room: "nobody was willing to risk a
Cycle 42." (Scene 1 IV).

**Rostova (Part VI, Catalyst Version A).** "**Philosophy:** Accelerated, radical adaptation. Releases
engineered mutagens into population centers." "One of RCC's own manufactured solutions to the plague
it created". Per `dr-tox/SCOPE.md` §3 the player meets her in the Finale "as the State's public villain":
she is a portrait on the State's broadcasts (chapters 2 and 4) and, once, a woman behind calibration
glass in the clinical suite (chapter 8). She says nothing in the Finale.

**The author's ending.** The Reset, above. The bible gives it in the Finale stub, which is marked
"**(stub)**" and attributes the act to "the Blueprint" ("Per the Blueprint"), a document not in the
bible. `dr-tox/SCOPE.md` §5 records that "the author confirms the mark"; this design marks it
`as_written` on that confirmation and flags the stub mark again in section 20 (question 1). It is **The
Reset**, and it follows when the player keeps to the story's own path: Reese leaves room in his wall,
the Toxin State does not take the man to the floor, the two pans rest in the window, and he holds the
overload until it is done. Its state values are the defaults in `data/flags.json`, so a run in which the
player only does what the story does ends there.

## 4. Investment

**The hook, minutes 0 to 5.** A raw concrete corridor under failing tubes, the one that led him down
into Snakehead in Rise; caption 2236. His own sentences are painted on the walls in a hand that is not
his: STAY OUTSIDE THE RING. TWO MARKS. NOT THREE. NOT MY BLOOD. NOT ALL OF IT. *(added)* At the end of
the corridor the cavern hall is full, quiet, and facing away from him, toward a lectern made from a
steel drum where Mercy Abiodun (Fall) reads from a stapled booklet. Above her is the composite of what
he was (if Fall's settlement pinned one) or a painted ring of names (the 999, as many as he read in
Rise). Nobody turns round when he comes in: they are not waiting for him. By minute three the page has
pushed into The Creed: a table of dominoes, each tile half of one of his own lines, and Mercy laying
tiles faster than he can. A dark-haired woman in the back row deals for her. The player who played Rise
has seen that face wake in a column (Part VIII Scene 3).

**What the player comes to care about, before it is threatened.**

- *The people of the following, as people.* Everyone in the march has a name and a want that is not
  about him, as in Fall: Mercy wants the settlement never to be afraid again; Petra Lind wants to be left
  alone and is the first to go home; Yusuf Demir, who "saw a light come out of him" in Fall, wants to be
  useful and preaches the light; Dov Ashkenazi keeps the copier running *(all from Fall; their Finale
  wants added)*. The player has three chapters with them before the march meets the guns.
- *What his words still do.* The Creed shows the player, tile by tile, that his sentences can be bent
  but not taken back. Procession measures it: his raised hand stops fewer people the higher the doctrine.
- *Cally.* The player knows what she is and Dr. Tox knows her face. She is beside him for four years,
  and the player decides whether she is shown the things that might make her say a word (section 12, The
  bond and the crack).
- *His own mind.* Self, carried from Rise and Fall, is now a floor: at 40 the Toxin State, not Alexander,
  would make the last dose (section 12). Every Pulse spike in the march is a choice about who arrives at
  the hub.
- *Reese.* The player played him as a boy in Sky Room and as the hero who could share a room with the
  doctor. At the end he holds the last pane.

**The question that pulls them to the end.** *Will he go through with it?* The bible says he does, and
the game lets the player not. Behind it, two more that only the player sees both halves of: *Will Reese
leave room?* and *Will Cally say the word?*

### Rules of the world

- **One entity.** RCC is "the parent behind every engineered catastrophe in the setting" (Part III).
  The Finale shows its last address: the State, the Biosynth Division, the clinical suite and the hub
  are one building. *Seen*: every plate reads RCC.
- **The Terminology Rule.** "Heroes and elitists say 'HOPE.'" "Villains, Insurgents, the
  plague-marked, and everyone else say 'RCC' or 'the State.'" "physical corporate signage, plaques,
  terminals, and legal documentation still read *RCC*" (Part III). In play: Dr. Tox and the march say
  RCC; the tract says neither, only HIM *(added)*; Reese chooses at the hub; Cally's first "RCC" is the
  episode's to give. *Done*.
- **The two classes.** "**The wealthy and healthy** — behind glass, inside the arcologies, protected
  from a crisis they engineered." "**The mutated and suffering** — exiled to a wasteland they didn't
  create, marked by a plague they didn't choose." (Part III). In the Finale the line is a duct: the
  plinth's shanties breathe only what the towers exhale *(added)*. *Done* (Switchboard).
- **The cure that always existed.** "The devastation isn't that the cure never existed. It's that it
  always did — it was just never a cure." (Part III). In the clinical suite the wealthy are given a
  private peace weighed from the same two things his body makes *(added)*. *Done* (Two Pans 2).
- **Architecture.** "brutalist, sterile corporate arcologies — glass and steel monoliths disconnected
  from the dying earth they sit above." (Part III, Architecture, by phase). The final arcology stands on
  a concrete plinth over a dust plain; "Their cities are glass and skybridge and air that costs money to
  breathe clean." (Part III, The Mega-Cities). *Seen*.
- **The Toxin State**, as in Rise and Fall: "It is a form language, not a form." "He does not remember
  what happened. He reads the room afterward like evidence of a crime he did not witness." (§3). The
  Finale adds blame: the State broadcasts every harm as his; Split Field sorts what was his body's from
  what his followers did in his name. *Done*.
- **The cost and the stake** (Part IX Q1, Q2, adopted by the author, `dr-tox/SCOPE.md` question 6): each
  State burns Amber and takes Self; Self never comes back. In the Finale Self has a floor (section 12).
- **The poison as sacrament.** Origin: "an isolated Amazonian tribe that achieved functional biological
  immortality through constant exposure to a specific poison toad" (Part VI, Dr. Tox, Story: Origin).
  The cult's rite and the Reset are the same chemistry at different weights; the outside read says so
  ("The same toad-derived chemistry that gives Dr. Tox his power (and his cage) is also, at a different
  dose and delivery method, the tool his own cult uses to bind itself to him", Part VII §6). *Done* (Two
  Pans), never as a real quantity.
- **The leash** and **the crack**, as in Fall. *Done*.
- **Material ceiling.** "concrete is the material of the future. Fluorescent tubes, conserved power,
  salvaged tools, and CRT-era hardware are not anachronisms" (Part III). The tract is printed on a
  salvaged copier; the switchboard is patch cords; the State's broadcasts play on CRTs.

## 6. Characters

### Dr. Tox (Alexander Balklin), lead, chapters 1 to 8 and 10

- **Want:** in the bible, to hunt "the organization that made him" (§1); in the Finale, to take back
  what he started, and then to give it to everyone. **Fear:** his own body; and now his own words, in
  other mouths.
- **Voice:** says "RCC" and "the State", never "HOPE" (voice note). Almost silent; Self strikes words
  from his captions. Added lines, few: "Not all of it." (to the lector, chapter 1); "You're still not on
  the manifest." (to Cally, chapter 3, the bible's line turned back); "Go home." (at the march's front);
  "Who built you?" (to Cally at the hub door; Fall gave him the line for Aegis).
- **Look:** as locked (§11), with Fall's shroud; in the airway, the shroud tied close.
- **Arc by ending.** *The Reset:* he does what the bible says, and the peace follows. *The Cage Holds:*
  he lives, and the cult goes on without him. *Green Wins:* the thing that keeps him alive does it
  instead. *The Light Bringer's Wall:* he dies outside the glass. *The First Word:* he does it, and is not
  alone in the room.

### Reese (RCC53-Δ1, Aegis), lead at the end: chapter 6 (second half) and chapter 9

- **Want:** "his need to be good is *genuine.*" (Part VI, Aegis). In the Finale, to hold a city's glass.
  **Fear:** the cold "like a door closing somewhere he can't see" (Scene 2 V).
- **Voice:** says "HOPE" "Always, until the arc breaks him." One choice of word at the hub: "HOPE",
  "RCC", or nothing. No other spoken lines *(added: the choice)*.
- **What carries in:** his Words (Rise's `reese.word`, `reese.said_rcc`; Fall's `reese.gala_word`), his
  flinches.
- **Arc by ending.** *The Light Bringer's Wall:* he seals the city and keeps saying HOPE; the glass
  stands. *The Reset* and *The First Word:* he leaves room, and his panes are the light around the hub
  that Altrulan will call halos. *The Cage Holds:* he holds a wall that nobody comes through. *Green
  Wins:* he watches green fill the glass he left open.

### Cally (RCC53-Δ2; Cycle 41), chapters 1, 3, 4, 8, 10 (not played)

- **What she is for in the Finale** *(added)*: the program's "SECONDARY APPLICATION" (Scene 3), placed
  in the cult to bond with Dr. Tox and to make his following ungovernable. She deals the lector's tiles.

- **Voice:** "HOPE" while she believes she serves it. Added lines, in her register: "For HOPE." (the
  default, at the hub door); "Forty-two." (reading the printer's plaque); and, only in The First Word,
  "RCC." Nothing else is added.
- **The crack, in play:** she deals tiles that are not his lines (a painted sky, a loop of string);
  she stays behind in the cavern when the cult leaves; she reads her own replacement's plaque. The
  player's choices move `cally.crack` (section 12); she never explains herself.
- **Arc by ending.** *The First Word:* she says it, opens the hub, and stays inside. Otherwise she keeps
  the key, and the program never learns what nearly happened.

## 11. 1D games

Every page is a medical instrument turned (`docs/COLLECTION.md` §4). No page touches a real substance,
procedure, dose or route: The Creed's unit is a word; Split Field's is a striation; Two Pans weighs in

Switchboard's is a patch cord; Panes' is a pane. The substances the bible names are named in this design

### The Reset (as written)

The bible's act, kept to the bible's words: "Dr. Tox infiltrates the final corporate arcology's central hub
and overloads his glands, flooding the city's life support with an aerosolized mix of 5-MeO-DMT and
Bufadienolides. His heart fails. This is his sacrifice" (Part VI, Dr. Tox, Story: The Finale, "(stub)",
"Per the Blueprint"). In play: he holds. The plant's lights go soft. The camera is the bunker's, static,
50 mm, one amber work lamp with hard falloff (§9): the bible's first grammar in the city's last room. His
breathing slows and stops being heard under the plant; the frame stays on the lamp, not on him. Cut. Then
the plain at dawn: the floodlights are off; the march, or what is left of it, is sitting down in the dust;
nobody is shouting; on the plinth the shanties' people stand under vents breathing the same air the towers
breathe (if `switchboard.plinth`). Through the arcology's glass, a ring of hard light still stands around
the hub where Reese's panes are: the halos Altrulan will remember. No caption but the date, 2240. The
ending card quotes the bible: "What makes it land isn't spectacle; it's the peace that follows it".

### The Cage Holds

He rested the pans where his heart holds, or let go. He walks out of the hub alive the way he came, and
nobody stops him. Months later, at Snakehead under the amber lamp, he doses ("Pain that is absolute and
routine", §10), and on the CRT the State broadcasts the cult's next attack, with resin, on the plinth's
water, without him: as many hurt as the doctrine was high. The last image: the vial in his hand, the
broadcast's glow on the locker.

### Green Wins (terminal)

The man was at the floor when he reached the core; the thing that keeps him alive did what he came to do,
without the weighing. Green self-illumination spills across the hub's glass (§9, Toxin State: "Low angle,
wide, unstable, 18–28mm"); the frame is green and nothing in it is amber ("When green begins to win the
frame, he is losing.", §6). The dose goes out without the sacrament: no peace follows; the episode cuts to
black and the save closes. No depiction reads as a win ("Any depiction where the transformation reads as a
win", §12, What to Avoid): no form is shown, only light on glass.

### The Light Bringer's Wall

Reese said HOPE and set the keystone. The hub is sealed; Dr. Tox is on the gallery, outside. He overloads
anyway, against the glass, and what he makes goes down the supply duct he came by and out of the plinth's
vents: the road and the shanties get it, the towers do not. His heart fails on the gallery floor with the
hard light between them. Reese does not look away. The last image: the wall, whole, lit from inside, and a
city behind it that will not change. "The rot must be excised so the tree can live." (Part VI, Aegis) is
the ending card's line.

### The First Word

Cally said it. She opens the door and comes in, and she does not leave: her hand is on the balance when he
rests it in the window. He holds; the cut comes; the plain at dawn as in The Reset. The ending card's
cost: she was in the hub when it flooded, and her printed body did not come through it. The last image
before the plain: two people in the lamp's light, one of them saying nothing, the other having said the
only word she chose.

### Why only one can be true

The resolver stops at the first true condition. A sealed hub means he never reached the core, so nothing
after the wall can happen. A man at the floor cannot choose, so Green Wins outranks every ending that is a
choice. A held-back dose is no Reset, so The Cage Holds outranks the two Resets. Of the two Resets, the one
with Cally inside outranks the one without.

### How a player reaches each one on purpose

- **The Reset:** as Reese, say "HOPE" and keep the keystone, or say nothing; keep Self above 40 (reconstruct
  Split Field, keep Pulse below the line in Procession 2, wait in the gallery before forcing the door); rest
  the pans in the window; hold to the end.
- **The Cage Holds:** rest the last pans in the heart band, or let go of Hold.
- **Green Wins:** let the State fire in Procession 2 and leave its Split Field unreconstructed, or force

- **The Light Bringer's Wall:** as Reese, say "HOPE" and set the keystone.
- **The First Word:** keep every sky tile in The Creed, keep Cally's secret in chapter 3, show her the

## 20. Risks and open questions

**Risks.**

- *The sacrifice as spectacle.* The bible's law is that it is "not a twist, not a rescue" and lands on
  "the peace that follows it". Mitigation: the overload is a held input the player can let go of at any
  moment, with no meter, no score and no sting; the camera stays on the lamp; the last scene is the plain,
  not the hub; the ending card leads with the cost.
- *Drugs and dosing.* The bible names real substances for the Reset and the cult. Mitigation: they are
  named only in this design and in State text; Two Pans weighs invented pips in pans labelled RED and BLACK;
  no page shows a source, a quantity of a real substance, a procedure or a route; the rite and the Reset show
  only stillness.
- *A mass event.* The Reset reaches a city. Mitigation: nobody is harmed on screen in any ending; the plain at
  dawn shows people sitting down, alive; Green Wins shows light on glass only; Switchboard keeps the
  assisted-breathing patients on their own line by choice, and the page refuses to cut anyone's air.
- *The cult as glamour.* Mitigation: every follower has a name and a want that is not about him; the doctrine
  is shown as misreading; the resin's harm is named on Split Field's record.
- *Violence at the station and the perimeter.* Mitigation: Split Field shows marks on objects only and names
  the hurt; the State's form is one cut; no child at either.
- *A child in peril.* None on screen; the march and the plinth are adults on screen.
- *Look-alikes.* The Toxin State follows §3 and §8; Aegis's panes are tangram geometry, not any existing
  hero's shield.
- *Canon drift.* The Finale is a stub; every chapter is an addition (section 21) and can be struck without
  touching the bible or the Reset's own words.
