# Dr. Tox: Rise

Episode 1 of *Dr. Tox: Age of Glass & Greed*, the collection's eleventh game (`docs/COLLECTION.md`
row 11). Scope accepted by the author on 2026-11-19 (`dr-tox/SCOPE.md`, all thirteen
recommendations). This file is stage A of Phase 1: the design, the data, the storyboard, the asset
manifest, the Blender build files and the pictographs. The art kit, the web modules and the key art
are stage B and later.

**Source.** The author's bible, `dr-tox/source/Dr_Tox_Compiled_Bible_Obsidian.md`, is not in the
vault. It is never edited and is cited here by part and section ("Part VI, Dr. Tox §3" is Part VI,
the Dr. Tox profile, section 3). Every quotation below is verbatim from it. Anything the bible does
not contain is an addition and is listed in section 21.

## 1. Summary

A forensic scientist poisoned as a child by Rockcore Chemical Corp makes his own cage at fifteen,
walks out at twenty-four, and breaks into the company's buildings until the ground gives way under
him; in the chapters between, a boy in a concrete room painted like the sky is made into the
company's hero, and the player plays the friends he will not be allowed to remember. Rise ends where
the bible's Origin ends: "Alexander is fully established as Dr. Tox — armored, armed, based in
Snakehead." (Part VI, Dr. Tox, Story: Origin).

## 2. Why this game

Dr. Tox is the collection's only game whose source is a series bible rather than a finished work,
and the only episodic one. Rise is the episode in which the player makes the thing (the cage, the
hero) before anyone uses it.

## 3. Source fidelity

What the bible gives Rise, in order of the bible's own clocks, with the beats the game keeps.

**Alexander (Part II; Part VI, Dr. Tox, Story: Origin; Part VIII Scene 3).**

1. The river. RCC "dumps an engineered biohazard into the river of a small rural Idaho town." "One
   thousand children pass through the study, disguised as an environmental contamination event. The
   town dies around them." "Only one subject survives it in any meaningful form: **Subject CMXCIX —
   999 of 1,000.** A child named Alexander." (Part VI, Dr. Tox, Story: Origin). Chapter 3.
2. The room. "His body fights the toxin from inside a sterile hospital room from childhood until age
   24." "At fifteen (~2217), inside that hospital room, he engineers the volatile radioactive
   medication that becomes his cage." (Story: Origin). "It is not a power source. It is a cage, and he
   built it himself, and he is the only person who can make more of it." (Part VI, Dr. Tox §2).
   Chapter 1.
3. Walking out. "He walks out with a doctorate in forensic medical sciences, specializing in
   biological anomalies — in 2226, the same year the world falls." (Story: Origin). Chapter 3.
4. The rebuild and the forging: six months of rehabilitation; then "martial arts, boxing, and
   mechanical engineering — not hobbies, disciplines"; "a hunch starts forming: someone was behind
   what happened to him." (Story: Origin). Chapter 5.
5. The hunt. "He begins breaking into privately owned and restricted facilities, chasing the hunch.
   Twelve months of undercover reconnaissance follow — pin boards, red string connecting photographs
   of things that would look mundane to anyone else" (Story: Origin). Chapters 5 and 7.
6. The fall. "Two years and one week after walking out of the hospital, mid-trek, the ground gives
   out beneath him. He falls through a cavern into a reinforced subterranean bunker." (Story:
   Origin). Chapter 9.
7. The archive. "He finds his own file. He finds the other nine hundred and ninety-nine only as
   names." (Story: Origin). Chapter 9, Fiche.
8. The printer room, concurrent with the descent ("Read these two threads as concurrent, not
   sequential.", Story: Origin, note): the plaque, the spec sheet, Cycle 41's waking (Part VIII Scene
   3). Chapter 9.
9. The escape: "Cornered by facility security, he escapes with the help of the bunker's failed
   experiments — see the Observers of the Whole, below. He later returns and claims the fortress as
   his own." (Story: Origin). Chapters 10 and 11.

**Reese (Part III, Project HOPE; Part VI, Aegis; Part VIII Scenes 1 and 2).**

1. Sky Room, "a concrete holding block painted with a blue sky and green fields. Bunks, a play mat,
   20–60 children depending on concurrent trials." (Part III, Project HOPE). "Cally and Carmy were
   friends since Sky Room. Rolan was an oddball who got them in trouble and made up for it by being
   the top prospect." (Part VI, Aegis). Chapter 2.
2. The protocol. "Cally discovered the final protocol — all groups would fight down to the last four.
   She told her cohort. Everyone was horrified except Rolan." (Part VI, Aegis). Chapter 4.
3. The arena: "The floor split. *Only two of you can remain.*" "All of them threw down their weapons
   — except Rolan" (Part VI, Aegis). Played as the refusal (section 8, chapter 4).
4. Two doors down. "His memory gets *shaved.* Hers gets *harvested.* Different verbs for a similar
   theft." (Part VIII Scene 1 II). Chapter 4's last shot.
5. Aegis, mantled: first light, the mirror, the word, the ceremony, the deployment (Part VIII Scene 2
   I–VI). Chapters 6 and 8.

**The author's ending.** The Origin ends on a state, not a scene: "By the end of this sequence — the
fall, the archives, the escape, the return — Alexander is fully established as Dr. Tox: armored,
armed, based in Snakehead." (Story: Origin). The author accepted it as Rise's written ending
(`dr-tox/SCOPE.md` question 3). It is **Fully Dr. Tox**, marked `as_written`, and follows when the
player keeps to the Origin's path: leaves the spec sheet on the screen (Scene 3: "whoever built this
thing was proud enough of the number to leave it on the screen"), escapes with the Observers without
the Toxin State taking him, and returns.

## 4. Investment

**The hook, minutes 0 to 5.** A fluorescent room at 85 mm, locked off. A breath fogs a clear plastic
tent from the inside. Past it, against the wall, a bench a child has built out of IV poles, steel
trays and clamps. Caption: 2217. The player is fifteen and lethal to share a room with: the tubing
reaches four metres. In the first minute he lifts the tent; by the second he is at the bench, and the
page pushes in to The Cage: a dropper, a flask, an amber band holding against a green front. The
player makes the first batch by hand. At minute four he loads the lead-shielded syringe, and the
monitor's line, green since the game opened, turns amber when he drives it in. Nothing has been
explained. The bible's first signature image ("The injection", Part VI, Dr. Tox §10) is the first
thing the player does.

**What the player comes to care about, before it is threatened.**

- *The cage itself.* The player makes every dose of Amber in the game. Every Toxin State burns two of
  them, and the cage is what makes him safe to stand near (section 12).
- *Four children in a painted room.* Chapter 2 is eighteen minutes of Sky Room before the arena is
  named: a play mat, a string, three friends with habits of their own. Cally takes the crossings Reese
  offers; Carmy passes only to Cally; Rolan grabs out of turn and is forgiven because he is the best of
  them. The player makes string figures with their hands. Only then does chapter 4 come.
- *Four children by a river.* In chapter 3 the five-year-old Alexander plays beside Dale, June, the
  Ostrowski twins and Hollis, and the player notices one thing about each (a training wheel, chalk
  numbers, matching scabs, a jar of minnows). Fiche, in chapter 9, finds them again by that detail.
- *His own mind.* Self falls with every Toxin State and never comes back (Part IX Q2, adopted). The
  player learns this in chapter 7, at 85, and has three chapters to keep it there.

**The question that pulls them to the end.** Two questions, one per thread, that only the player can
see both halves of: *Who did this to him, and will he find out before his body takes him?* and *Will
Reese ever learn what the player watched happen in the arena?* Rise answers the first (the archive:
"his illness was not the plague, and never was", Story: Origin) and refuses the second; the threads
touch only in a line on a terminal Alexander may or may not take: `PRIMARY VECTOR: SUBJECT RCC53-Δ1
("AEGIS") — BOND INTEGRITY 94.6%` (Part VIII Scene 3).

### Rules of the world

- **One entity.** RCC is "the parent behind every engineered catastrophe in the setting" (Part III).
  It runs three branches that "should not be conflated" (Part III table): I, the plague; II, True
  Mutation, Subject CMXCIX (Alexander); III, Project HOPE and Project Catalyst. Rise shows II and III
  only; the plague is not yet released in Rise's years and is mentioned only by Insurgents. *Told* by
  Nell Okafor (chapter 8), *found* on fiche cross-references (chapter 9).
- **The Terminology Rule.** "Heroes and elitists say 'HOPE.'" "Villains, Insurgents, the
  plague-marked, and everyone else say 'RCC' or 'the State.'" "physical corporate signage, plaques,
  terminals, and legal documentation still read *RCC*" (Part III). In play: every RCC plate is
  legible to the player and to no HOPE character; Reese's first "RCC" is a recorded choice
  (section 12). *Seen* and *done*.
- **The cage.** "The radiation forces his hyper-aggressive immune system to work inward, trapping the
  antibodies deep in his veins." (Part VI, Dr. Tox §2). Untreated, "his antibodies attempt to escape
  through sweat and exhaled breath" (§2): the breath tent and the four metres of tubing. *Done* (The
  Cage) and *seen* (the tent).
- **The Toxin State.** Trigger: "Severe trauma or an extreme adrenaline spike accelerates his heart
  rate." (§3). "It is a form language, not a form." (§3). "He does not remember what happened. He
  reads the room afterward like evidence of a crime he did not witness." (§3). *Done* (Pulse at 100,
  then Aftermath). Its cost model and its stake are the bible's two recommended open items, adopted by
  the author for the game (Part IX Q1, Q2; `dr-tox/SCOPE.md` question 6).
- **The coagulation anomaly.** "Wounds do not bleed; they armor over into black lacquered plate."
  (§2). *Seen* on his arm after each dose and *found* as scab flakes among the Aftermath tents.
- **Project HOPE.** "Health, Optimization, and Peak Evolution" (Part III). Twenty children per trial;
  Sky Room ages 1–5; cohorts of four at 5; "*purposefully traumatic*" training ages 5–10; live combat
  at 11; the arena at 12 (Part III). *Seen* (Sky Room), *told* (Cally, chapter 4), *found* (the
  announcer's "Nineteen others", chapter 6).
- **The leash.** "those flashbacks are not residue — they are a control mechanism. HOPE signals them
  to mean something to him on demand." (Part VI, Aegis). *Done* (Cat's Cradle with three hands blank;
  section 11).
- **The Enforcer Program and Project Catalyst.** "Cally, Carmy, and Rolan did not stay dead." (Part
  III). Cally's line: "a genetic printer built to reproduce her on demand" (Part III). *Found* (the
  plaque and the terminal, chapter 9) and *seen* (Cycle 41 wakes).
- **Material ceiling.** "concrete is the material of the future. Fluorescent tubes, conserved power,
  salvaged tools, and CRT-era hardware are not anachronisms" (Part III). *Seen* everywhere: the fiche
  reader, the CRT terminal, the rota typed on paper.

## 6. Characters

### Alexander Balklin (Subject CMXCIX), lead, chapters 1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 10, 11

- **Want:** to know who did it ("someone was behind what happened to him. It wasn't random. It wasn't
  an accident.", Origin). **Fear:** his own body; what he does when he is not there.
- **Voice:** says "RCC" and "the State", never "HOPE" (Part VI, Dr. Tox, voice note). Almost nothing
  aloud; his panel captions are clinical and short (added): "Pulse 112. Hold." "Three minutes later."
  "Not my blood. Not all of it." In Scene 3 the bible gives him no line: "Alexander says nothing."
- **Look:** as locked (§11); at fifteen the same face younger, the tent's plastic between him and the
  camera; at five, a child's height and nothing of the face shown.
- **Arc by ending.** *Fully Dr. Tox:* the man who makes his own cage takes a fortress and a family, and
  doses alone in its bay; the 999 stay names. *The Cage Alone:* the same fortress, and the family he
  left to save himself. *Green in the Archive:* the fortress begins with what the State did in it, and
  he is fifteen points less himself than he would have been. *The Spec Sheet:* he knows a name he
  should not, and RCC knows his face.

### Reese (RCC53-Δ1, Aegis), lead, chapters 2, 4, 6, 8

- **Want:** "his need to be good is *genuine.*" (Part VI, Aegis). As a child: to be with the other
  three; "Reese had feelings for Cally that shyness and program rules never let him act on." **Fear:**
  as a child, the protocol; after, the cold "like a door closing somewhere he can't see" (Scene 2 V).
- **Voice:** says "HOPE" "Always, until the arc breaks him." (Part VI, Aegis). The bible gives him no
  spoken line in Rise; the player's choices are his only words: "For HOPE?" at the mirror (added), and
  at the lower ring "HOPE sent me" or "RCC sent me" (added; the choice is the Terminology Rule).
- **What he keeps:** "Sky Room. The four of them playing and training. The moment the other three
  died." "What he does not retain: their names" (Part VI, Aegis). In play, the string figures he made
  come back in chapter 6 with three pairs of hands blank.
- **Arc in Rise:** fixed by the bible up to the deployment; the player's flags (`reese.word`,
  `reese.flinches`, `barrier.released`) carry into Fall, where he is played again. No Rise ending is
  his; the four endings are Alexander's, and Reese's state is reported on each ending card.

### Cally (RCC53-Δ2; Cycle 41), chapters 2, 4, 9

- **As a child:** friends with Carmy since Sky Room; takes the crossings Reese offers; she discovers
  the protocol and tells them. She dies "refusing to kill Reese" (Part VI, Catalyst Version B), shot
  from above; the shot is never shown.
- **As Cycle 41:** in narration and UI she is **Cally** (`dr-tox/SCOPE.md` §3); the word "Catalyst"
  appears only in the State's text on the plaque and the terminal. Her lines are the bible's, verbatim
  (Part VIII Scene 3): "Cold." "You're not on the manifest," "That's alright. I'm not particular about
  who watches me wake up." "I'm told I'm very good at being watched." "Do I have a name yet," "There it
  is," "That's the look. I don't even know your name and I already know what you're worth to somebody."
- **The crack**, not shown in Rise and never explained to the player: "something that used to be a girl
  in a concrete room painted with a fake sky is still refusing to kill her own" (Scene 1 IV). The player
  who made string figures with her knows what Alexander does not.

### Carmy (RCC53-Δ3), chapters 2, 4

- Cally's friend since Sky Room; in Cat's Cradle she passes only to Cally (added habit). In the arena
  she throws down her weapon; Rolan "embraced Carmy, kissed her, and stabbed her while smiling" (Part
  VI, Aegis). The camera stays on Reese's face; the embrace is behind him and the cut comes first.

### Rolan (RCC53-Δ4), chapters 2, 4

- "an oddball who got them in trouble and made up for it by being the top prospect." (Part VI, Aegis).
  In Cat's Cradle he grabs out of turn; letting him raises his bond and tangles the figure (added).
  The night before, he alone is not horrified. His line, verbatim, heard over the cut and never seen:
  *"I promise, my love, my love is real. Though maybe I only love myself. Thank you for loving me too."*
  He "died having *chosen* the program" (Part VI, Carmy & Rolan). Reese "beat Rolan to death with his
  hands" (Part VI, Aegis): the game cuts at the seam, on the leap.

### The woman at first light and the staff, chapter 6

- Unnamed (Part VIII Scene 2). Lines verbatim: "There he is." "Welcome back, Aegis." "You look like
  you're supposed to," "That matters more than you'd think." "Orientation's this afternoon,"
  "You're going to save a lot of people, Aegis. Try to enjoy it. Not everyone gets to." The sentence
  repeated by three staff in three rooms: *You survived something terrible so you could protect people
  from it happening again.*

### The announcer, chapter 6

- Verbatim: "Nineteen others walked this path beside him," "and from among heroes, a hero rose
  highest. Elevated not by chance, but by strength, by loyalty, by sacrifice beyond what any of us
  could ask of a child." "Citizens of the Spire — I give you *Aegis.*" (Part VIII Scene 2 IV).

### The other hero, chapter 8

- Unnamed and not Chitin (`dr-tox/SCOPE.md` question 11). "unhurried in a way that unsettles him more
  than urgency would" (Scene 2 VI). Lines verbatim: "New," "They finally sent the amber-lit one down
  from the Spire. I wondered when." "Try to keep up, hero," In the Barrier escort she is a second line
  of motion on the page that the player cannot steer; she never needs a wall.

### Nell Okafor and the penned (added), chapter 8

- Insurgents and bystanders in the supply corridor, each with a name and one want spoken through the
  wall: Nell Okafor (wants her brother's water drum back from the transport's path), Tomas Reyes (wants
  to get his mother's medicine across the corridor before the curfew), Ruth Abara (wants the boy she is
  holding to stop coughing), and up to five more by run. Nell is the one who says "RCC sent you", with
  her hand on the plate stamped RCC on the emitter. None dies in Rise.

### The river children (added), chapter 3, found again in chapter 9

- Dale (a two-wheeler with one training wheel left on), June (chalk numbers on the kerb, counting down
  to her birthday), Mira and Pell Ostrowski (matching scabs on matching knees, from the same fall),
  Hollis (a jar of minnows from the river, kept on a windowsill). They are five and six. Each is on
  the fiche with one line under "distinguishing marks" that matches what the player noticed; each
  found is read aloud by name. The game never shows how any of them died.

### The Observers of the Whole, chapters 10 and 11

- The Split ("Half-human, half-toad", Part VI, Observers): leaps a gutter in the escape and carries
  him. The Cauldron ("pulsating translucent acid boils"): sprays a doorway clear; the etch is a false
  trace on the Green ending's Aftermath page. The Ghost ("peerless active camouflage, total
  invisibility"; "the water must be chemically pure or he suffocates instantly"): visible only in his
  tank; the clean-water tank is his. The Resonance Twins ("acoustic shockwaves that shatter concrete";
  "Recoil shatters their own skeletons"): open the wall at the end of the escape and pay for it. Story
  content is an open item in the bible ("[OPEN ITEM] Story content not yet written."), so everything
  they do here is added. They do not speak in Rise; they sign, and the Split's tongue is never used on
  a person on screen.

### Security at Snakehead and Arlo Benn (added), chapters 7, 10

- Arlo Benn is the depot guard in Panel run 2. His locker has a child's drawing taped inside it. In the
  Aftermath he lived: the long scrape ends at the door he dragged himself through. The Snakehead team
  is faceless in 3D; in the Green ending three names are written on the tents.

## 10. 2D modes

### Mode A: Panel run (`m2_panel_run`, scroll)

Graphic style: **Noir spotted-black brush ink** (`docs/graphic_styles.json`).

- **Story reason:** "He begins breaking into privately owned and restricted facilities" (Origin). His
  break-ins are told as the comic the bible says the Age is drawn in ("Grounded, cynical, traditional
  comic styling", Part I); the white gutters are the time he waits.
- **Movement:** side-scroll inside one panel at a time. Walk, crouch, press to a wall (cover), climb a
  marked edge. **Inputs:** move; crouch; cover; dart (aim and release); choose the next panel.
- **Rules.** A page is 6 to 9 panels. Each panel is a small side-on room. Leaving a panel at its right
  edge (or its exit) opens a choice of the next two or three panels; each is a different route and a
  different jump in time. A **gutter** skips time: guards advance their rounds by the gutter's caption
  ("THREE MINUTES LATER."). Guards have torch cones; a camera sweeps; a dog (Panel run 2) smells,
  ignoring cover. A **dart** drawn from the shunt grazes a guard and drops him for one gutter (a
  doctor's placement, never a kill in Rise) and costs Pulse +12. Being seen costs +8 and a guard's
  approach; seen three times in a panel restarts the panel at its gutter. Sprinting +3 a second;
  stillness in cover −2 a second, to the floor.
- **Pulse at 100.** In Panel run 1, Pulse is capped at 95: the page fails and he aborts out of the
  nearest exit (his body is new to the discipline). In Panel run 2 the last panel drives it to 100
  (scripted). In Panel run 3 it can reach 100 by the player's own darts and sprinting: the Green ending.
  At 100 the panel border turns green from the corners, the suit splits, and the page cuts to black;
  the State resolves the rest of the page off it.
- **Progress shown:** the page itself, panels filling with ink as they are crossed; the Pulse line in
  the bottom margin, amber in stillness and green-tinged per dart.
- **Failure and retry:** a restarted panel; the run never ends in death.
- **Sequences:** `s2_panel_1` (a research floor; 6 panels, 2 guards, a camera; finds the first RCC
  plate), `s2_panel_2` (a cold-storage depot; 8 panels, 3 guards, a dog; scripted Toxin State in the
  last panel), `s2_panel_3` (the Snakehead escape; 9 panels; security; with the Observers, each acts
  once in a gutter at the player's call: the Split carries him over a gap, the Cauldron clears a
  doorway, the Ghost walks ahead unseen and marks a safe panel, the Twins open the last wall and pay
  for it; without them, 9 panels alone and two more guards).
- **Difficulty curve:** 1 teaches darts and gutters; 2 adds the dog and the forced choice; 3 is the
  longest and the only one whose 100 is the player's.

### Mode B: Barrier escort (`m2_barrier_escort`, scroll)

Graphic style: **Franco-Belgian ligne claire**.

- **Story reason:** "a *containment action* against Insurgent elements holding a supply corridor on
  the city's lower ring" (Scene 2 VI). Aegis "projects hard-light physical barriers" and "absorbs
  toxins" (Part VI, Aegis, abilities). The clear line is HOPE's world as it wants to be seen: even,

- **Movement:** Aegis runs alongside a transport that scrolls left to right at a fixed pace.
  **Inputs:** move along the floor; draw a wall (press, drag, release: a straight hard-light bar);

- **Rules.** Walls cost 2 Load a metre. Debris thrown from the barricades bounces off walls; a

  clouds (+20 each). The transport stops at a standing barricade (three stops and the escort
  restarts). **Every wall pens someone.** People in the corridor (Insurgents, bystanders, a child)

- **The leash in the escort:** when Reese stops for more than two seconds beside a penned person, the

  wall can be dropped with one tap (releases that pen, `barrier.released` +1), which also opens a gap

## 12. Core systems

### Pulse (Alexander)

Range 0–100, floor 20. +12 per dart; +8 seen; +15 hit; +3 per second sprinting; −2 per second still in
cover. Floor 40 when Amber is 0 at a chapter start; +10 to the floor after a leaky batch. At 100 the
Toxin State takes the controls (see below). Shown as a monitor line in the margin of every Alexander
page and as his breathing in 3D.

### Amber

A counter, 0–6. The Cage makes it (3 or 4 per batch, capped at 6 on hand). One dose is taken at the
start of chapters 3, 5, 7 and 9 (a new day each; chapter 10 is the same night as 9). Each Toxin State
burns 2 (Part IX Q1, adopted: "every transformation burns down a finite supply he must synthesize by
hand"). A typical run: 4 after chapter 1, then 6, 5, 2 after the depot, 1 entering Snakehead, plus
chapter 11's batch.

### Self

0–100, starts 100. Each Toxin State event −15, or −10 if its Aftermath is reconstructed; Green in the
Archive −25 more. Never restored (Part IX Q2, adopted: "the damage is cumulative"). Shown as his
captions: below 90, one word in each caption is struck out; below 70 (reachable only in the Green
ending), two words, and one caption a chapter is left blank. In Fall, Self is the floor every Toxin
State threshold is measured from.

### The Toxin State

Trigger at Pulse 100 in a Panel run (capped at 95 in Panel run 1). The bible's constants hold in every
drawing of it ("Pale skin, stretched taut", "Thick blackened veins", "Eyes fully consumed by sickly
luminous green", "Distended jaw", "Mass pulled from internal reserves", §3); its form follows the
threat (§3 table) and is never the same twice. It is shown for one panel only, as the suit failing
("Seams splitting, plating lifting away, straps snapping", §8), and the panel is cut before any act.
Then Aftermath. Each event is logged in `toxin_events`. In Rise it happens once for every player
(scripted, the depot) and once more only if the player lets Pulse reach 100 in the escape.

### Load (Aegis)

0–100. +20 per canister cloud absorbed; +10 on each flinch; −2 per metre of wall. A wall cannot be drawn
without the Load for it.

### The Word (the Terminology Rule as a flag)

`reese.word` (hope, rcc, silent) and `reese.said_rcc`. Reese's only spoken choices in Rise: the mirror
("For HOPE?" or nothing; `reese.mirror_answer`) and the lower ring ("HOPE sent me", "RCC sent me", or
nothing). The first "RCC" is recorded and carries into Fall, where HOPE's leash answers it (Fall's
"First Word" ending, `dr-tox/SCOPE.md` §5). Alexander never chooses: he says "RCC" in every caption.
Cally (Cycle 41) never says either in Rise.

### Bonds

`reese.bond_cally`, `reese.bond_carmy`, `reese.bond_rolan`, 0–100, start 30, +10 per figure made with
that child's hands, +10 for Rolan when allowed out of turn. In the arena the held seconds before Drop
are recorded beside them; nothing in Rise changes because of them. They carry into Fall as the
attachment profile Cally is aimed at.

### Colour as HUD

Amber and green never share a frame at equal weight (§6). Every Alexander screen keeps a thin margin
line: amber when Pulse is below 70, amber with green spikes 70–94, green from the corners in at 100.
Reese's screens have no margin line; their warmth is the program's (amber light in the Spire), and
cools to gray-teal for one second each time he flinches.

### Carry-over into Fall

`self`, `amber`, `observers_choice`, `took_spec_sheet`, `security_knows_face`, `reese.word`,
`reese.said_rcc`, `reese.flinches`, `barrier.released`, `fiche.names_read` (listed in `game.json`
`carry_over`). They are scars, never a changed premise (`dr-tox/SCOPE.md` §2): Fall starts from the
bible's fixed points whatever Rise's ending.

The resolver stops at the first true condition, and the priorities make the precedence explicit: the

the door), the door outranks the printout (a lost family outweighs a name), and the as-written ending
holds only when none of the three deviations happened.
