# The Redacted Testament of Lira Calyx: design

## 1. Summary

You are Dr. Lira Calyx, a field scientist who climbs a mountain world remade by a nuclear cataclysm, measuring it by hand, while someone corrects, censors and forges the journal you keep. Four acts follow her four books from the Mid-Slopes down to the Lowlands, up to the High Peaks and into the core under Peak 12, and a playable epilogue hands the book to the stranger who wrote in its margins.

## 2. Why this game

Lira Calyx is the collection's instrument game. Every other investigation game asks the player to notice; this one asks the player to take a number, decide whether to trust it, and live with the model built from it.

| | Lira Calyx | Behind the Peephole | Missing Campsites |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| What you investigate | The world, with instruments | Other people's hidden lives | Records against reality |
| How you know | You measure; a number is evidence | You watch and deduce | You compare |
| What can go wrong | Your own data is falsified behind your back | You state the wrong answer | The world loses things |
| Whose hand is in the record | Yours, an unknown helper's, and the Continuum's | Nobody's | The ranger's |
| What decides the ending | Share of data verified by hand, and whether you answered the margin notes | Accuracy of statements, plus one choice | What you carried |

| | Lira Calyx | People of the Bark |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scale | One woman on one mountain | A whole people on a trunk |
| Myth | Myth is measurement in disguise; songs decode to coordinates | Myth is interpretation; meanings become policy |
| Board game | The Node Board: a network against a hidden opponent, solved with real physics | Trunk and Shadow: rules mutate with belief |

The collection gives Lira Calyx the **Cross-section climb** and **Under the lens** 2D modes, and five 1D games: **The Assay**, **The Key**, **Storm-Song Cipher**, **Journal Audit** and **The Node Board**. No other game uses them.

## 3. Source fidelity

All quotations are verbatim from `05_Worldbuilding/02_The_Redacted_Testament_of_Lira_Calyx.md`.

**The world.** "After the nuclear cataclysm, the tectonic plates didn’t just shift — they collided with such force that the crust folded upward into sky‑piercing megapeaks." Three realms: the Lowlands ("The Scorched Biome"), the Mid‑Slopes ("The Verdant Reclamation Zone"), the High Peaks ("The Aetheric Frontier"). "This vertical world is the spine of the story."

**Book I, Biology of the Modern World.**
- Lira "reestablishes baselines—light spectra from crystalline bark, spore densities in Spore‑Weaver webs, migration counts for Ridgeback Striders".
- "On the third night she records the Spore‑Weavers pulsing in a valley like a single organism." She labels it Pattern α.
- Page 47: "Phase offset miscalculated. Re‑sample at 0.5 s resolution." Lira writes, "Who wrote this?" "The question is left unanswered."
- Ember Wolf juveniles too high; Aether‑Vines curve "toward a narrow azimuth on the Peaks"; a storm-struck outpost; the fragment "threaded with micro‑filaments".
- Hypothesis 1: "Aetheric gradients act as directional cues, biasing evolutionary trajectories upward." A threat from a corporate patron; a redaction; Operation ReGenesis. "Life is not choosing upward. It is being chosen."

**Book II, The Aetheric Systems.**
- Isotope fractionation shows "evidence of deep‑earth seeding".
- A scrap behind a console: "She’s close. Move the logs."
- A hearing turns violent "when a prototype harvester destabilizes a ridge"; she watches "Aetheric pulses alter gene expression in fungal cultures".
- A sealed archive names "Dr. Solace Veylor" and "Operation ReGenesis"; her brief is redacted and she is branded "a saboteur"; "A misfiled footnote" matches the margin hand.
- "You are looking at the wrong end of the needle. Look below."

**Book III, The Human Threshold.**
- "The Peakborn sing phase offsets that match AFU spikes; Midlander storytellers encode nodal maps in harvest chants."
- A subterranean city; "an archivist who speaks in half‑truths and riddles"; "A Midlander engineer she trusted betrays her for promises of restoration."
- A stolen log is "overwritten with false timestamps and fabricated sensor readings".
- "The Second Genesis—a plan to destabilize the Peaks to force a selective reset." She "broadcasts a raw data dump to settlements". A page carries "a second set of annotations in a different hand—the archivist’s hand—trying to nudge her away from a fatal assumption."

**Book IV, Final Reckoning.**
- The coalition: "Mara, a Peakborn storm‑singer; Joren, the repentant Midlander engineer; and Kade, a Lowland scout."
- The model: `\ddot{x}_i + \gamma \dot{x}_i + \omega_i^2 x_i = \sum_{j} K_{ij} (x_j - x_i)` and `\lambda_{\text{max}}(L) < \frac{\gamma}{2}`, "where \(L\) is the Laplacian of the coupling network."
- The hidden variable: a deep core beneath Peak 12 "alters local coupling constants \(K_{ij}\) and injects false phase data". "The model is brilliant; the data are compromised."
- Veylor: "a man who believes in necessary cruelty"; "the old world was weak; a hard reset is a mercy."
- "A key term in her coupling matrix—an apparent constant of environmental damping—was a Continuum artifact." The schedule staggers "by \(2\pi/N\)".
- "Some nodes damp; others amplify." "Veylor escapes deeper into the mountain." "She is taken."

**Final chapter.** "This is not paranoia. This is presence." "Her plea is clear: phase offsets, not fire." "If I don’t make it out, if this book is all that remains, then let it be known that the Peaks are—". The archivist: "They were not the person Lira trusted. They were the one who arrived too late." The pulses "produced a fragile pause and a partial fracture." "If you are reading this, you are a target." "Close the book if you want. Or keep going. Either way — they will come."

**The author's ending.** Lira fires the phase-offset pulses on partly corrupted data; the result is a fragile pause and a partial fracture; she is taken into the mountain, and the archivist says plainly that she died: "She paid with her life. The Continuum took her into the mountain." The source's own summary agrees: "knowledge survives, but its author does not". The archivist steals the books and warns the reader. In the game this is **The Redacted Testament**, marked as written.

## 4. Investment

**The hook in the first 5 minutes.** The game opens in the dark, in the shear tunnel under Peak 12, as someone who is not Lira. A torn journal lies in fresh rubble. Its last line breaks off: "the Peaks are—". The player picks it up, opens it at page 1, and the ink of page 1 bleeds into the morning light of Hollin Terrace. By minute 5 the player is Lira, holding a clicker, counting Ridgeback Striders as they cross a terrace bridge, and the journal draws her first graph live as she counts. The question is planted: *whose hands is this book in, and how did it end up under a mountain?*

**What the player comes to care about, before the game threatens it.**

| What | How the game makes you care, in play | When it is threatened |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Hollin Terrace** (the Mid-Slope research terrace) | You spend Book I living there: you set its flood-siren baseline, count striders with Pell, a terrace child who keeps her own wrong but earnest tally, and watch the river glow from your bench. Your numbers decide when the terrace evacuates. | Book IV: the synchronized storms crack it. The terrace drill on the Node Board is fought over its own six nodes. |
| **Lira's method, and her journal** | The journal is the interface. Every reading you take by hand is drawn into it in her hand. You learn to trust it because you made it. | Book I ends with a redaction; Book II redacts your brief; Book III forges your entries. You have to audit your own handwriting. |
| **The hand in the margins** | A stranger corrects you on page 47, and is right. Notes keep coming. If you reply, the hand sometimes answers. | The Continuum is reading the same book. Every reply raises your exposure. Book IV asks whether you trusted them enough. |
| **The companions** | Joren is the terrace's engineer in Book I; he helps you mount every instrument. Kade walks you through radiation in Book II. Mara teaches you to hear a storm in Book III. | Joren betrays you in Book III. All three go down into the core with you. |

**The question that pulls them to the end.** Can a careful person, measuring honestly, save a world whose data was poisoned before she arrived? And who is writing in my book?

## 8. Levels

Every hand reading below can instead be taken from a relay beacon (section 12). A relay reading is instant, lowers exposure by 3, and is never verified.

### Cold open: The Torn Book (4 min)

- **Goal.** Find the book.
- **Where.** The shear tunnel under Peak 12, an unnamed figure with a lamp.
- **Sequence.** `co-tunnel` (3D, 4 min). Walk 60 m of fresh rubble; a lamp beam; dust falls on a 7-second rhythm the player will later recognize as the mountain's oscillation. The journal lies open; the last line is torn: "the Peaks are—". Pick it up, open it to page 1; push-in to the page; the page becomes Hollin Terrace.
- **Learn.** Someone else has this book. It ends badly.
- **Flags.** None.
- **Ends.** Push-in to `b1-terrace`.

### Book I: Biology of the Modern World (38 min)

- **Goal.** Re-establish baselines; explain the anomalies.
- **Where.** Hollin Terrace, the Spore-Weaver valley, the Aether-Vine ridge, the storm-struck outpost. Nodes N1 to N3.
- **Sequence.**
  1. `b1-terrace` (3D, 7 min). Unpack. Count Ridgeback Striders over the rope bridge with Pell (30-second clicker window, three windows). Set the flood-siren baseline (read the river glow meter). Joren mounts instrument posts and shows the relay beacon on the terrace mast: "Saves you the walk." Gear tier 0 issued. Writes `readings` (baselines, no node), `gear_tier`.
  2. `b1-key-1` (1D, 5 min). Classify the terrace collection. The juvenile Ember Wolf pelt breaks the key. Writes `readings`, `key_branches_added`.
  3. `b1-assay-1` (1D, 4 min). Crystalline-bark spectra baseline. Writes `readings`.
  4. `b1-valley` (3D, 6 min). The third night. Place three photodiode posts; set the sampling dial; watch the pulse cross the valley. Pattern α is drawn into the margin. Node **N1**. Then page 47: margin note 1 appears beneath the Strider gait sketch. The player may circle it, reply, re-sample at 0.5 s, or leave it. Writes `readings`, `pattern_alpha`, `margin_notes`, `margin_notes_answered`, `margin_notes_acted`, `nodes_verified`, `relay_used`, `exposure`.
  5. `b1-climb` (2D, 5 min). Up the cross-section to the ridge. Juvenile Ember Wolves at 2,600 m, above their limit. At the top, sight the Aether-Vine azimuth: node **N2**.
  6. `b1-outpost` (3D, 4 min). A localized aetheric storm returns every 90 seconds. Read the gravimeter between gusts: node **N3**. Margin note 2 ("Not lightning. Read the gravimeter first."). Salvage the fragment. Writes `fragment_salvaged`.
  7. `b1-lens-1` (2D, 4 min). The fragment under magnification; find the lattice.
  8. `b1-terrace-night` (3D, 3 min). Link three anomalies in the journal to write Hypothesis 1. Publish a cautious note (as the source does) or keep it private. If published: the threat letter arrives under the door; one page of the official field log comes back with a black bar; an anonymous packet names Operation ReGenesis. Margin note 3 ("Publish less than you know."). Lira writes "Life is not choosing upward. It is being chosen." Writes `hypothesis_1`, `note_published`, `exposure`.
- **Encounters.** No combat in the game. Obstacles are weather, air, dose, time and people watching.
- **Ends.** Flatten into the descent.

If the player keeps the note private, the threat and packet still arrive (the patron has read the official log, not the note), and exposure does not rise. The story's beats hold either way.

### Book II: The Aetheric Systems (35 min)

- **Goal.** Follow the fragment to its makers.
- **Where.** The Scorched Biome, Cinder Reach archive, the harvester ridge, the corporate lab. Nodes N4 to N6.
- **Sequence.**
  1. `b2-descent` (2D, 4 min). Down the cross-section; a chemical storm front sweeps right to left; glassback crawlers react to AFU spikes. Kade meets you at the bottom and gives you a Lowland mask: gear tier 1.
  2. `b2-lowlands` (3D, 4 min). Kade guides you across the fungal mats. The reading for node **N4** sits inside a radiation pocket: 45 seconds of dose before you must step out. Kade: "Count to forty in there. Not forty-one."
  3. `b2-archive` (3D, 3 min). Cross-reference the fragment's microstructure with pre-Convergence patents. Pull drawers; match lattice drawings. Find the consortium's signature.
  4. `b2-assay-2` (1D, 5 min). Isotope fractionation on a core sample from a Lowland well: node **N5**. Non-natural ratios: `seeding_evidence`.
  5. `b2-hearing` (3D, 4 min). A public hearing on harvesting at the ridge. The prototype harvester starts. Clamp a strain gauge and read the ridge's harmonic before it fails at 40 seconds: node **N6**. The crowd turns; you get out.
  6. `b2-lab` (3D, 6 min). Infiltrate the corporate lab after hours. Take logs, not fights: guards walk fixed routes with lamp cones. Watch pulse rigs alter fungal cultures. Find the scrap behind the console: "She’s close. Move the logs." Find the Continuum manifest with a misfiled footnote in the margin hand: margin note 4 ("Batch 9 is not from the well.").

### The Node Board (board game, network strategy)

- **Purpose.** Lira's plan, played: map nodes, deploy phase-offset emitters, use fungal networks as sensors, trigger. The finale is a match against Veylor's hidden pieces.
- **The rule underneath, explained simply.** The author's model, `\ddot{x}_i + \gamma \dot{x}_i + \omega_i^2 x_i = \sum_{j} K_{ij} (x_j - x_i)`, says each node is a bell with its own note (ω), which quiets on its own at a rate (γ, the damping), and which is shaken by the bells it is joined to, more strongly the thicker the spring between them (K). The stability criterion, `\lambda_{\text{max}}(L) < \frac{\gamma}{2}`, says the whole network calms when its loudest shared shake (the largest eigenvalue of the coupling Laplacian L) is less than half the damping. The board shows this as one bar: **Strain** (λmax of L, computed exactly by the engine) against the **Calm line** (γ/2).
- **The per-node rule.** Each node's **load** is the sum of the K on its edges (its weighted degree, the diagonal of L). The largest eigenvalue of L is never more than twice the largest load, so if every node's load is at most γ/4, the network is guaranteed calm. A node whose load is at most γ/4 when the pulses fire is **damped** and its terrace district holds. A node above it **amplifies**. The player sees each node's load ring against its own small calm mark.
- **Pieces.**
  - Nodes: 6 (drill) or 12 (core), each showing ω from the player's reading (or ■ where redacted and not restored).
  - Edges: K values 1, 1.5, 2 or 3, drawn by thickness.
  - Emitters: 4 (drill) or `emitters_available` (core: 7, +1 if Joren is forgiven, −1 if the timing crystal failed).
  - Veylor's seeds (core only, hidden): one on N12 always, and one on each node N1–N11 whose reading is not verified.
- **Placing an emitter.** Choose a node and a firing slot from 1 to N (N = the number of emitters), giving phase φ = slot × 2π/N: the source's stagger "by \(2\pi/N\)".
  - Tuned (the node's shown ω is true, that is, no seed): every edge touching it × 0.5.
  - Mistuned (seeded): every edge touching it × 1.5. "Some nodes damp; others amplify."
  - Two emitters on the ends of one edge: if their slots differ by N/2 (opposite phase), the edge is cancelled to 0, provided both are tuned (destructive interference). If their slots are equal or adjacent, the edge × 1.5 (they reinforce). Timing is everything.
  - N12's edges are fixed: emitters do not change them. The deep core alters its own couplings.
- **Damping.** True γ = 8 (calm line 4; per-node mark 2). The board shows γ = 10 (calm line 5; per-node mark 2.5) unless `gamma_remeasured`, because the "apparent constant of environmental damping" was "a Continuum artifact" (source). A player who did not re-measure plans against a calm line that is too generous.
- **Veylor's turn (core only).** After each emitter, Veylor makes one hidden move: move one seed along an edge onto an adjacent unverified node, or add +0.5 K to one edge touching a seeded node. He picks the move that most raises the true load on nodes holding emitters (ties: lowest node number). A seed cannot enter a verified node: hand data resists him.
- **Companion actions (core only).** Mara, **Sing** (2 uses): reveal whether one node is seeded. Kade, **Scout** (1 use): preview Veylor's next move. Joren, **Retune** (1 use, if forgiven): change one placed emitter's slot.
- **Trigger.** After the last emitter, seeds are revealed, true values replace shown ones, loads are computed, nodes light green (damped) or red (amplified). Then the core fractures: N12's neighbour with the highest true load is forced to amplify. "A Continuum core fractures partially and collapses, but other cores shift and re‑synchronize" (source). Because N12's own load stays above the calm line, Strain never falls below it: the network is never fully calm. `terraces_held` = the number of damped nodes among N1 to N11, and so is never 11.
- **The choice.** Veylor's line comes at round 6 of the core board (the board dims; three lines; play resumes). After the last emitter, three buttons: **Trigger the pulses** (`core_choice = pulse`), **Stand down** (`withhold`), **Set the charges** (`fire`). The board shows the projected result, honest only as far as your data.
- **Layout.** Full page: the node map on a contour drawing of the Peaks, with the Strain bar along the top, the emitter tray and slot wheel on the right, companion action cards on the left, and Lira's equations in her hand along the bottom margin.
- **Inputs.** Select node, select slot, place; companion card; "Trigger" (and the two other buttons in the core).
- **Win and finish.** Drill: success if every one of the 6 nodes is damped (Strain under the calm line is reachable here, the game's one complete success). Core: no win; the result is a count.
- **Difficulty.**

| Appearance | Nodes | Emitters | Opponent | Seeds | Writes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `b4-board-terrace` | 6 (Hollin's network) | 4 | None | One on the drill node fed by N1 if N1 is unverified | `home_terrace_held`, N11 entry |
| `b4-board-core` | 12 | 6 to 8 | Veylor, 1 hidden move per round | 1 + one per unverified node | `core_choice`, `terraces_held` |

- **Expected outcomes.** Players with verified share around 40% hold 3 to 5 terraces; around 60%, 5 to 7; a careful run with the damping re-measured holds 8 to 10. Starting values; tune in playtest.
- **Hints.** The drill shows each node's load ring live. In the core, the projected Strain bar is shown in Lira's pencil, and nodes fed by relay readings are drawn with a dotted outline if the player has looked at the dossier.

## 12. Core systems

### Readings and the node dossier

Every measurement goes into the `readings` ledger with fields `node`, `book`, `instrument`, `method` (`hand`, `relay`, `cipher`), and `status` (`ok`, `error`, `forged`, `restored`, `doubted`, `misclassified`). The journal's back pages hold the **node dossier**: twelve tabs, N1 to N12, each showing the latest reading, its method and status.

| Node | Place | Taken in | Instrument | What makes the hand reading correct |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| N1 | Spore-Weaver valley | `b1-valley` | Photodiode array | Posts at least 300 m apart and the dial at 0.5 s (note 1 says so; a careful player can find it unprompted) |
| N2 | Aether-Vine ridge | `b1-climb` | Sighting compass | Five vine tips aligned; 3 s steady hold |
| N3 | Storm outpost | `b1-outpost` | Gravimeter | Bubble level while reading; between gusts (90 s cycle) |
| N4 | Fungal mats | `b2-lowlands` | Flux probe | Three depths within the 45 s dose clock |
| N5 | Lowland well | `b2-assay-2` | Assay | The Assay won |
| N6 | Harvester ridge | `b2-hearing` | Strain gauge | Clamp seated and read before the ridge fails at 40 s |
| N7, N8 | Peakborn ledges | `b3-cipher-2` | Song | Cipher locates each |
| N9, N10 | Midlander chant | `b3-cipher-3` | Chant | Cipher locates each; N10 needs the rest from note 5 |
| N11 | Hollin Terrace | `b4-board-terrace` | Drill | The drill succeeds |
| N12 | Peak 12 core | none | none | Never verifiable |

`nodes_verified` counts nodes whose latest entry is `hand` or `cipher` with status `ok` or `restored`. Audits, The Key III and acting on notes change it after the fact. `verified_share = round(nodes_verified / 12 × 100)`, frozen when The Key III closes. Thresholds: 8 nodes = 67%; 7 nodes = 58%. The maximum is 11 nodes = 92%.

### Hand versus relay

Every node site in 3D or 2D has a Continuum relay beacon (added). Pressing it gives the number instantly. Hand readings take 20 to 60 seconds of tactile play and sometimes a clock (storm, dose, failing ridge). Failing a hand reading by the clock leaves a fallback: take the relay, or log the incomplete reading as `error`. A relay reading lowers exposure by 3 (the Continuum sees you on its network and thinks you tame) and is never verified, because the Continuum injected false phase data years ago. The player is not told this until Veylor says it, but Lira's notes grow uneasy: from Book II on, she writes "relay" in a smaller hand.

The clandestine note of Book II ("You are looking at the wrong end of the needle. Look below." *(source)*) is not one of them. The source leaves its sender unsaid and the archivist's confession does not claim it, so it arrives loose and unsigned, in no hand the journal knows; it has no Circle or Reply prompt, and it points the coalition down.

For each note the journal offers: **Circle it** (Lira writes "Who wrote this?", as in the source; does not count as an answer), **Reply** (choose one of three replies in Lira's voice or type up to 80 characters; counts toward `margin_notes_answered`; exposure +5), or **Leave it**. Acting is separate: doing what the note says. Replies are sometimes answered in the next chapter, in the same small hand: after the 2nd reply, "I can't say who. Not yet."; after the 4th, "They read this book when you sleep. So do I."; after the 6th, "I am behind you. I am always behind you." This is the relationship the player builds with an unseen person.

## 13. Endings

The resolver runs once, when the Node Board II closes: every condition is final by then (`verified_share` was frozen at The Key III; `margin_notes_answered` cannot change after note 8 in `b4-descent`; `core_choice` is set on the board). The epilogue then plays the ending's own scenes. The epilogue writes only `pages_restored`, `names_protected` and `book_closed`, which no ending reads, so resolving at the game's end would give the same result.

Priorities are checked from 1. Every ending keeps a cost.

| Priority | Ending | As written | Plain words | `data/endings.json` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Directed Resilience** | No | You chose Stand down. | `{"is": "core_choice", "eq": "withhold"}` |
| 2 | **Fire** | No | You chose Set the charges. | `{"is": "core_choice", "eq": "fire"}` |
| 3 | **The Archivist in Time** | No | You fired the pulses, answered at least 6 of the 8 margin notes, and at least 60% of your node data was verified by hand (8 nodes). | `{"all": [{"is": "core_choice", "eq": "pulse"}, {"is": "margin_notes_answered", "gte": 6}, {"is": "verified_share", "gte": 60}]}` |
| 4 | **The Redacted Testament** | **Yes** | Everything else: you fired the pulses on partly corrupted data, or answered the hand too rarely for it to find you. | `true` |

### The Redacted Testament (as written)

- **Final scenes.** `b4-collapse`: the pulses fire; green and red nodes; the core fractures; Veylor walks deeper into the mountain; tunnels shear; Lira is cornered at a sealed exit; the sound "like a thousand small fractures"; the camera stays on her lamp as it goes out. `ep-tunnel`: the follower arrives at the sealed exit and finds the book. `ep-vault`, `ep-audit-3`, `end-written`. Nothing hints that she is held alive: at the desk the archivist's three admissions are read verbatim, ending "She paid with her life. The Continuum took her into the mountain."
- **Variation.** `terraces_held` is spoken in the epilogue as a count of terraces still standing. If the player answered 6 or more notes but verified less than 60%, the follower's first line in `ep-tunnel` is "You told me where. I came. The shaft was already gone." This is the source's "the one who arrived too late", earned.
- **Last image.** The archivist's desk. The final warning is read in the archivist's hand, verbatim: "If you are reading this, you are a target." … "Close the book if you want. Or keep going. Either way — they will come." Two prompts: Close the book / Keep going. A knock.

### The Archivist in Time

- **Final scenes.** `b4-collapse`: the pulses fire; the north shaft's nodes hold long enough; a lamp ahead in the dust that is not hers. `ep-tunnel`: as the archivist, the player reaches Lira at the north shaft and pulls her through (a 20-second carry in 3D). `ep-vault`: the books still have to be taken back. `ep-audit-3`. `end-in-time`: a safe room; Lira writes her name on the cover.
- **Cost.** The Continuum hunts both; Veylor lives; terraces still fall (all those not held). The last line is the archivist's: "You are one of us now." *(source)*, said to Lira, not the reader.

### Directed Resilience

- **Final scenes.** `b4-collapse` plays without a collapse: the Second Genesis runs; the Peaks shudder into a new shape. `ep-tunnel`: the follower walks an intact tunnel. `ep-vault`: Lira's journals are shelved under the Continuum seal as a catalogue. `ep-audit-3`: the archivist restores what the Continuum's edition left out. `end-resilience`: Lira, alive, at a sterile bench, cataloguing the reset world; her name on the cover under the seal.
- **Cost.** The terraces, Hollin among them, are gone in the reset. Joren receives his restored terrace and cannot live in it.
- **Last image.** Lira's hand writing a baseline for a species that did not exist yesterday.

### Fire

- **Final scenes.** `b4-collapse`: Kade's charges; the core destroyed; the mountain drops a tier; the Continuum falls. `ep-tunnel`: rubble. `ep-vault`: the vault is open to the sky. `ep-audit-3`. `end-fire`: the archivist digs the books out of the rubble of the terraces.
- **Cost.** "Phase offsets, not fire" was her plea; the Mid-Slope terraces fall. If Joren was forgiven, he dies keeping the water lifts running during the evacuation; one line, off-screen.
- **Last image.** Silent water lifts; the river still glowing.

### How a player reaches each one on purpose

- **The Redacted Testament.** Play the story's path: fire the pulses. Either do not answer the margin notes (circle them, as Lira does), or let the relay and forgeries take your data.
- **The Archivist in Time.** Reply to at least 6 of the 8 notes, take at least 8 node readings by hand or cipher, catch the forgeries without striking your own lines, then fire.
- **Directed Resilience.** At the end of the core board, choose Stand down.
- **Fire.** At the end of the core board, choose Set the charges.

A designer reading a run's state can always say why: the debug panel shows `core_choice`, `margin_notes_answered`, `verified_share`, and the dossier.

## 15. Art direction

- **Mood.** A field naturalist's journal brought into a vertical, luminous, wounded world. Wonder in Books I and III; clinical cold in Book II; paranoia expressed through the page, not through monsters.
- **Palette by band.**

| Band | Colours |
| --- | --- |
| Lowlands | Rust, ochre, bleached bone, crystal white |
| Mid-Slopes | Deep moss green, slate, river cyan glow |
| High Peaks | Violet dusk, ice blue, bioluminescent teal |
| Continuum | Matte black, cold white, seal red |
| The journal | Warm paper, iron-gall ink brown-black, pencil grey; the archivist's ink faintly blue; the Continuum's ink a flat black |

- **Materials.** Crystalline bark (translucent facets with subsurface glow); fungal mats (velvety, slightly emissive); web (thin glowing strands with travelling pulses); Continuum surfaces (matte, seamless).
- **Lighting.** Natural light shifts by band; aetheric sources are cool emissives; the core is white-violet. Night scenes rely on bioluminescence.
- **One look across layers.** 3D materials use a light ink outline pass so the world reads as an illustration; 2D sprites are drawn in the same ink weight; 1D pages use the same paper and ink. References by mood only: naturalist field plates, survey cross-sections, redacted state documents.
- **Colour-blind safety.** Green and red node states on the board also differ by shape (ring versus starburst) and pattern.

## 20. Risks and open questions

| Risk | Mitigation |
| --- | --- |
| Measuring feels like chores | Every hand reading is 20 to 60 s, tactile, has a sound and a live journal plot; no reading is repeated for its own sake; the relay is always there for players who want speed |
| The Node Board is too mathematical | The rule is shown as bells and springs, one bar and one ring per node; the drill teaches with full information; the equations are there for those who want them |
| Players never learn the relay is poisoned until too late | Lira's notes grow uneasy from Book II; Audit II forgeries cluster on relay nodes; the drill shows a mistuned emitter on a relay node |
| Exposure punishes answering the archivist too hard | Tune +5 per reply in playtest; relay credit and note 3 give counterweights |
| 176 minutes overruns | Each chapter has a hard ceiling in playtest; the climbs can lose a camp each |
| Dialogue creep | Two lines per companion per scene, enforced in the script tool |

**Answered** (by Claude at the author's request, 2026-11-12, from the source text; the author can overrule any of them).

1. **The city's archivist and the hand in the margins are one person, and Book III does not say so.** The source calls the margin hand's owner "the archivist" from the first note on: page 47 is "the first fingerprint of the person who will later call themselves the archivist", and the throughline names "An unknown follower, later revealed as the archivist". Book III meets "an archivist who speaks in half‑truths and riddles" in the city, and two paragraphs later the annotations on Lira's page are "the archivist’s hand". Book III's last paragraph still calls the follower "someone who is following, not yet revealed", so Lira does not know whom she met. The design keeps the one hooded figure; the only tell is that the coordinate card (note 6) is in the margin hand, which a player can match as Lira matches the footnote in Book II. The reveal stays where the source puts it, in the epilogue ("VII. The Archivist Revealed"). Changed: the `b3-city` step, the note table and the characters table say this; section 21 lists the reading.
2. **In the author's ending Lira is dead, and she may live in The Archivist in Time.** "She is taken" (Book IV) is how she dies, not a captivity: the archivist says "She paid with her life. The Continuum took her into the mountain.", the final warning speaks of "Lira’s sacrifice", and the summary says "knowledge survives, but its author does not". The Redacted Testament no longer keeps captivity open: her lamp goes out, and the archivist's three admissions are read verbatim at the desk. The Archivist in Time is the one change the source itself names, that the follower "arrived too late"; the player earns the other timing, and the ending keeps its cost (Veylor lives, both are hunted, every terrace not held falls), so it is not a tidy rescue. Changed: section 3, the arcs table, section 13 and `data/endings.json` (the as-written summary).
3. **The needle note is not the archivist's.** The source says only "A clandestine note arrives", and the archivist's confession lists "the tiny correction on page 47, the misfiled footnote, the misnumbered page", not this note. Attributing it would add to the author's mystery, so the game cuts the attribution: the note arrives loose and unsigned, in no hand the journal knows, cannot be answered, and still points the coalition down. There are now eight margin notes, renumbered (the old 6 to 9 are now 5 to 8). The Archivist in Time asks for 6 of the 8 replies (75%, against the old 7 of 9, 78%), and a player who answers every note and publishes everything reaches exposure about 80 instead of 85. Changed: Book II to IV steps, the note table, sections 11, 12, 13, 17 and 21, `data/endings.json`, `data/flags.json` (`margin_notes`, `margin_notes_answered`, `margin_notes_acted`) and `data/scenes.json` (`b2-cipher-1` no longer writes the margin flags).
