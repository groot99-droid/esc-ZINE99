# The Lord of the Sea: deep dive

Everything marked NEW is a proposal for the author to approve or strike (section 6). The source is
`02_Novels/02_WrymWretch.md`, lines 270–357, quoted verbatim and never changed. It has no ending,
so every ending here is a proposal.

## 1. Where it stands

**What works.** Stir is a real verb: one gesture drives a cup, a tide arrow, a wave and a region.
Alexi's body is the joke and the meter at once (lurching ashore, exact at the tideline). A tideline
stone stays locked until its person has been met in calm water, so the toll lands on someone the
player knows. "A tie, in my books, is mine" is a rule and a character in seven words.

**What is weak.**

1. **Alexi has nothing to answer for.** His want is negative (the sea "left wild") and his arc is a
   posture. Nothing in his past is at stake, so the story has no secret and he cannot be wrong
   about himself.
2. **The opposing force is absent for 153 minutes.** The Lord of Tea is quoted as "hungry" and then
   met once, at a board. His order works, but he never argues for it, so the player fights paperwork.
3. **The middle is one spine run four times.** Arrive, meet, play, stone, leave. Between minute 77
   and minute 122 nothing reverses; only meters move. The halls then arrive as one more region.
4. **The princess is a hint system.** She reads paper and holds a pen. The source gives her a
   stronger position than the design uses: "The princess watched him, waiting." (353). She is
   judging him and the game never asks what the verdict is for.
5. **Modes are one trick each and the layers meet only as numbers.** Each web module is a single
   sequence; later appearances scale counts (two cutters, more pages). What a layer hands the next
   is a meter, almost never a thing.
6. **The best material on the page is unused**: the bottles, the tea jars in the cabin of a tea
   company's enemy (276), the "future scholars" (314). And the four reference stills read as
   live-action film frames, not as this game.

## 2. The story, developed

**Dramatic question.** Can Alexi pay what the sea owes without owning it again or selling what is
left of it?

**Want and need.** He wants a quiet morning and the sea left wild. He needs to answer for it: for
its dead, and for a debt he signed himself.

### The secret: the tab (NEW)

The seed: "a clattering, off‑key xylophone of glass and
regret" (274), a cabin of "jars of spices and teas collected like talismans from far‑off markets"
(276), and "A pull. A promise he couldn’t name." (286), all in `02_Novels/02_WrymWretch.md`.

For as long as there have been ship-chandlers, Alexi has drunk on credit. Each chit is signed with a
clockwise spiral and pledges something small: a fog, a slack water, the luck of the dice, "one pair
land legs, hardly used". Chandlers sell their paper. Over forty years one buyer
collected all of it. The Company's lights, lanes and calms are built on Alexi's
pledges, lawfully bought. The king is in debt because his
house stood surety for an old shipmate. "All waters adjoining" is a foreclosure, and the princess
came to the beach with the writ in her sleeve.

Nothing here is remembered: the chits are paper, found and read, so COLLECTION's rule holds.

### The opposing force

**The Lord of Tea** wants one year in which no name is entered in the Wreck Book. He began as the
quay boy who chalked the lost lists. He thinks he is the first lord the sea has had
who pays its bills. His account is headed "The Sea, Dr." and it is overdrawn. What makes him wrong is "every soul that sails" (336): to close
the book he must own everyone who could appear in it.

### Endings (all proposals)

- **The Wild Sea.** He repudiates the tab. The surety is called, and she goes ashore to face it.
  The toll is sung by name.
- **The Lord's Peace.** He takes the salary. The last bottle is Company issue. The Wreck Book
  closes, and so does every hymn.
- **No Lord** (still the default). The debtor ceases to exist, so the debt dies. So does anyone who
  could answer for the drowned.
- **The Charter.** The tab becomes a schedule of the terms. The toll is read as the reason for the
  lanes.
- **Paid in Kind** (NEW). After a win, with all eight chits matched, he unsigns them: eight
  counter-clockwise stirs at the tideline, and each one takes a power tier off the rose ring for
  good. The Company's claim on the sea is gone; what it built and pays for stays. He is a god who
  owes nothing and can raise a ripple. Cost: he can never dive for anyone again, the wild regions
  stay as wild as the player left them, and the toll is entered as "outstanding, for ever".

## 3. Obstacles and puzzles

Every element below is NEW unless marked.

### The Engraved Sea (2D)

- **Teach:** hide in a convoy's lee with only a ripple (as designed).
- **Test:** chases where the sea answers back.
- **Twist:** *still* the water for a whole run.
- **Returning swell.** Every crest you raise comes back off the coast 20 seconds later, from ahead,
  at half height.
- **Oil.** A cutter pours lamp oil; that patch cannot be stirred for 10 seconds.
- **Bell time.** A crest under a buoy rings it off the beat and every cutter turns toward the bell:
  a decoy, once per buoy.
- **Set-piece, The Glass** (`e-blockade-run`): five minutes counter-clockwise. You iron the sea
  flat and ghost through a sleeping fleet, while the king's oarsmen hum the hymn under their breath
  and every bar raises a ripple you must put down.

### The Deep (2D)

- **Teach:** swim, kick, carry (first dive).
- **Test:** the trench, where light is a cost.
- **Twist:** rescue. Alexi has no air meter; the person in his arms does.
- **Glow debt.** Below 60 m a stir lights 8 m around you for 3 seconds, and dredge chains swing
  toward light.
- **Wreck bells.** Push a current through a sunk ship's bell: its ring draws the hull's interior
  for 3 seconds, and gives you the ship's name.
- **The sounding lead.** A Company survey lead drops on a 6-second beat. Touch it: notice +5, and a
  net follows it down.
- **Set-piece, The Standing Fleet** (`d-pearl-dive`): at 140 m, every ship the old sea took stands
  upright on the floor in rows, the sea's side of the ledger. Here the pull stops. You swim the
  lanes between hulls, ringing bells to read names; the compact stone is the flagship's ballast.

### Liar's Tide (1D)

- **Teach:** Wenna's tell.
- **Test:** the Gullwife bluffers and suspicion.
- **Twist:** the table itself is in play.
- **The loaded die.** One opponent holds a Company-issue die weighted to Lantern. The chalk bid
  ledger is the evidence; call it out correctly and that die is yours for the game.
- **Double or drown.** Once a game a loser may double the stake instead of losing a die. The
  tallyman does it with the licence; the player may do it with a chit.
- **Set-piece, The Flooding Table** (`g-liars-salt`): played on the reef at the turn of the tide.
  Each round the water climbs and takes one die from the lowest bidder's cup. Stirring holds it
  back for a round, in full view of everyone.

### The Charter (1D)

- **Teach:** follow the red thread.
- **Test:** decoys and defects.
- **Twist:** the answer is not in the document.
- **Sea-swollen pages.** Stir one drop of seawater onto a page to raise struck-out ink. Each wetting
  blurs one live clause for good.
- **The green tick.** The Lord of Tea initials every clause he has balanced. The binding clause is
  always one he could not tick.
- **Set-piece, Schedule C** (`g-charter-tidewater`): the red thread runs to the page edge, leaves
  the slope, crosses the cabin and stops at the bottle that has hung on the wall since minute one.

### The Tea Board (1D)

- **Teach:** majority and the tide clock.
- **Test:** Debt.
- **Twist:** the board can be damaged.
- **Cracked lanes.** A second Stir in one High tide cracks the porcelain: that lane scores for no
  one, ever, and cannot be laid as a safe lane on the chart.
- **Insure.** The Company may fix a Chest so it cannot be stirred off, at the price of one reserve
  piece.
- **Named Debt.** In the final match each Debt is a name from the player's own toll.
- **Set-piece, the last four rounds:** the Lord of Tea opens the Wreck Book and plays each Debt by
  reading it. "Corran. Lamp-keeper. A light went out." Click.

### Dead Reckoning (1D)

- **Teach:** one leg, steady tide.
- **Test:** a tide that reverses.
- **Twist:** plot backwards.
- **Lying tables.** Company tide tables are exact only in Ordered water. In Wild water each vector
  carries a cone of error; a crew singing in sight narrows it.
- **Soundings.** Once a leg, drop the lead: the depth gives one position line.
- **Set-piece, the back-plot** (at each Tide Reckoning): from where a boat was found and the hours
  since, plot back to where it went over. A good plot starts the rescue dive above the person.

### Across the game

**Three links between layers.**

1. **A crew is a verb.** Each crew won at dice gives its own call in 2D: Gullwife, one tier up;
   Kettle, the reef stands covered; lamp-boys, fog; divers, 20 seconds more air for a rescued
   person; Wenna's crew, the ferry shown on the coast strip so crests can be timed around it.
2. **Names answer debts.** Each ship named by its bell in the Deep cancels one Named Debt in the
   final match.
3. **A charter's fate redraws its plate.** Struck: that region's Engraved Sea is cut in lamp-black,
   lit only by the foam you raise, and its Deep is hung with abandoned nets. Rewritten: lights stay,
   and their beams mark the surface in rescue dives.

**The long puzzle: the tab.** Eight chits, one per region, each found a different way (the bottle
neck, a dice stake, the strongbox, a lease schedule, the trench, under the Comptroller's board, the
mirror gallery, the Lord's own hand). At the tally the player pins each
chit to the region whose order it bought: the fog bought the Strait's lights, the luck of the dice
bought the Company's insurance. All eight correct opens Pay.

**A second playthrough.** Show the princess the bottle among the cabin's 14 objects in chapter 1
and she hands over the writ at once: an open-book run. The "future scholars" (314) footnote a
second run's pages, disagreeing about the ending the player got last time.

## 4. Art plan

**Style bible.**

1. **Palette.** Order: ink-blue `#1F2E4A`, ledger red `#8C2B2B`, tea brown `#6B4A2B`, lamp white
   `#F4F1E8`. Wild: sea-green `#2F6B5E`, foam `#E8F2EE`, storm violet `#4B3F63`, dawn gold
   `#E7A93B`. The Voice and every spiral: sea-ink `#3C7A6A`.
2. **Line.** One burin line everywhere. Ruled hatching means Order, wave hatching means Wild.
3. **Texture.** Cream laid paper, plate marks; salt bloom on anything that has been wet.
4. **Lighting.** Dawn gold from low left for Alexi; lamp-white from above for the Company.
5. **Never shown.** A body. A trident, crown or halo. The Voice's face. The name of the god.

## 5. Roadmap

**Phase 1: the tab, on paper (smallest work, largest change).** Eight chits; Schedule C inside the
existing harbour-book Charter; The Slate, with Mother Halse at the dice table; the countersigned
Tide Reckoning; the matching step at the tally. No new mode; mostly text and SVG.

**Phase 2: the modes get their ladders.** Returning swell, oil and The Glass; glow debt, wreck
bells and The Standing Fleet; the Flooding Table; cracked lanes and Named Debt; lying tables and the
back-plot. Then the three links.

**Phase 3: the people and the end.** The Second Collision, The Inventory, The Oars, The Cup; Paid in
Kind in `data/endings.json` with `python tools/validate.py lord-of-the-sea`; portraits, ending
cards, the second-run footnotes.

**The author decides before work starts:**

- whether Alexi signed the sea away himself (it makes him complicit, and comic);
- whether the king stood surety and the princess withheld the writ;
- the Lord of Tea's history and his seasickness;
- a fifth ending, and whether No Lord stays the default beside it;
- whether "future scholars" may annotate a second run.

Unchanged: the narrator is never named or revealed, and nothing is remembered.
