# The Lord of the Sea: design

## 1. Summary

A god of the sea, long past his age, wakes hungover on an old boat in a century when a trading
company has made the ocean safe, ordered and owned. You sail an open sea chart region by region,
stirring each one back toward the wild that makes Alexi strong, and every turn of the hand that
brings back his hymns also brings back wrecks to people who were safe under the Company's peace.

| Vault path | `02_Novels/02_WrymWretch.md`, lines 270–357 (the rest of the file belongs to the WrymWretch game and is not used) |
| Companion note | `_Annotations/02_Novels/02_WrymWretch.md` |
| Author's premise (given to the coordinator, not in the vault) | "The Lord of the Sea, it follows a weakened Poseidon, long after the age of Greek gods, into the age of maritime peace and order due to the East India Company." |
| Target length | 160–180 minutes; this design runs 174 |
| Players | 1 |
| Core verb | **Stir** |
| Camera in 3D | Third person, at sea |
| 2D modes | The Engraved Sea (storms and chases, side-on); The Deep (dives, side-on) |
| 1D games | Liar's Tide (dice, bluffing); The Charter (document hunt); The Tea Board (trade-route board game); Dead Reckoning (navigation) |
| Endings | Four, all proposals. The author has written two chapters and no ending, so none is marked as written. No Lord is the proposed default: the ending the chapters point toward (section 13) |

The chapters never say "Poseidon". The game never says it either: the god is Alexi, and what he
was is shown by what the sea does around him. The Company is fictional, the Great British Tea
Company, modelled on the East India Company and named only as the chapters name it.

## 2. Why this game

**Stir** is both the verb and the input. Alexi draws a circle with the stick or the mouse: clockwise
rouses the water, counter-clockwise stills it. He stirs a cup of dice, a tide vector on a chart, a
wave under a cutter, a current in the deep, and a whole region at a tideline stone. Every stir moves
a region along one line, from Order to Wild, and that line is the game.

| | The Lord of the Sea | WrymWretch (same source file) | People of the Bark (the other god game) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Where power comes from | The sea's wildness and sailors' devotion: political and social | Memory restored by recording and retelling | The player is never a god; the people mistake a squirrel and a hand for gods |
| The repeated decision | How hard to stir each region, knowing who lives under its calm | Which memories to restore and spend | How to interpret an omen into policy |
| Structure | Open sea chart, any order within a chapter | Six prologues converging, then a party act | Four seasons, two scales |
| Tone | Comic on the surface, mythic underneath; swagger returning | Solemn epic, awe and kinship | Comic and mythic, affection for tiny people |
| Endings decided by | The Order and Wild map plus the final Tea Board match | A chosen pair and how much the world remembers | Balance of three institutions |
| Memory | Deliberately not a mechanic. Nothing Alexi does is "remembered back" | The economy | Not central |

Against the other open-world or travel games: Heartstream is one river, rowed together, once.
Endless Temple is a hub of book-worlds. This is the only open chart in the collection, and the only
game where the player's power and the player's damage are the same number.

### What the game keeps

- Chapters 0 and 1 of the game are these two chapters, in order, with no added event between beat 1
  and beat 18. Added play (learning to stir, the comic run as a control scheme) happens inside them.
- The source's own word for the moment is "stirred" (lines 286 and 351). The core verb comes from it.
- "Restless devotion" (276) and "drunken hymns to the tide" (346) are the two seeds of the devotion
  system. The game reads devotion as what sailors give the sea, not as anything Alexi recalls.
- "A man on the edge of remembering something important" (298) is quoted on screen once, at the
  tideline, and left open. COLLECTION.md rules that this game is not about memory, so remembering
  is never a mechanic: Alexi grows by what the sea and its sailors do, not by what comes back to him.

### The author's ending

None. The text stops at line 357. All four endings in section 13 are proposals and none is marked
as written.

## 4. Investment

### The hook in the first 5 minutes

1. **0:00.** Black screen, then sound: bottles rolling on boards, chiming off-key with each swell.
   The first prompt is the core input: "Stir". The player circles the stick, the swell under the
   boat answers, and the bottles roll into a short, wrong, funny tune. The player has played the
   sea before they have seen it.
2. **0:45.** Alexi sits up with the bottle still in his hand. The cabin is his life on the walls:
   the player can look at any of 14 objects (sketches, foreign poems, maps, jars of spice and tea).
   Each gets one line of caption in the comic voice.
3. **3:00.** Deck at dawn, sea "a pane of molten gold". The dinghy. Rowing is a two-button rhythm
   that is easy, because his arms know it.
4. **4:30.** He beaches the dinghy and steps into the tideline. The controls, sluggish on the sand, go precise. The
   player stirs out of habit, and the water winds itself round his ankles in a slow spiral and does
   not stop when the stick does. The first line of the unnamed narrator appears.

The question the hook plants: why does the sea move when this drunk man turns his hand, and who is
the "I" walking beside him?

### What the player comes to care about, and how, before it is threatened

| What | How the game makes the player care, in play | When it is first threatened |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Alexi's swagger | His animations, gait and camera framing grow with devotion: hunched and stumbling at 0, upright at 45, standing on the bowsprit in a storm at 75. The player watches him straighten because of things they did. | Chapter 3 onward: letting a region fall to Order silences its crew and his posture sags back a step. |
| The boat and its walls | Every region adds an object to the cabin walls (a crew's song sheet, a lamp lens, a pearl, a ledger page). The cabin is the save point and the chart table. By chapter 4 the walls are the player's own record of the run. | Chapter 5: Company clerks board and inventory the cabin as an asset of the king's estate. |
| The princess | She is useful from her first scene: she reads Company paper Alexi cannot ("the letters swim ashore"). She has opinions on every region and is right about half of them. She never asks to be rescued. | Chapter 5, in her father's halls, where her trust decides whether she will hold the pen at the end. |
| The people of the peace | In each peopled region the player meets one person whose life the calm made possible, and does one small thing with them in calm water before the region's tideline stone can be used: crosses the harbour with Wenna's ferry and the children she counts aloud; trims the lamp with Corran; dives the shallow bank with Ide. | The first Tide Reckoning, end of chapter 3, when a region the player made wild is checked for wrecks. |

The rule that enforces this: **a region's tideline stone is locked until its person has been met**,
and the meeting is always in calm water.

### The question that pulls them to the end

"Can a sea that cannot be owned be ruled by a god either?" In play it becomes a sum the player can
see on the chart table at all times: how much of the sea is wild, who has paid for it, and whether
that is enough to beat the Lord of Tea at his own board.

## 6. Characters

Dialogue is sparse throughout, as in the source. No scene runs on dialogue; most lines are one
burst, then play.

### Alexi

- **Want:** a quiet morning, and then, once warned, the sea left wild.
- **Fear:** the ocean as "a factory, a fleet, a machine" (verbatim, 347).
- **Voice:** few words, slurred ashore, plain at sea. Verbatim: "Tea has a lord." / "Yer Hine‑ness."
  Added: "It used to come when I called." / "Hold on to something. Not me."
- **Arc by ending:** *The Wild Sea*: god again, standing in the storm, with the hymns and the
  wrecks. *The Lord's Peace*: a man on an old boat in a calm the tide no longer breaks. *No Lord*:
  a mortal sailor who chose it, rowing in at dawn. *The Charter*: a god under terms, keeping some
  water calm by his own hand.

### The princess (not named, as in the source; see section 20, answer 2)

- **Want:** her father's halls back, and her people not handed to the Company as stock.
- **Fear:** a life that is "normal" in the Company's sense: owned, ledgered, quiet.
- **Voice:** sharp, quick, dry. Verbatim: "Alexi, I bring a warning." / "Everything has a lord."
  Added: "Read it again. Slower. No, I will." / "Calm water is not the same as safe people."
- **Role in play:** reads Company paper, gives hints in The Charter and The Tea Board when her
  trust is 40 or more, and holds the pen for the Sign action when it is 60 or more.
- **Arc by ending:** *The Charter*: co-signs the terms. *No Lord*: takes the halls back and the
  sea is no one's. *The Wild Sea*: leaves the boat at the halls, unsure she helped. *The Lord's
  Peace*: becomes a Company ward with a pension and a view.

### The king, her father (added beyond the source's mention; his kingdom is not named)

- **Want:** to be out of debt. **Fear:** that his daughter will see how he got into it.
- **Voice (added):** "I signed what they put in front of me. They always put something in front of me."
- **Arc:** redeemed if the halls' charter is broken or rewritten; a tenant in his own house if not.

### The Lord of Tea

- **Want:** "every ship, every vessel, every soul that sails" (verbatim). **Fear:** a column that
  will not balance.
- **Voice (added):** courteous and arithmetical. "I do not sink ships. I own them." / "A tie, in my
  books, is mine."
- Seen only in chapter 6. Before that he is present as paper, lights and patrols.
- **Arc:** loses the sea (*Wild Sea*, *No Lord*), keeps it (*Lord's Peace*), or signs (*Charter*).

### The Comptroller (added)

The Lord's man at the Spice Mouth and the halls. Plays the second Tea Board match. Precise, tired,
honest by his own rules. "The dock was paid on time. Ask them if they want it back the old way."

### Wenna (added), ferry pilot at Tidewater

Counts the harbour-school children aloud at the gangplank every crossing, always to the same
number, and starts again if she loses count. Plays Liar's Tide cautiously. Her ferry crew will sing
only if Tidewater's Wild reaches 40. If Tidewater is made wild she is the person a drowning there
would take; the game never shows that without first showing her counting.

### Old Marrow and the Kettle crew (added), the Salt Steps

A crew who still sing to the tide, badly and every night. Marrow's grandchildren fish the reef,
which is only safe because the Company's patrols keep the reef pilots honest.

### Corran (added), lamp-keeper of the Lamplit Strait

A wrecker's son who keeps the lights that ended wrecking. Trims the wick at the same minute every
evening. Proud of the light and ashamed of the songs he still knows.

### Ide (added), pearl diver of the Pearl Banks

Dives the shallow bank for a Company wage. Knows the trench beyond it, and will not go near it.

### The Gullwife crew (added)

Drunks and poachers who fish the Unwritten. The first crew that sings to Alexi's water. Loud, kind,
untrustworthy at dice.

### The tallyman (added)

The Company's man on the Salt Steps. Gambles. Stakes the island's fishing licence at Liar's Tide
because he cannot imagine losing it.

### The factor (added)

Teaches Alexi the Tea Board in the Tidewater tavern, as a courtesy to a drunk.

### The narrator: "I—who am him and not him"

The source gives one line in the narrator's own voice (357) and one slip (304: "My toes—his
toes"). The design reads the narrator as the god inside the man: the "something older than memory,
older than the man he had become" that stirs at line 351, one sentence before the "I" speaks. It
is never named and no ending reveals it (section 20, answer 1). Section 12, "The Voice Beside",
gives the rules for its lines.

## 8. Levels

Each level lists its scenes in order with minutes. Scene ids match `data/scenes.json`.

### Chapter 0: The Shore Between Worlds (`ch0-shore`, 14 minutes)

- **Goal:** get from the cabin to the tideline.
- **Where:** the boat at anchor in Home Reach; the beach.
- **Scenes:** `s-cabin-dawn` (3D, 3), `s-deck-dinghy` (3D, 2), `s-beach-tideline` (3D, 9).
  The cabin can be left after the first stir; the 14 objects stay on the walls for later. The
  tideline scene holds the longer play: a free stir by the water (lifting the beached dinghy,
  turning a stranded jellyfish back to sea, drawing the spiral wider) that teaches the gesture and
  its tiers before anything depends on them.
- **Encounters:** no threat. The cabin is a looking game: 14 objects, each with a one-line caption.
  The deck has the dinghy. Rowing is alternate left and right triggers, forgiving. On the beach,
  ashore controls are heavy (section 12); at the tideline they turn precise.
- **What the player learns:** the stir gesture; that the walls record a life; that the sea answers.
- **Flags set:** `tideline_answered` (true when the player stirs at the tideline).
- **How it ends:** the spiral at his ankles; the narrator's first line on screen; "And the day,
  still young and golden, waited to see what he would do next." (300). Cut to chapter card.

### Chapter 1: The Princess on the Shore (`ch1-princess`, 16 minutes)

- **Goal:** hear the warning; read the deed.
- **Scenes:** `s-beach-princess` (3D, 6), `g-charter-deed` (1D, 6), `s-cabin-princess` (3D, 4).
- **Encounters:** "Alexi!" The player must run to the voice. Ashore the run lurches and zigzags and
  cannot stop (section 12): the collision is guaranteed and is the joke. Then the bow, which is a
  short input that always fails ("a shape the gods would find amusing"). The warning plays as
  verbatim lines with no added dialogue.
- **The deed:** she hands over the sale deed of the halls. The Charter tutorial (section 11): find
  the clause through which the sale reaches the sea. It is "and all waters adjoining", buried in a
  schedule. Nothing can be struck yet.
- **The cabin:** she comes aboard and pins the deed to the wall. She picks up three objects and
  comments on each. The player can show her any of the 14; each choice is cosmetic except that
  showing her the chart sets `princess.trust` +5 (she asks to be taught it).
- **Flags set:** `princess_aboard`, `deed_read`; `princess.trust` from 30 up to 45.
- **How it ends:** the narrator's second line slot; the boat weighs anchor.

### Chapter 2: The Ordered Sea (`ch2-ordered-sea`, 24 minutes)

- **Goal:** find where the old sea is left.
- **Scenes:** `s-company-lane` (3D, 5), `e-cutter-heave-to` (2D Engraved Sea, 4), `s-tidewater`
  (3D, 6), `g-liars-tidewater` (1D Liar's Tide, 5), `g-tea-parlour` (1D Tea Board, 4).
- **The lane:** buoys at fixed intervals, licensed ships, one flag on every mast. Alexi tries to
  raise a wave and gets a ripple (Wild 10: below every tier). The comic beat is the size of the
  ripple against the size of his gesture. The princess counts the buoys.
- **The cutter:** a revenue cutter signals heave-to. There is no power to fight it. The player
  slips into the lee of a convoy and hides in its wake (section 10). Caught: a fine, `notice` +10,

## 10. 2D modes

Both modes use the engine's orthographic layer with `scroll` movement. The world scrolls right as
the player makes headway.

### The Engraved Sea (`engraved`, scroll)

**Look.** The sea is a copperplate engraving come alive: wave lines cross-hatched, ships drawn in
line, a compass rose and a scale bar in the margins. The top margin is a chart strip with the
course inked and the boat's position as a pin.

**Controls.**

| Input | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Left stick X / A–D | Sail trim: spill wind (slow) to full (fast) |
| Left stick Y / W–S | Helm: climb the wave face or run the trough |
| Right stick circle / hold right mouse and circle | Stir. Clockwise raises a crest at the reticle; counter-clockwise flattens it |
| Right stick aim / mouse | Place the stir reticle, up to 40 m ahead or behind |

**Rules and physics.**

- The surface is a sum of three to five travelling waves. Stirring adds or removes one wave of
  height 0.5 m (Ripple) up to 6 m (Storm) at the reticle, set by the effective power tier in that
  region (section 12). A stir needs one full circle for Ripple and one more per tier.
- Boat speed is wind × trim, plus a surf bonus of up to +60% when running down a wave face.
- Capsize: broadside to a face steeper than 35° for 1.5 seconds. Respawn at the last checkpoint
  buoy (every 30 to 45 seconds of travel). No other penalty.
- **The coast strip.** Below the chart strip, a thin band shows the nearest coast and its people in
  silhouette. Every wave Alexi raises rolls on toward it, and the player sees boats lift at moorings
  there. It costs nothing in the sequence; it is shown because the Tide Reckoning will count it.

**Hazards.** Rocks (engraved, fixed); revenue cutters (pursue from behind; grapnel range 25 m; a
grapnel held 4 seconds ends the chase as caught); lighthouse beams (raise `notice` +2 per second in
the beam); waterspouts from the player's own storms (capsize on contact); floating wreckage.

**Progress.** The pin on the chart strip; distance in nautical miles; the target port inked at the
right edge.

**Failure and retry.** Capsize: checkpoint. Caught by a cutter: the sequence ends as "caught", with
`notice` +10 and, in the blockade, the payment delayed (section 12). Never a game over.

| Sequence | Chapter | Length | Power available (typical) | Twist |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `e-cutter-heave-to` | 2 | 4 min | Ripple | No fight possible: stay in the convoy's lee, out of the cutter's sight line, for 1.2 nm |
| `e-first-squall` | 3 | 5 min | Swell, Current (song surge) | The crew's song is a visible bar; stirring in time with its beat gives +1 circle free |
| `e-salt-reef` | 4 | 2 min | Swell to Squall | Raise the tide over the reef so the Kettle boats pass; the coast strip shows the grandchildren's skiffs lifted with them |
| `e-spice-chase` | 4 | 2 min | Region-dependent | Two cutters if `notice` ≥ 50, one otherwise |
| `e-blockade-run` | 5 | 5 min | Region-dependent | Patrol sweeps follow the timetable plotted in Dead Reckoning; arriving off-plan adds one sweep |
| `e-storm-approach` | 6 | 4 min | Weak (calm Company lane) | Convoy cover again, but now with the player's own storm behind them, from the regions they made wild |

### The Deep (`deep`, scroll)

**Look.** The same engraved line, inverted: white lines on deep blue-black, getting sparser with
depth. Four light bands: 0–20 m (bright), 20–60 m (blue), 60–150 m (dark), below 150 m (only what
glows or what Alexi's stir stirs up).

**Controls.** Left stick: swim in any direction with momentum (top speed 3 m/s, turn in 0.3 s).
Tap A: kick (burst to 6 m/s for 1 s, 2 s cooldown). Right stick circle: stir a current (a 10 m
ribbon in the direction of the circle's last quarter, strength by tier) that carries Alexi, debris
or a person. Hold B: grip or carry.

**Rules.** Alexi does not breathe; there is no air meter. Instead, **the pull**: the deep wants to
keep him. A soft timer (90 seconds in the first dive, 120 later) shown as the screen edge darkening.
At zero, he sinks gently to the floor and the dive restarts from its last checkpoint. Contrast with
land is the point: here the controls are the most responsive in the game.

**Hazards.** Company dredge chains (sweep slowly; contact pushes him 5 m back); nets (slow to 20%
until cut by gripping and pulling); collapsing hulls; cold currents (stir against them).

**Progress.** Depth gauge at the left margin; the target marked by a pale ring.

| Sequence | Chapter | Length | Goal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `d-first-dive` | 3 | 6 min | Down 80 m to the sunken tender; free the strongbox from the hull; carry it up (carrying halves speed; currents help) |
| `d-pearl-dive` | 4 | 3 min | Past Ide's 20 m bank, down the trench to 140 m, for the compact stone; take a rubbing (hold B for 5 s) |
| `d-rescue` | 3–5 (at each Tide Reckoning) | Up to 2 min | Reach a drowning person sinking from the surface within 60 seconds and lift them clear. Success turns the toll entry's outcome to `saved` |
| `d-under-pilings` | 6 | 4 min | Under the counting-house wharf, through the Company's mooring chains, up into the hall |

`d-rescue` is offered once per Tide Reckoning, for one person only. If two drown at one
reckoning, the player chooses whom to dive for. The other is lost.

## 11. 1D games

Four games, fourteen appearances. Each is a full page that pauses the world (`world_runs: false`)
unless stated. Every page has text size settings and a "slow" option for any timer, per TECH.md.

### Liar's Tide (`liars-tide`, dice, bluffing)

**Purpose.** The only way to talk to sailors without talking much. Crews, news and one fishing
licence are won or lost at the cup.

**Rules.**

- 2 to 4 players: Alexi and 1 to 3 sailors. Each has 5 tide dice under a horn cup.
- Faces: 1 Gull, 2 Shell, 3 Rope, 4 Bottle, 5 Lantern, 6 Wave. Gulls are wild (count as any face)
  unless a bid has been made on Gulls this round.
- **The tide die.** At the start of each round one shared die is rolled face up in the middle. It
  counts for everyone.
- A bid is a quantity and a face ("four Lanterns"): how many of that face the bidder claims are on
  the table, under all cups plus the tide die. Each bid must raise the quantity, or keep it and
  raise the face.
- Instead of bidding, a player may **call the tide**. Cups lift. If the bid holds, the caller loses
  one die; if not, the bidder loses one. Loser starts the next round.
- A player with no dice is out. The last with dice wins the game.
- **Stir the cup.** Once per round, at any table within sight of water (all of them), Alexi may
  stir: secretly re-roll up to 2 of his own dice. Each stir adds 25 to the table's **suspicion**
  (shown as the sailors' glances, 0–100); in a region with Wild 60 or more, only 10 ("the sea's
  luck"). At 100 he is caught: the game ends as a loss, and the stake is forfeit. Suspicion falls by
  10 each round he does not stir.

**Layout.** Top-down on a tavern table. Cups at the four edges; the tide die centre; the bid
ledger as chalk marks on the table's right; the suspicion meter as faces of the sailors at the top,
eyes turning toward the player as it rises. Stakes laid on the table at the left.

**Inputs.** Quantity up/down, face left/right, Bid, Call, Stir (circle gesture over own cup).

**Win / finish.** Last with dice. A loss is never a dead end on the critical path (section 8).

**Opponents and difficulty curve.**

| Appearance | Opponents | Their bluff rate | Tell | Stake | Win gives | Loss gives |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `g-liars-tidewater` (ch2) | Wenna and two dockhands | 10% / 20% | Wenna counts aloud under her breath when she holds 3+ of a face | News | Two `news` entries (Gullwife, Kettle crew); devotion +3 | One `news` entry (Gullwife) |
| `g-liars-gullwife` (ch3) | Three Gullwife hands | 40% | Laughs before a bluff | Their song | The crew joins (+1 crew, devotion +8); devotion +3 | Crew joins after the squall instead if not caught |
| `g-liars-salt` (ch4) | The tallyman and Old Marrow | Tallyman bids by probability, 25%; Marrow 30% | None for the tallyman | The Salt Steps fishing licence | `port.salt` = `broken`; devotion +3; Kettle crew joins | Licence stays; `port.salt` = `bound`; crew joins only if the stone raises Salt to 50+ |

The AI computes the probability of each bid from its own dice and the tide die, bids with a
threshold of 50% (Wenna 65%, tallyman 50%, Gullwife 35%), bluffs at its rate, and calls when the
current bid has less than 30% chance of holding.

**Hints.** The princess (trust 40+) whispers the odds of the current bid once per round.

**Writes.** `crews`, `devotion`, `news`, `port.salt`, `liars.caught` (counter).

### The Charter (`charter`, document hunt)

**Purpose.** The Company holds the sea by paper. Each port is bound by one clause in one document.
Find it; then strike it, rewrite it, or leave it.

**How it differs from The Endless Temple's Rule Tablet:** that game finds what a rule does not
forbid. This one is a hunt through pages for the single clause that does the binding, by its
cross-references, and then a match between that clause and a physical fact that breaks it.

**Rules.**

1. **Find.** The document has pages, clauses, schedules and pinned amendment slips. Clauses refer
   to each other ("subject to clause 7(b)"; "as set out in Schedule II"). The binding clause is
   the one every other obligation routes through. The player follows references by clicking them
   (a red thread appears between linked clauses) and pins the clause they think binds.
2. **Ink.** Each document gives 3 ink. A wrong pin costs one. At 0 the clerk takes the paper back:
   `notice` +10 and the document can be re-requested at the chart table (one retry, then it stays
   bound).
3. **Break.** With the binding clause pinned, the player drags one item from the **evidence drawer**
   (the cabin walls, right of the page) onto it, or points at a defect inside the document (an
   unsigned amendment, a date passed, a seal that differs from the one on page 1). A correct match
   lights the clause.
4. **Decide.** Three buttons: **Strike** (the port goes `broken`; the Company's order goes with it:
   that region's Wild +15), **Rewrite** (only if `princess.trust` ≥ 50: she redrafts it; the port
   goes `rewritten`, Wild unchanged, and a lane is kept there for free), or **Leave** (port stays
   `bound`; trust −5 if she watched you find it).
5. Strike or Rewrite gives one **clause** token for the final Tea Board.

**Layout.** A writing slope seen from above. The document fills the centre, tabbed by page along
the top. The margin on the left lists clause numbers as an index. The evidence drawer on the right
shows what the run has collected. The princess's annotations (hints) appear as pencil in the margins.
Seals can be lifted to a loupe view.

**Difficulty curve.**

| Appearance | Document | Pages / clauses / references | Decoys | Breaks by | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `g-charter-deed` (ch1) | Sale deed of the halls | 2 / 6 / 1 | 0 | Nothing yet | Tutorial: find "and all waters adjoining". No decision. Trust +10 on finding it |
| `g-charter-tidewater` (ch4) | Tidewater harbour book (raised in ch3) | 3 / 9 / 2 | 1 | A date passed: the harbour dues ran "for forty years from the lighting of the quay lamp"; the lamp's date is on page 1 | Port `lanes` |
| `g-charter-strait` (ch4) | Lighthouse lease | 4 / 12 / 3 | 1 | Corran's logbook (evidence, given if met): the Company never paid the keepers' clause | Strike darkens the lights (Wild +15, and lit/dark changes `g-reckon-strait`) |
| `g-charter-pearl` (ch4) | Diving-rights deed | 4 / 12 / 3 | 2 | The compact stone rubbing from `d-pearl-dive`: the elders could not sell what the stone gave to every diver | No rubbing: cannot break; Leave only |
| `g-charter-halls` (ch5) | The halls' deed and its debt schedule | 6 / 20 / 5 | 2 | The payment receipt from the blockade, plus the ch1 deed's "all waters adjoining" (the sea was never the king's to sell) | Without the receipt: Rewrite only (if trust ≥ 50), else Leave |

**Hints.** Trust 40+: the princess reads one page aloud as a plain summary. Trust 60+: she circles
the page the binding clause is on. Ashore the text wobbles slightly ("the letters swim"); at the
chart table it is still. Comic, and a nudge to read at sea.

**Writes.** `port.*`, `wild.*`, `lane.*`, `clauses`, `princess.trust`, `notice`, `deed_read`.

### The Tea Board (`tea-board`, board game, trade routes)

**Purpose.** The Company's own parlour game, played on a printed chart of the same eight regions.
"Everything has a lord" made literal. The final negotiation with the Lord of Tea is a match on it,
seeded by the campaign.

**How it differs from the collection's other board games:** Six Realms is hex tactics; the Node
Board places emitters against hidden pieces; Trunk and Shadow changes its rules; Bone Counters is
sowing. The Tea Board is open information, majority control of lanes and ports, with a tide clock
that changes what pieces are worth and a debt mechanic that can take a port without a lane.

**The board.** Eight ports (one per region) and twelve lanes:

| Lane | Joins | Lane | Joins |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| L1 | Home – Tidewater | L7 | Strait – Spice |
| L2 | Home – Unwritten | L8 | Pearl – Spice |
| L3 | Home – Salt | L9 | Pearl – Halls |
| L4 | Tidewater – Strait | L10 | Spice – Halls |
| L5 | Unwritten – Salt | L11 | Tidewater – Spice |
| L6 | Salt – Pearl | L12 | Strait – Halls |

Each lane has 3 slots.

**Pieces.** Company: ink-blue Chests. Alexi: sea-glass Hymns. Company also has Debt markers;
Alexi has Clause tokens.

**A turn: one action.**

| Action | Who | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Place | Both | A piece from reserve onto an empty slot of a lane touching a port you hold or no one holds |
| Shift | Both | Move one of your pieces to an empty slot on a lane sharing a port with its lane |
| Lend | Company | Put a Debt on a port (max 2 per port). A port with 2 Debt is held by the Company regardless of lanes |
| Clause | Alexi | Spend a Clause token to remove one Debt |
| Stir | Alexi | At High tide only, once per tide: push one Chest from a lane touching a Wild port onto an adjacent lane's empty slot (or off the board if none) |
| Sign | Alexi (final match only) | See below |

**Control.** A lane is held by whoever has more pieces on it. A port is held by whoever holds more
of its lanes; ties keep the previous holder.

**The tide clock.** Four phases, two rounds each: Low, Rising, High, Falling.

- **Low:** Chests on lanes touching an Ordered port (campaign Wild ≤ 40) count double.
- **High:** Hymns on lanes touching a Wild port (campaign Wild ≥ 60) count double; Stir is
  available.
- Rising and Falling: nothing counts double.

**End.** After the last round, count ports held. More ports wins. A tie goes to the Company ("A
tie, in my books, is mine"), unless the match was signed.

**Sign.** In the final match only. Available on Alexi's turn when the ports held are exactly 4–4
and `princess.trust` ≥ 60 and `devotion` ≥ 50 and `chart.balanced_regions` ≥ 3. The princess holds
the pen; the Lord of Tea signs because at 4–4 he cannot show a profit on continuing. The match ends
at once as a draw. The page explains every unmet condition on the Sign button's tooltip.

**Layout.** The board fills the page, drawn as the same engraved chart. Reserves at the bottom
(Hymns left, Chests right). Tide clock as a dial top-right. Clause tokens and Debt markers beside
reserves. The opponent's hand gestures over the board for each move (a cue, not information).
Move history as a ledger strip on the left.

**Inputs.** Select piece or reserve; select slot. Buttons for Lend/Clause/Stir/Sign. Undo the last
selection before confirming.

**Appearances and difficulty.**

| Appearance | Opponent | Rounds | Ports in play | Debt | Seed | AI | Win gives | Loss gives |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `g-tea-parlour` (ch2) | The factor | 6 | Home, Tidewater, Unwritten, Salt (lanes L1, L2, L3, L5) | Off | Fixed: 2 each | Greedy, one move ahead, 30% random | `tea.parlour_won`: +1 Hymn in the final | Nothing |
| `g-tea-comptroller` (ch4) | The Comptroller | 8 | Six (no Halls, no Pearl) | On | From current campaign Wild (rule below) | Looks two moves ahead | `port.spice` = `broken`, +1 clause, `tea.comptroller_won` | `port.spice` stays bound; `notice` +15 |
| `g-tea-final` (ch6) | The Lord of Tea | 12 | All eight | On | Full seed (below) | Looks three moves ahead; plays Lend whenever a port is one Debt from flipping | `tea.final_result` = `win` | `loss` (or `draw` if signed) |

**Seeding the final match** (also used, cut down to its six ports, for the Comptroller):

| Source | Effect on the board |
| --- | --- |
| Each region with Wild ≥ 60 | One Hymn on that port's first lane (lowest lane number) |
| Each region with Wild ≤ 40 | One Chest on that port's first lane, or its second if the first is full |
| Each port `bound` | One Debt on it |
| Each port `broken` or `rewritten` | No Debt; starts held by Alexi |
| Home and Unwritten | Never bound; start held by Alexi |
| Alexi's reserve | 5 + `crews`, plus 1 if `tea.parlour_won`; at most 12 |
| Company reserve | 12, plus 1 if `notice` ≥ 70 |
| Clause tokens | `clauses` |
| First move | Alexi if `devotion` ≥ 50, else the Company |

**What the seed means for the player.** A run that freed most ports and kept the chart wild
starts ahead and usually wins. A run that left the chart ordered starts behind by three or four
ports and usually loses. A balanced run starts near 4–4, which is where Sign lives.

**Hints.** Trust 40+: the princess can highlight one recommended move, three times per match.

**Writes.** `tea.parlour_won`, `tea.comptroller_won`, `port.spice`, `clauses`, `notice`,
`tea.final_result`.

### Dead Reckoning (`reckoning`, navigation)

**Purpose.** Where there is no chart, no light or no safe passage, Alexi plots one. It is also how
safe lanes are laid.

**Rules.**

- The page is a chart sheet with a **fix** (last known position), a **target** circle, hazards,
  a compass rose, a scale of nautical miles, and a **tide table** (set and rate for each hour).
- A course is one to three **legs**. For each leg the player sets a heading (lay the parallel rule
  from the rose), a speed (from the log-line reading, given) and a time (hours and quarters).
- For each hour, the tide moves the boat by the table's set and rate. The player draws the tide
  vector from the end of each hour's run (drag from the table onto the chart).
- The **dead-reckoned position** is the end of the last vector. The player marks it with the
  dividers and commits.
- **Stir the tide.** In a region with Wild 45 or more, Alexi may stir one hour's tide vector,
  turning it up to 30° either way. Each use raises that region's Wild by 5.
- **Result.** The engine computes the true track. Inside the target circle and clear of hazards:
  arrived. Outside: arrived off-target, and the next scene starts harder (below). A track crossing
  a hazard: the commit is refused and the player sees where.

**Layout.** A chart sheet filling the page, instruments in a tray at the bottom (parallel rule,
dividers, pencil, eraser), the tide table pinned top-left, a clock top-right showing elapsed hours.

**Inputs.** Drag the rule; click the rose to read a bearing; drag dividers to measure; drag tide
rows; set time with a dial; commit.

**Difficulty curve.**

| Appearance | Legs | Tide | Tolerance | Hazards | Off-target result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `g-reckon-unwritten` (ch3) | 1 | Steady set | 2 nm | Two rocks | Arrive in fog: `s-unwritten` starts 300 m from the Gullwife boat instead of 50 |
| `g-reckon-strait` (ch4) | 2 | Reverses after hour 2 | 1 nm | Old wrecks; lights help if lit (bearing lines from each lit light) | Lit: a near miss, no cost. Dark: a wreck of Alexi's own boat is avoided only by a stir; Wild Strait +5 and `notice` +5 |
| `g-reckon-blockade` (ch5) | 3 | Slack water window of 1 hour | 0.5 nm, and arrival inside the slack window | Patrol sweep lines with times | One extra patrol sweep in `e-blockade-run` |
| `g-reckon-lane` (ch4, ch6) | 1 | Region's own | 1 nm | None | None; lanes always succeed, the page teaches by doing |

**Laying a lane.** At the chart table, for a peopled region with Wild 45 or more and no lane: plot
one leg from its port to open water. Committing sets `lane.<region>` true and lowers that region's
Wild by 10 ("the calm has to come from somewhere"). A lane raises the region's wreck and drowning
thresholds by 15 (section 12). Lanes are what the Charter ending is made of.

**Hints.** The cabin's old maps (found in chapter 0) show a pencilled worked example. Trust 40+:
the princess checks one leg and says if it is off.

**Writes.** `wild.*`, `lane.*`, `notice`, `blockade_plotted`.

## 12. Core systems

### Order and Wild (`wild.<region>`, meters 0–100)

One meter per region: `wild.home`, `wild.lanes`, `wild.unwritten`, `wild.salt`, `wild.strait`,
`wild.pearl`, `wild.spice`, `wild.halls`. Start values are in section 5. 0 is a sea of buoys and
ledgers; 100 is the old sea.

| Event | Change | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tideline stone: Still | −10 | That region |
| Tideline stone: Ripple | 0 | That region |
| Tideline stone: Raise | +15 | That region |
| Tideline stone: Rouse | +30 | That region |
| Strike a charter | +15 | That port's region |
| Rewrite a charter | 0 (and a free lane) | That port's region |
| Lay a lane | −10 | That region |
| Stir a tide vector in Dead Reckoning | +5 | That region |
| Strait lit/dark crossing gone wrong | +5 | Strait |
| Company pressure, at each Tide Reckoning | −10 | Every peopled region whose port is still `bound` |

Thresholds used across the game: **Ordered** ≤ 40; **Wild** ≥ 60; between is **Contested**.
The chart table shades each region on a five-step scale from Company blue (0–20) to sea-green and
foam (81–100).

### The tideline stones

Every region except the Unwritten has one stone at its tideline. Home Reach's is used in chapter 3
and again at the tally in chapter 6; Tidewater's is reached on the short run made to read the harbour
book in chapter 4; the halls' stone is at the sea-gate in chapter 5; the four chapter 4 regions have
theirs on their beaches. Each stone is used once per visit and is locked until that region's person
of the peace has been met.

At the stone Alexi stands in the tideline and stirs. The direction and number of circles choose:

| Choice | Gesture | Needs | Wild | Devotion | Notice |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Still | 2 circles counter-clockwise | Nothing | −10 | −3 | −5 |
| Ripple | Let go | Nothing | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| Raise | 2 circles clockwise | Effective tier Swell or higher | +15 | +3 if any crew has joined | +5 |
| Rouse | 4 circles clockwise | Effective tier Current or higher | +30 | +6 if any crew has joined | +10 |

Each stone use is written to the `stirs` ledger (region, choice, chapter). The ledger is what the
debug panel shows when a designer asks why a region ended where it did.

### Stir power

Alexi's **effective tier** in a place is the lower of his devotion tier and the place's wild tier,
plus one at the tideline. Ashore (out of the water) there is no stir at all.

| Tier | Devotion needed | Region Wild needed | In the Engraved Sea | In the Deep |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 Ripple | 0 | 0 | 0.5 m crest | 1 m/s current |
| 1 Swell | 15 | 20 | 1.5 m | 2 m/s |
| 2 Current | 30 | 35 | 2.5 m, plus a 5 m/s surface current | 3 m/s, carries a person |
| 3 Fog | 45 | 45 | Fog bank 80 m wide that breaks a cutter's sight | 4 m/s, moves debris |
| 4 Squall | 60 | 55 | 4 m crest, gusts | 5 m/s |
| 5 Storm | 75 | 70 | 6 m crest, waterspouts | 6 m/s, lifts a hull |
| 6 Tide-turn | 90 | 85 | Reverses the tide for 20 s | Raises the floor's sand into a wall |

**Song surge.** While a joined crew is singing in sight (scripted in `e-first-squall`; the player
can call a crew to sing once per Engraved Sea sequence), Alexi's devotion counts as 25 higher for
tier purposes. It never changes the stored `devotion`.

No tier is "remembered". A tier is open when the sea and the sailors make it so, and closes again
when they stop.

| Event | Change |

| Raise / Rouse at a stone, with any crew joined | +3 / +6 |

5 at each Tide Reckoning. At 50 or more, chases have two cutters. At 70 or more, the Lord of Tea
starts the final match with one more Chest.

### Ports, lanes and clauses

- `port.lanes`, `port.salt`, `port.strait`, `port.pearl`, `port.spice`, `port.halls`: enum
  `bound`, `broken`, `rewritten`. All start `bound`.
- `lane.lanes`, `lane.salt`, `lane.strait`, `lane.pearl`, `lane.spice`, `lane.halls`: bool.
- `clauses`: counter, +1 per charter struck or rewritten, +1 for winning the Comptroller match.
  Spent only on the final Tea Board.
- `ports_freed`: counter of ports `broken` or `rewritten`.

### The Tide Reckoning and the toll

Three reckonings: end of chapter 3 (Home, Lanes, Unwritten only), end of chapter 4 and end of
chapter 5 (all regions). Each reckoning is a full-page sheet over the chart table, filled in line by
line as the player watches. In order:

1. Company pressure: −10 Wild for each peopled region whose port is `bound`.
2. For each peopled region, check its thresholds:
   - **Wreck** if Wild ≥ 60 (≥ 75 with a lane) and that region has had no wreck yet.
   - **Drowning** if Wild ≥ 80 (≥ 95 with a lane) and that region has had no drowning yet.
3. Each is written to the `toll` ledger (region, kind, person, outcome, chapter).
4. One rescue dive (`d-rescue`) may be made for one drowning. Success sets outcome `saved`.
5. Crews whose region is below Wild 30 fall silent (devotion −4 each).
6. `notice` −5.

Each region can give at most one wreck and one drowning in the whole game. Who it would be is set
before the player can cause it, and the player has met them:

| Region | Wreck | Drowning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lanes / Tidewater | The ferry, on its six-minute crossing | Wenna |
| Salt Steps | Two of the reef skiffs | Old Marrow, going out for his grandchildren's nets |
| Lamplit Strait | A Company packet on the old wreck ground | Corran, rowing to a light that went out |
| Pearl Banks | A diving boat over the trench edge | Ide |
| Spice Mouth | A lighter loaded with the week's pay | The dock-warden |
| King's Halls | The halls' supply boat | An old oarsman of the king's |

The sheet names them in plain words, with one line each about something the player saw them do
(Wenna's counting; Corran's wick at the same minute every evening). It never shows a body. A
`saved` entry gets a second line: what they did the next morning.

### Ashore, tideline, aboard

| State | Walk speed | Stop distance | Turn | Stir |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ashore | 2.2 m/s, run 4.5 m/s | 2.5 m (run: 4 m, usually into something) | 140°/s with overshoot | None |
| Tideline (water at ankle or deeper, within the band) | 3 m/s | 0.3 m | 360°/s | Effective tier +1 |
| Aboard | Deck walk 2.5 m/s; helm by stick | — | — | Normal tier |

The ashore lurch is a lateral sway of ±0.6 m on a 1.4 s cycle when running. Collisions with people
play a scripted pratfall and an apology bark; they never harm anyone. Stairs slow him by half.

### The tally (chapter 6)

At `s-cabin-tally` the chart table writes three counters, shown with each region's colour:

| Counter | Counts |
| --- | --- |
| `chart.wild_regions` | Regions (of 8) with Wild ≥ 60 |
| `chart.order_regions` | Regions (of 8) with Wild ≤ 40 |
| `chart.balanced_regions` | Peopled regions (of 6) with a lane kept and Wild ≥ 45 |

These, the ports, `crews`, `devotion`, `clauses`, `notice` and `tea.parlour_won` seed the final
match (section 11).

### The Voice Beside (the narrator)

The source's narrator is "I—who am him and not him, who walk beside him and inside him" (357). The
design reads it as the god inside the man, the thing that "stirred" at 351, and never says so.

- **Form.** Short first-person lines in a separate type (an upright italic in sea-ink green) that
  appear without a speaker plate. No portrait and no voice actor: it speaks from inside him, so it
  is read, not heard.
- **When.** Nine fixed places: the tideline in chapter 0; the ends of chapters 1 to 5; the tally;
  the tideline in chapter 7; the last line of each ending except The Lord's Peace.
- **What it may say.** What Alexi does, what the sea does, and the word "us", as at 357. It may
  speak of the sea as something it knows from inside ("older than memory", 351). It never says
  "Poseidon", "god" or its own name, and it never explains itself. The two verbatim lines are used
  as the chapter 0 and chapter 1 lines; every added line is marked in the script as added.
- **No reveal.** No ending names it, and nothing in the data tracks who it is. What each ending
  does to it is shown by whether it speaks: in The Lord's Peace, where the sea stops answering, the
  last-line slot is left empty and the ending closes on buoy bells alone.

## 13. Endings

None is marked as written: the author has written two chapters and no ending.

**The default ending is No Lord.** It is a proposal, not the author's ending, and `as_written`
stays false. It is named the default because it is the one the chapters point toward: the
stirring is "Something that knew the sea was not meant to be owned" (351), which a god who keeps
the sea (The Wild Sea) or signs terms for it (The Charter) does not honour, and the Lord of Tea
owning it (The Lord's Peace) is the warning itself. Its last image, Alexi rowing the dinghy in at
dawn, returns to the chapters' own "He took up the oars ... and began to row" (288). A player who
frees ports, wins the final match and lets go at the tideline is on the story's path.

The ending is decided at `s-tideline-final` by the final match result, the map at the tally, and,
after a win, one choice. The resolver checks in priority order.

| Priority | Id | Name | Conditions (plain words) | Condition in `data/endings.json` | Final scenes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `the-charter` | The Charter | The final match was signed as a draw, with at least 3 balanced regions, devotion 50 or more and trust 60 or more | `all: tea.final_result eq draw; chart.balanced_regions gte 3; devotion gte 50; princess.trust gte 60` | `s-tideline-final`, `s-end-charter` |
| 2 | `no-lord` | No Lord | Alexi won the final match and gave up the sea | `all: tea.final_result eq win; final_choice eq relinquish` | `s-tideline-final`, `s-end-no-lord` |
| 3 | `the-wild-sea` | The Wild Sea | Alexi won the final match, kept the sea, and at least 5 regions were Wild at the tally | `all: tea.final_result eq win; final_choice eq keep; chart.wild_regions gte 5` | `s-tideline-final`, `s-end-wild-sea` |
| 4 | `the-lords-peace` | The Lord's Peace | Anything else: a lost match, or a tie the Lord took | `true` | `s-tideline-final`, `s-end-lords-peace` |

No two can be true together: `draw` and `win` exclude each other, and `keep` and `relinquish`
exclude each other. The Charter's extra conditions are the Sign button's conditions, so a draw
without them cannot occur; they are repeated in the data so the debug panel can show them.

### The Charter

With the princess, Alexi writes terms between a god and the people who sail: the lanes kept calm by
his own hand, the wild water left wild, and the halls' clause that reached the sea struck out. The
Lord of Tea signs at 4–4. Final image: dawn on the bay, the ferry crossing a glassy lane while
beyond it the water rises and falls on its own, and a crew sings the hymn to the tide (section 16),
in tune for once. The narrator's last-line slot. Cost kept: every wreck and drowning in the `toll` ledger is
read out on the terms' last page as the reason for the lanes.

### No Lord

He beats the Lord of Tea and then, at the tideline, lets go: the sea "was not meant to be owned"
(351), by anyone. Alexi becomes a mortal sailor. The princess takes back the halls. The Company's
charters fall; so do its lights, and no one keeps them. Final image: Alexi rowing the dinghy in at
dawn with blistered hands, an ordinary man on an ordinary sea. The Gullwife crew sings the hymn from the
anchorage, and he does not look round. The narrator's last line is its last: the god leaves the
story it said would swallow them. Cost kept: the epilogue lists the regions where wrecks will now
come back with no god to answer to, one line each.

### The Wild Sea

He beats the Lord of Tea and keeps the sea. Alexi is the god again. The hymns come back, every crew
on the chart singing, and so do the wrecks. Final image: Alexi on the bowsprit in a storm he made,
the coast strip of the Engraved Sea at the bottom of the frame with its moorings lifting. Cost kept:
the princess leaves the boat at the halls; the `toll` ledger is sung to the hymn's tune, by name,
with no other words.

### The Lord's Peace

The Lord of Tea owns every ship that sails. The sea is safe. Alexi is "a man on an old boat" who
cannot make the tide answer; the game does not say he forgot, only that the sea stopped answering.
Final image: noon on the beach, buoy bells at even intervals, Alexi turning a bottle in his hand and
stirring the water at his ankles, which does nothing. The princess is a Company ward with a pension.
Wenna counts the children across, on time. The narrator says nothing.

### How a player reaches each one on purpose

| Ending | Do this |
| --- | --- |
| The Charter | Rewrite charters rather than strike them (needs trust 50, so read with her and lay lanes early); keep five or six regions between 45 and 70 with lanes; gather crews to reach devotion 50; at the final match aim for 4–4 and press Sign. |
| No Lord (default) | Free ports and push regions wild enough to win the final match (5+ wild regions or most ports freed); then choose to give up the sea. |
| The Wild Sea | As No Lord, with at least 5 regions at Wild 60+ at the tally, and choose to keep it. Expect most of the `toll` ledger to fill. |
| The Lord's Peace | Still the stones, leave charters bound, or lose the final match. |

**How the layers look different.**

- **3D:** painted colour, soft outlines, real light.

  uses `flatten`: the camera swings to side-on and the colour drains to ink over 0.8 s.
- **1D:** physical paper and objects seen from above, filling the page. Entering uses `push-in`: the
  camera moves into the table or the document until it becomes the page.

- **Palette.** Two families that the player learns to read. *Order*: Company ink-blue (#1F2E4A),
  ledger red (#8C2B2B), tea brown (#6B4A2B), lamp white (#F4F1E8). *Wild*: sea-green (#2F6B5E),
  foam (#E8F2EE), storm violet (#4B3F63), dawn gold (#E7A93B). Colour-blind safe pairs are checked:
  Order and Wild also differ in pattern (ruled hatching against wave hatching) on every chart.
- **Mood references (not titles to copy).** Copperplate sea charts and their decorative margins;
  maritime folk art; travel sketchbooks; the clutter of a lived-in cabin; the clean white of a
  colonial-era trading port seen as too clean.
- **Materials.** Tarred wood, salt-dulled brass, glass bottles, paper in every state from crisp
  Company stock to sea-swollen ledgers.
- **Lighting.** Every chapter has a time of day. Chapter 0 is dawn; the endings return to dawn
  except The Lord's Peace, which is flat noon.
- **Alexi.** Middle-aged, heavy, sun-damaged, bare feet "rough from the constant embrace of salt and
  grit" (304). Nothing in his design says "god" until devotion is 75, when the rim light at sea
  takes on a faint green.
- **The princess.** A pale dress that never recovers from the beach. Sharp eyes, "ocean‑colored"
  (326).
- **The Lord of Tea.** Seen only in chapter 6. Neat, still, ink-stained cuffs.
- **One look across the layers.** The engraving style of 2D is the ink of 1D's paper; the 3D
  world's outlines are the same ink. A Company ship is drawn with the same silhouette in all three.
  The palette and materials are shared across the layers, while each page's drawing style differs
  (see Gameplay graphics).

### Gameplay graphics

Each 2D mode and 1D game keeps this palette but is drawn in its own graphic style, unique in the
collection (`docs/COLLECTION.md` section 7). Pictographs: `pictographs/<key>.svg`.

**Key effects.** The stir (a low, rising water drone whose pitch climbs with each circle); the
tideline answer (a spiral of small splashes); the ripple (a tiny, disappointing plip); dice in horn
cups; the tide die; the Tea Board's wooden clicks; the pen of Sign; the Tide Reckoning's quill
writing each line.

**Voice.** Sparse. Alexi, the princess, the Lord of Tea and the Comptroller are voiced for their
few lines. Sailors bark in short phrases. The narrator is never voiced (section 12). Every spoken
line is subtitled.

**The hymn.** The source says the crews "sang drunken hymns to the tide, praising her like a lover
who always returned" (346), so they sing words, not a hum. There is one hymn, added for the game
(section 21), four lines long; each crew sings it to its own arrangement, and no crew has other
words:

> Drink to her going, drink to her coming,
> she never stays and she never stays gone.
> Ship oars, all hands, for the tide is a-turning:
> sing her back in and we'll drink till the dawn.

## 20. Risks and open questions

### Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
| --- | --- |
| The trade-off feels like a morality meter | The toll is never good or evil points. It is people the player met, named, with a rescue dive that can change one outcome per reckoning. Wild also gives real power the player wants |
| The Tea Board final is decided before it starts | Seeds are capped (reserve at most 12; Debt at most 1 per bound port at start) so a strong run still has to play, and a weak run can still win with clauses and High-tide Stir. Playtest target: a balanced seed ends 4–4 in about a third of matches without Sign |
| Comic voice undercuts the drownings | Comedy lives ashore and in Alexi's body. The Tide Reckoning sheets are written plainly, never as jokes |
| 1D load (14 appearances) in a sailing game | No two 1D pages in a row except chart-table lanes and the harbour book; every 1D is under 6 minutes except the final match |
| The open chart order makes pacing uneven | Every region has the same spine and length (8–9 minutes) and its own featured game |
| Imperial setting | The Company is fictional; ports and people are invented, not modelled on any real colony or people. Sensitivity read before M6 |

**Answered** (by Claude at the author's request, 2026-11-12, from the source text; the author can overrule any of them).

1. **The narrator is the god inside the man, and no ending reveals it.** The text places it: "somewhere deep inside him—somewhere older than memory, older than the man he had become—something stirred" (351), and six lines later the "I" speaks, "who am him and not him, who walk beside him and inside him" (357), with "My toes—his toes" (304) as the one earlier slip. That is the older self ADAPTATIONS.md proposed. The source names nothing, so neither does the game: the `narrator.identity` flag and the empty "identity line" fields are cut from the data and section 12, the narrator stays unvoiced, and its lines never say "god", "Poseidon" or a name. What an ending does to it is shown only by whether it speaks; in The Lord's Peace it is silent.
2. **Neither the princess nor her father's kingdom is named.** The chapters call her "a princess", "Your Highness" and "she", and the kingdom is only "my father's", "his halls" and "the empire" (324–336). She keeps her title; the region stays "the King's Halls"; UI and save labels say "the princess". Nothing in the data changed: it never named either.
3. **No Lord is closest, and it is named the default; the crews sing, with one short hymn.** The chapters' turn is "Something that knew the sea was not meant to be owned" (351), which No Lord alone honours on both sides of the table, and its last image rows in at dawn as the chapters do (288). It stays `as_written: false`, with its conditions unchanged; section 13 says why it is the default. Humming would contradict "sang drunken hymns to the tide, praising her like a lover who always returned" (346), so the crews sing one four-line hymn built on that line (section 16, listed in section 21). The seven crew hymns become seven arrangements of it; the Wild Sea toll is sung to its tune with only the names; the cue count is unchanged.
