# Dr. Tox: Altrulan

Episode 4, the last, of *Dr. Tox: Age of Glass & Greed*, the collection's eleventh game
(`docs/COLLECTION.md` row 11). Scope accepted by the author on 2026-11-19 (`dr-tox/SCOPE.md`, all
thirteen recommendations). This file is stage A of Phase 4: the design, the data, the storyboard, the
asset manifest, the Blender build files and the pictographs. The art kit, the web modules, the
generated images and the key art come later, as they did for Rise, Fall and the Finale.

**Source.** The author's bible, `dr-tox/source/Dr_Tox_Compiled_Bible_Obsidian.md`, is not in the
vault. It is never edited and is cited here by part and section ("Part VI, Dr. Tox §3" is Part VI, the
Dr. Tox profile, section 3). Every quotation below is verbatim from it. **The bible gives the Epic of
Altrulan less than any other episode**: one line in Part I, one paragraph in Part III ("The Altrulan
myth-distortion"), one line of architecture, two notes on Aegis's myth name, and an outside read in
Part VII §7. It gives Altrulan no story and no ending. Almost everything that follows is an addition;
every addition is marked *(added)* where it first appears and listed in section 21. Rise, Fall and the
Finale (`games/dr-tox-rise/`, `games/dr-tox-fall/`, `games/dr-tox-finale/`) are canon for this episode
in what the author accepted, but their people appear here **only as the scripture remembers them**.

## 1. Summary

Seven years after the Breath, the keeper of the holy cave at Snakehead takes the year's pilgrims from
the cave by the Glass Mountains and through the breathing city to the Light Bringer's shrine, with an
invisible companion who signs and a finite store of sacrament that only a dead man could make; the
player measures it out, keeps the scripture's plaster whole or looks into its cracks, and decides what
she writes, what she says aloud, and how much of the store is left for anyone after her. The bible's one
rule for this phase holds in every ending: the survivors "aren't lying, they genuinely remember it that
way now" (Part III, The Altrulan myth-distortion).

## 2. Why this game

Altrulan is the episode in which the dose is a sacrament and the horror is scripture. The trilogy's
player fought RCC, his own body and his own cult; here there is nothing to fight. There are pilgrims to
bring up a mountain, a store that runs down, and a wall whose plaster cracks.

## 3. Source fidelity

What the bible gives Altrulan, in full, and what the game keeps from each line.

**The phase (Part I, "The two phases").**

> 2. **The Epic of Altrulan** — the post-Reset mythic afterimage. Survivors' altered consciousness
> repaints the horror as divine history. Sculptural, textured, non-comic illustration style.

The working title puts the arrow in the name: "Dr. Tox: Age of Glass & Greed → Epic of Altrulan"
(Part I). The game is the afterimage: no chapter is set in the Age, and the Age is seen only through what
covers it. "Sculptural, textured, non-comic illustration style" is the whole art direction (section 15)
and the reason every Altrulan page is a made surface: carved stone, wet plaster, wax on paper, slip on
clay, cast bronze, hammered gold.

**The myth-distortion (Part III), in full.**

> The twelve most powerful surviving mutants are permanently elevated by the Bufadienolide wave and
> mythologized. Aegis becomes Aleglethan the Light Bringer, his hard-light barriers romanticized into
> halos. Skyscrapers become "the Glass Mountains." Executives become "Suit-Demons." The horror gets
> smoothed into scripture by the survivors' altered consciousness — they aren't lying, they genuinely
> remember it that way now.

1. "The twelve most powerful surviving mutants": the shrine has twelve niches; the scripture names some
   (section 5, Groups). The Ghost is venerated among them *(added)*.
2. "Aegis becomes Aleglethan the Light Bringer, his hard-light barriers romanticized into halos": the
   shrine is his; its relief rings his head and hands with gilded halos; the Finale's last frames left
   "a ring of hard light still stands around the hub where Reese's panes are: the halos Altrulan will remember"
   (`games/dr-tox-finale/DESIGN.md` §13). Aegis's note says the same in other words: "Post-Reset, his hard-light barriers are romanticized
   by Altrulan survivors as blinding halos of pure divine energy." (Part VI, Aegis, Notes).
3. "Skyscrapers become 'the Glass Mountains.'": a location (`glass-mountains`) and the pilgrimage's
   climb.
4. "Executives become 'Suit-Demons.'": the frescoes' demons sit on the Mountains; the brass's other face
   gives them names (section 11, Rubbing).
5. "they aren't lying, they genuinely remember it that way now": the design's governing rule. **No
   ending treats the scripture as a lie** (`dr-tox/SCOPE.md` §5). Section 12 makes it a number: the
   Ghost's reading can come level with the keeper's memory and never above it.

**The architecture (Part III, Architecture, by phase).**

> **Epic of Altrulan:** deeply metabolic. Vines, bioluminescent fungi, and living systems have fused
> with the old glass towers; the buildings breathe and regulate themselves. Synthetic ruin fused with
> organic overgrowth.

The breathing city (`metabolic-city`) and Root-way, whose vines swell on the city's breath.

**The Terminology Rule's exception (Part III).**

> **Exception:** physical corporate signage, plaques, terminals, and legal documentation still read
> *RCC* — the corporation never rebranded its own hardware. This is a deliberate irony: the truth is
> stamped on the equipment, and no one inside the program reads it.

The shrine's door is the hub's old inner door, and its plate still reads RCC. The Stamp is the ending in
which someone reads it.

**The outside read (Part VII §7, "Catastrophe Becomes Scripture").** Part VII is marked "not part of the
original canon documentation", so the game uses it as a reading, never as a fact. It is the clearest
statement of the rule the design keeps: "the myth-distortion is involuntary and neurological, not
cultural erosion over time — the survivors' consciousness itself was altered by the Reset, so the
scripture isn't a game of telephone across generations, it's the immediate, sincere testimony of people
who were there." And: "these people aren't lying or forgetting. They are, biologically, remembering it
differently now." The pilgrims were there. The Closing Synthesis gives the time the game takes: "four to
ten years later a broken civilization will remember not as history but as scripture". Altrulan is set in
the seventh year *(added)*.

**The Ghost (Part VI, The Observers of the Whole).**

> **The Ghost (ability):** Cuttlefish and chameleon splice, peerless active camouflage, total
> invisibility.
>
> **The Flaw (cost):** Lost all toad traits but dermal respiration — must breathe through skin in
> water, and the water must be chemically pure or he suffocates instantly.

"**[OPEN ITEM]** Story content not yet written." Everything he does here is added. Rise and Fall gave
him a tank at Snakehead and a language of signs; the Finale put him in the plinth's cistern in 2240. The
game draws one inference from the bible's Flaw *(added)*: a man who "must breathe through skin in water"
and was under water when the Breath came did not breathe it. He remembers the Age plainly. He is the only
one who does, and he cannot speak.

**What makes the sacrament (Part VI, Dr. Tox §2).** "It is not a power source. It is a cage, and he
built it himself, and he is the only person who can make more of it." The line is about the cage, and
the game borrows its shape *(added)*: the sacrament is the last of what was mixed at Snakehead's bench,
the scripture calls its jars the Toads, and nobody can make more.

**The Reset (Part VI, Dr. Tox, Story: The Finale; Part II).** "**2240** — the Finale ends. The
Bufadienolide Reset. Dr. Tox's sacrifice." (Part II). "What makes it land isn't spectacle; it's the peace
that follows it" (Story: The Finale). Altrulan is that peace, seven years on, and the question of what it
costs to keep.

**The author's ending.** There is none. "Fall and Altrulan have no written ending; theirs are all
proposals." (`dr-tox/SCOPE.md` §5). All four endings below are proposals and **none is marked
`as_written`** (`docs/PILLARS.md` §4). They refine the sketch in `dr-tox/SCOPE.md` §5: Scripture Kept, The
Stamp, Two Readings, The Last Toad, each with its cost.

## 4. Investment

**The hook, minutes 0 to 5.** Before dawn in a cave. Far off, one amber lamp burns on a steel bench
draped like an altar. Close by, a tall tank of perfectly clear water lit cold, and the water moves with
nobody in it. Two hands appear in it, only the hands, and sign: *pilgrims. bells.* *(added: the Ghost
drops his camouflage at the hands to sign.)* Caption: "The seventh year of the Breath." The keeper gets up
from her pallet and fills his travelling flask at the spring with three unmarked clay vessels, to a glaze
line inside the flask, no more and no less: The Measure, at minute 2. At minute 5 she walks out up a
corridor whose walls are painted floor to ceiling as scripture, and a crack opens in the plaster beside
her. Behind it, in black paint on grey concrete, in a hand that is not scripture's: STAY OUTSIDE THE RING.
The player who played the trilogy knows whose sentence that is. The keeper does not.

**What the player comes to care about, before it is threatened.**

- *The pilgrims, as people.* Each has a want that is not about the gods, and each has a crack: something
  from the Age that surfaces in them as grief with the label torn off (section 6). The player walks two
  Root-ways with them and sits with them at rests before the store is opened.
- *The Ghost.* He breathes through his skin in water that must be pure; the keeper carries his flask and
  fills his bowl every night. He beats her at the Mill and tells her things with his hands. He is the only
  person alive who remembers it plainly, and the scripture has made him a saint nobody has seen.
- *The scripture itself.* Its frescoes, bells and reliefs are beautiful and the keeper loves them, and the
  game lets the player love them for an hour before it asks what is under them.
- *The keeper's own peace.* She was fourteen on the plinth when the Breath came down. What she remembers is
  scripture, and it is true to her.

**The question that pulls them to the end.** *What will she keep?* The scripture, the plain reading, the
store for next year, or a word nobody has said since the Age. Behind it, the player's own: *what do they
remember, and what was it?*

### Rules of the world

- **They remember it this way.** "they aren't lying, they genuinely remember it that way now" (Part III).
  Every pilgrim who was there remembers the scripture as what happened. The game never shows a pilgrim
  lying, never shows the keeper lying, and never lets a page or an ending call the scripture false.
  *Done*: section 12, the balance.
- **The cracks** *(added)*. What the Breath did not smooth surfaces in them now and then as a crack: an
  image, a smell, a word, with no story attached, the way Reese's leash surfaced as "a flash with no picture
  attached, just pressure, just grief with the label torn off" (Part VIII, Scene 2 III). A crack is not a
  truer memory; it is another one. *Told* (at rests), *seen* (the wall's cracks mirror them).
- **The sacrament** *(added)*. The scripture's measure, given once a year at the shrine. It does not change
  what anyone remembers: it stills what surfaces, and a pilgrim who takes it goes up into the drum's cap and
  sits in a light that is even. The keeper never takes it. It is never given to a child. Its unit is the
  **lull**, an invented word for an invented amount; a pilgrim's measure is four. *Done* (The Measure).
- **The Toads** *(added)*. The sacrament is the last of what was mixed in jars at Snakehead's bench, kept
  in amber-glazed jars sealed with a clay disc pressed with a toad. The scripture says the Caged left them.
  Nobody can make more: as the bible says of his cage, "he is the only person who can make more of it"
  (Part VI, Dr. Tox §2). Twenty-six lulls are in the open Toads; six more under the seal of the Last Toad.
  *Found*, *done*.
- **Nothing returns to the Toad** *(added rule)*. What has been poured once cannot go back into the store:
  it is given, carried in a vessel, or poured to the earth. *Done* (The Measure).
- **The plate** (Part III, the Terminology Rule's exception). The shrine's door was the hub's inner door, and
  its plate still reads RCC. In the Age the plinth's people said RCC every day ("Villains, Insurgents, the
  plague-marked, and everyone else say 'RCC' or 'the State.'", Part III); in scripture the word is gone, or,
  if a hero said it at the Door, kept as a Name nobody speaks (section 12). *Found*, *done*.
- **The two classes, walking together** *(added)*. Tower-born and plinth-born pilgrims climb side by side.
  The Breath reached both, and the scripture has no class line in it except the verse of the Low. It is the
  peace; it is also the line smoothed over ("Two classes, one sin", Part IV). *Seen*.
- **The breathing city.** "the buildings breathe and regulate themselves" (Part III). The vines between
  the towers swell on the in-breath and slacken on the out; the towers the Breath did not reach are Still
  Mountains, dead glass with dry vines *(added)*. *Done* (Root-way).
- **The bells** *(added)*. The survivors recast the towers' brass and bronze fittings (lobby doors, rails,
  plaques) into bells and hung them in the Glass Mountains' broken crowns and on the shrine's cap. A bell
  rung true is heard across the city; a peal rung true calls pilgrims. *Done* (The Peal).
- **Material ceiling** carried from the Age: "concrete is the material of the future" (Part III, Practical
  result). Now the concrete is plastered, the brass recast, the glass grown over. Nothing new is made except
  by hand.

## 6. Characters

### Hesper Quaye, the keeper (lead) *(added)*

- **Who:** plinth-born; fourteen in the year of the Breath, twenty-one now; keeper of the holy cave since
  the third year. She lives at Snakehead with the Ghost and takes the pilgrims up once a year.
- **Want:** to bring every pilgrim up, and to keep the scripture whole. **Fear:** her own crack, which she
  has never told anyone: a ring painted on a concrete floor, and someone standing outside it who will not
  come in.
- **Voice:** plain to pilgrims, scripture's cadence when she recites. Sample lines *(added)*: "Four for
  each, and nothing back to the Toad." "Mend it while it's wet." "He says: again." The greeting she gives
  at the shrine depends on the Word (section 12): "Hope." or "Breath."
- **She never takes the measure** (the keeper's rule, *added*): what she remembers stays as the Breath
  left it, which is what makes the balance hers and not a dose's.
- **Arc by ending.** *Scripture Kept:* she sits on the stair below the cap with the crowd, the plaster
  smooth. *The Stamp:* she said three letters and stays at the door while people leave. *Two Readings:* she
  goes home with two columns in one book and nobody to read the second. *The Last Toad:* she meets next
  year's pilgrims in front of an empty shelf.

### The Ghost (companion; of the Observers of the Whole)

- **From the bible:** "Cuttlefish and chameleon splice, peerless active camouflage, total invisibility."
  "must breathe through skin in water, and the water must be chemically pure or he suffocates instantly."
  "Every member is a victim first and a weapon second" (Part VI). From Rise, Fall and the Finale: his tank at
  Snakehead; he signs and does not speak; in 2240 he was in the plinth's cistern (with the Observers) or in
  an RCC tank in the arcology's Biosynth floor (without them) *(the latter added)*.
- **In Altrulan** *(added)*: he was under water when the Breath came and breathed none of it. He remembers
  the Age as it was. The scripture counts him among the Twelve as the Unseen; nobody but the keeper knows
  he is real. He drops his camouflage only at the hands, to sign.
- **Want:** that one person knows what was done to the Observers, and that he was a person before he was
  a saint. **Fear:** impure water; and being only a niche.
- **Voice:** signs, shown as short lower-case captions under two hands *(added)*: *pilgrims. bells.*
  *again.* *offices. people at desks.* *a boy in a suit. he held the glass shut.* *he was thin. he hurt.*
  He never signs "liar". He never signs at a pilgrim.
- **Arc by ending.** *Two Readings:* his last sign in the tank, and his hands going still in the water,
  resting. In the others he is beside her, unseen, and the ending card says where he was.

## 10. 2D modes

### Mode A: Root-way (`m2_rootway`, scroll)

Graphic style: **Carved stone frieze** (`docs/graphic_styles.json`): a long limestone frieze in low relief
read left to right; towers as carved pillars, vines carved as ropes that swell into bridges, the band in
file, a meander border marking the way.

- **Story reason:** "the buildings breathe and regulate themselves" (Part III, Architecture). The city
  breathes and the pilgrims walk on its breath. A frieze is how a procession is told in stone: the band is
  carved into the scripture as it goes.
- **Movement:** side-scroll, left to right (Root-way 1) and climbing up and to the right (Root-way 2). The
  keeper leads; the band follows her path at their own speeds, Agathe slowest, Tove beside her father.
  **Inputs:** move; wait; brace (plant her staff in a vine node); sit (at a rest).
- **Rules.** The city breathes on a cycle (6 seconds, steady, if the first peal rang true; ragged, 4 to 8
  seconds, if not, *added*). On the in-breath the vines swell and the spans between towers rise into
  bridges; on the out-breath they slacken into gaps. A pilgrim who reaches a gap waits on the tower's ledge
  for the next in-breath; **nobody falls** (the vines catch them). **Brace** holds a span swollen one
  breath longer for whoever is on it, and costs the keeper the time it takes. **Still Mountains**: a tower
  the Breath did not reach (section 12) has dry vines that never swell; the band goes in and up through its
  broken floors, a longer way. The Ghost goes ahead: his wet footprints on a vine show the span that will
  swell first.
- **Rests.** Three in Root-way 1, two in Root-way 2: a ledge where the band sits for one breath. **Sit with
  one pilgrim** and hear their crack (`pilgrims.heard`); the page draws it as a small carved panel in the
  frieze's upper register, without words. Or press on.
- **Agathe (Root-way 2).** Every span she crosses can be braced. If every one is, she reaches the crown on
  her own feet (`agathe.own_feet`); if not, Bram carries her the last stretch.
- **Progress shown:** the meander border along the frieze's foot with an amber marker; the file's length.
- **Failure and retry:** none. Slowness is the cost: the rests are fewer if the band is behind.
- **Sequences:** `s2_rootway_1` (through the city, about 6 minutes, five towers), `s2_rootway_2` (up the
  Mountain's face, about 5 minutes, four ledges).
- **Difficulty curve:** the first is level and forgiving; the second climbs, the spans are longer, and
  Agathe's crossings ask for every brace.

### Mode B: Fresco walk (`m2_fresco`, scroll)

Graphic style: **Buon fresco on cracked plaster**: earth pigments laid into wet lime plaster in day
patches with visible seams; branching cracks, and where plaster has flaked, the cold grey concrete of the
Age beneath.

- **Story reason:** "The horror gets smoothed into scripture" (Part III). The scripture is literally a
  smoothing: plaster over concrete, painted while wet so the colour becomes the wall. Buon fresco is the one
  painting that is part of its wall, and the one whose damage shows what the wall was.
- **Movement:** side-scroll along a scaffold of two planks, upper and lower, the wall on the far side of
  the planks. **Inputs:** move; change plank; trowel (mend); lean in (look).
- **Rules.** The day's plaster (the *giornata*) is wet in a patch ahead of her, and a band of daylight moves
  along the wall; behind the light the plaster dries *(added)*. Cracks open in the painted wall as she goes.
  At each, three choices: **mend** (trowel fresh lime into it and repaint the verse while the patch is wet,
  a short held stroke; `fresco.mended` +1, `readings.balance` −3); **look** (lean in; the camera tilts into
  the crack and the Age beneath is shown in the Age's own look, gray-teal and film grain, the only place it
  appears in Altrulan; `fresco.looked` +1, `readings.balance` +3, and what was there goes into
  `fresco.seen`); or **pass**. A crack reached after its patch has dried can be looked into or passed, not
  mended.
- **What is under the cracks.** *Fresco 1 (the corridor):* his painted lines from the Finale's corridor (STAY
  OUTSIDE THE RING; TWO MARKS. NOT THREE.); one name from the cult's ring of the 999, with the one detail
  kept of that child; an RCC plate on a conduit; under the Many Shapes panel, a black lacquered plate (§2,
  "Wounds do not bleed; they armor over into black lacquered plate") or, with no `toxin_events`, the cult's
  painted angels. *Fresco 2 (the drum):* the hub's own glass with its etched panel code; an air-handler
  grille (under the verse of the Low); a hard-light emitter housing stamped RCC (under Aleglethan's halos);
  a stack of brass pip tokens set into the plaster (under the Scales); under the Sky-Daughter (First Word
  only), the stencilled words CYCLE 41; a printed tract page, its verse in his words (under the doctrine).
  Never a body, and never a wound.
- **Progress shown:** the wall itself, scene by scene; the daylight band.
- **Failure and retry:** none. A crack left is left.
- **Sequences:** `s2_fresco_1` (the corridor, about 5 minutes, five cracks), `s2_fresco_2` (round the drum,
  about 6 minutes, six cracks, a full circle back to the door).
- **Difficulty curve:** the first is short and lit; the second is long, the light moves faster, the cracks
  open on both planks, and what is under them is the Finale's own room.

## 11. 1D games

Every unit on every page is invented: lulls in unmarked vessels, rows of bells, wax on brass, bronze
pieces. No page names a real substance, shows a source, a quantity, a procedure or a route. The
substances the bible names appear in this design only inside quotations; the game calls the sacrament the
measure and its jars the Toads. **No child is ever given the measure.**

### The Measure (`g1_measure`, exact amounts with fixed vessels)

Graphic style: **Slip-glazed terracotta**: unmarked red clay vessels dipped in cream slip with sgraffito
lines scratched through, an amber pour, tallies scratched into a slip band.

- **Purpose:** the core verb. "a pilgrim's (Altrulan's sacrament)" (`dr-tox/SCOPE.md` §1). The trilogy's
  dose was a shielded syringe and a drip (Rise's The Cage) and a balance (the Finale's Two Pans); here the
  instrument is a set of unmarked clay vessels, and exactness is a matter of care. The finite store is the
  bible's cage turned sacrament: "he is the only person who can make more of it" (Part VI, Dr. Tox §2).
- **Rules.** Vessels of 3, 5 and 8 lulls, unmarked *(added)*. **Fill** a vessel from the source (the spring
  in The Measure 1; the open Toad in The Measure 2). **Pour** one vessel into another until the receiver is
  full or the pourer is empty. **Pour into the bowl.** **Pour to the earth** (empty a vessel onto the
  floor). The target is exact: the Ghost's flask to its glaze line (7 lulls of water), a pilgrim's bowl to
  four lulls. A bowl over four has its excess poured to the earth. **Nothing returns to the Toad**: what
  is in a vessel stays in a vessel (carried to the next pilgrim) or is given or poured out. Every lull poured
  to the earth in The Measure 2 is `measure.waste` and comes off `sacrament`. Every target on the page has a
  solution with no waste, using what the last pilgrim's measure left in the vessels.
- **The Measure 1 (the spring):** water only, nothing to waste. The flask filled exactly
  (`ghost.flask_true`): above the line the water takes up the clay ("the water must be chemically pure",
  The Flaw); below it he runs short. Exact, he breathes easy on the road, and his mills in The Mill tell her
  more (section 12). Not exact, the keeper tops it up at the city's spring and he travels short of breath.
- **The Measure 2 (the altar):** each adult pilgrim kneels in turn, in the order they joined. For each:
  - **Measure** four lulls into their bowl. The page writes their name and want into `measure.given`.
  - **Ask** (only a pilgrim whose crack she heard on the Root-way): "Do you want to keep it?" The pilgrim
    answers by their want (section 6): Lucan, Zora and (if the keeper tells her keepers take none) Iskra keep
    their crack and go up without the measure (`pilgrims.kept_cracks`, +5 balance each; four lulls saved).
    The others take it.
  - **Zora's offering:** if she kept her crack or was measured, she asks for her brother's measure, to pour
    into the altar's basin for him. Give it (`zora.offering`, four lulls) or not.
  - **The Last Toad:** when the open store and the vessels together cannot make the next measure, the page
    shows the Last Toad's seal. **Break it** (`last_toad.opened`; six more lulls; there will never be more)
    or **send the pilgrim up without** (`pilgrims.unmeasured`).
  - Tove kneels with her father and is given her rubbing.
- **The arithmetic** *(added)*: 26 lulls open, 6 sealed, 32 in all. Five adults with no waste use 20; six
  use 24; seven use 28 and need the seal; eight use all 32. Seven with Zora's offering also use all 32.
- **Layout:** the altar's terracotta floor; the vessels on a tray; the open Toad left, the Last Toad set
  apart right under its corded seal; the Ghost's white flask, never to be used for the sacrament; the
  pilgrim's bowl centre; a slip band across the top with each pour scratched as a tally and a crossed tally
  for each lull poured out.
- **Inputs:** tap a vessel, then a target (a vessel, the bowl, the earth); fill; ask; break the seal.
- **Finish:** every pilgrim has knelt. **Failure:** none.
- **Appearances and curve:** `s1_measure_1` (the spring): one target, two vessels (3 and 5), no cost.
  `s1_measure_2` (the altar): three vessels, five to eight targets in a row, the leftovers carried from one
  to the next, a cost for every slip.
- **Hints:** after a minute on one target, the pour that starts a waste-free solution is outlined in slip.
- **Writes:** `ghost.flask_true`; `sacrament`, `measure.waste`, `measure.given`, `last_toad.opened`,
  `pilgrims.kept_cracks`, `pilgrims.unmeasured`, `zora.offering`, `readings.balance`.

### Rubbing (`g1_rubbing`, relief reveal by rubbing)

Graphic style: **Heelball brass rubbing**: black heelball wax rubbed over white paper laid on an engraved
brass: the rubbed field black with every engraved line left white, the rest still blank paper.

- **Purpose:** "the truth is stamped on the equipment, and no one inside the program reads it" (Part III).
  A rubbing makes a copy of what a surface holds without changing it; it shows only what is raised or cut,
  and it shows all of it. The scripture's saints were engraved on the Age's metal.
- **Rules.** A sheet of paper over the relief; four weights to lay at its corners (an unweighted sheet slips
  and the image doubles). The heelball is a block that wears down: each stroke spends some, and there is
  enough for most of a sheet, not all of it *(added)*. Rub in short strokes in one direction: raised parts
  take the wax and come up black, cut lines stay white. Holding a stroke on one place too long tears the
  paper (the sheet still counts, torn). The relief is divided into fields; the player chooses which to spend
  the wax on.
  - *Rubbing 1 (the brass of the Twelve):* the front is twelve standing figures in two rows, the tallest
    haloed: Tove's sheet. The brass stands on two pins and can be turned. Its **other face** is an RCC
    dedication plate cut down to make the brass: RCC, TOWER THREE, DEDICATED BY THE BOARD, and eleven names
    *(added; invented names, none a real person's)*. Rubbing it (`rubbing.reverse_read`, +6 balance) costs
    wax Tove's sheet would have had: her picture then has a blank corner.
  - *Rubbing 2 (the shrine door):* the halos of the relief above (a sheet for the shrine), and below, under
    garlands the player must lift first, the door's small plate. Its raised letters come up black: RCC
    (`rubbing.plate_revealed`, +4 balance). The letters do not mean anything on the page; it only shows them.
- **Layout:** the paper sheet on the relief, the weights, the heelball block at the side; on Rubbing 1 a
  turn handle at the brass's edge.
- **Inputs:** lay a weight; rub (drag); lift garlands; turn the brass; finish.
- **Finish:** when the player finishes or the wax is gone. **Failure:** none.
- **Appearances and curve:** `s1_rubbing_1` (the market): one face is easy, two is a choice of wax.
  `s1_rubbing_2` (the shrine): garlands to lift, a curved relief that tears more easily, and the plate small
  and low.
- **Writes:** `rubbing.sheets`, `rubbing.reverse_read`, `rubbing.plate_revealed`, `readings.balance`.

### The Mill (`g1_mill`, board game, alignment: morris family)

Graphic style: **Cast bronze with patina**: a morris board cast as a bronze plaque with raised bosses at its
points; polished bronze pieces against the same casting gone dark with matte verdigris.

- **Purpose:** the Ghost cannot speak and will not sign in front of the pilgrims *(added)*. At night, over a
  board cut into a bench, he tells her. Morris boards were cut into stone benches wherever people waited; the
  bronze is the scripture's metal, and his pieces are the same casting as hers, gone dark.
- **Rules.** Standard morris *(the family is old and unowned; the rules are the common ones)*. Place pieces
  in turn on the points; then move them along the lines to an empty neighbouring point. Three of one side's
  pieces in a line along a board line is a **mill**: the side that made it lifts one of the other's pieces
  (not from a mill, unless all are in mills). A side reduced to two pieces, or unable to move, loses; thirty
  moves without a mill is a draw. **Each of his mills** is a sign: his hands rise from the board's edge and
  sign one plain memory (`readings.signs` +1, `readings.balance` +4, or +5 with `ghost.flask_true`). **Each
  of hers** is a verse: she says one line of scripture aloud as she lifts his piece (`readings.verses` +1,
  `readings.balance` −2). Winning or losing changes nothing else. The player can play to win, or leave a line
  open so he can tell her more.
- **What he signs** *(added)*, in order, one per mill, never the same twice: *offices. people at desks.* /
  *air. sold by the hour.* / *men. a board. their names on a plate.* / *a boy in a suit. he held the glass
  shut. then he didn't.* / *we were kept in cells. children first.* (with the Observers; or *they took the
  others. i was in a tank.*) / *it was the same man every time.* (the Many Shapes) / *he was thin. he hurt.
  he breathed out.* / *a girl. printed again and again.* (First Word only) / *i was under water. i didn't
  breathe it.*
- **Layout:** the bronze plaque centre; her pieces right, his left; the two hands at the plaque's edge when
  he signs; a line of her verse cast into the border as she recites it.
- **Inputs:** tap a point to place; drag to move; tap a piece to lift after a mill.
- **Finish:** a win, a loss or a draw. **Failure:** none.
- **Appearances and curve:** `s1_mill_1` (the city, six men's morris: two squares, six pieces each); he
  plays gently and makes a mill only if she leaves one open. `s1_mill_2` (the crown, nine men's morris: three
  squares, nine pieces each, flying at three); he plays to make mills, because he wants to tell her.
- **Writes:** `mill.ghost_mills`, `mill.keeper_mills`, `readings.balance`, `readings.signs`, `readings.verses`.

### The Peal (`g1_peal`, change ringing)

Graphic style: **Repoussé gold**: a gold sheet hammered up from behind: bells as domed bosses, the rows of a
touch chased in, one bell's path raised as a ridge, hammer marks over all.

- **Purpose:** the bells call. Change ringing is the one music made entirely of exact rules: no row twice,
  only neighbours change, home to rounds. A city with no clock keeps time and gathers by it *(added)*.
  Repoussé is metal shaped from behind: the bells and the gold are the towers' old fittings, beaten into
  holy things.
- **Rules.** The bells are numbered from the treble (1) to the tenor. Each **row** is an order in which all
  the bells strike once; the first is **rounds** (1234, 123456). Each **change** to the next row swaps one or
  more pairs of **neighbouring** bells, no bell moving more than one place. No row may come twice; the touch
  ends when it comes back to rounds. The player sets each change by tapping the pairs to swap (or, at a lead
  end, by calling **plain** or **bob**); striking is drawn for them as the bells swing. The page raises the
  treble's path through the rows as a ridge, so the method's shape can be seen.
- **Layout:** the gold sheet; the bells across the top as domed bosses; the rows chased in below; the
  treble's ridge; a sally at the side; a punched mark where the next call goes.
- **Inputs:** tap a pair; call plain or bob; confirm.
- **Finish:** back to rounds, or a repeated row (the page lets the touch finish, rung false).
  **Failure:** none.
- **Appearances and curve:**
  - `s1_peal_1` (four bells, the lower loft): plain hunt (8 rows) taught, then the choice to go on into the
    extent, plain bob minimus (all 24 rows). `peal.true_1`: `extent` calls three more pilgrims, `course` one,
    `false` none (`pilgrims.called`). It also sets the city's breath in Root-way.
  - `s1_peal_2` (six bells, the crown): plain hunt on six (12 rows), faster. True: the shrine's hour is heard
    across the city (`peal.true_2`; `shrine.crowd` = following ÷ 2 + 20, else following ÷ 4).
  - `s1_peal_3` (the shrine's cap): the closing touch, call changes from rounds to queens and home. No way
    to ring it false. If the store is spent, the tenor's clapper is leathered and it rings half-muffled, as
    for a death (`peal.muffled`).
- **Hints:** the pair the method wants next glows in the gold after five seconds (Peal 1 only).
- **Writes:** `peal.true_1`, `pilgrims.called`, `peal.true_2`, `shrine.crowd`, `peal.muffled`.

### Not in Altrulan: a reconstruction page

Rise has Aftermath, Fall Composite and the Finale Split Field, each a reconstruction of what his body did
(the author's ruling of 2026-11-19, `dr-tox/SCOPE.md` §9). That ruling applies to the trilogy. Altrulan's
investigation is the reverse of reconstruction: the keeper does not rebuild the Age, she decides how much of
it to let through. The design does not want a reconstruction page, and has none.

## 12. Core systems

### The balance: two readings, and the rule that holds them

`readings.balance`, 0 to 60, starts at 10. It is how much of the Ghost's plain reading the keeper holds
beside her own scripture.

| Source | Change |
| --- | --- |
| A crack looked into (Fresco walk) | +3 |
| A crack mended (Fresco walk) | −3 |
| A Ghost mill (The Mill) | +4, or +5 with `ghost.flask_true` |
| A keeper's mill (The Mill) | −2 |
| The brass's other face (Rubbing 1) | +6 |
| The plate (Rubbing 2) | +4 |
| A pilgrim keeps their crack (The Measure 2) | +5 |

**It never passes 60** *(added)*. Her memory is the bible's: "they aren't lying, they genuinely remember it
that way now" (Part III). The Ghost's reading can come level with hers and never above it; there is no state
in which the game says the scripture was false. At **40 or more**, the Book at the lectern offers a second
column (Two Readings). A player who looks into every crack (11 × 3), lets the Ghost make four mills with a
true flask (20) and turns the brass (6) reaches 60 from 10 without anything else; one who mends every crack
and wins every mill ends near 0.

### The sacrament

`sacrament`, 0 to 32 lulls: 26 open, 6 under the Last Toad's seal. −4 per measure given, −4 for Zora's
offering, −1 per lull poured to the earth. At 0, The Last Toad. Leftovers in the vessels at the end of the
rite are stoppered and carried home: still sacrament.

### The band

`pilgrims.adults` = 5 + `pilgrims.called` (0 to 3, from the first peal). Tove is not counted: she is never
measured. `pilgrims.heard` (at the Root-way's rests, up to five) decides who can be asked at the altar.

### The crowd

`shrine.crowd` = `following` ÷ 2 + 20 if the second peal rang true, `following` ÷ 4 if not (default 50 or
15). It is how many hear the plate read in The Stamp, and how many sit on the stair in Scripture Kept.

### The Book and the plate

At the lectern the keeper writes. **Scripture** (the default): the year's ascent in the shrine's words. **Both**
(only at balance 40 or more): two columns, the right in her own hand. Then, if the plate was rubbed, one more
choice: **read it aloud** through the open door (`stamp.read_aloud`). She does not know what the letters mean.
If the scripture holds the Name (below), the crowd hears a holy word on the Suit-Demons' door.

### The Word, as scripture

The Terminology Rule (Part III) arrives as liturgy. `reese.hub_word` `hope`: the shrine's greeting is "Hope"
and Aleglethan's verse is "he said Hope and set his light aside". `silent`: the greeting is "Breath", and the
verse "he kept silence and held the light". `rcc`: "he spoke the Name, and his halo went out and came again".
If `reese.said_rcc` or `cally.said_rcc` is true, the scripture holds **the Name**: a holy word of three
sounds, written as three empty rings, never said *(added)*. Reading the plate aloud is then saying it.

### Colour as HUD

There is no HUD meter in Altrulan. The bible's grammar survives in what it means *(added)*: amber is the
sacrament and the votive lamp (contained, kept); the Age's cold gray-teal appears only through cracks and on
the back of the brass; **luminous green appears nowhere**: the toxin unbound is gone from the world, and the
Many Shapes are carved in plain stone.

## 13. Endings

The resolver checks endings in priority order and takes the first whose condition is true; the last is
`when: true`, so exactly one ending fires. **None is marked `as_written`**: the bible gives Altrulan no ending
(`dr-tox/SCOPE.md` §5). Each ending card puts its cost first. None of them treats the scripture as a lie.

| 2 | The Last Toad | no | The sacrament is spent: the open Toads and the Last Toad | `{"is": "sacrament", "lte": 0}` | `s1_measure_2`, `s1_peal_3`, `s3_end_last_toad` | Every pilgrim this year ascended; no pilgrim ascends again |

| 4 | Scripture Kept | no | None of the above: the plate unread, the store not spent, the Book kept as scripture | `true` | `s1_measure_2`, `s1_peal_3`, `s3_end_kept` | Peace; the Age only as myth |

### Scripture Kept

Every pilgrim who asked was given the measure, and they sit on the drum's cap in a still, even light while the
crowd sits on the stair below. The keeper sits with the crowd. The cracks she mended are smooth; the ones she
passed are still there, and nobody looks at them. If she sent pilgrims up without the measure rather than
break the seal, their names are on the ending card, with their wants. The bible's rule is kept to the letter:
"they aren't lying, they genuinely remember it that way now" (Part III). **Cost:** peace; the Age only as
myth. The names under the plaster stay under it: the ring of the 999, the Board on the brass's back, the
plinth's people as people. The dead are gods and demons only. Last image: the cap from above, the pilgrims
small in the light, the city breathing round the drum.

### The Stamp

She reads the three letters through the open door: "R. C. C." The scripture does not break: every pilgrim
still remembers the Breath as she taught it, because that is how they remember it. But the plinth-born in the
crowd know the letters in their mouths before they know why (in the Age they said them every day, Part III's
rule), and a name now sits under the Suit-Demons that nobody can place. Where the scripture holds the Name, the
crowd has heard a holy word read off the Door, and does not know what that means. Some kneel; some go down the
stair and do not come back to the shrine. Nobody calls the scripture a lie, and nobody can now hear it without
the three letters under it. The bible's irony is spent: "the truth is stamped on the equipment, and no one
inside the program reads it" (Part III), and someone read it. **Cost:** truth; the peace cracks: as many of the
crowd go home unsettled as heard it. Last image: the plate bare in grey daylight, wax still on its letters,
and the stair emptying.

### Two Readings

She held the Ghost's reading level with her own and wrote both: the Breath on the left in the shrine's hand,
and on the right, in hers, what two hands signed to her over a morris board. Neither column is a lie; neither
corrects the other. The pilgrims went up as the scripture says. Back at Snakehead she reads the Book by the
tank, and the Ghost's hands sign one more thing, *thank you* or the last of his signs not yet given, and go
still in the water, resting. **Cost:** the keeper carries both alone. She can say neither to anyone without the
other, and nobody else reads the right-hand column. Last image: the open Book, two columns, one lamp, the water
still.

### The Last Toad

The store is spent: the open Toads, and the Last Toad under its broken seal. Every pilgrim this year who
wanted the measure was given it and went up into the light. The closing peal rang half-muffled. A year later the
bells call from the Glass Mountains and the pilgrims come down the road to Snakehead, and the keeper meets them
in front of an empty shelf. Only the Caged could make it, as only he could make his cage (Part VI, Dr. Tox §2),
and he is in the scripture. **Cost:** every pilgrim this year ascended; no pilgrim ascends again. Last image: the
broken seal in her hand under the amber lamp, the bells far off.

### Why only one can be true

The resolver stops at the first true condition. A word read aloud cannot be unread, so The Stamp outranks
every other ending, whatever else she did. A spent store is spent whatever she writes, so The Last Toad
outranks the Book. Two Readings needs a choice only the balance unlocks. Scripture Kept is what remains.

### How a player reaches each one on purpose

- **Scripture Kept:** mend the cracks, play the Mill to win, rub the front of the brass only, leave the plate
  under its garlands; measure without waste; write scripture.
- **The Stamp:** in Rubbing 2, lift the garlands and rub the plate until its letters come up; at the lectern,
  read it aloud.
- **The Last Toad:** ring the first peal's extent (eight adults), measure without waste, break the seal; or ring
  the plain course (six adults), give Zora's offering and waste a little; spend it all.
- **Two Readings:** fill his flask exactly; look into the cracks; let the Ghost make his mills; turn the brass;
  sit with Lucan, Zora and Iskra on the Root-way and let them keep their cracks; write both; do not read the
  plate.

- **Voice:** the keeper speaks plainly and recites scripture; the pilgrims speak their wants and their cracks. The

## 20. Risks and open questions

**Risks.**

- *The scripture read as a lie.* The bible's rule is the episode's spine. Mitigation: the balance cannot pass 60,
  so no state of the game sets the Ghost's reading above the keeper's memory; no ending, card or line calls the
  scripture false; the pilgrims are never shown lying or fooled; the Ghost never signs at a pilgrim and never
  signs "liar"; cracks are "another memory", not a truer one; The Stamp's cost is that the peace cracks, not that
  the scripture falls.
- *Belief as mockery.* Mitigation: the frescoes, bells and rite are made with care and played with care first;
  the keeper loves them; the player is asked to keep them before being shown what is under them.
- *The sacrament as drug use.* Mitigation: the substances the bible names appear only in quotations in this
  design; in play there is only "the measure" in "lulls"; no page shows a source, a procedure, a real quantity or
  a route; the sacrament stills and does not intoxicate on screen (a pilgrim sits in an even light); **no child
  is ever given it**; the keeper never takes it.
- *Memory manipulation as a happy ending.* Mitigation: the sacrament does not change what anyone remembers
  (section 5); every ending's cost is printed first; Scripture Kept's cost is the names left under the plaster.
- *The Amazonian origin.* The bible's toad comes from "an isolated Amazonian tribe" (Story: Origin), and Part VII
  §6 names a real practice as its root. Mitigation: the sacrament's rite is invented and modelled on no real
  ceremony; no Indigenous people, dress, iconography or practice appears; the toad appears only as a stamp on a
  clay seal; nothing in Altrulan names a real people or rite.
- *Invention load.* Altrulan is the bible's thinnest material. Mitigation: every addition is marked and listed in
  section 21 and can be struck without touching the bible's own lines (the Glass Mountains, the Suit-Demons,
  Aleglethan and his halos, the twelve, the metabolic city, the plate).
- *Look-alikes.* The bible's own comparisons (Part VII §7) stay contrast. The pilgrimage and the morris and
  change-ringing rules are old common forms, not any game's.
- *The trilogy's people as icons.* Mitigation: none is shown alive or as a person; reliefs and frescoes follow the
  myth only, and no key art is made of them before the author's design systems.

**Questions only the author can answer.**

1. **Altrulan after The Cage Holds and The Light Bringer's Wall** (`games/dr-tox-finale/DESIGN.md` §20 question 7,
   still open). This design plays from The Reset and The First Word only, and the menu does not open Altrulan from
   the other two. How it would play after them, if the author rules that it does:
   - *After The Light Bringer's Wall* (`panes.sealed`): "only the plinth and the road get the dose" (the Finale's
     ending). Only the plinth's and the road's people were altered; the towers' people were not, and the Glass
     Mountains stayed sealed glass. The pilgrimage would go from Snakehead along the road to the plinth and stop at
     the wall: the shrine would be the maintenance gallery outside the hub, where the Caged died, with the hard
     light still between him and the city, and Aleglethan the Light Bringer who walled the Mountain. Root-way would
     run only over the plinth's shanties (every tower Still); the Fresco walk would be painted on the outside of the
     glass. The tower-born pilgrims (Agathe, Lucan, Pim) would not exist; in their place, people from inside who
     remember plainly and do not come. The Stamp would read the plate on the sealed door from outside it. The bible's
     rule would hold for the pilgrims; the city behind the glass would be a second, unaltered memory, which is a
     larger change than this design makes, and why it is the author's.
   - *After The Cage Holds* (`hub.held_back`): no Reset in 2240; Dr. Tox lives and the cult attacks without him. There
     is no Breath, so no scripture, unless a later Reset is made. Proposed: Altrulan does not follow The Cage Holds
     unless the author rules that he made the Reset later (the Finale's §20 proposal: "a later one after The Cage
     Holds"). If so, the scripture would count from that later Breath, the plinth's verse would be the cult's attack,
     and the doctrine would be higher. Otherwise the save ends at the Finale.
